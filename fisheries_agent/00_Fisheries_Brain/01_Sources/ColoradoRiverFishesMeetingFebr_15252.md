# Colorado River Fishery Project Annual Meeting Announcement and Research Prioritization Discussion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #recovery-program

## Executive Summary

This document consists of correspondence from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] announcing the annual meeting for February 14-15, 1989, in [[Grand Junction, Colorado]]. The primary focus is planning discussion of the [[Biotechnical Group]]'s research prioritization process for endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The correspondence includes a letter from Tom Nesler of the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] outlining concerns about the prioritization process and its relationship to the FY 1989 Work Plan, along with budget tables showing project allocations totaling approximately $2,092,000.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] coordinates annual meetings of basin workers involved in endangered fish recovery efforts. The 1989 meeting was scheduled at the Grand Junction Hilton with a block of 20 rooms reserved. A $5 registration fee was assessed to cover refreshments. The meeting agenda included a specific discussion session on the [[Biotechnical Group]] (BTG) research prioritization process, led by Tom Nesler.

The [[Biotechnical Group]] developed a research prioritization process to evaluate and direct research needs for endangered fishes during recovery implementation. This was the second year of using this process, which solicited ideas and project outlines from field biologists and researchers, then rated submissions according to specific criteria resulting in numerical rankings. Confusion existed among contributors regarding the appropriate level of detail required (brief sketch versus full research proposal with hypotheses, methodology, and detailed budget).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The work focuses on endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The FY 1989 Work Plan included multiple project categories managed by different groups including the Habitat Management Group (HMG), Research and Monitoring Division (RMD), and others. Projects addressed habitat management, flow management, species monitoring, stocking programs, and inter/intraspecific interactions.

The correspondence emphasized the importance of maintaining biological assessment of endangered fish needs while administering the recovery program, noting the "profusion of committees and task forces" that had studied specific topics over the past decade. The goal was to establish a feedback link between administrative decision-making structures and field biologists working directly with the species.

## Key Findings

**Meeting Logistics:**
- Annual meeting scheduled February 14-15, 1989, at [[Grand Junction]] Hilton
- 20 rooms reserved; government rate $37.00 single, $47.00 double
- Meeting room provided at no charge if room commitment fulfilled
- $5 registration fee for refreshments

**Research Prioritization Process Concerns:**
- Lack of clarity about submission requirements (brief sketch vs. full proposal)
- Limited feedback to contributors about results and how prioritization influences ongoing and new research
- Unclear impact of prioritization on FY 1989 Work Plan due to many ongoing projects predating the process
- Need for feedback link between administrative structure and field biologists

**FY 1989 Budget Allocations:**
- Total program funding: approximately $2,092,000
- Contingency projects (Table 3.2): $216,000 total
  - Survey available water rights: $100,000
  - Cataract [[Gila]] and CSF monitoring: $20,000
  - Inter/intraspecific interactions: $96,000

**Project Categories in Work Plan:**
- Program Management (PMD): multiple projects including flow management, stocking programs, habitat development
- Habitat Management Group (HMG): habitat mapping, hydrologic modeling, flow studies
- Research and Monitoring (RMD): species monitoring, population studies, life history research
- Native Nonnative (NN): species interactions, competition studies

**Discussion Objectives:**
- Allow basin workers to air questions about prioritization process and resulting work plan
- Provide [[Biotechnical Group]] with feedback
- Put concerns, criticisms, and priorities of field biologists on record
- Assess progress on critical needs of endangered fishes
