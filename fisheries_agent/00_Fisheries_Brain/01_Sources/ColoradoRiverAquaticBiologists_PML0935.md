# Colorado River Aquatic Biologists Annual Meeting Compilation (Laughlin, NV, January 2001)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation gathers annual reports and presentations from agencies managing fishes of the [[Colorado River]] system, presented at the Colorado River Aquatic Biologists (CRABS) annual meeting in Laughlin, Nevada, on January 10-11, 2001. Topics span Lake Powell sport fisheries, hatchery production of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] at several facilities, native fish recruitment studies, fish health and disease policy, flow experiments below [[Glen Canyon Dam]], [[Lake Mead]] shad and gill net surveys, habitat use by adult razorback suckers in the [[Imperial Division]], and a GIS model for prioritizing razorback sucker stocking sites. Recurring themes are low recruitment of native fishes, predation by nonnative species, disease in hatcheries, and the difficulty of stocking success in a degraded river.

## Background & Description

The document is a mixed set of items, not a single study. It includes:

- **Lake Powell report** (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources) on the sport fishery: shad, [[Striped Bass]], and [[Smallmouth Bass]].
- **Hatchery reports** from [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]] (Arizona Game and Fish Department, 1999-2000), [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]], [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] stocking tables, and [[Wahweap Fish Hatchery]] (2000).
- **Research summaries** on native fish recruitment in the upper basin, [[Lake Mead]] shad and gill net surveys (Nevada Division of Wildlife), razorback sucker studies in Lake Mead and Lake Mohave (BIOAQUATIC Research), and USGS fishery investigations on the Lower Colorado River.
- **Policy essay** by fish pathologist Ron Goede (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, 1994) on aquaculture disease, wild fish, and incremental degradation.
- **Management tools**, including a Bureau of Reclamation GIS stocking-prioritization model and a meeting agenda.

Species mentioned include razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*), bonytail (*Gila elegans*), humpback chub (*Gila cypha*), flannelmouth sucker (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake Powell.** Striped bass were in poor condition, with few large fish, and the population was dominated by mature males with rare mature females. Smallmouth bass growth was slowing and fish were small and numerous. Shad production was modest in canyons.

**Glen Canyon Dam flow experiments (2000).** Low steady summer flows (LSSF, constant 8,000 cfs) were tested from 6/01/00 to 10/01/00, along with two 4-day "spike" flows (31,000 cfs). Young-of-year rainbow trout comprised 50% of the September catch at Lees Ferry, the highest proportion on record. Modeling indicated survival of young-of-year trout is negatively related to daily flow fluctuations. Salt cedar colonization above the 8,000 cfs stage was extensive, and mainstem water temperature reached 19°C at RM 225.

**Lake Mead.** Shad production in 2000 was substantially below average, the third straight year of low numbers. Largemouth bass and bluegill dominated electrofishing catches. [[Smallmouth Bass]] appeared in fall gill nets and had grown to 5.11% of the fall catch.

**Environmental stressors (Goede).** The author describes environmental episodes in arid western rivers: water diversion, reservoir drawdown, agricultural runoff, grazing of riparian vegetation, and channelization. Together these compound with pathogens to erode survival.

## Key Findings

### Hatchery Production (Bubbling Ponds, 1999-2000)
- About 78,000 razorback sucker fry, fingerlings, and subadults were received in 1999; 12,264 were stocked into the Colorado River system that year.
- About 36,000 were received in 2000. Growth fell short of the projected 0.75-1.0 inch/month, reaching 0.3-0.5 inch/month, attributed to higher stocking densities.
- Production in 2000 was 4,275 fish below 1999. Stocking fell to 5,7xx razorbacks (OCR-garbled count).
- Mortality causes were bacterial infections (*Aeromonas* sp., *Pseudomonas* sp., *Flexibacter columnaris*), parasites including Ich and monogenean flukes, and predation by herons, eagles, ospreys, mergansers, otters, and raccoons.
- Scoliosis (spinal deformity) appeared in a lined pond, prompting a diet change that added spirulina, krill, and catfish feed.
- Pond 8 Upper was completed in 1999; Ponds 1, 2, and 3 Upper were finished in October 2000, and construction costs fell by about $15,000.

### Ouray National Fish Hatchery
- Twenty-two to twenty-five single-mated-pair razorback sucker broodstock lots were held.
- Estimated capacity is about 50,000 fish/year under existing water supply, rising to about 150,000 with increased flow and water quality.
- Density trials showed fish at higher stocking densities are smaller at year's end, regardless of food availability.
- Distribution in 2000 was 24,471 fish and 50,290 eggs.

### Willow Beach and Wahweap Hatcheries
- Willow Beach stocking tables list lot numbers, stocking dates, and lakes (Havasu, Mohave, Parker, Verde River). Losses to mergansers were recorded.
- Wahweap (2000) stocked 15,037 bonytail into the Colorado River and 19,997 into the Green River, plus 10,000 transferred to Colorado and 21,237 more in the fall, for about 114,466 bonytail in total. Smallmouth bass fry were shipped to South Carolina and 270,000 wipers went to Northern Utah.

### Native Fish Recruitment (Upper Basin Study, Mueller)
- Over 40,000 fish were captured across sampling zones, using seines, electrofishing, trammel nets, and hoop nets.
- Inflow areas held higher relative abundance than reservoir sites; red shiner, threadfin shad, carp, and striped bass were most common.
- No evidence was found that native fish survive in floodplain habitat; floodplain habitat was rapidly exploited by nonnative predators.

### Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Studies (Abate and Holden)
- Lake Mohave has produced recruitment for about 40 years, while Lake Mead recruitment appears limited.
- Las Vegas Bay had higher phosphorus and nutrient levels than other sites, and the amount of cover near spawning areas (about 2.5-3 times the cover at other sites) may be a key factor for larval recruitment.

### Imperial Division Habitat Use (Gurtin and Bradford, Arizona Game and Fish Department)
- Phase III (1998-1999): 62 razorback suckers were tracked; 603 contacts over 32 surveys. Logistic regression models classified backwater use with 84.0% overall accuracy (83.0% in validation).
- Models predicted non-use better than use. Surface area and depth were more influential than dynamic variables such as temperature or conductivity.
- Phase IV (December 1999-present): 75 fish were released; 293 contacts over 15 surveys. Some fish used newly restored backwaters.
- Flathead catfish use was restricted to main and side channels, with no catfish found in backwaters.

### GIS Stocking Prioritization Model (Broderick, Callahan, Mueller; Bureau of Reclamation)
- The model weights distance to hazard (under 15 km high risk), predator presence, reservoir backwater presence, and distance to riverine backwaters on a 10 m grid.
- High survivability: Lake Mead, Lake Mohave (upper two-thirds), Mohave Valley, Topock, and Havasu (upper half).
- Moderate survivability: Parker (upper two-thirds), Palo Verde, Cibola, and Imperial (upper two-thirds).
- Low survivability: Laguna, Yuma, and Limitrophe.

### Fish Health Policy (Goede, 1994)
- Disease in wild populations follows the same three-condition model as hatchery disease: susceptible hosts, virulent pathogens, and stress.
- The author argues that the greatest threat to wild fish is introducing new pathogens through hatchery stocking, and that agencies should be conservative about introductions.

### Other Notable Results
- Mexico: the Parker office worked with Pinacate and Upper Gulf of California/Colorado River Delta Biospheres to support desert pupfish and Quitobaquito pupfish populations.
- Refuges: pupfish refugia were established at Imperial National Wildlife Refuge, with a further refugium planned at Bill Williams NWR.
- Achii Hanyo produced bonytail and monitored them in 2000 (22,252 fish placed in four ponds).
- Lake Havasu round-ups collected 38 razorback suckers and one bonytail in the first annual round-up.
