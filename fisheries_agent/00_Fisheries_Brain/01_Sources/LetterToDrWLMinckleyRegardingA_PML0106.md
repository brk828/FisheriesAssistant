# Native Fish Field Survey Summary: Virgin River, Bonita Creek, and Tonto National Forest (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This September 30, 1992 letter from [[Rob Bettaso]], Native Fish Field Projects Coordinator with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], reports field observations from several sites in Arizona and Nevada. It was written to [[W. L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]] as a follow-up to a discussion on native fish management. The letter summarizes depletion seining on the [[Virgin River]], native fish surveys on [[Bonita Creek]] and the [[Gila River]], and ongoing work in the [[Tonto National Forest]]. It notes that native fishes remain present at several sites but are often outnumbered by non-native species. The letter also raises a question about possible hybrid chub on [[Silver Creek]] and proposes future collaboration.

## Background & Description

- **Author:** Rob Bettaso, Native Fish Field Projects Coordinator, Arizona Game & Fish Department (cc: Kirk Young, NFP Program Manager).
- **Recipient:** Dr. W. L. Minckley, Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe.
- **Purpose:** To share survey summaries Minckley had requested, following a meeting earlier that month on native fish management in Arizona.
- **Field team (Virgin River):** Staff from the Arizona Game & Fish Department's Native Fish Program, Nevada Division of Wildlife, Arizona Game & Fish, USFWS, and the Las Vegas Valley Water District.
- **Method:** Depletion seining, used at three sites in Nevada and two in Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Virgin River:** Running moderately high and very muddy during sampling on September 23–24, which likely affected capture conditions.
- **Bonita Creek:** Upper reaches held a healthy native community. Lower reaches were dominated by [[Red Shiner]] and [[Gambusia]].
- **Gila River near the San Francisco confluence:** Numerous young channel and flathead catfish were present.
- **Cave Creek (near Ashdale):** High numbers of non-native and native species, including an occasional [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Pinto Creek:** Described as a riparian area with abundant native fish and no non-native fish found in the section surveyed.
- **Management context:** The Silver Creek and Cave Creek sites were chosen partly because of recent work tied to highway expansion (the Beeline Highway) and a proposed controlled burn. The Pinto Creek site was reviewed in connection with a proposed dredging operation and placer mine near the Henderson Ranch above Roosevelt Lake.

## Key Findings

**Virgin River (September 23–24, 1992):**
- Two [[Woundfin]] were caught at Halfway Wash.
- [[Gila Chub]] were found in low numbers at Mesquite, Beaver Dam Wash, and Cedar Pockets.
- About 3 [[Spinedace]] were found in Beaver Dam Creek; none were found in the mainstem.
- Two [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and several [[Desert Sucker]] were recorded at Mesquite, Beaver Dam Wash, and Cedar Pockets, along with numerous [[Speckled Dace]].
- Red shiner made up a large share of total species occurrence across the five sites. The exact percentage is illegible in the scanned source.
- Gambusia, yellow bullhead, and channel catfish together outnumbered all native species combined.

**Bonita Creek and Gila River:**
- Upper Bonita Creek supported a healthy native community: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], Desert and Sonora suckers, and Gila chub.
- Lower Bonita Creek was dominated by red shiner and gambusia.
- Numerous young channel and flathead catfish were found in the Gila near its confluence with the San Francisco River.
- The author noted interest in baseline fish inventories of the Gila River and its tributaries within and adjacent to the National Conservation Area (N.C.A.).

**Tonto National Forest:**
- A multi-year fish survey program is underway on Sycamore, Cave, and Silver creeks, part of the wider Tonto Creek drainage work.
- **Silver Creek:** Only chub were found, in numerous pools along the intermittent section. The author suspects these are [[Hybridization|hybrids]] and asked Minckley to review specimens.
- **Cave Creek:** High numbers of longfin dace, fathead minnow, and green sunfish, plus an occasional Gila topminnow near Ashdale.
- **Sycamore Creek:** Scheduled for survey the following week.
- **Pinto Creek:** Abundant desert sucker and longfin dace; no non-native fish were found in this section.

**Requests and next steps:**
- The author will attend the DFC meeting in November.
- Proposed short course training for Native Fish Program personnel at ASU.
- Minckley was asked to send information on large, long-term data sets to help set priority survey points.
- Field data sheets were offered on request.
