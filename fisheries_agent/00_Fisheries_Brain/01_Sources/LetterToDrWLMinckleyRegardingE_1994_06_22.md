# Letter to Dr. W.L. Minckley Regarding Endangered Species Permit Guidelines

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae #colorado-river-basin

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary biological research paper but an **administrative/regulatory correspondence packet** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Arizona Fishery Resources Office (AzFRO), dated June 22, 1994. It transmits **draft permit guidelines** ("Guidelines for Biological Investigations of Threatened and Endangered Fishes," Region 2) to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), a leading ichthyologist working on Colorado River Basin fishes. The letter and attachments establish standardized protocols for the issuance, review, and monitoring of endangered species collection/research permits for six federally listed fish taxa native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. While it contains no direct morphological or novel ecological data, it is highly informative regarding **species management jurisdiction, permitting criteria, sampling/handling standards, and inter-agency coordination** for these taxa.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or physical descriptive data for the listed species are provided in this source. The document instead specifies **data collection standards** that permittees must follow when morphological data *are* gathered in the field, including:
- Standardized **metric units** for all weights and measures.
- Recording of **length/weight, meristics, morphology,** and other physical measurements as part of approved sampling protocols.
- Collection of **genetic material** (biochemical genetics, mtDNA, nuclear DNA), blood, and gametes under permitted studies.
- Documentation of **tagging/marking type and number** of specimens marked, submitted in standardized (IBM-compatible) data formats to the Lead Station.

No species-specific morphology (e.g., body shape of [[Razorback Sucker]] or [[Humpback Chub]]) is described in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The document identifies jurisdictional and geographic ecological context rather than niche/trophic data:
- **[[Apache Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus apache*) — subject of permitted field studies (J. Rinne); native salmonid of Arizona's White Mountains streams.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*), **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, = Colorado pikeminnow), **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*), and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-river fishes of the mainstem **Colorado River**, with AzFRO's lead responsibility explicitly **limited to the lower basin** (below Glen Canyon Dam); upper basin permitting falls under **Region 6 (Denver)**.
- **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*) — endemic to the **Little Colorado River** drainage; contact designated for Little Colorado River/Grand Canyon studies (Dr. Owen Gorman).
- Studies on the **San Juan River** population(s) of these species require coordination with the **New Mexico Fishery Resources Office (Dexter, NM)**.
- Referenced ongoing field programs: Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (ASU activities), razorback sucker work (G. Mueller), Apache trout work (J. Rinne) — indicating active population monitoring, likely involving mark-recapture and translocation within Colorado River Basin reaches and tributaries.

## Key Findings
- **Lead Station designation:** AzFRO holds lead USFWS responsibility for six T&E fish taxa: [[Apache Trout]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — based on expertise, geographic proximity, and recovery team involvement.
- **Permit policy shift:** "Blanket permits" will no longer be issued to non-resource agencies; permits must be tied to a **specific study plan** demonstrating benefit to the species.
- **Evaluation criteria for permits** include: scientific merit and soundness of study design, contribution of new biological information to recovery/management, coordination to avoid duplicative sampling, justified sampling protocols, [[Section 7 Consultation]] (Endangered Species Act) clearance, and applicant compliance history.
- **Required protocol components** for any permitted study: field handling/collection, fish transportation, acclimatization/therapeutants, sampling methodology, housing/propagation, and disposition — each requiring lead-station approval, including equipment sterilization and back-up life-support/emergency procedures.
- **Fish Health Center (FHC) role:** Must be notified at least two weeks prior to any inter-station or inter-basin fish movement; responsible for examination, testing, quarantine, and pathogen-transfer prevention.
- **Data standards:** Metric units mandatory; tag numbers and marking data must accompany all datasets; capture data submitted in IBM-compatible digital format; Lead Station shares data with resource agencies and stakeholders.
- **Geographic/jurisdictional split:** Lower Colorado River basin managed by AzFRO (Region 2); upper basin (above Glen Canyon Dam) managed by Region 6 (Denver); San Juan River studies coordinated through the New Mexico Fishery Resources Office.
- **Reporting requirements:** Permittees are classified as "cooperators" in recovery programs and must submit annual reports and notify the Lead Station of unusual findings, mortalities, or problems affecting species status or permit compliance.
- **Named personnel/contacts:** James N. Hanson (AzFRO Supervisor), Dr. Stuart Leon and Dr. Chuck Minckley (lower Colorado River contact), Dr. Owen Gorman (Little Colorado River/Grand Canyon contact), John Rinne (Apache trout studies), Gordon Mueller (razorback sucker studies) — indicating an active multi-agency [[Endangered Species Recovery Program]] network for Colorado River Basin fishes in the early 1990s.
