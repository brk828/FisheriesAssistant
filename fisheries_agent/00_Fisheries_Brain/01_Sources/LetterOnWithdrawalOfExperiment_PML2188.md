# Letter on Withdrawal of Experimental Population Designation for Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 letter from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (Inland Fisheries Division) to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Endangered Species Office, Sacramento) reports that the state no longer considers it necessary to designate planned reintroductions of [[Bonytail]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the lower Colorado River as [[Experimental Population]]s under Section 10(j) of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Cooperative arrangements with federal refuge managers and tribal representatives made the designation unnecessary. The Department therefore asked the Service to halt the rulemaking process it had requested in July 1985.

## Background & Description

The letter follows up a July 1985 request for Service help in developing a rulemaking package. That package would have designated the reintroduction of both species as experimental populations under Section 10(j) of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The purpose was to reintroduce the two [[Endangered Species]] into suitable habitat in the lower basin, as called for in their [[Recovery Plan]]s.

The recovery plans call for reintroducing "grown-out" fish, meaning juveniles raised to larger size before release. The letter explains that this step reduces the vulnerability of transplanted fish to the many [[Predaceous Introduced Fishes]] now established in the Colorado River. The Department has the capacity to grow out both species at its [[Niland Fish Hatchery]], in ponds recently built to rear endangered Colorado River fishes. Its planned source of young fish was the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The plan was to release subadults into isolated or partially isolated backwaters along the lower river.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Threat:** Introduced predatory fishes are established in the Colorado River and prey on transplanted native fish. Growing fish to a larger size before release is the stated method for reducing this predation.
- **Release habitat:** The search for suitable sites identified several backwaters on [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]], [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], and a third refuge whose name is illegible in the scanned copy. Tribal lands with reservation areas along the lower river were also considered.
- **Land-use constraints:** Originally, the Department expected that designating the releases as experimental populations would be needed to secure cooperation from affected landowners and land-management agencies.

## Key Findings

- **Cooperation removed the need for experimental status.** Refuge managers at Imperial and Cibola NWRs and Region 2 Endangered Species staff helped identify suitable release sites. They also agreed to allow releases of grown-out fish into refuge waters without experimental population status.
- **Prior releases already made.** The Department reported that it had already released over 300 grown-out bonytail into a backwater on Imperial NWR as a cooperative venture.
- **Tribal support.** Representatives of Indian tribes with reservation lands along the lower Colorado River expressed a positive attitude toward reintroducing native Colorado River fishes on their lands.
- **Request to withdraw.** Because of these cooperative arrangements, the Department asked the Service not to proceed further with the rulemaking initiated by its July 1985 letter.
- **Contact:** Questions were directed to Darlene McGriff of the Department's Endangered Species Program. The letter was copied to the Inland Fisheries Division, Region 5, and the Natural Heritage Section.
