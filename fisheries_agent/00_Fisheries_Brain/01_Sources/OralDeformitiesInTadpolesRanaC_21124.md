# Coal Ash-Associated Oral Deformities in Bullfrog Tadpoles and Their Effects on Grazing and Growth

#herpetology #aquatic-ecology #ranidae

## Executive Summary

Bullfrog tadpoles (*Rana catesbeiana*) living in [[Coal Ash]] settling basins and a downstream [[Drainage Swamp]] at the [[Savannah River Site]] had far more oral deformities than tadpoles from reference sites. Deformed tadpoles had fewer [[Labial Teeth]] and malformed [[Labial Papillae]]. In laboratory tests, deformed tadpoles grazed much less [[Periphyton]] and lost mass when periphyton was their only food. When particulate food was added, growth did not depend on mouth type. The authors conclude that the deformities, which are associated with coal ash contamination, can reduce individual energy acquisition and growth at sublethal exposure levels. The study does not establish a causal link to any specific contaminant, and population-level effects remain untested.

## Background & Description

The study addresses a gap in [[Sublethal Effects]] research. Most toxicology focuses on lethal thresholds, but moderately polluted habitats can support apparently viable amphibian populations while still reducing individual energy budgets, growth, and reproduction. Amphibians are widely used as [[Bioindicators]] of environmental stress, and their larvae feed in close contact with sediments.

Coal combustion is a major source of trace-element pollution. The authors cite roughly 57 million tonnes of U.S. coal ash produced in 1984, with projected production of about 99 million tonnes by 2000. Most ash (~80%) is landfilled or sent to settling basins.

The study site is the [[Savannah River Site]] near Aiken, South Carolina. A coal-fired plant (the "400 D-Area Plant") discharges sluiced fly and bottom ash into open settling basins: a 15-ha primary basin and a 6-ha secondary basin. Surface water from the secondary basin flows into a 2-ha drainage swamp, which then discharges into [[Beaver Dam Creek]], a tributary of the Savannah River. Earlier studies at the site reported enrichment of water, sediments and biota in Al, As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Ni, Se and Zn.

The authors tested three hypotheses:
1. Oral deformities inhibit tadpole grazing of periphyton.
2. Oral deformities negatively affect growth of tadpoles fed only periphyton.
3. Particulate food reduces the effects of deformities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sites:**
- **AB (ash basins):** primary and secondary settling basins, combined as one site.
- **ABS (drainage swamp):** receives discharge from the secondary basin.
- **OS (reference):** a local farm pond outside the Savannah River Site.
- **DB (reference):** [[Dry Bay]], a Carolina Bay on the Savannah River Site.

Tadpoles were abundant in the settling basins and swamp, and recently metamorphosed juveniles were also found there. The authors therefore infer that conditions were not lethal to developing larvae, but sublethal effects could still occur.

**Comparison species:** Only 32 [[Hyla Cinerea]] (green treefrog) tadpoles were inspected, all at AB, and none had oral deformities. The authors suggest that differences in larval period may matter. Green treefrog larvae develop in about 4–6 weeks, while bullfrog larvae may take up to 2 years locally. If chronic exposure is needed to produce deformities, species with long larval periods would be more vulnerable.

**Contaminant context:** Whole-body and sediment concentrations are summarized in the Key Findings. Selenium is the element most often linked to deformities in other vertebrates and invertebrates, including [[Mallard Ducks]] and chironomid larvae, making it a leading candidate. The authors caution that they cannot assign a specific cause from this field study.

## Key Findings

**1. Oral deformities were common in ash-affected sites and rare at reference sites.** Deformities were scored as missing teeth in at least one labial tooth row.

| Site | Deformed / Classified | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| AB (ash basins) | 1008 / 1048 | 96.2% |
| ABS (drainage swamp) | 177 / 208 | 85.1% |
| Reference (OS + DB) | 17 / 589 | 2.9% |

Within reference sites, 17 of 575 OS tadpoles and none of 14 DB tadpoles were deformed.

**2. Tooth counts were significantly reduced.** In 100 tadpoles of similar size and stage (50 from OS, 50 from AB/ABS), AB/ABS tadpoles had on average 90% fewer teeth in anterior row 2 and 40% fewer in posterior row 1 than OS tadpoles (P < 0.001 for both). Deformed animals also had aberrant labial papillae.

**3. Tadpole tissues were enriched in several trace elements.** Three tadpoles per site were analysed by [[Icp-Ms Analysis]] (mg/kg dry mass, mean ± SE):

| Element | AB tadpoles | OS tadpoles | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Arsenic]] (As) | 48.9 ± 2.6 | 2.5 ± 0.2 | P < 0.001 |
| Barium (Ba) | 211.5 ± 14.9 | 81.2 ± 5.2 | P < 0.001 |
| Cadmium (Cd) | 1.71 ± 0.10 | 0.15 ± 0.04 | P = 0.001 |
| [[Selenium]] (Se) | 25.70 ± 3.60 | 3.37 ± 0.09 | P < 0.001 |

Cu and Pb did not differ significantly, and Cr was also measured. Concentrations were tested with one-way ANOVAs on ln-transformed data and a [[Bonferroni Correction]] across seven comparisons. Sediment data (two samples per site) were not analysed statistically. AB and ABS sediments were enriched relative to OS and DB for all elements except Pb, which was highest at DB. AB sediment Se was 6.21 ± 0.59 and ABS sediment Se was 7.78 ± 0.36, compared with 0.46 ± 0.04 at OS.

**4. Deformed tadpoles grazed far less periphyton.** In a 3-day test, deformed tadpoles removed 0.4 ± 0.3% of periphyton per gram of wet body mass, while normal tadpoles removed 24.8 ± 6.8%. The authors did not statistically analyse these data because variances were heterogeneous and could not be transformed.

**5. Growth depended on food type and mouth type.** In a 24-day test with five replicates per treatment (one tadpole per treatment died early), [[Growth Rate]] was analysed by [[Anova]]:
- Food type: P < 0.001 (particulate food increased growth regardless of mouth type).
- Mouth type: P = 0.285 (not significant overall).
- Mouth × food interaction: P = 0.035.

With periphyton only, deformed tadpoles lost mass, while normal tadpoles grew slightly. The addition of [[Particulate Food]] eliminated this difference.

**6. Experimental conditions.** Tadpoles were at [[Gosner Staging]] stage 25–27 and were collected only from AB, using normal-mouthed animals from the small fraction without obvious deformities. Experiments were run at 24 °C on a 14:10 h light:dark cycle. Periphyton was grown on [[Plexiglas]] plates, and particulate food was a 2:1 mixture of ground rabbit food and flake fish food.

**7. Conclusions and limits.** The authors conclude that oral deformities reduce a tadpole's ability to acquire food and grow when periphyton is the only resource. Several questions remain open:
- The causative agent is not identified. Selenium is a candidate, supported by [[Mallard Ducks]] studies showing embryo deformities at roughly 10 ppm dietary Se.
- The developmental or physiological mechanisms are unknown.
- Population-level consequences need testing under natural conditions.
