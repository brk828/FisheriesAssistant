# Interrelationships of Higher Euteleostean Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #euteleostei

## Executive Summary

This monograph by [[Donn Eric Rosen]] (American Museum of Natural History) reexamines the phylogeny of the higher euteleostean fishes, with the goal of laying a foundation for analysing the [[Acanthopterygii]], the largest fish group. Rosen uses pharyngobranchial (gill-arch) structure, the [[Retractor Arcuum Branchialium]] (RAB) muscle, upper jaw mechanisms and the caudal skeleton as primary character systems. The study proposes a new classification of the [[Neoteleostei]], places the [[Stomiatoidei]] as the primitive sister-group of other neoteleosts, splits the former Myctophiformes into [[Aulopiformes]] and true [[Myctophiformes]], and argues that the traditional [[Beryciformes]] and [[Perciformes]] are not demonstrably monophyletic.

## Background & Description

The paper is a reprint from the Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society (Supplement No. 1, Vol. 53, 1973, pp. 397-513), edited by Greenwood, Miles and Patterson. The introduction notes that the euteleosteans comprise roughly 16,000-17,000 species, of which about 6000 are ostariophysans. The [[Acanthopterygii]] contains more than 7000 species in 12 orders and 218 families, about 35% of living fishes, and the [[Perciformes]] alone holds 20 suborders, the Percoidei including 71 families.

Materials were examined across major euteleostean groups, including examples of all recognised families within Salmoniformes, Myctophiformes, Percopsiformes, Gadiformes, Batrachoidiformes, Beryciformes and Zeiformes. Only selected perciform families (Centropomidae, Serranidae, Carangidae, Gerridae) were studied in detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is primarily systematic and morphological. Its ecological content is limited to functional observations: [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) feeding, where Nyberg (1971) showed that suction feeding and fast-approach strikes operate together, with premaxillary protrusion more advantageous for smaller fish. Many deep-sea and paedomorphic euteleosteans show reduced ossification, and the monograph notes lantern fishes, bioluminescent [[Anomalopidae]] and deep-sea [[Cetomimoidei]] as examples of specialised deep-water forms. Fossil Cretaceous taxa from Lebanon and the English Chalk are discussed as comparative material.

## Key Findings

- **Euteleostean subdivision:** Euteleosteans are split by the presence or absence of the RAB muscle. No ostariophysan, argentinoid, alepocephaloid, salmonoid or esocoid has RAB; it occurs in stomiatoids, myctophoids, alepisauroids, giganturoids, paracanthopterygians and acanthopterygians.
- **Stomiatoids as sister-group:** Stomiatoids are regarded as the primitive sister-group of the other Neoteleostei, based on upper jaw structure, separate maxillary musculature (A₂β), RAB, and a protractor pectoralis muscle (a finding attributed to R. Winterbottom).
- **Aulopiformes vs. Myctophiformes:** The former Myctophiformes divide into aulopiforms (aulopoids and alepisauroids, with the synodontoid-harpadontid and giganturoid lines) and true myctophoids (Myctophidae and Neoscopelidae). Aulopiforms are defined by an elongate uncinate process on the second epibranchial.
- **Myctophoid affinity:** Myctophids and neoscopelids align with paracanthopterygians and acanthopterygians, sharing a hinged fourth tooth-plate, a separate RAB bundle on the third pharyngobranchial, and a low crest on the second preural centrum.
- **Cretaceous placements:** Sixteen Cretaceous enchodontoid genera are assigned to Alepisauroidea. *Sardinius* and *Volcichthys* are synodontoids, and only *Sardinioides monasteri*, *S. crassicaudus* and *Acrognathus libanicus* are confidently myctophoids. *Ctenothrissa* and *Aulolepis* form a sister-group of paracanthopterygians plus acanthopterygians; *Pateroperca* and *Pattersonichthys* are excluded.
- **Caudal skeleton:** A full second preural neural spine is argued to be primitive for euteleosteans and possibly teleosteans. Reacquisition in paracanthopterygians is attributed mainly to fusion of preural centra rather than to spontaneous regrowth or epural fusion.
- **Beryciform grade:** Beryciforms are not monophyletic. The paper recognises trachichthyoids, berycids, holocentrids, zeoids/lampridiforms-related groups, a stephanoberycoid assemblage (including cetomimoids, melamphaeids, anoplogasterids and barbourisiids), and polymixiids. Polymixiids are placed as the primitive sister-group of trachichthyoids plus stephanoberycoids.
- **Ateleopodids:** Their upper pharyngeal tooth-plates are separate from the endoskeleton, a secondary condition; the gill-arch evidence does not support earlier alignment with lampridiforms.
- **Revised classification:** The proposed Neoteleostei includes Section Stenopterygii (Stomiatiformes), Section Eurypterygii (Aulopiformes, Myctophiformes), and Sept Acanthomorpha with Ctenothrissa and Aulolepis, Paracanthopterygii, and Acanthopterygii (series Atherinomorpha and Percomorpha).
- **Conclusion on Acanthopterygii:** Rosen concludes that no strong advanced feature or sufficient weak one shows that beryciforms form a monophyletic taxon, and that all prior demonstrations of perciform monophyly were methodologically unsound. Comparisons "upwards" with perciforms are called necessary.
