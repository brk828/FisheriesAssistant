# Biological Opinion on U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Stocking of Rainbow Trout and Channel Catfish into the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (dated June 1994, reference 2-21-94-F-244) evaluates a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) program to stock catchable (8–10 inch) [[Rainbow Trout]] from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Channel Catfish]] into selected waters of the lower [[Colorado River]] between Hoover Dam and the International Border. The consultation focuses on two endangered species, the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], and on their designated critical habitat in [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]] and the river from Parker Dam to Imperial Dam.

The final opinion concludes that the trout stocking is not likely to jeopardize either species nor adversely modify critical habitat, provided reasonable and prudent measures and terms are followed. Channel catfish stocking in waters without direct connection to the river is not likely to adversely affect either species. The document also contains a draft version (dated 1994, labelled "D.R.A.F.T") that reached the opposite jeopardy finding and proposed two alternative RPAs, plus internal comments from fisheries staff disputing the findings.

## Background & Description

- **Action agency:** Service Fisheries Resources program, Region 2; trout raised at Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery; catfish obtained from Service hatcheries in Texas.
- **Project area:** portions of Mohave, La Paz and Yuma counties (Arizona); Riverside, San Bernardino and Imperial counties (California); Clark County (Nevada).
- **Consultation timeline:** request received March 9, 1994 by the Arizona Ecological Services State Office; a 1992 biological evaluation (BE) and a 1994 BE supplied the stocking data.
- **Purpose:** support Service trust responsibilities to Native American tribes, state and federal goals for recreational sport fishing, and continued recovery of the two listed fish.
- **Stocking scale:** about 560,500 trout proposed in total, distributed across Lake Mohave (three zones, roughly 459,000 fish across seven areas, with Areas 1–2 fished November–March, Areas 3–5 November–May, and Areas 6–7 year-round), the river below Davis Dam (about 146,000 fish, mostly August–May), four Colorado River Indian Reservation sites (13,000 fish, winter-only fisheries) and two Fort Yuma sites (8,500 fish).
- **Catfish:** of eight areas listed in the 1992 BE, stocking was halted at seven because of proximity to native fish populations; only the Alamo Canal on Fort Yuma remains.
- **Reintroduction programs:** bonytail stocking began in 1981; razorback sucker recovery began in 1981 in the Gila River basin. Both species now receive cove-reared advanced fingerlings in Lakes Mohave and Havasu.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Colorado River:** heavily altered by dams (Hoover, Davis, Parker), flow regulation, channelization and diversions. Only 36 native fish species occur in the basin, 64% of them endemic; over 70 species have been introduced.
- **Lake Mohave:** a "run-of-the-river" reservoir with a water residence time of 0.24 years. Hypolimnion water from Hoover Dam is about 11–12.5 °C year-round; surface summer temperatures can reach 29 °C, which keeps a cold-water trout habitat in the lower depths.
- **Davis Dam to Needles:** tailrace temperatures of 12–16 °C; at least 55% of shoreline modified by bank stabilization.
- **Lake Havasu:** shallow, wide basins (maximum depth about 18 m), short retention time (about 0.5 days at maximum discharge); too warm to sustain rainbow trout through summer and supports striped bass, sunfish and catfish.
- **Colorado River Indian Reservation and Fort Yuma:** small ponds, backwaters and canals; warm-water species dominate in summer, and water temperatures can reach about 30 °C.
- **Razorback sucker:** Lake Mohave holds the largest known population, estimated at about 60,000 adults earlier, declining to about 20,000 adults by the mid-1990s, with natural recruitment undocumented. Spawning occurs January–April in coves of Areas 3–5, larvae use warm shallow nursery areas, and Lake Havasu's population is considered near extinction.
- **Bonytail chub:** Lake Mohave may hold the largest remaining wild population. Between 1974 and 1991 researchers captured 57 individuals; spawning was recorded in May at Eldorado Canyon. Natural recruitment is believed to be minimal.
- **Predation:** rainbow trout were observed preying on razorback sucker larvae during 1992 surveys; striped bass in Lake Mohave consume large numbers of stocked trout; green sunfish and carp are also common in spawning habitat.
- **Cumulative effects:** most activities on the river connect to Federal water management or Section 404 permits, so they are subject to Section 7; non-federal entities were evaluating Section 10 permits.

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy finding (final):** the continued trout stocking is not likely to jeopardize the razorback sucker or bonytail chub, and not likely to adversely modify critical habitat.
- **Channel catfish:** stocking in tanks and waters without direct connection to the river is not likely to adversely affect the two species or their critical habitat.
- **Incidental take:** take of razorback sucker and bonytail larvae by stocked trout cannot be quantified. Take was considered exceeded if reasonable and prudent measures were not implemented, or if stocked trout were shown to limit recruitment or modify critical habitat.
- **Reasonable and prudent measures:** stocking was restricted from Areas 1–5 in the spawning period (November through April) unless larval surveys found no razorback sucker larvae; trout stocking in Areas 6 and 7 could continue.
- **Recovery commitments (Terms and Conditions):** annual census of the Lake Mohave razorback population; attempts to locate bonytail spawning areas; provision of 10,000 advanced razorback suckers (about 300 mm) annually for 1995–1999; provision of 25,000 advanced bonytail chub (250–300 mm) annually for 1996–1999.
- **Conservation recommendations:** include Lake Mohave conservation elements in the future Lake Mohave aquatic ecosystem management plan and in a Lower Colorado River Basin Native Fish Management Plan.
- **Reversibility:** stocked trout do not reproduce in the reservoir, so stocking can be discontinued if it is found to harm the listed species.
- **Draft opinion (superseded):** the 1994 draft concluded jeopardy and proposed RPA 1 (no trout in Lake Mohave) and RPA 2 (aggressive reintroduction and studies). The Region 2 regional director's memo and a Service press release of July 1, 1994 describe the modified, non-jeopardy program, with stocking resuming at previous levels.
- **Dissent from Arizona fisheries biologists:** the Pinetop Fishery Resources Office and Phoenix Ecological Services staff argued there was no evidence trout are a major predator, that the draft's RPAs were unfunded and unsatisfactory, and that stocking should be non-jeopardy with suspension only in Lake Mohave Areas 1–5 during spawning.
- **Other items:** the document also includes a July 1994 Bureau of Reclamation request for proposals for a Lower Colorado River basin study of management alternatives (not a biological finding, and not detailed here). The source contains OCR errors and draft-review debris, which were not reproduced.
