# Memorandum on Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #desert-fishes #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but an **administrative memorandum and agenda** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, organizing a February 1992 meeting of the **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]** in Phoenix, Arizona. The meeting addressed multiple concurrent recovery efforts for imperiled desert fish species across the American Southwest and northern Mexico (Sonora/Yaqui drainages). Rather than presenting novel morphological or ecological data, the memo outlines the policy, management, and translocation issues facing several federally listed and candidate species, reflecting the collaborative, multi-agency (USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Centro Ecológico) nature of desert fish conservation in this era.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (meristics, coloration, body plan) are presented in this source. The document is purely administrative/logistical, focused on recovery planning rather than species description. For taxonomic and morphological detail, refer to dedicated species accounts such as [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The memo implicitly references the ecological contexts of several distinct desert aquatic systems:
- **Sonora Chub** ([[Sonora Chub]]) — proposed for transplant by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), implying occupation of small perennial streams in the Sonoran desert borderlands.
- **Spikedace** ([[Spikedace]]) and **Loach Minnow** ([[Loach Minnow]]) — associated with the upper Verde River and other Gila Basin streams; under discussion for potential uplisting to "endangered" status, and subject of a potential Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP).
- **Desert Pupfish** ([[Desert Pupfish]]) — associated with springs and marshes; overlaps with topics like the **Bog Hole** and **Redrock Wildlife Area** reintroduction sites.
- **Gila Topminnow** ([[Gila Topminnow]]) — closely tied to spring systems, including **Bylas Springs**, which was flagged for a global warming impact study and habitat renovation.
- **Yaqui Fishes** (multiple species, e.g. [[Beautiful Shiner]]) — native to the Río Yaqui drainage in Sonora, Mexico, with proposed transplants into the **Mimbres drainage** (New Mexico) and ongoing monitoring by the **Centro Ecológico**.
- **Razorback Sucker** ([[Razorback Sucker]]) — a large-river catostomid referenced regarding the implications of its (at the time) proposed/candidate federal listing; explicitly noted as outside this team's direct responsibility.
- **Sport fish vs. native fish conflict** — discussion of stocking programs (Central Arizona Project, FWS 1992 stocking consultation) in **Lakes Pleasant, Havasu, and Bartlett**, highlighting the competitive/predatory ecological pressure introduced sport fish exert on native desert fish assemblages.

## Key Findings
- The **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** convened Feb. 5–6, 1992, in Phoenix, AZ, to address recovery planning for multiple species, indicating an active, multi-species, multi-agency management framework rather than isolated single-species efforts.
- **Recovery Plan updates** were slated for: [[Desert Pupfish]], Yaqui fishes, [[Gila Topminnow]] (revision), and [[Sonora Chub]].
- A core management debate was raised regarding **transplant philosophy**: specifically, whether it is appropriate to conduct recovery transplants into streams already containing relict, non-listed native fish populations when historical occupancy by the target listed species is uncertain.
- **Spikedace** and **Loach Minnow** were being considered for **uplisting to "Endangered"** status (from Threatened), with an explicit agenda item on improving data-sharing/consolidation among researchers and agencies.
- A potential **Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP)** was under discussion for the upper Verde River, relevant to Spikedace populations — an early example of HCP application to desert fish conservation.
- Significant agenda time was devoted to the **conflict between sport fishing programs and native fish conservation**, including:
  - Central Arizona Project (CAP) consultation
  - 1992 FWS stocking consultation
  - Potentially federally-funded state stockings
  - A proposed Pigeon Creek reservoir
  - Sport fish habitat improvements at Lakes Pleasant, Havasu, and Bartlett
- International collaboration was evident via an update on the **Centro Ecológico's** fish survey work (Mexico), and a proposed transplant of the **Beautiful Shiner** into the Mimbres drainage (New Mexico), representing cross-border/cross-basin conservation strategy.
- The **Razorback Sucker's** federal listing implications were discussed, though explicitly noted as falling outside this Recovery Team's jurisdiction — indicating overlapping but distinct recovery team structures for different desert fish taxa.
- Site-specific conservation actions flagged for discussion included the **Redrock Wildlife Area** reintroduction program, the **Bylas Springs** global warming study/renovation, and the **Bog Hole** site, reflecting concern over climate change impacts on isolated desert spring habitats even as early as 1992.
