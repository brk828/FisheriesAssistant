# Formal Section 7 Consultation on Miner Flat Dam Project - Loach Minnow Impact Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #conservation

## Executive Summary

This October 1983 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to the Bureau of Indian Affairs addresses a formal Section 7 consultation regarding the proposed [[Miner Flat Dam]] project on the [[White River]] system in Arizona. While no effects are anticipated on [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Bald Eagle]], and [[Apache Trout]], the [[Loach Minnow]] (Tiaroga cobitis) will be adversely impacted. The White River population represents the only known remnant of loach minnow in the entire lower [[Gila River]] basin's Salt/Verde River portion and is considered critical to species survival and recovery. The Service indicates that insufficient biological data exists to complete the consultation and recommends deferring the biological opinion pending collection of additional information.

## Background & Description

The [[Loach Minnow]] population in the [[White River]] system extends approximately 7.5 miles from above [[Nash Canyon]] on the [[East Fork White River]] downstream into the mainstem White River at least 2.5 miles below the confluence. Limited sampling has been conducted above and below this stretch. Biologists believe loach minnows likely extend downstream a significant distance from the last known collection near [[Canyon Day]], though this downstream portion has very limited vehicular access making documentation difficult.

The presence of loach minnows in the [[North Fork White River]] remains unknown. Sampling conducted there failed to locate any individuals, but much sampling occurred under adverse conditions. The Service states that without extensive sampling focused specifically on suitable habitat, they must assume loach minnows are present in the North Fork based on their presence at the mouth and large amounts of apparently suitable habitat below [[Miner Flat]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The White River loach minnow population represents the only known remnant in the entire lower Gila River basin portion of historic loach minnow range. The Service assumes, based on best available information, that loach minnows have been extirpated from all portions of the [[Salt River]] basin except the White River. The 1985 White River survey that located this remnant population also surveyed several other Salt River tributaries but found no loach minnows.

All other existing loach minnow populations are located in the upper [[Gila River]] basin. The [[Aravaipa Creek]] population is the only remnant from southern Gila River tributaries. The [[Blue River]]/[[San Francisco River]] and Gila River populations in New Mexico are presently the most healthy and generally considered the stronghold of the species.

From a conservation biology perspective, survival of the White River loach minnow population is considered critical to species survival and recovery. Separate pools of genetic stock and habitat variability are essential for: (1) maintenance of genetic diversity, (2) providing basis for identification of limiting factors and habitat tolerances, and (3) avoiding catastrophic loss when all members are in a single population or location. All existing populations presently face one or more imminent threats and none are considered safe from extirpation.

The White River loach minnow likely represents a genetic pool that may differ slightly from other populations. Gila basin tributaries separated by considerable river mileage along the connecting Gila River are assumed to have a degree of genetic distance. This genetic diversity may provide the key variability that allows the species to adapt to changing environments. Loss of that genetic diversity could critically affect long-term survival of the species.

## Key Findings

**Identified Project Impacts on Loach Minnow:**

1. Changes in water temperatures due to dam releases including lowering of summer temperatures and changes in the annual thermal regime
2. Deprivation of substrate gravel source for the North Fork and mainstem White Rivers
3. Changes in stream flow and related concerns
4. Complete dewatering during portions of some years
5. Changes in water quality below [[Canyon Day]] diversion
6. Reduction in loach minnow habitat in the North Fork

**Conservation Status:**
- Effects of the [[Canyon Day]] diversion are expected to be significant but were not discussed in the Biological Assessment
- The distance in miles of occupied loach minnow habitat that would be affected by the diversion is important to assess but currently unknown

**Information Deficiencies:**
The Service states that formulation of a biological opinion is difficult due to insufficient information on:
- Proposed project specifications and operation
- Distribution and status of the loach minnow population
- Answers to questions submitted August 26, 1988

Limited biological information on loach minnow abundance, habitat use, and flow needs has been made available through site visits by biologists and a June 1988 sampling survey conducted by Kestech for the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]].

**Regulatory Requirements:**
Service regulations require that when significant information is lacking, they must err on the side of the species. The Service has two alternatives:
1. Issue a biological opinion based upon best available scientific and commercial data
2. Request the action agency extend the Section 7 consultation period or delay project initiation to allow gathering of further data

**Recommendation:**
The formal consultation on the Miner Flat Dam project should be deferred pending collection of more comprehensive biological data. The Service will not provide the proposed biological opinion until an agreement is reached to allow the Bureau of Indian Affairs to receive adequate information.
