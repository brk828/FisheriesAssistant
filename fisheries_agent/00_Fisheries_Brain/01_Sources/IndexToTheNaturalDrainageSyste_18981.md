# Index to the Natural Drainage Systems of Arizona: A Computer-Compatible Digital Identification of Perennial Lotic Waters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This Wildlife Unit Technical Series publication, prepared by [[William Silvey]], [[John N. Rinne]] and [[Roger Sorenson]] for the Southwestern Region, provides a computer-compatible digital index of all naturally occurring perennial waters in Arizona. It was written as a working tool for fisheries and wildlife biologists implementing the [[Resources Planning Act]] and [[National Forest Management Act]] planning processes. The index recognizes **554 perennial waters**; supporting data and references are available for **320**, while **234** systems lack the minimum data needed for inclusion. Each stream receives a hierarchical digital identifier, a name, a latitude-longitude location, a fish-species code set and bibliographic references.

## Background & Description

Before this work, perennial stream information for Arizona was fragmentary. Apart from [[Miller 1954 Drainage Map]] and later [[Brown Carmony and Turner Drainage Maps]] (1977, 1978, 1981), most compilations were limited to major surface waters studied by the [[United States Geological Survey]]. The catalog spans waters from the [[Colorado River]] to streams only a few hundred meters long.

The identification system is built on the [[Arizona Resources Inventory System]] (ARIS) 600-series aquatic wildlife habitat code and is linked to the [[RUN WILD]] storage and retrieval program of the [[USDA Forest Service]]. Key conventions:

- **Hydrographic regions:** 610 Colorado River (over 90% of the state's 113,909 square miles); 620 Yaqui; 630 Sonoran (631 Rio Magdalena, 632 Rio Sonoyta); 640 Closed Basins.
- **Hierarchy:** Tributaries are numbered from lowermost to uppermost; for example, Colorado River tributaries run from the [[Gila River]] (611.) to the [[San Juan River]] (615.).
- **Notation:** An asterisk marks ephemeral channels used only for location; parentheses mark unnamed streams; quotation marks denote local names.
- **Location:** Coordinates to the nearest 15 seconds; north and west designations are omitted because all of Arizona lies within 31°–38° N and 109°–115° W.
- **Species codes:** An RW number links each stream to the species list in Table 5 (adapted from [[Patton 1978 Vertebrate Checklist]]).
- **References:** Numbered citations, many to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] survey reports, [[Museum Registers]] (Arizona State University, University of Michigan), and federal recovery plans, current to 1981.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Arizona's perennial waters support a fauna dominated by fishes adapted to desert and intermittent conditions. The text highlights several ecological themes:

- **Native and endangered fishes:** Many listed taxa are endemic to the Colorado River system or to the Yaqui and Sonoran basins. Table 5 flags threatened or endangered species with an asterisk (e.g., [[Woundfin]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]]).
- **Introduced species:** Table 5 marks introductions (e.g., [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Tilapia]] species), which compete with or prey on native fishes.
- **Hybridization:** Table 5 lists hybrid combinations among native species, such as Gila elegans × Gila cypha and Salmo apache × Salmo gairdneri.
- **Intermittent and disturbed streams:** Cited studies address survival in intermittent streams, flood impacts on desert fish populations and effects of copper mining on desert streams.
- **Habitat concerns:** Stream sites were listed because they support or once supported fish populations, and the authors note that many entries need field verification.

## Key Findings

- **Inventory size:** 554 perennial waters identified; 320 with data and references; 234 without minimum data.
- **Distribution of land area:** The Colorado River watershed covers more than 90% of Arizona's 113,909 square miles (Table 2 lists 105,749 square miles in the Colorado drainage).
- **Largest tributary subsystems (Colorado drainage):** [[Little Colorado River]] (614.) at 21,668 square miles; [[Gila River]] (611.) at 51,091 square miles including sub-basins such as the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]].
- **Data coverage gaps:** The authors state that additional perennial waters likely exist and that some entries may be erroneous; users are invited to contribute corrections.
- **Update plans:** Periodic updates are anticipated every 3–5 years because survey work is ongoing.
- **Principal purpose:** Integrating the drainage index with the RW program so that biologists can determine documented species distributions by combining Table 3 and Table 6.
- **Reference base:** The bibliography of 119 entries spans 1922 to 1981 and includes museum registers, state survey reports, federal recovery plans and peer-reviewed papers.
- **Fish species catalog:** Table 5 lists roughly 100 code entries (native, introduced and hybrid taxa), with threatened and endangered species and introductions flagged.
