# Green River Fisheries Rehabilitation Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonids

## Executive Summary

The [[Green River Fisheries Rehabilitation Project]] was a large-scale fishery rehabilitation program completed in early September by the [[Wyoming Game and Fish Department]] and the [[Utah Game and Fish Department]], with financial assistance from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Described as the largest fishery rehabilitation program ever undertaken in the United States, its goal was to eliminate [[Coarse Fish]] populations from 415 miles of the [[Green River]] and its tributaries in western Wyoming and northern Utah. The operation used [[Rotenone]] applied through [[Drip Stations]]. The source states the project was successful and that the restored waters will support game fish. Stocking of [[Rainbow Trout]] fingerlings and catchables is planned as the next phase, along with restocking of [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]].

## Background & Description

- **Project name:** Green River Fisheries Rehabilitation Project
- **Completion:** Early September (year not stated in source)
- **Agencies:** Game and Fish Departments of Wyoming and Utah; U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (financial assistance)
- **Scope:** 415 miles of the Green River and its tributaries, western Wyoming and northern Utah
- **Target species removed:** Coarse (rough) fish, including unspecified species; the document does not name them
- **Planning:** Intensive pre-project studies verified the types and extent of coarse fish populations. Meetings began more than a year before the project. A final briefing was held September 3 for more than 100 technical personnel.
- **Logistics:** Food and toxicant were distributed on strict time schedules. Truckloads of equipment and supplies were needed to set up and alternate drip stations downstream.
- **Treatment method:** Rotenone-based toxicant administered from drip stations. Valves controlled flow to achieve five parts chemical to one million parts water flowing past each station. The toxicant flowed from barrels through a hose strung across the full width of the river.
- **Supplemental treatment:** [[Helicopter Application]] treated backwaters and sloughs not in direct contact with the stream flow. Still waters required individual treatment.
- **Quantities:** More than 21,000 gallons of chemical were used.
- **Fish recovery:** Fishermen gathered affected fish on horseback, by boat, and on foot. The source states very few trout or whitefish were wasted, and that the killed fish were fit for human consumption.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Problem addressed:** Coarse fish populations posed a documented threat to prime trout fishing areas upstream.
- **Toxicant safety (as stated by source):** The rotenone-based toxicant was described as not harmful to humans, livestock, or game animals.
- **Stream recovery:** Recent studies of the stream channels indicated conditions would support development and maintenance of high-quality game fish populations.
- **Reservoir management:** Flaming Gorge Reservoir was to be stocked with fingerlings only, because all predaceous fish had been removed.
- **Downstream reservoir:** [[Fontenelle Reservoir]] was described as due for completion in the near future and expected to be prime trout fishing water once rough fish were eliminated.

## Key Findings

1. **Scale and success:** The Green River project was the largest fishery rehabilitation program ever undertaken in the United States. It removed great numbers of coarse fish from 415 miles of waters.
2. **Method:** Rotenone was applied at a ratio of five parts chemical to one million parts water, delivered through drip stations and supplemented by helicopter treatment of backwaters. More than 21,000 gallons were used.
3. **Logistics and coordination:** Success depended on advance studies, extensive meetings, a briefing of more than 100 technical personnel, and precise timing across large, rugged areas.
4. **Restocking plan:**
   - [[Rainbow Trout]] fingerlings to be planted the following spring by Wyoming fishery crews.
   - Catchable trout to be stocked in high-demand areas for immediate seasonal fishing.
   - Joint Wyoming, Utah, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service stocking of Flaming Gorge Reservoir with fingerlings, starting the next summer.
5. **Reservoir outlook:** With rough fish removed, Flaming Gorge and Fontenelle reservoirs were expected to become prime trout fishing waters.
6. **Human use:** Killed fish were collected and used for food, with very few trout or whitefish wasted.
