# Ouray Razorback Hatchery Summary 1987

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #conservation-biology #hatchery-operations

## Executive Summary

An experimental [[Razorback Sucker]] hatchery was established in 1987 at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] to evaluate streamside spawning and rearing techniques for this endangered species. The facility consisted of three 1/10 acre ponds and a building with incubators and rearing tanks. From April 26 to May 13, 1987, sixteen females and twenty-eight males were captured at [[Razorback Bar]] near [[Jensen Bridge]]. Approximately 127,500 eggs were collected, yielding 3,299 fry. Pond rearing from May through November demonstrated exceptional growth rates, with 94% survival in pond 1 (2,725 fish at 33 fish per pound, average 109mm) and 72% survival in pond 3 (261 fish at 12 fish per pound, average 149mm). Growth rates significantly exceeded those observed at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]]. The experimental hatchery at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] was established with three primary objectives: 1) determine if streamside spawning and rearing could be successful; 2) evaluate logistics of rearing at a small facility using ponds; and 3) determine survival of different age classes when released into the [[Green River]].

The facility infrastructure included three 1/10 acre ponds and a building housing [[Heath Incubators]] and rearing tanks. Personnel from multiple agencies collaborated on this project: [[Vernal Colorado River Fish Project]], [[Jones Hole National Fish Hatchery]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]], and [[Arizona State University]] Tempe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Broodstock were collected from [[Razorback Bar]], located a few miles above the [[Jensen Bridge]] on the [[Green River]]. Egg-taking operations occurred during the natural spawning period from April 26 through May 13, 1987.

Water temperature regimes varied by incubation method and season:
- Indoor incubation: 11°C
- Outdoor incubation: 16-21°C
- Pond temperatures: 17°C in May, maximum 24°C in July, declining to 11°C by early November

Two incubation protocols were tested. Early eggs (April 26-May 1) were incubated indoors at 11°C with fertility of 50-90%, but all trays developed fungal mats by May 4 despite hand cleaning. Later eggs (May 8-13) were incubated outdoors at 16-21°C with daily [[Malachite Green]] treatment at 2.2 ppm, which successfully prevented fungus.

## Key Findings

**Egg Collection and Hatching:**
- Total broodstock: 16 females and 28 males captured
- Estimated total eggs collected: 127,500
- Total fry produced: 3,299
- Approximately 97% of stocked fry came from five females and nine males
- Indoor incubation at 11°C resulted in complete fungal loss despite 50-90% initial fertility
- Outdoor incubation at 16-21°C with daily malachite green treatment (2.2 ppm) was totally successful in preventing egg fungus
- Hatching time: 7-8 days at 16-21°C

**Pond Rearing Results:**
- Pond 1: 2,894 fry stocked, 2,725 recovered (94% survival), 33 fish per pound, average 4.29" (109mm)
- Pond 3: ~360 fry stocked, 261 recovered (72% survival), 12 fish per pound, average 5.88" (149mm)
- Rearing period: May 16-29 through October 28-November 4 (159 days)

**Growth Performance:**
- Pond 1 fish: 0.75" per 30 days over 159-day period
- Pond 3 fish: 1.05" per 30 days over same period (including time in fiberglass tank)
- [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] comparison: 0.48-0.51" per 30 days over 230 days (mid-March to November 1)
- Growth at [[Ouray]] was exceptional, exceeding [[Dexter]] rates by 56-119%

**Feeding Protocol:**
- Pond 3 fry initially held in 300-gallon fiberglass tank saturated with plankton from pond 3
- Commercial dry feed used: [[Kyowa A-400]] (Japanese firm, St. Louis office)
- Fry transferred to pond 3 first week of July (no sample count or measurements recorded at transfer)
