# Authorization Letter for Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish Transfer to Phoenix Zoo

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) authorization letter, not a primary field or laboratory study, yet it contains meaningful data on the conservation management of two federally protected imperiled desert fishes: the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*). The letter authorizes The Phoenix Zoo to receive up to 50 individuals of each species from captive stock held at the Phoenix Botanical Garden, for the explicit purposes of **captive propagation and recovery**. The transfer is placed under the scientific supervision of Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University), a leading authority on southwestern desert fish ecology. The letter establishes chain-of-custody, mortality/specimen-preservation protocols, and annual reporting requirements to USFWS Technical Services Division, reflecting standard practices in [[Ex Situ Conservation]] programs for endangered aquatic species.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body proportions) are provided in this administrative letter. However, taxonomic identifications are explicit and precise:
- **Gila Topminnow** — *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family [[Poeciliidae]]; a small live-bearing (viviparous) cyprinodontiform fish.
- **Desert Pupfish** — *Cyprinodon macularius*, family [[Cyprinodontidae]]; a small, robust-bodied egg-laying killifish adapted to extreme desert aquatic conditions.

Both species are noted in the broader literature for extreme physiological tolerances (high salinity, high temperature, low oxygen) typical of desert spring and marsh habitats, though this letter itself does not elaborate on these traits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are native to isolated desert aquatic habitats of the American Southwest and northern Mexico (Gila River Basin and Sonoran Desert wetlands/lower Colorado River drainage, respectively), historically occupying springs, cienegas, and slow desert streams — extreme, fragmented, and often thermally/chemically stressed environments.
- The specimens referenced originate from an established **captive stock at the Phoenix Botanical Garden**, indicating both species are already maintained under managed *ex situ* propagation as part of broader recovery efforts, likely due to habitat loss, introduced predatory/competitive species (e.g., mosquitofish, *Gambusia*), and range fragmentation — well-documented threats to both taxa.
- The transfer to The Phoenix Zoo expands the network of **refuge populations**, a core recovery strategy for these federally listed species, reducing extinction risk via distributed captive holdings (see [[Species Survival Plan]] concepts and [[Refugium Population Management]]).
- Supervision by Dr. W. L. Minckley links this action to broader academic and applied research on [[Desert Fish Conservation]] and the ecology of endangered fishes in the [[Gila River Basin]] and [[Sonoran Desert Aquatic Ecosystems]].

## Key Findings
- USFWS authorized transfer of **up to 50 individuals each** of Gila topminnow and desert pupfish from Phoenix Botanical Garden captive stock to The Phoenix Zoo.
- Purpose of transfer: **captive propagation and recovery** of two imperiled desert fish taxa.
- Transport/transplantation supervised by **Dr. W. L. Minckley** (Arizona State University), underscoring the involvement of academic ichthyological expertise in conservation logistics.
- Strict **chain-of-custody and welfare conditions** imposed: proper care of live specimens; mandatory preservation and transfer of any mortalities to Dr. Minckley for research/educational use.
- Documentation requirements: a cover letter specifying recipient and numbers must accompany every shipment; copies retained by both the Zoo and USFWS Technical Services Division (Albuquerque, NM).
- Any **disposal, release, or deviation** from authorized use requires prior written USFWS approval, reflecting strict regulatory control consistent with the species' protected status under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- An **annual activity report** was mandated (due December 1, 1991, and yearly thereafter), indicating an ongoing, monitored, multi-year captive management relationship rather than a one-time transfer.
- Distribution list (State and Federal fisheries/law enforcement/ecological services offices) reflects **interagency coordination** typical of imperiled species recovery programs in [[Arizona]].
