# Nutrioso and Rudd Creeks Fish Survey (April 2006)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

In April 2006, the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) and the [[US Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) surveyed fish in [[Nutrioso Creek]] and [[Rudd Creek]] to assess the native fish assemblage, with particular focus on the threatened [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]. The survey re-sampled 24 sites first surveyed in 2005. Spinedace numbers rebounded sharply after drought-driven lows in 2005: the population above [[Nelson Reservoir]] rose more than 18-fold, and most fish were young. Below the reservoir, no spinedace were found, and [[Fathead Minnow]] dominated the catch. Drying of habitat and nonnative fish are identified as the main threats. The authors recommend moving spinedace to perennial water or refugia, removing nonnative fish below the reservoir, and restocking.

## Background & Description

The survey was prompted by the status of the Little Colorado spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*), listed as threatened by the USFWS in 1987. About 5 miles of Nutrioso Creek is designated [[Critical Habitat]]. Spinedace numbers in 2005 were very low compared with earlier years (Lopez et al. 2001). Drought conditions had persisted in the region, with a particularly dry year noted in the period before the survey, and drying of habitat was believed to be a major cause of the low 2005 numbers. A wet winter and spring 2004/2005 followed.

The fish assemblage in the study area includes one native species in the lower reach, [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), and three nonnative species: [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*). The upper reach contains native [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], speckled dace, and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*), along with fathead minnow.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study design and methods**

- Nutrioso Creek: 19 sites, 11 below Nelson Reservoir and 8 above. Rudd Creek: 5 sites.
- Sites were 100 to 320 m long. Three sites were on private land ([[EC Bar Ranch]]); the rest were on US Forest Service and state land, including [[Sipe Wildlife Area]].
- Single-pass [[Electrofishing]] was done with a Smith-Root Model 12 POW backpack electrofisher. Fish were identified to species and measured for total length.
- Under USFWS Section 7 consultation, rainbow trout were checked for [[Tetracycline Marking]] by viewing bone cross sections under ultraviolet light. Stomach contents were checked for spinedace.

**Habitat conditions**

- Below Nelson Reservoir, the lowest site was dry and site 2 was isolated pool habitat. Crayfish were common.
- Above the reservoir, all sites had sustained surface flow at the time of the survey.
- In Rudd Creek, sites R1 and R2 were dry during the survey. Sites R3 to R5 (on Sipe Wildlife Area) had continuous flow at R3 and R4 and intermittent pool habitat at R5.

**Threats and management context**

- Nonnative fish are a documented threat. Green sunfish and other nonnatives prey on native southwestern fishes elsewhere. High fathead minnow numbers below the dam may compete with spinedace for food and possibly prey on them.
- Reduced maintenance flows below the dam, linked to recent drought, likely contributed to drying downstream.
- Much of the habitat between the EC Bar Ranch and Nelson Reservoir is expected to dry in late spring and early summer.
- Riparian restoration on the EC Bar Ranch, led by landowner Jim Crosswhite, is expected to improve conditions for spinedace and other native fishes.

## Key Findings

**Downstream of Nelson Reservoir (Nutrioso Creek, 1,730 m surveyed, 11 sites)**

- Fathead minnow made up 96.5% of all fish captured.
- Speckled dace was the only native species. Green sunfish and one rainbow trout (total length 380 mm) were also captured.
- The rainbow trout tested positive for tetracycline. Its stomach held 3 caddisfly larvae, caddisfly case fragments, and two unidentified macroinvertebrates. No spinedace were found in its stomach or in any site.
- Spinedace were common in this reach in surveys from 1991 to 2000. They were absent in 2005 and 2006.

**Upstream of Nelson Reservoir (Nutrioso Creek, 810 m surveyed, 8 sites)**

- Relative abundance was: fathead minnow most abundant, followed by Little Colorado spinedace (15.6%), speckled dace (7.2%), and bluehead sucker (about 1.2%).
- A total of 125 Little Colorado spinedace were sampled, with lengths from 30 to 112 mm (mean 53.3 mm, SD 11.04).
- No rainbow trout or green sunfish were sampled.

**Rudd Creek (300 m surveyed, 5 sites, 3 sampled)**

- Two native species were found: 40 speckled dace and one bluehead sucker.
- No spinedace or nonnative fish were found. Crayfish were present but less abundant than in Nutrioso Creek.

**Population trends and recruitment**

- The upper-reach spinedace total increased more than 18-fold between 2005 and 2006.
- Mean total length fell from 80.9 mm in 2005 to 53.3 mm in 2006, indicating successful recruitment in the past year.
- Individuals under 80 mm made up 95% of the 2006 sample, compared with 43% in 2005 ([[Length-Frequency Analysis]]).
- The largest concentration of spinedace was at site 19 on EC Bar Ranch, associated with submerged woody debris and willow roots.
- Nearly 70% of all spinedace sampled were on EC Bar Ranch, where perennial flows exist.
- High peak flows are associated with recruitment spikes in native fish (Minckley and Meffe 1987; Rinne 2005).
- Bluehead sucker showed no sign of recovery compared with 1994 to 2000 data, and numbers were described as "disturbingly low."

**Recommendations**

1. Move Little Colorado spinedace from Nutrioso Creek reaches likely to dry (between EC Bar Ranch and Nelson Reservoir) to perennial habitat or [[Refugia]].
2. Remove nonnative fish from Nutrioso Creek below Nelson Reservoir by mechanical means and restock with Little Colorado spinedace.
3. Monitor Rudd Creek during the driest part of the year to find perennial stretches for possible refugia.
4. Continue monitoring with backpack electrofishing and remove nonnatives sampled in Nutrioso Creek.
5. If an unusual spill from Nelson Reservoir occurs in early summer after restocking, electrofish the entire critical habitat reach below the dam and remove nonnative predators.
6. Work with private landowners through stewardship agreements, and improve irrigation delivery by piping and lining ditches to increase instream flow.
7. Establish and maintain a secure refugia for the Little Colorado River, Nutrioso Creek, and Rudd Creek spinedace population, as outlined in the USFWS recovery plan. A refugia pond is being built at the AGFD White Mountains Grasslands Wildlife Area.
8. Evaluate land and water rights acquisitions when offered by willing sellers.
9. Evaluate the need for, and effectiveness of, an additional fish barrier above Nelson Reservoir by documenting nonnative fish movement from the reservoir.
