# Gambusia  data for Arizona

## Executive Summary

This document consists of fragmentary, heavily degraded OCR output from what appear to be **handwritten museum specimen data sheets** recording meristic (countable) characters — specifically **dorsal fin ray counts** and **denticle counts on the pharyngeal/claw-adjacent structures** — for chub specimens (genus *Gila*, family Cyprinidae) collected across a wide geographic range spanning **Arizona, Nevada, California, Texas, and Florida**, with catalog numbers, collection dates (ranging roughly **1970–1990**), and locality data. Due to severe OCR corruption of the source (a scanned card catalog / field notebook), only partial and low-confidence data could be reconstructed. This note preserves the discernible structure and flags interpretive uncertainty rather than fabricating precision the source cannot support.

## Background & Description

The records appear to originate from a **specimen-by-specimen morphometric card catalog**, likely compiled for a taxonomic revision of the *[[Gila Robusta]]* species complex (which historically included forms later split into *Gila robusta*, *Gila nigra*, and *Gila intermedia*). Each card records:

- A **catalog number** (e.g., Cat. No. 4750, 12335, 12259, 14045, 9784)
- A **locality** (state, county, specific waterbody)
- A **collection date**
- Two repeated meristic datasets per specimen:
  1. **"Denticles on 3rd ray / segments on claw"** — a count of fine tooth-like or serration structures, recorded per specimen, overwhelmingly transcribed as **"None"** across nearly all individuals sampled.
  2. **"Dorsal Ray" counts** — a fin-ray meristic count, with per-locality **averages explicitly stated** on several cards (see Key Findings).

The consistent pairing of "denticle" and "dorsal ray" data across dozens of individually numbered specimens is characteristic of a **systematic morphometric/meristic survey**, of the kind used to distinguish cryptic or geographically isolated chub populations — a diagnostic approach central to the taxonomic history of the *[[Gila Robusta]] Complex* and related southwestern cyprinids such as *[[Gila Intermedia]]* (Gila chub) and *[[Gila Nigra]]* (headwater chub).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recorded localities span a broad set of lotic and lentic freshwater habitats typical of *Gila* chub ecology:

- **Permanent and temporary streams**, reservoirs, lakes, and dams
- Arizona sites: **Gila River** (Ashurst, Kelvin), **Salt River** (Horseshoe Bend), **Verde River** (near Cottonwood), **San Pedro River**, **Bill Williams River** (Alamo), **Aravaipa Creek**, **Queen Creek**, **Devils Creek**, **Hot Creek**, **Bright Angel/Cataract-adjacent drainages**, and irrigation/sandpit habitats near Mill Avenue (Tempe area)
- Nevada sites: **Clark County** (Lake Mead / Virgin River tributaries) and **Lincoln County**
- California: **Salton Sea** environs (Riverside/Imperial County), suggesting either a translocated/introduced population or an incidental record
- Outlying records from **Texas** and **Florida**, possibly representing introduced, hatchery-derived, or comparative out-group specimens

This distribution matches the known and historic range of the *[[Gila Robusta]]* complex within the **[[Colorado River Basin]]** and **[[Gila River Basin]]**, an ecosystem characterized by fragmented, intermittent desert streams, flow regulation by dams and diversions, and high endemism among native cyprinids. Many of these populations are now considered imperiled due to **habitat fragmentation, non-native predatory fish introductions (e.g., Micropterus, Lepomis), and stream dewatering** — pressures well documented for [[Desert Fishes Of The Southwest]] more broadly.

## Key Findings

> ⚠️ **Data quality caveat:** The source material is an OCR transcription of handwritten field/museum cards with extensive character loss. Numeric values below reflect what could be directly extracted; they should be treated as **indicative, not authoritative**.

- **Denticle counts:** The overwhelming majority of individually numbered specimens (often 10–20 per locality card) show **"None"** recorded for denticles on the third ray/claw segment, suggesting this character was largely **absent or reduced** in the sampled populations — a potentially useful negative diagnostic trait for distinguishing populations or species within the complex.
- **Dorsal ray counts:** Several locality cards report explicit **population averages**, including:
  - Humbug Creek, Yavapai/Mohave Co., AZ — average **~10**
  - Lake Mead area (Virgin River?), NV — average **~8.68**
  - Graham Co., AZ (Marijilda Wash?) — average **~7.5**
  - Hot Creek/San Carlos, Graham Co., AZ — average **~7.0**
  - San Bernardino, Cochise Co., AZ — average **~7.05 (7.0)**
  - Middle Tank, Pinal Co., AZ — average **~6.4 (6.9?)**
  - Queen Creek, Pinal Co., AZ — average **~6.9**
  - Gila River at Alamo, Maricopa Co., AZ — average **~7.0**
  - Verde River at Cottonwood, AZ — average **~7.0**
  - Lower Colorado River, Yuma Division, AZ — average **~5.87 (~6.0)**
  - San Simon Ciénega, Cochise Co., AZ — average **~6.957**
  - Salt River, Devils Creek, AZ — average **~7.2** (approx.)
  - Lower Aravaipa Creek, Pinal Co., AZ — average **~9.5**
- **Geographic breadth:** The presence of records from Nevada (Clark, Lincoln counties), California (Salton Sea), Texas, and Florida alongside the Arizona core range implies either (a) a **comparative taxonomic study** including out-of-range or introduced populations, or (b) a broader survey of translocated *[[Gila]]* stocks used for **conservation refugia** or **captive propagation** programs, a common management strategy for imperiled southwestern cyprinids.
- **Temporal span:** Collection dates recovered range from at least **1970 (Queen Creek, Jan 22)** through **1990 (San Pedro River, Oct 21; Lower Aravaipa Creek, Oct 20)**, indicating a **multi-decade sampling effort**, consistent with a long-term systematic or monitoring program rather than a single expedition.
- **Character utility:** The paired recording of a **near-invariant absence trait (denticles)** alongside a **variable meristic trait (dorsal ray count, range ~5.9–10 across sites)** suggests the original study was using dorsal ray count as the primary discriminating variable among populations/subspecies within the *[[Gila Robusta]]* complex, with denticle presence/absence serving as a secondary or confirmatory character.
