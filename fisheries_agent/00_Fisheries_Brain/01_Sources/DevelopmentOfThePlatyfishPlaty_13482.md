# Development of the Platyfish, *Platypoecilus maculatus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1947 descriptive study by [[William N. Tavolga]] and Roberts Rugh (Washington Square College, New York University) establishes a graded series of 26 embryonic stages for the viviparous [[Platyfish]], *Platypoecilus maculatus*. The stages are defined by gross and superficial characters, including pigmentation, and the paper reports reproductive timing, brood sizes, embryonic rate of development, and the structure of the extra-embryonic pericardial membranes. The authors frame the work as the first phase of a broader program on [[Pigment Cell Growth]] and [[Melanoma]] biology, and as a baseline for comparison with the [[Swordtail]] (*Xiphophorus hellerii*) and platyfish-swordtail hybrids. Developmental rate could not be reliably estimated because of variability among embryos.

## Background & Description

- **Study organism:** The platyfish is described as a valuable experimental animal for work on physiological and population genetics, chromosomal sex-determination, endocrinology, and melanoma biology. Its micro- and macromelanophores are central to pigment-cell research.
- **Stock and husbandry:** Foundation stock was obtained in 1939 by [[Myron Gordon]] from the Rio Jamapa, Veracruz, Mexico. Most strains belonged to New York Zoological Society culture broods 159 and 167. Adults were hardy in quart glass jars for up to four months, with Nitella providing hiding places for newborns. Fish were fed powdered dried shrimp, a liver-Seravim-cereal paste, and live Daphnia and Tubifex.
- **Reproductive cycle:** Sexual maturity was reached in six to eight months at about 75 °F (winter) to 80 °F (summer). Broods occur year-round at roughly 28-day intervals. Brood size was one to eight in young females and averaged 30 to 40 in fully mature fish; one wild-caught 55 mm female carried 168 embryos, an exception.
- **Sperm storage:** Inseminated females isolated from males produced four or more broods. Sperm remain viable in the oviduct folds for up to seven months. Fertilization of a new ovarian complement occurs about seven days after parturition.
- **Viviparity:** Fertilization and embryonic development occur within the ovarian follicle, which ruptures just before birth. Superfetation is not characteristic of this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Comparative viviparity:** The authors place the platyfish within the order Cyprinodontes. Poeciliids such as guppy (*Lebistes reticulatus*) and swordtail show viviparous adaptations, while Cyprinodontidae (e.g., [[Common Killifish]], *Fundulus heteroclitus*, and the [[Japanese Medaka]], *Oryzias latipes*) are oviparous with macrolecithal eggs.
- **Maternal nutrition:** Cited literature includes [[Scrimshaw]] (1944, 1945), who found that many viviparous forms, including *Xiphophorus*, *Lebistes*, and several Goodeidae, showed no dry-weight loss during gestation, indicating continuous nutrient supply.
- **Other viviparous families:** Structures such as follicular pseudoplacentae, trophotaeniae (Goodeidae), and absorptive ovarian tissue (Jenynsiidae) are summarized from [[Turner]]'s work as alternative maternal-nutrition strategies.

## Key Findings

1. **Graded embryonic series:** Twenty-six stages are described, from the mature ovum (Stage 1, about 1.5 mm diameter) through birth (Stage 26, 7.9 mm; fry average 8.7 mm within 24 hours).
2. **Brood variability:** Embryos within one female differ widely in morphological age. Because a brood is born within one hour, late embryos either catch up or die and are resorbed.
3. **Developmental rate:** Of 68 females examined, 55 carried embryos. Only 21 broods met reliability criteria. Variation was too great to estimate true chronological age per stage.
4. **Blastopore and neural differences from *Fundulus*:** In the platyfish, the blastopore stays open up to tail bud formation (Stage 11), whereas in *Fundulus* it closes earlier. Optic vesicle cavities form at Stage 9 in the platyfish, but much later in *Fundulus*.
5. **Pigmentation:** The first melanophores appear in the retina at Stage 13, and the first extra-ocular melanophores at Stage 15. The brain micromelanophores number about 50 at 4.5 mm and over 250 at 5.5 mm, and are perineural. No macromelanophores were detected in pure *P. maculatus* embryos.
6. **Pericardial membranes:** The pericardial sac envelops the anterior one-quarter of the embryo in a two-layered sac, termed "pericardial amnion" and "pericardial serosa." It regresses from Stage 22, producing a transient "neck strap" (Stage 22 to 24). The yolk sac begins to involute at Stage 23, suggesting a shift from maternal supply toward yolk nourishment.
7. **Viviparity:** The authors conclude that the platyfish is truly viviparous, not ovoviviparous.
8. **Open questions:** The mechanism of maternal absorption, the embryonic origin of melanophores (possibly neural crest, as shown in amphibians), and the mechanism of pigment cell differentiation remain unresolved and require extra-ovarian culture.
