## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) letter notifying interested parties of a Federal Register proposal to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]]. The letter itself contains no direct morphological or ecological data but establishes the regulatory and conservation context for the species, citing habitat loss and population decline across the [[Colorado River Basin]]. It solicits public comment and additional status/threat data ahead of a July 23, 1990 deadline, with critical habitat designation explicitly not proposed at this time.

No morphological data (body plan, coloration, size, or distinguishing features) is provided in this source. The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a member of the [[Catostomidae]] (sucker) family, native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], but descriptive taxonomic detail is absent from this correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Range:** Found in "limited numbers" throughout the **Upper and Lower [[Colorado River Basin]]**, spanning multiple southwestern U.S. states.
- **Habitat Alterations Cited as Threats:**
  - **Water diversion** — reduction/alteration of natural flow regimes.
  - **Introduction of nonnative fishes** — implying competitive or predatory pressure on native populations.
  - **Construction and operation of dams** — habitat fragmentation and flow regime disruption, consistent with known impacts on large-river [[Catostomidae]] species.
- **Critical Habitat:** Not proposed for designation at the time of this letter, despite the species-level endangered listing proposal.

## Key Findings
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] was formally proposed for listing as **endangered** under the Endangered Species Act via Federal Register publication (referenced page 21160).
- Population decline is attributed to cumulative anthropogenic habitat change: **damming, water diversion, and nonnative species introduction** — a well-documented threat triad for native [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes.
- The USFWS [[Grand Junction Field Office]] and [[Salt Lake City Field Supervisor's Office]] were coordinating the listing process, indicating a multi-state/regional management concern.
- Public and stakeholder comment period was open through **July 23, 1990**, with a specific request for updated status and threat information beyond previously submitted data.
- No **critical habitat** was proposed concurrently with the endangered listing, a notable regulatory gap flagged in the letter.
