# Geographic Variation in Mitochondrial DNA and Biogeography of *Culaea inconstans* (Gasterosteidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gasterosteidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 *Copeia* study by Gach examines mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) length and restriction-site variation among 32 populations of the [[Brook Stickleback]] (*Culaea inconstans*), sampled from 23 drainages. Phylogenetic and geographic patterns were analysed with [[Maximum-Parsimony Analysis]] using [[Threespine Stickleback]] and [[Ninespine Stickleback]] as outgroups. The study finds that *Culaea* mtDNA is unusually large and variable, and that the species comprises two highly divergent, geographically disjunct [[Mitochondrial Lineage]]s. The distribution is interpreted as the product of [[Postglacial Dispersal]] from separate [[Glacial Refugia]] in the Mississippi and Ohio basins, with a [[New Mexico Population]] most likely introduced accidentally rather than a Pleistocene relict.

## Background & Description

*Culaea inconstans* is a small freshwater stickleback with little morphological variation and no clinal pattern in morphology across its range. Its distribution comprises cool waters across the northern interior of North America, with absence from Atlantic and Pacific coastal drainages and disjunct populations in northeast New Mexico.

Previous biogeographic hypotheses proposed:
- Survival in [[Mississippi Basin Refugium]] during the Wisconsin glaciation (McPhail and Lindsey, 1970; Mandrak and Crossman, 1992).
- A four-refugia model for the Great Lakes watershed (Bailey and Smith, 1981).
- A [[Missouri River Refugium]] hypothesis (Nelson, 1969; Mandrak and Crossman, 1992), which lacked supporting morphological or parasite evidence.
- Competing views on New Mexico origins: a Pleistocene relict (Koster, 1957) versus a Great Lakes origin (Nelson, 1969).

mtDNA was chosen because of its uniparental inheritance, lack of recombination and rapid base-pair substitution rate, which make it suitable for population-level gene genealogies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sample base:** mtDNA from 352 *C. inconstans* individuals from 23 drainages; outgroups comprised six [[Threespine Stickleback]] and four [[Ninespine Stickleback]] individuals. Most fish were captured with minnow traps and seines; others came from bait dealers (Minnesota and Wisconsin, n = 10) or multiple-generation crosses (Alberta, n = 10).
- **Historical geography:** Great Lakes topology changed substantially during Pleistocene glaciation, lake formation and isostatic rebound. Postglacial dispersal corridors included the Maumee River, the Glacial Grand River, Lake Saginaw/Lake Whittlesey tributaries and the Mackinac River, which connected the Michigan peninsulas.
- **Anthropogenic influence:** Evidence suggests human-mediated introductions, including a 1967 release into the Saline River via a contaminated fathead minnow shipment, and introduction into the Huron River drainage with trout stocking or bait.
- **Related taxa:** Comparison was made with two cyprinid shiners, *Luxilus cornutus* and *Luxilus chrysocephalus*, which show contrasting Great Lakes mtDNA patterns.

## Key Findings

1. **Unusual mtDNA size:** *Culaea* mtDNA was far larger than typical for vertebrates (<19 kb). The smallest genomes measured 17.4 kb (n = 58 fish), while the remaining 274 fish had genomes of 20 to 24 kb. The largest originally sampled was 21.9 kb. The reference [[Threespine Stickleback]] and [[Ninespine Stickleback]] genomes measured 16.6 kb and 16.8 kb.
2. **Size variation sources:** Large tandem direct duplications (2.7 to 5.8 kb) centred on the control region, small variations (100 to 300 bp) near ND5, ND6 and cytochrome b, and a 1000-bp insert between ND4 and ND5. Removing this variation gives a genome of 16.4 kb.
3. **Heteroplasmy:** Multiple mtDNA forms occurred in 134 *Culaea*; none was found in the outgroups.
4. **Two divergent lineages:** Lineage I occurred across the northern range, while lineage II occurred in Ohio, Pennsylvania, southern Michigan and New Mexico. The lineages differ by 6.4 ± 1.2% sequence divergence.
5. **Haplotype diversity:** Fourteen haplotypes were found, nine in lineage I (average divergence 0.528%) and four to five in lineage II (average divergence 1.04%). Lineage II haplotypes are more divergent and less geographically cohesive.
6. **Population structure:** Wright's F_ST was high (mean 0.7751 ± 0.1071 by population; 0.5428 ± 0.1702 by drainage), indicating little gene flow.
7. **Phylogeny:** Strict consensus of 15 equally parsimonious trees (100 steps) was reduced to four trees (183 steps) under 3:1 weighting. Both lineages were monophyletic.
8. **Biogeographic interpretation:** The disjunct lineage distribution supports recolonisation from at least two regions within the Mississippi basin. Evidence for a Missouri refugium is weak. Lineage I divergence in Alberta is small, so a Missouri refugium cannot be inferred.
9. **Southeastern Michigan:** Lineages overlap here, but this is attributed to introductions rather than natural sympatry. The regional pattern is partly concordant with post-Wisconsinan habitat availability.
10. **New Mexico population:** Its mtDNA is identical to Michigan haplotypes (Mich-B), and morphology is consistent with a Great Lakes origin. The population most likely represents an accidental introduction, probably through bait or exotic salmonid stocking, rather than a Pleistocene relict.
11. **Evolutionary implications:** High genetic divergence with morphological conservatism suggests stabilising selection, and *Culaea* is proposed as a model for testing hypotheses of morphological stasis.
