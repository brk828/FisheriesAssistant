# Mortality, Growth, Swimming Activity and Gill Morphology of Brook Trout and Atlantic Salmon Exposed to Low pH With and Without Aluminum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This experimental study compares how young-of-the-year [[Brook Trout]] and [[Atlantic Salmon]] respond to 30 days of acidified stream water (pH 5.6 ± 0.5) with and without added exchangeable aluminum (107 ± 57 µg/L). The work was carried out in outdoor [[Flow-Through Channels]] fed by water from Bear Brook, Maine, a low acid-neutralizing-capacity stream. Brook trout showed no difference in survival or growth across treatments, whereas Atlantic salmon survival and growth declined in the acid + Al treatment. Gill damage and swimming behaviour also differed between species, supporting the view that Atlantic salmon are more sensitive to acidified, aluminum-rich waters than brook trout.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Brook trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), described as acid-tolerant, and Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*), described as more sensitive to acidic conditions. Both support important sport fisheries in northeastern North America.
- **Knowledge gap:** The basis for the species difference in tolerance of acidified waters was poorly understood.
- **Study design:** Similar life stages of each species were exposed to acidity alone and in combination with aluminum in a semi-natural environment. Direct measures (mortality, growth) and indirect measures (swimming activity, gill morphology) were compared.
- **Test fish:** Brook trout averaged 1.1 g (from the Cobb Hatchery, Enfield, Maine); Atlantic salmon averaged 0.9 g (from the Craig Brook National Fish Hatchery, East Orland, Maine). Each channel held about 300 fish of each species, placed on 5–6 June 1990.
- **Background mechanism:** Aluminum solubility increases as pH falls, and total mortality in acidic waters depends on pH, inorganic monomeric Al, and calcium.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Experimental system**

- Three parallel arrays of fiberglass-coated plywood channels: one control (unaltered stream water), one acidified (HCl-adjusted, nominal pH 5.5), and one acidified plus aluminum chloride (nominal 200 µg/L inorganic Al).
- Channel water depth was 20 cm, channel volume 240 L, flow 2.5 L/min, and replacement time about 1.5 h.
- Channels were outdoors, so water temperature followed natural daily and seasonal cycles, rising from 11 °C to 18.5 °C (mean 14.3 ± 1.6 °C).
- Automatic feeders delivered commercial pellets at 3% of body weight per day.

**Water chemistry**

- Mean pH: control 6.2 ± 0.2; acid and acid + Al channels 5.6 ± 0.4. pH fell to 5.0 or below only twice, each for under 24 h, following precipitation-driven flow changes.
- Mean exchangeable Al: acid + Al 107 ± 57 µg/L; control 11 ± 6 µg/L; acid 13 ± 8 µg/L. Al exceeded 150 µg/L three times during the experiment.
- Speciation modelling ([[Alchemi Speciation Model]]) indicated that Al was mostly hydroxide-bound in the control, and fluoride and organic forms dominated in the treatment channels. Solutions were undersaturated with respect to Al(OH)3, so polymer formation was judged unlikely.

## Key Findings

**Mortality**

- Brook trout mortality was below 3% in all treatments.
- Atlantic salmon mortality was 10% in the control and acid channels but 26% in the acid + Al channel.
- Mortality in the acid + Al channel rose during the episodes when Al exceeded 150 µg/L.

**Growth**

- Brook trout growth slopes did not differ among treatments.
- Atlantic salmon growth slope in the acid + Al treatment was significantly lower (p < 0.05) than in the control and acid treatments.
- Atlantic salmon in the acid + Al treatment ate very little, suggesting reduced food intake caused the growth deficit.

**Gill morphology (scanning electron microscopy, Day 30, five fish per treatment)**

- Brook trout: control and acid gills were healthy; acid + Al gills showed minor swelling, wrinkling and fusion of secondary lamellae near filament tips.
- Atlantic salmon: control and acid gills were normal; acid + Al gills were grossly deformed, with swollen and fused secondary lamellae along the filament length.

**Swimming activity (distance moved in 30 s, Wilcoxon and Kruskal-Wallis tests)**

- Brook trout were significantly more active than Atlantic salmon in corresponding treatments.
- Brook trout activity was highest in the acid treatment; non-feeding activity in the acid + Al group was significantly lower than in control and acid groups.
- Atlantic salmon were significantly less active in both the acid and acid + Al treatments than in the control.

**Conclusions**

- Atlantic salmon were more sensitive than brook trout, showing reduced survival, growth, and activity, plus severe gill damage under acid + Al exposure.
- Gill morphology and behavioural measures detected sublethal stress that survival and growth alone did not reveal, with behavioural analysis judged the most sensitive indicator.
- Mortality variation among published studies is likely linked to differences in Al saturation of test solutions.
