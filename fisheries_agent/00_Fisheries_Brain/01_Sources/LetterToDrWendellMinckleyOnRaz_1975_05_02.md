# Razorback Sucker Pathology Examinations – Lake Mohave (1973–1975)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #parasitology #fish-pathology

## Executive Summary
This 1975 letter from Bruce D. Rosenlund (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) to Dr. Wendell Minckley (Arizona State University) summarizes findings from three pathological surveys (October 1973, March 1974, and Spring 1975) of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations from **Lake Mohave**, conducted as part of the Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team's efforts. The examinations focused on parasitic, bacterial, and viral agents affecting the species, with particular attention to a recurring and poorly understood **ocular pathology** (exophthalmia and progressive blindness) linked to a possibly undescribed *Myxosoma* sp. myxosporean parasite, secondary bacterial infections (*Aeromonas hydrophila*, *Pseudomonas* sp.), and copepod parasitism by *Lernaea*. Findings suggest that stress from handling and captivity exacerbates fungal (*Saprolegnia*) and systemic bacterial infections, raising management concerns for the conservation of this now-endangered catostomid.

## Background & Description
While this document is primarily pathological rather than taxonomic, several morphological/pathological features of affected razorback suckers are described:

- **Ocular Pathology (Exophthalmia/"Popeye" Condition):**
  - Complete corneal opacity
  - Abnormal accumulation of humor between cornea and iris
  - Presence of bacteria (mainly *Aeromonas hydrophila*) in humoral fluid anterior/posterior to iris
  - *Myxosoma* sp. spores found in large numbers in the choroid-sclera region of the eye
  - Spore morphology: **7.5 × 9 µm in face view**, possibly representing an **undescribed species** (per Dr. Glenn Hoffman)

- **Progressive Eye Degeneration (March 1974 cohort, n=38 spawning fish):**
  | Eye Condition | Percentage |
  |---|---|
  | Normal | 40% |
  | Some abnormalities | 34% |
  | Probably nonfunctional | 5% |
  | Nonfunctional/degenerated | 21% |

- **External Parasite Damage:** Skin/tissue damage attributed to *Lernaea* (anchor worm) infestation, though actual parasite loads were often low. Approximately 4% of the 38 examined fish had *Lernaea* attached to or near the eye/orbit.

- **Systemic Infection Signs (1975 moribund specimen):** Heavy fungal growth (*Saprolegnia*-type) on body and gills, accompanied by diverse bacterial colonization; no external protozoans observed; internal systemic *Pseudomonas* sp. infection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Lower Colorado River]], serving as critical spawning habitat for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Spawning Site:** Cottonwood Cove area — repeatedly identified as the primary collection and spawning location across all three survey periods.
- **Seasonal Spawning:** Spawning activity documented around March 27, 1974, coinciding with surface water temperatures of ~60°F (15.6°C).
- **Host-Parasite Interactions:**
  - *Lernaea* (copepod ectoparasite) — attaches to skin, occasionally within eye orbit
  - *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* — rare occurrence (Ich)
  - Monogenetic trematode (suborder Polyopisthocotylea) — rare occurrence
  - *Myxosoma* sp. — myxosporean endoparasite localized to ocular choroid-sclera tissue
- **Anthropogenic Stress Factors:** Handling stress during collection/transport correlated with increased susceptibility to fungal and systemic bacterial disease. Fish transported to California pond died post-transfer (1973 cohort); more careful handling in 1974 (transfer to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Page Springs State Hatchery]]) resulted in improved survival.
- **Wild Mortality Events:** Dead razorback suckers observed in situ during the 1974 Cottonwood Cove spawning collection, with kidney tissue gram stains revealing bacterial presence (causal role undetermined due to decomposition state).

## Key Findings
1. **Recurring Pathogens Identified Across Surveys:** *Lernaea* sp., *Myxosoma* sp. (possibly undescribed), *Saprolegnia* (fungal), *Pseudomonas* sp., and *Aeromonas* sp. (primarily *A. hydrophila*) represent the core suite of pathogens affecting Lake Mohave razorback suckers.

2. **Novel/Unidentified Myxosporean:** Spores recovered from ocular tissue (7.5 × 9 µm) sent to Dr. Glenn Hoffman (USFWS Parasitologist) were tentatively identified as a possibly **new, undescribed *Myxosoma* species**. Notably, Dr. Hoffman and Dr. David Burke were unable to replicate detection in a separately submitted symptomatic eye sample, raising questions about detection consistency or spore distribution.

3. **High Prevalence of Ocular Degeneration:** In the March 1974 cohort, **60% of spawning-ready fish showed some degree of eye abnormality**, with 21% completely nonfunctional/degenerated — a substantial welfare and possibly fitness-relevant condition in a wild spawning population.

4. **Etiology of Blindness Remains Unresolved:** The causative agent(s) of eye degeneration is debated among three (possibly interacting) causes: *Myxosoma* infection, *Lernaea* infestation, and/or secondary bacterial infection (*Pseudomonas*, *Aeromonas*). No single pathogen was conclusively established as primary cause.

5. **Blindness Does Not Appear to Impair Feeding/Condition:** Both blind and sighted fish maintained good body condition, suggesting functional compensation, though this warrants further study.

6. **Sex-Linked Spawning Behavior Observation:** Blind males showed **no evidence of spawning-related physical damage**, whereas sighted males exhibited **marked spawning damage**; females (regardless of vision status) showed spawning damage in all cases — suggesting visually-mediated behavioral differences in male spawning activity/aggression.

7. **Handling Stress as a Disease Modifier:** Systemic bacterial infection and fungal disease appear strongly **stress-related**, with mortality far higher in fish subjected to rougher handling/transport (1973 California-transferred cohort) versus carefully handled fish (1974 cohort at Willow Beach/Page Springs).

8. **High Mortality in 1975 Collection:** 9 of 12 examined dead razorback suckers from the Arizona State University 1975 collection carried lethal bacterial concentrations (mostly *Aeromonas* sp.), prompting initiation of **antibiotic injection protocols** as a management intervention.

9. **Treatment Limitations:** Topical potassium permanganate (KMnO₄), effective for treating [[Squawfish]] gill-net wounds, proved **unsuccessful** in controlling fungal infections in razorback suckers.

10. **Management Recommendations:** The author recommends careful future handling protocols and consideration of **prophylactic antifungal and antibiotic treatments** for endangered razorback sucker populations undergoing collection/spawning operations, given the apparent stress-disease linkage.
