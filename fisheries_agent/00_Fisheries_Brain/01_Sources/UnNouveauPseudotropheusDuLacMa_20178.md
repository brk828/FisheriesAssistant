# Pseudotropheus greshakei n. sp. and the Pseudotropheus-Melanochromis Complex

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This French-language taxonomic paper, by [[Manfred K. Meyer]] and W. Foerster, describes a new cichlid, *Pseudotropheus greshakei* n. sp., from the rocky reefs of [[Lake Malawi]]. The species is compared morphologically and cytogenetically with several related [[Mbuna]] species. The authors propose a new subgenus, *Maylandia*, with *P. greshakei* as its type species, and argue that the genera [[Pseudotropheus]] and [[Melanochromis]] can no longer be separated on pharyngeal teeth alone. The paper also reports a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 44.

## Background & Description

- **Type locality:** Makokola, southeast arm of Lake Malawi, collected 20 January 1983 by Norman Edwards.
- **Holotype:** male, SMF 17846, 89.65 mm standard length (LS), deposited at the [[Senckenberg Museum]], Frankfurt. One specimen was deposited at the [[British Museum (Natural History)]].
- **Type series:** 1 female, 1 male, 1 female, and 3 males (SMF 17850-52) as paratypes, plus two juveniles (SMF 17853-54), all collected with the holotype.
- **Etymology:** named after German aquarium-fish importer Alfons Greshake. The subgenus honours Hans J. Mayland.
- **Diagnosis:** rounded head profile from the occiput to above the eye, then a slope of about 45° to a terminal mouth with thick lips. Jaw teeth are in rounded bands with a single row of few teeth, averaging 39 in the outer row of the upper jaw. Lower pharyngeal teeth are fine and dense posteriorly.
- **Coloration:** adult males are sky blue with orange-edged scales, orange dorsal and caudal fins, and a pale blue extended pelvic ray. Females are olive-brown with a bronze sheen. Both sexes show faint vertical bars, six or seven of which lie beneath the dorsal fin.
- **Meristics:** dorsal fin XVII-XVIII, 8-9 soft rays; anal fin II, 7-8. Lateral scale series 27-29; 22-24 perforated upper lateral-line scales and 9-12 lower. First gill arch has 14-16 gill rakers, 10-11 on the lower limb.
- **Vertebrae:** 30 in three radiographs and one cleared-and-stained preparation. One fish had 27, possibly due to deformation and fusions.
- **Holotype measurements (mm):** total length 108.10, standard length 89.65, head length 36.15, body depth 30.80.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** observed only near the western shore of the southeast arm of Lake Malawi, close to the Makokola Club, roughly halfway between Mangochi and Monkey Bay.
- **Habitat:** collected on reefs at depths of 30-35 m. The associated Mbuna species were not yet known.
- **Cytogenetics:** the karyotype (2n = 44) matches that of related Lake Malawi cichlids. The authors list other species with the same number: [[Pseudotropheus brevis]], [[Pseudotropheus kingstoni]], [[Pseudotropheus crabro]], [[Pseudotropheus williamsi]], [[Pseudotropheus elongatus]] and [[Cynotilapia afra]]. This count is lower than the 2n = 48 common in most other fishes. Chromosome lengths range from about 2 to 6 µm, with a longest-to-shortest ratio of about 1:3.
- **Sex chromosomes:** no heteromorphic pair was identified, so no sex chromosome was recognised.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *P. greshakei* is described from 18 well-spread metaphases of a male and from 1 male and 1 female holotype-series fish. Its diagnosis rests on dentition, colour and body proportions.
- **Subgenus proposed:** *Maylandia* n. subgen., type species *P. greshakei*. It groups *P. greshakei* with *P. zebra*, *P. aurora*, *P. lombardoi* and *P. livingstonii*. The authors suggest the *zebra* complex belongs in *Maylandia*, while inclusion of *P. livingstonii* and *P. lombardoi* depends on how much weight is given to colour pattern.
- **Comparison with relatives:**
  - Eye diameter is 3.70-4.10 times in head length, smaller than in *P. aurora* (2.60-3.20), *P. lombardoi* (3.20-3.80), *P. livingstonii* (3.50-4.20) and *P. zebra* (3.60-4.30).
  - Gill rakers on the lower limb (10-11) are among the lowest in the subgenus, compared with 9-10 in *P. livingstonii*, 11-12 in *P. aurora*, 12 in *P. zebra* and 12-13 in *P. lombardoi*.
  - Pharyngeal teeth resemble those of *P. aurora* and *P. livingstonii*, while the tricuspid tooth form is close to *P. zebra*.
  - Its colouration differs from all previously known *Pseudotropheus*.
- **Type species of Pseudotropheus:** *P. williamsi* has regularly arranged curved tooth bands, tricuspid inner teeth, sparse and relatively large pharyngeal teeth, and a dark pattern of longitudinal spots or broken lines. *P. greshakei* differs from it in these features.
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** the genera cannot be defined by pharyngeal teeth alone. The authors suggest redefining *Melanochromis* around its longitudinal-band colour pattern, and state that a full revision of the Pseudotropheus-Melanochromis group is still needed.
