# New Locality of Cycleptus Elongatus in the Rio Bravo, Mexico and United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This short note reports a new Mexican locality for the [[Blue Sucker]] ([[Cycleptus Elongatus]], Le Sueur) in the [[Rio Bravo]] near [[Ciudad Acuña]], [[Coahuila]]. A single male specimen (142.0 mm standard length) was collected on 11 July 1968, 2 km upstream of the town. The authors summarize the species' known distribution in Mexico and the United States, and provide meristic and morphometric data for the new specimen. The record is significant because the species had been reported from Mexico only on the basis of a 1907 collection from the [[Rio Salado]] in [[Nuevo León]].

## Background & Description

[[Cycleptus Elongatus]] is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers). It is best known from the [[Mississippi River]] and [[Pearl River]] in the United States, where it is considered uncommon (Moore, 1968). The species was first recorded in Mexico by [[Meek 1907]], who collected three specimens measuring 48 to 52 mm standard length from Rodríguez, Nuevo León, in the Rio Salado. Later Mexican checklists and keys repeated this record, and it became the basis for all later mentions of the species in Mexico.

**Morphological data for the new specimen (UANL 1376):**

- Deposited in the Coleccion de Peces, Facultad de Ciencias Biologicas, [[Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon]]
- Standard length: 142.0 mm
- Fin rays: dorsal 32, anal 7, pectorals 17-17, pelvics 10-10
- Lateral line scales: 57
- Head length fits 4.9 times into standard length; maximum body height fits 4.5 times
- Within the head: eye 6, snout 2, postocular 2.4 (postocular is 1.2 times the snout length)
- Sex: male, with fine, uniformly sized tubercles covering the dorsal head, lateral surfaces above the eyes, snout, and cheeks. Sex was confirmed as male by external examination only, as dissection was not carried out because of the rarity of the specimen.
- Coloration: brown and fairly uniform, slightly darker on the back; the lower half of the caudal fin is darker.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimen was taken in the [[Rio Bravo]] (the [[Rio Grande]] in the United States), at a site about 130 km downstream of the confluence of the Rio Bravo and the [[Pecos River]]. The authors' earlier searches for additional material were unsuccessful, which suggests that the species is rare in this reach.

Previous surveys in the region gave varying results:

- [[Guerra 1952]] did not find the species in the Rio Salado of Coahuila.
- [[Hubbs]] and Hubbs (1958), while examining Guerra's material for a different species, noted that the blue sucker is part of the river's fauna but gave no specific localities.
- [[Hubbs]] (1957, 1972) listed the species from Texas rivers and regions bordering Mexico, again without localities.
- [[Robinson 1959]] did not collect the species during a survey of the Rio Bravo from the mouth of the Pecos River to the Gulf of Mexico.

These gaps mean the species' presence in the Mexican portion of the Rio Bravo was undocumented before this record.

## Key Findings

- A single male *Cycleptus elongatus* (142.0 mm standard length) was collected on 11 July 1968 in the Rio Bravo, 2 km upstream of Ciudad Acuña, Coahuila. This is a new locality for the species in the Rio Bravo and in Coahuila.
- The first Mexican record was [[Meek 1907]]'s collection of three specimens (48 to 52 mm standard length) from the Rio Salado at Rodríguez, Nuevo León. Subsequent Mexican records all depend on this report.
- The new specimen has 32 dorsal, 7 anal, 17-17 pectoral, and 10-10 pelvic fin rays, and 57 lateral line scales.
- Repeated attempts to collect more material failed, so the species appears to be rare in the study area.
- Earlier regional surveys ([[Guerra 1952]]; [[Robinson 1959]]) did not record the species, which underlines the value of this new record.
