## Executive Summary

This document is a **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Federal Register notice** (September 15, 1993) rather than a primary morphological or ecological research paper. It announces the availability of a **Biological Support Document** and reopens the public comment period regarding the proposed designation of **critical habitat** for four endangered fish species native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Colorado Pikeminnow), [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The notice is procedural/regulatory in nature, referencing an underlying biological analysis without reproducing detailed species-specific data (morphology, life history, or population metrics) in the text itself. It outlines the regulatory timeline under the **Endangered Species Act (ESA) of 1973**, including plans for economic analysis and public hearings across seven western states.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice confirms these four species are endemic to and co-occur within the **[[Colorado River Basin]]**, spanning multiple states including Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah, and Wyoming. All four species were listed as **endangered** under the ESA prior to this notice, indicating significant historical habitat degradation, flow alteration, and/or competition/predation from non-native species (common threats to this fish assemblage, though not detailed in this specific text). The document treats these species as a co-managed guild of [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes]], suggesting overlapping critical habitat requirements within the mainstem river and associated tributaries.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory Action**: A proposed rule to designate **critical habitat** for the four species was originally published January 29, 1993 (58 FR 6578), amending 50 CFR 17.95.
- **Reopened Comment Period**: The public comment period (originally Jan 29–Apr 15, 1993) was reopened indefinitely to allow review of the newly completed **Biological Support Document**.
- **Biological Analysis**: The FWS completed a biological analysis underpinning the critical habitat designation, available upon request from the Utah State Supervisor's office (Salt Lake City, UT).
- **Pending Economic Analysis**: A separate economic analysis and "overview document" summarizing both biological and economic findings were in preparation, expected within approximately 60 days.
- **Public Hearings Planned**: At least eight public hearings were scheduled across seven states (AZ, CA, CO, NV, NM, UT, WY) to gather further comment once economic data were released.
- **Legal Authority**: Action grounded in the **Endangered Species Act of 1973** (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.).
- **Administrative Contact**: Authored by Harold M. Tyus (FWS Denver Regional Office); regional director Ralph O. Morgenweck signed the filing (September 9, 1993).
