# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Aquatic Coordination Team Correspondence and Little Colorado River Habitat Review (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This 1992 document packet, issued under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, collects four items: a program update memo from [[Dave Wegner]] to the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT), a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) report justifying its intensive study sites in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), an expert review of that justification by [[Duncan T. Patten]], and a draft [[Bio/West]] mapping plan for the LCR confluence. The central dispute concerns whether the USFWS study reaches for [[Humpback Chub]] microhabitat research are representative. The USFWS analysis says yes, using habitat similarity indices. The review says the method is too limited to prove this. The packet also covers electrofishing safety, data management, and concerns about the impact of sampling on native fish in the [[Grand Canyon]].

## Background & Description

The packet is a set of program correspondence and technical documents, not a research article:

- **Program update memo (April 30, 1992):** Wegner's memo to ACT members covers five items: the LCR site justification, confluence mapping, an electrofishing accident, database development, and impacts of GCES sampling. It requests comments and sets up the next ACT meeting.
- **USFWS study site report (dated 10 April 1992, final draft):** Prepared by [[Owen T. Gorman]] to justify the representativeness of two study areas in the LCR. The report is titled "Habitat characteristics of the Little Colorado River: selection and justification of USFWS study areas."
- **Patten review memo (April 23, 1992):** A critical review of the USFWS justification, addressed to Wegner.
- **Bio/West mapping plan (memo dated April 7, 1992; draft plan):** A proposed scheme for detailed mapping of the LCR confluence, prepared by [[Rich Valdez]] and [[Bill Masslich]].

The enclosures also include figures, frequency histograms, and similarity matrices (Appendices I and II). These are summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The LCR is a perennial tributary of the [[Colorado River]] that enters the Grand Canyon. Its lower reaches are fed by [[Blue Springs]] (km 21), the source of perennial base flow. The confluence zone is affected by backwatering from the Colorado River. Native fish, especially humpback chub, depend on this system.

Key ecological points from the documents:

- **Confluence habitat:** The confluence (km 0) is the most unusual segment. It has slow currents, greater average depth, and finer substrates, mainly sand and gravel. Its similarity to upstream segments was consistently lower.
- **Habitat variation:** The Atomizer reach (km 13-16, Triple Drop to above Chute Falls) and km 1-2 (USGS gauge to AGF camp) differ detectably from the rest of the river. Between km 13 and 16, mean depth falls by almost half, mean current falls by about one-fifth, and sand becomes the dominant substrate above Chute Falls.
- **Access to the LCR:** The Bio/West plan treats access as controlled by physical barriers, water depth, and water velocity, assuming water quality is not limiting.
- **Spawning conditions:** The plan identifies areas where the LCR thermal plume may provide spawning temperatures (>16°C). It also identifies potential spawning substrate, from small cobble (<6 inches) to gravel (>0.25 inches).
- **Recreation pressure:** Wegner notes that the Grand Canyon recreation season was starting, so field activity in the confluence had to be minimized.

## Key Findings

### 1. Program management and coordination (Wegner memo)

- **Confluence mapping:** The [[National Park Service]] wants one consolidated mapping approach at the LCR confluence. Multiple mapping efforts will not be approved. [[Bio/West]] was charged with a consolidated mapping requirements document, and comments were requested from ACT members. The most detailed request will serve as the base for the rest of the effort.
- **Electrofishing accident:** An [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF) electrofishing incident in the Grand Canyon was nearly fatal. The memo sets five required actions for electrofishing crews:
  1. Check the setup for shorts and poor connections.
  2. Have units inspected by an experienced person (suggested: Coffelt or Smith Root). Homebuilt or improvised systems must be changed.
  3. Train permanent and temporary staff on the equipment and its hazards.
  4. Ensure a trained first-aid person is present on every electrofishing effort.
  5. Write a protocol that every field employee reads before using the gear.
  
  The USFWS annual [[Electrofishing]] workshop is recommended, and GCES may sponsor it.
- **Data management:** GCES and Patten are building a scientific information management system and a [[Geographic Information System]]. AGF will remain the final data repository. The memo also calls for faster data access for cooperators and researchers.
- **Impacts of sampling:** At the request of the National Park Service, GCES is preparing a report on how extensive GCES sampling may affect native Grand Canyon fish. AGF data are needed before finalizing it.

### 2. USFWS study area justification (Gorman report)

- **Field data:** Stream habitat was mapped in July and August 1991 from the confluence to Blue Springs (km 21). Transects were spaced 100 m apart. Depth, current, and substrate were measured at 1 m intervals along transects, using the methods of [[Gorman and Karr]] (1978) and [[Gorman]] (1988).
- **Sample size:** A total of 6,809 points were sampled along 201 transects. Segment sample sizes were 235-407 points for km 1-20, 110 points for the confluence (km 0), and 101 points for Blue Springs (km 21).
- **Study areas:**
  - [[Powell Camp]] (km 2.5-3.5): 347 points
  - [[Salt Trail Canyon Camp]] (km 10.5-11.5): 319 points
- **Similarity analysis:** Habitat frequency distributions were compared with [[Schoener's Percent Similarity]]. Mean similarity for km 1-21 was 0.75 (SD 0.024). The confluence was excluded from the pooled dataset because its similarity values were consistently lower.
- **Study area similarity:**
  - Powell Camp was very similar to segments 3 and 4 (mean combined similarities 0.93 and 0.87).
  - Salt Camp was very similar to segments 10, 11, and 12 (0.82, 0.96, and 0.85).
- **Substrate:** Substrate similarity between segments was lower than for depth and current, because the proportions of sand, travertine, and large boulders varied among segments. Sand and travertine are the dominant substrates for the LCR as a whole.
- **Study area structure:** Both study areas show less sand and more travertine and large particles than the LCR as a whole. The authors interpret this as more complex habitat that may support larger local fish populations.
- **Conclusion:** The authors found no justification for moving or adjusting the study area boundaries. They argue that microhabitat use analysis only requires representative arrays of habitat types, not identical relative availabilities.

### 3. Critique of the justification (Patten review)

- **Reaches are representative:** Patten agrees the study reaches represent the LCR channel where they lie.
- **Method is limited:** Using only depth, current, and substrate, the similarity indices show a high proportion of the river is similar. This may indicate the river is relatively homogeneous rather than that the study areas are uniquely suitable.
- **Confluence should not be excluded:** The confluence's different habitat is expected, but Patten says it is probably one of the most critical locations on the river and should remain in consideration.
- **Insufficient justification:** Patten does not accept the evidence as justification for selecting Powell Camp and Salt Trail or for excluding the confluence. He recommends:
  1. Using more characteristics, such as ripples and runs, to separate reaches.
  2. Applying multivariate ordination, such as [[Detrended Correspondence Analysis]], to test whether many reaches cluster around the selected study reaches.
- **Timing:** Patten says the review is not meant to delay the USFWS field season, but notes much of the spawning run has already been missed.

### 4. Confluence mapping plan (Bio/West draft)

- **Objectives:** The plan covers four objectives:
  1. Access to the LCR for humpback chub and other native fish.
  2. Temperature dynamics in relation to stage changes and LCR plume migration.
  3. Physical habitat dynamics in relation to stage changes.
  4. Substrate characteristics and potential spawning substrate.
- **Mapping unit:** The main channel is mapped from 600 ft upstream to 650 ft downstream of the confluence. The LCR boundary is set where the main-channel water surface elevation at 32,500 cfs equals the LCR base-flow water surface elevation. Ponding may extend further upstream, and LCR researchers are asked to set that limit.
- **Depth threshold:** Bio/West believes adult humpback chub need at least 15 cm of water to enter the LCR, about twice adult body depth.
- **Velocity thresholds:**
  - Juvenile humpback chub can sustain about 0.78 m/s.
  - Adults are assumed to sustain 1-2 m/s.
  - Bursting speed is assumed adequate for 3-4 m/s over short distances.
- **Mapping products:** Four products are planned: a 1:1200 bathymetric base map tied to temporary bench marks, plus temperature, habitat, and substrate maps. Together they are intended to identify potential spawning areas at various flows.
- **Substrate classes:** A modified [[Wentworth Classification]] is used, from clay and silt (<0.06 mm) through boulder (>256 mm).
- **Schedule:** GPS equipment will not be available until August 1992. Comments on the draft were due by May 1, 1992.
