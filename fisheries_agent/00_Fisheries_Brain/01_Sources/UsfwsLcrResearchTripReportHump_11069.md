# USFWS LCR Research Trip Report — Humpback Chub Monitoring, August 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Gila-cypha #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) field trip report documenting an intensive 9-day research expedition (9–18 August 1993) conducted under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), a major tributary of the Colorado River in [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona. The primary research target was the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), with sampling conducted across three study areas: **Powell Camp**, **Salt Camp**, and the **LCR–Colorado River Confluence**. The trip achieved record sampling effort (net sets, transects, seine hauls, fish observations/captures) and documented strong dominance of young-of-year (YOY) fishes in catches, especially at Salt Camp (>7,000 YOY fish). Eighteen incidental YOY Humpback Chub mortalities occurred during the trip, pushing cumulative seasonal mortalities to 33, exceeding permitted incidental take limits. Notable ecological observations included a shift in YOY chub habitat use from shallow edges (June) to deep offshore adult habitat (August), evidence of cannibalism by adult chubs on YOY fishes, and unusually clear water conditions (turbidity as low as 2.5–3 NTU) that facilitated visual/video surveys.

## Background & Description

**Study Program:** Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES), a multi-agency research effort (USFWS, Bureau of Reclamation/BOR, GCES, Arizona State University/ASU personnel) investigating fish populations and habitat in the Colorado River ecosystem downstream of Glen Canyon Dam.

**Study Areas:**
- **Powell Camp** — Upper LCR study area; exceptionally clear water (turbidity <3 NTU, as low as 2.5 NTU); deep pools inhabited by adult [[Humpback Chub]]; occasional rainbow trout (RBT) sighting.
- **Salt Camp** — Lower LCR study area; turbidity fluctuated between 3.1–9.7 NTU; extended seining survey conducted from Chute Falls downstream to determine upper distributional limits of YOY chubs, suckers, and dace.
- **Confluence** — Junction of LCR and mainstem Colorado River; focus on mixing zone thermal profiling and habitat transect surveys around "Zero Rock" and the confluence island (both north and south channels).

**Species Recorded:**
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) — primary study species, both adult and YOY life stages (HBC>150mm and HBC<150mm size classes tracked)
- [[Speckled Dace]] (SPD)
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (FMS)
- [[Bluehead Sucker]] (BHS)
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (FHM)
- Channel Catfish (CCP/CCF, abbreviations used in original text — likely catfish life stages)
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (RBT) — single individual observed at Powell Canyon transect DP-DR

**Sampling Methods Employed:**
- Minihoop net grids (midday and midnight runs), stacked/anchored/suspended in water column
- Minnow traps
- Triple-transect habitat measurements (20 m and 100 m transects)
- Small-area seine sampling with habitat measures (minimum 50 samples targeted)
- Snorkel-based visual fish observation surveys along gridded and non-gridded edge habitats
- Ryan Tempmentors used to profile cold-water plume/thermal mixing zones at the confluence
- Water quality monitoring: secchi disk, turbidimeter (NTU), staff gauge for river surface elevation, air temperature (high-low thermometers)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water clarity:** Base flow conditions produced unusually clear water throughout the LCR; Powell Camp recorded turbidity of 2.5 NTU (later reports) and <3 NTU during the trip, greatly aiding visual and video observation of fish habitat use. Salt Camp water clarity fluctuated more (3.1–9.7 NTU).
- **Ontogenetic habitat shift:** YOY [[Humpback Chub]] in June (20–40 mm TL) were restricted to shallow edge habitats; by August, larger YOY (40–75 mm TL) had moved into deep, offshore habitats typically used by adults — a notable behavioral/ecological transition.
- **Predation/cannibalism:** Adult Humpback Chub were observed regurgitating partially digested YOY fishes (species indeterminate) during June–July handling, suggesting adult chubs prey on YOY fishes of multiple species, potentially including conspecifics.
- **Condition factor variability:** Substantial variation in K (condition) factors noted among YOY chubs; many individuals were thin/emaciated, possibly linked to dietary shifts accompanying the habitat transition from edge to offshore zones.
- **Thermal mixing zone:** Confluence area (−0.2 to +0.1 km) profiled for cold-water plume dynamics where the cooler LCR water mixes with the Colorado River mainstem — relevant to thermal habitat suitability for native fishes.
- **Extreme heat stress:** Field conditions reached 110+°F, contributing incidentally to at least one chub mortality (fish flipped from net onto hot ground, died within <30 seconds).
- **Fish community distribution survey:** Seining from Chute Falls to Salt Camp's upper study area aimed to delineate the upstream-most extent of YOY chub, sucker, and dace distributions in the LCR.

## Key Findings

- **Record sampling effort:** Trip yielded record numbers of net sets, measured transects, seine samples, fish observations, captures, and mortalities compared to previous GCES trips.
- **YOY dominance:** YOY fishes dominated catches; Salt Camp alone captured >7,000 YOY fish.
- **Incidental Humpback Chub mortalities (this trip): 18 total**
  - 17 found dead in minihoop/minnow trap net sets under otherwise normal conditions (co-occurring with healthy live fish)
  - 1 mortality caused by a fish flipping out of hand onto hot ground during workup (<30 seconds exposure at 110+°F)
  - Detailed mortality log (Powell Camp, n=12 events; Salt Camp, n=6 events) with total lengths (TL) ranging approximately **37–72 mm**
- **Cumulative seasonal incidental take:** 15 mortalities (June–July, per 8/3/93 memo) + 18 (this trip) = **33 total incidental YOY Humpback Chub mortalities**, exceeding the permitted incidental take limit for the season.
- **Additional mortality context:** Several additional dead/near-dead YOY chubs observed during edge habitat visual surveys outside formal FWS study areas (not included in official mortality counts).
- **Confluence survey outcomes:** 18 transects mapped on south channel, 12 on north channel, 33 transects upstream of confluence in the LCR; only single (not triple) habitat measurements were completed at most transects due to time constraints; north channel transects extending into the Colorado mainstem were not completed.
- **Species observations at Powell Canyon:** Approximately 6 YOY channel catfish (CCP, 100–120 mm TL) observed along transects DD-DG and DP-DR; numerous large adult catfish (CCP/CCF) also observed; single Rainbow Trout (RBT) sighting confirmed by multiple personnel.
- **Interagency cooperation:** Arizona Game and Fish Department provided facility support (Boulder Camp) to the confluence survey team; no reported conflicts between ASU and FWS personnel at Salt Camp.
- **Personnel performance note:** USFWS volunteer Norm Jensen recognized for exceptional field performance at Salt Camp.
