# Spikedace and Loach Minnow Recovery Plans: Comments and Responses

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This appendix from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS, Region 2, Albuquerque) compiles the public and agency review of draft [[Recovery Plan]]s for two threatened cyprinids, the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). Drafts were circulated in July 1988, revised and advertised in the [[Federal Register]] in October 1990, and a total of 73 letters of comment were received. The document reproduces the most substantive letters and provides FWS responses to 55 numbered comment topics.

The central dispute concerns [[Critical Habitat]] designation for the lower Gila River in the Cliff-Gila Valley, New Mexico. Landowners, the [[Gila Fish and Gun Club]], [[Phelps Dodge Corporation]], irrigation and county interests, and the [[New Mexico Interstate Stream Commission]] opposed designation, citing flood control, water rights, and economic impacts. The FWS defended the biological basis of the plans and stated that economic considerations belong to later, separate critical habitat rulemaking.

## Background & Description

The two species are small, endemic cyprinids of the Gila River basin in the southwestern United States. Both were listed as threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The plans were prepared principally by [[Paul C. Marsh]] of Arizona State University with the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], a group of State, Federal, Mexican, and university biologists.

Reviewers were numerous and varied:
- **Technical and agency reviewers:** about 50 parties on the July 1988 list, including the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], and [[Bureau of Reclamation]].
- **Public reviewers:** conservation groups, county governments, ranchers, mining and industrial firms, and individuals, many submitting form letters.
- **Final 1990 comment period:** ended November 26, 1990, with late comments accepted until February 4, 1991.

Several reviewers (notably [[Walter Courtenay]], [[Paul R. Turner]], [[David L. Propst]], and [[John Rinne]]) provided scientific comments on habitat data, citations, typographical errors, and the structure of the two plans. A recurring suggestion was to combine the two plans into one document; FWS judged this impractical at that stage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Key ecological points raised across the review:

- **Non-native species:** Reviewers agreed that predation and displacement by non-native fishes, such as the [[Red Shiner]], largemouth bass, catfish, and trout, has been a major threat. The FWS stated that direct data on interactions are limited but that circumstantial evidence is strong.
- **Flow regimes:** Commenters including [[Gene Maughan]] and the Phelps Dodge reviewers debated whether diversions, dams, and flow reduction are primary causes of decline. FWS responded that fish cannot persist in dewatered reaches and that flooding is a natural, necessary process for these species.
- **Reservoirs and impoundments:** The FWS found that upstream reservoirs generally harm native fishes by altering flow, fostering non-natives, and changing temperature and chemistry. It considered still water unsuitable for either species.
- **Historical distribution:** Reviewers questioned the speculative language on historic abundance. FWS replied that statements reflect collection records and habitat preferences, and that both species were most likely abundant within a large historic range before major reductions.
- **Genetics and parasites:** A reviewer recommended a baseline genetic survey, a priority of 2. FWS also reported that neither species was infected by Asian tapeworm in earlier parasite studies.
- **Reintroduction:** Reintroductions would proceed only where the original cause of extirpation was removed, unless the cause was unknown and habitat appeared suitable. Experimental designation under Section 10(j) was not recommended.

## Key Findings

**Priorities and plan structure**
- The Albuquerque FWS memo (September 19, 1988) argued that only Task 1.3 (designation of critical habitat) should carry Priority 1, because the Priority 1 designation implies possible extinction in the foreseeable future.
- Several state agencies asked that the priority of range mapping and recovery-stream selection be raised from 3 to at least 2.
- The [[Bureau of Land Management]] (New Mexico) planned to inventory spikedace in its Gila Lower and Middle Box reaches, and noted no loach minnow records on its lands.

**Critical habitat disputes (Cliff-Gila Valley)**
- Proposed critical habitat covered about 73 km of Gila River from the mouth of Middle Box Canyon to the confluence with Mogollon Creek for spikedace, and about 37 km downstream for loach minnow.
- Opponents, including the Gila Fish and Gun Club and the Hooker Dam Association, argued this reach has "never sustained viable populations" in the Middle Box (citing [[Propst 1986]]), is largely private and man-made, and is threatened by predators.
- The Gila Fish and Gun Club's supplementary material cited flood damage "on the average of at least every five years" and a 1949 flood.
- FWS response: the Cliff-Gila Valley populations are considered the best remaining and relatively stable; the reach has not been substantially modified relative to extirpated stretches. Removal of these populations for translocation was rejected.
- The FWS stated that the forks populations (East, West, Middle) are "vital" and "slightly more secure."

**Economic and legal objections**
- [[Phelps Dodge Corporation]] requested exclusion of Eagle Creek and related Morenci-area water sources. The company said the Morenci Mine employs over 1,700 people and produced over 600 million pounds of copper in 1989. It also requested an environmental impact statement.
- The [[Coalition of Arizona/New Mexico Counties for Stable Economic Growth]] (Catron, Hidalgo, Luna, Apache, Greenlee, Navajo, Graham counties) requested a stay and extension, and argued for [[Takings Implications Assessment]] under Executive Order 12630.
- The Coalition argued the plan's estimate of "minimum total cost" of $115,000 per year did not meet the statutory cost requirement. The plan's estimates were stated to be "as accurate as possible."
- Catron County stated that private lands comprise less than 25% of the county.

**FWS responses on compliance**
- Recovery plans are categorically excluded from NEPA, are not rules under EO 12291 (no Regulatory Impact Analysis), and are planning activities exempt from EO 12630 (no TIA).
- Critical habitat rulemaking will include economic analysis, NEPA documents, and public comment prior to a final rule.
- Acquisition of land or water rights will occur only from willing sellers.
- The FWS declined to hold a public hearing or extend the comment period beyond the stated timeframe.

**Stocking and bait fish**
- The New Mexico Department of Game and Fish stated trout stockings have no significant negative impact on either fish, and proposed rewording the call to curtail stocking.
- FWS will address ongoing stockings case by case; the burden of proof lies with stocking proponents.
- Bait fish use was described as an "even greater potential source of impact."

**Corrections and additions made**
- Mescal Creek was added as a potential spikedace reintroduction site.
- Maps of historic and present distribution were added.
- Campbell Blue Creek was retained as a creek (not a river) per Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest maps.
- Dry Blue Creek was to be corrected to New Mexico.
- A Turner and Tafanelli (1983) instream flow study was added to the literature but its recommended minimum flows were not adopted, because the Instream Flow Incremental Methodology was not considered reliable for these fishes.

**Key FWS position**
- The FWS stated that the recovery plans are biological documents "not intended to assess economic factors," and that the ESA does not allow exclusion of biologically necessary actions on cost grounds.
