# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife List: 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12 (1996 Special Reprint)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a special reprint of the U.S. federal [[Endangered and Threatened Wildlife]] list, compiled from [[Code of Federal Regulations]] Title 50, sections 17.11 and 17.12, and current as of October 31, 1996. It was prepared by the Division of Endangered Species of the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The text is primarily a regulatory preamble: it defines how species are listed, how status symbols are used, and what criteria govern [[Delisting]]. The species tables themselves are largely illegible in the source scan, so the document cannot be used to extract a reliable species count or individual species records.

## Background & Description

**Scope and definitions (§ 17.11(a)–(b)).** The list names all wildlife species determined by the Services to be Endangered or Threatened, plus species treated as such because of [[Similarity of Appearance]]. Entries are defined by three columns: common name, scientific name, and "Vertebrate Population Where Endangered or Threatened." Differently classified geographic populations of the same vertebrate subspecies or species are identified by their geographic boundaries. The term "Entire" means all populations across a species' present range are listed. Common names are explicitly not reliable for identifying specimens. The Services use the most recently accepted scientific name, following the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]].

**Status symbols (§ 17.11(c)).**
- "E": Endangered
- "T": Threatened
- "E (S/A)" or "T (S/A)": similarity-of-appearance species

**Supplementary symbols (§ 17.11(g)(2)).**
- "XE": essential experimental population
- "XN": nonessential experimental population
- "NA": no special rules or critical habitat applies

**Non-regulatory data (§ 17.11(d)–(e)).** Spelling, historic range, footnotes, synonyms, and current names may be amended without public notice. Changes to the species definition or its status require following [[Part 424 Procedures]]. The "historic range" is the known general distribution from the scientific literature and does not limit the Act's prohibitions, which apply to all individuals wherever found.

**Footnotes and cross-references (§ 17.11(f)).** The "When Listed" column cites [[Federal Register]] publications, with footnote numbering shared between §§ 17.11 and 17.12. The "Special rules" and "Critical habitat" columns cross-reference other parts of Title 50, including Parts 17, 217–227, and 402. Other federal, state, and local rules may also apply.

**Taxonomic scope (§ 17.11(g)).** Listing a taxon includes all lower units. The example given is the genus *Hylobates* (gibbons), listed as Endangered throughout its range in China, India, and Southeast Asia, so every species, subspecies, and population in the genus is treated as Endangered.

**Reprint symbols.** A "+" marks entries added by a rule after the October 31, 1995 republication. A dash-style marker flags entries amended by a rule since then. These symbols do not appear in the annual codification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is not a field study and contains no ecological measurements, population data, habitat descriptions, or water chemistry. Its environmental relevance lies in the legal framework for protecting imperiled wildlife. The Act's prohibitions apply to listed species wherever they occur, and the historic range column signals where populations were once known, not where they remain. The reference to Parts 217–227 and 402 indicates that listing decisions connect to broader federal rules governing wildlife protection and interagency consultation.

## Key Findings

- **Listing purpose:** The list identifies all species of wildlife determined to be Endangered or Threatened, plus similarity-of-appearance species.
- **Status classes:** Endangered (E), Threatened (T), and similarity-of-appearance (S/A) status, with experimental population designations (XE, XN).
- **Regulatory stability:** Non-substantive changes (names, footnotes, historic range) can be made without notice, but status changes require formal rulemaking under Part 424.
- **Taxonomic inclusion:** Listing a higher taxon applies to all lower units; *Hylobates* (gibbons) is the illustrated example.
- **Listing factors (§ 424.11(c)):** Species are listed if threatened by habitat destruction or curtailment, overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes, disease or predation, inadequacy of regulatory mechanisms, or other natural or manmade factors.
- **Delisting grounds (§ 424.11(d)):** Species may be removed due to:
  - **Extinction:** Only after a sufficient search period with no individuals found.
  - **Recovery:** When the species no longer meets the Act's threshold for endangered or threatened status.
  - **Original data error:** When the listing relied on data later found to be incorrect.
- **Reference scope:** The "Delisted, Removed, or Reclassified Entries" section cites many Federal Register notices spanning roughly 1973 to 1996. Most citations are too degraded in the scan to transcribe reliably.
- **Data limitation:** The species tables are heavily degraded by OCR errors. The number of listed species, their taxonomic groups, and their specific ranges cannot be reliably reported from this source.
