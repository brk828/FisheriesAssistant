# Salmo evermanni A Synonym of Salmo clarkii henshawi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #taxonomy #cutthroat-trout

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic revision demonstrates that *[[Salmo evermanni]]*, described by Jordan and Grinnell in 1908 from the upper [[Santa Ana River]] in the [[San Bernardino Mountains]], is a junior synonym of *[[Salmo clarkii henshawi]]* (Lahontan cutthroat trout) from [[Lake Tahoe]]. The authors discovered historical stocking records showing that cutthroat trout fry from Lake Tahoe tributaries were planted in the Santa Ana River in 1895 and 1896, eleven years before the *evermanni* specimens were collected. Morphological comparison revealed no significant differences between the type specimens of *evermanni* and *henshawi*, particularly in diagnostic characters including distribution and size of dark spots and high gill raker counts. The population is now extinct.

## Background & Description

*[[Salmo evermanni]]* was described by Jordan and Grinnell (1908) from specimens collected in the upper [[Santa Ana River]] above falls on [[Mount San Gorgonio]], [[San Bernardino Mountains]], southern California, at an elevation of 7,500 feet. The original description noted the presence of hyoid (basibranchial) teeth, a character distinguishing cutthroat from rainbow trout, though the describers did not directly compare their material with cutthroat trout specimens.

Jordan (1919) characterized *evermanni* as "a little trout, plain colored, with large black spots, and very small scales, like a cutthroat," isolated from lower rainbow trout populations by waterfalls. The original description mentioned the absence of red on the throat, though the authors noted this character was assessed from alcohol-preserved specimens where the cutthroat mark typically disappears.

*[[Salmo clarkii henshawi]]* (Gill and Jordan, 1878) is the [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] native to [[Lake Tahoe]] and its tributaries, including [[Taylor Creek]] and [[Blackwood Creek]]. This subspecies is distinguished from other cutthroat trout by the distribution, size, and shape of dark spots and characteristically high gill raker counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Sisson Hatchery]] operations during 1895-1896 obtained cutthroat trout spawn from [[Taylor Creek]] and [[Blackwood Creek]], tributaries to [[Lake Tahoe]]. These fish were reared and distributed throughout California mountain streams as part of state fish stocking programs.

Stocking records from the Fourteenth Biennial Report of the Fish Commissioners of California document specific plantings in the [[Santa Ana River]] drainage:
- July 30, 1895: 6,000 cutthroat trout fry in Santa Ana River, [[San Bernardino County]]
- July 26, 1896: 2,500 fry in "Santa Ana River" and 15,000 in "Santa Ana River, above falls"

The records indicate cutthroat trout fry were planted in most, if not all, streams of the higher parts of the San Bernardino Mountains. The location "Santa Ana River, above falls" likely refers to the same area where *evermanni* specimens were collected eleven years later in 1907.

The *evermanni* population became extinct, likely quite early after its description. Bryant (1929) reported that "pack train loads of rainbows and steelhead trout have been placed in the habitat of the San Gorgonio trout and as a consequence the native stock has been replaced by a hybrid fish." Gard (1953) concluded after careful search that the population was extinct before 1952.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Revision**: *[[Salmo evermanni]]* Jordan and Grinnell, 1908, is a junior synonym of *[[Salmo clarkii henshawi]]* Gill and Jordan, 1878. The specimens upon which *evermanni* was based were derived from planted cutthroat trout originating from [[Lake Tahoe]].

**Morphological Analysis**: Direct comparison of the type and two cotypes of *evermanni* with specimens of *[[Salmo clarkii henshawi]]* from Lake Tahoe revealed no significant differences. The specimens were alike in:
- Distribution, size, and shape of dark spots
- High number of gill rakers
- These characters distinguish *henshawi* from other cutthroat trout subspecies

**Historical Stocking Evidence**: Documentation from California Fish Commissioners reports establishes that 23,500 cutthroat trout fry from Lake Tahoe tributaries were planted in the Santa Ana River system in 1895-1896, with specific plantings "above falls" matching the collection locality of *evermanni* type specimens.

**Diagnostic Characters**: The presence of hyoid (basibranchial) teeth noted in the original description distinguishes cutthroat from rainbow trout. The absence of red throat coloration in preserved specimens is not diagnostic, as this mark typically disappears in alcohol-preserved cutthroat specimens.

**Taxonomic History**: The validity of *evermanni* went unquestioned due to the reputation of the describers and scarcity of specimens. Only five original specimens exist: three in the [[Stanford University]] collection and two in the [[U.S. National Museum]]. Miller (1950) recognized the specimens as belonging to the cutthroat series but was unaware of the stocking history.

**Biogeographic Considerations**: The presence of an endemic cutthroat in the San Bernardino Mountains is unlikely on distributional grounds, supporting the conclusion that the population originated from introduced stock.

**Population Status**: The *evermanni* population is extinct. Evidence suggests extinction may have occurred quite early, possibly due to subsequent stocking of rainbow and steelhead trout that replaced or hybridized with the cutthroat population.
