# Arizona Roundup: Boat Registration, a Record Razorback Sucker, and 1978 Wildlife Awards

#ichthyology #marine-biology #suckers

## Executive Summary

This 1978 *Arizona Republic* "Arizona Roundup" column covers three unrelated items from Arizona's wildlife community. The main fish-related item is a new [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] state record for a [[Razorback Sucker]], caught by [[Freeman Summers]] in [[Lake Havasu]]. The column also notes a pending boat registration deadline and announces several wildlife awards. The text is brief and reports no survey data, population counts or research methods.

## Background & Description

The rare fish is described in the column as having no scales, a head like a pike and a mouth like a sucker. The column gives its common names as razorback sucker and humpback sucker. No scientific name, family or detailed morphology is given.

The fish is treated as rare. [[Rich Beaudry]], a wildlife manager stationed at [[Lake Havasu]] for 10 years, said the catch was the first razorback he had seen in that time.

## Key Findings

**1. New state record razorback sucker**
- Caught by [[Freeman Summers]] of [[Lake Havasu City]] on January 23 in Lake Havasu.
- Weight: 9 pounds, 13 ounces.
- Bait: local freshwater clams.
- The previous state record was a 6-pound, 4-ounce fish caught the preceding October in [[Lake Mohave]].

**2. Rarity noted by local staff**
- Beaudry said this was the first razorback he had seen in the 10 years he had worked at the lake.

**3. Boat registration deadline**
- Boat registration was due February 28 that year.
- As of the column, 39,000 watercraft registrations were still outstanding.
- Ken Smart, supervisor of the game department's [[Fiscal Section]], warned that the short month, shortened further by two Monday holidays, could produce long lines of late filers.

**4. Wildlife awards**
- **[[Jim Higgs]]** of Prescott was named the department's Wildlife Manager of the Year. He joined the department in 1957 as a summer student, became a wildlife biologist in Willcox in 1959, and later moved to Prescott as wildlife manager.
- **[[Frances Werner]]** of Tucson received the Conservationist of the Year award from the [[Arizona and New Mexico Wildlife Society]]. She had previously been named Wildlife Conservationist of the Year by the Arizona Wildlife Foundation.
- The [[Doug Morrison Memorial Award]], given by the Arizona chapter of the Wildlife Society for outstanding professional field work, was shared by:
  - **[[Norman Woolsey]]**, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
  - **[[Melvin J. Wilhelm]]**, [[Bureau of Land Management]].
