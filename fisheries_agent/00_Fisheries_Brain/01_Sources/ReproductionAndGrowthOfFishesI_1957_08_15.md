# Reproduction and Growth of Fishes in Marion County, Iowa, Farm Ponds

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #pond-management

## Executive Summary

This 1957 study by Robert B. Moorman (Iowa State College) examines growth rates, length-weight relationships, condition factors, and reproductive timing/success of four key farm pond fish species — [[Largemouth Black Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[White Crappie]], and [[Black Bullhead]] — across 19–60 farm ponds in Marion County, Iowa. The work is part of the Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit's broader investigation into fish population dynamics in impounded farm ponds, and was submitted as part of a 1953 Ph.D. thesis. Key findings show that Iowa pond fish generally grew faster than fish in comparable Midwestern natural lakes but exhibited below-average to average body condition (plumpness), suggesting possible food limitation or water turbidity effects. Reproductive success was closely tied to pond "balance" (predator-prey ratios per [[Swingle's Balanced Pond Concept]]), stocking history, water clarity, and incidence of winterkill.

## Background & Description

The study forms part of a broader Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit project (Project 37) assessing fish populations in Iowa farm ponds relative to management history. Data were collected primarily via seining, with supplemental rotenone treatment, trapping, and angling. Growth was assessed using the standard scale-annulus method (validated via known-age fish, tracked year classes, and correlation between size/age).

Four species were analyzed:
- **[[Largemouth Black Bass]]** — the stocked predator species, intended to control forage fish recruitment.
- **[[Bluegill]]** — the primary forage species, valued for its extended intermittent summer spawning.
- **[[White Crappie]]** — occupies an intermediate predator/forage niche; prone to stunting if not periodically thinned.
- **[[Black Bullhead]]** — a hardy, easily-caught species associated with water turbidity ("roiliness") and prone to stunting/overcrowding without adequate bass predation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond typology**: Ponds were classified as "balanced" (sustained co-reproduction of bass and bluegill, per [[Swingle's Balanced Pond Concept]]), "overcrowded with bass" (e.g., Pond 25), or "unbalanced" (interrupted reproduction sequences, e.g., Ponds 14, 18, 30, 43).
- **Water turbidity**: Chronic muddiness (fine suspended clay), often attributed to black bullhead bottom-feeding activity, was repeatedly linked to poor bass condition and slowed bluegill/bass growth (e.g., Ponds 18, 33, 43, 57).
- **Winterkill**: A recurring disturbance affecting reproduction continuity (Ponds 27, 30, 51) and periodically reducing bass predation pressure, indirectly benefiting other species (e.g., Pond 25 bluegill success in 1951).
- **Interspecific competition**: Presence of black bullheads and white crappies could suppress bluegill growth (Ponds 2, 33) via competition for food/space; conversely, overcrowded bass populations suppressed bluegill reproduction (Pond 25).
- **Temperature cues**: Bass spawning onset linked to spring water temperature rise to ~70°F; bluegill spawning associated with temperatures below 80°F potentially causing delayed/late hatches (as late as September in Pond 43, 1951).
- **Predation dynamics**: Young-of-year bass documented preying on young white crappies and black bullheads (Pond 6, July 1950), indicating early onset of piscivory.

## Key Findings

### Largemouth Black Bass
- **Sample**: 257 bass, 3.5–17.5 in. TL, from 19 ponds (1950–1951).
- **Length-weight equation**: `Log W = -1.450 + 3.136 Log L` (W = lb × 100; L = total length in inches) — weight increased faster than length cubed.
- **Condition factor (C)**: Mean = 43.3 (range 27.3–82.5), rated "poor" to "average" per [[Bennett's Condition Standards]] (poor 35-45, average 46-55, good 56-85) — suggests possible food shortage or turbidity-limited foraging.
- **Growth**: 
  - Pond 25 (overcrowded with bass) growth: Age I=3.4in, II=6.6in, III=11.0in (rapid after fish large enough to eat mid-sized bluegill/bass), IV=13.7in.
  - Combined 18 other ponds (93 fish): Age I=5.2in, II=9.6in, III=12.6in, IV=14.4in, V=16.0in — growth rate above regional average, attributed to ponds not yet at full carrying capacity.
- **Reproduction**:
  - Minimum spawning weight ~5-6 oz (~9 in TL, per Swingle & Smith).
  - In newly stocked ponds, bass typically spawned as **2-year-olds** (17 ponds confirmed second spring after fall fingerling stocking).
  - Spawning onset tied to water temp reaching 70°F; young bass typically present by late June in Iowa ponds.
  - Annual spawning was the norm once initiated; only 3 of 17 ponds showed unexplained skipped years — more consistent than Indiana ponds (Krumholz: successful spawning only 1 in 2 years).

### Bluegill
- **Sample**: 1,435 fish, 3.5–8.6 in. TL, from 19 ponds; growth data from 567 fish across 12 ponds.
- **Length-weight equation**: `Log W = -1.360 + 3.282 Log L` — weight increased faster than cube of length. Reaches 0.25 lb ("desirable"/table size per Bennett, Thompson & Parr) at ~7.0 in; 0.5 lb at ~8.5 in.
- **Condition factor (C)**: Mean = 69.7 (range 36.2–104.5), intermediate between "poor" and "average" per Bennett's standards; comparable to other Iowa impoundments (Red Haw Lake C=70.4, East Lake C=69.4, Lake Ahquabi C=69.9) and Michigan lakes (C=56-75).
- **Growth by pond category** (mean TL in inches at annuli 1-4):
  | Pond Group | Annulus 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Balanced (combined, n=374) | 1.7 | 4.1 | 6.1 | 7.0 |
  | Overcrowded w/ bass (Pond 25, n=40) | 1.5 | 4.4 | 6.1 | 7.1 |
  | Unbalanced (combined, n=153) | 1.2 | 3.0 | 5.0 | 5.8 |
- Fastest growth observed in Pond 45 (annuli: 3.4, 6.2, 7.3 in) — historically produced bluegill to 11 in TL prior to 1946.
- **Reproduction**:
  - Minimum spawning size ~0.5 oz (~3.5 in TL); newly stocked ponds saw bluegill spawn at **age 1**.
  - In established ponds, minimum size often not reached until age 2, delaying first spawn.
  - Spawning window: May–August; some ponds showed no hatch until August, and one instance (Pond 43, 1951) showed hatching as late as mid-September.
  - Once initiated, annual spawning was typical, with failures linked to bass overcrowding (Pond 25), winterkill (Ponds 30, 51), or human disturbance (Pond 6, 1951 trapping).

### White Crappie
- **Sample**: Detailed data from Pond 2 and Pond 6 (1950).
- **Length-weight equations**:
  - Pond 2: `Log W = -1.458 + 3.208 Log L`
  - Pond 6: `Log W = -1.528 + 3.177 Log L`
  - Both show weight increasing faster than cube of length; Pond 2 fish notably heavier/plumper for given length than Pond 6 fish (same collection season, ruling out seasonal effect).
- **Condition**: Intermediate plumpness relative to a northern Iowa natural lake and three southern Iowa impoundments.
- **Growth**: Comparatively slow — approx. 3 growing seasons required to reach 6 in TL (minimum harvestable size).
- **Reproduction**: 
  - Pond 2 (stocked as yearlings from stream): spawned successfully in first year and annually thereafter.
  - Pond 6 (stocked as fingerlings): first spawned at age 2; reproduction intermittent (failed 1948, 1949).

### Black Bullhead
- **Sample**: 244 fish from 7 ponds (1950-1951).
- **Length-weight equation**: `Log W = -1.3518 + 3.0596 Log L`.
- **Condition factor**: Mean C = 51.6, slight positive trend with length.
- **Minimum desirable size**: ~6 in TL (~0.1 lb).
- **Growth**: Age determination via scale/vertebrae/spine methods not performed; populations typically showed only a **single age class** per pond due to narrow size-range cohorts.
- **Reproduction/Ecology**: Associated with water turbidity ("roiliness"); prone to overcrowding/stunting absent adequate bass predation. In Pond 27, a severe 1949–1950 winterkill eliminated competing species, allowing bullheads to spawn successfully for the first time in years — reaching ~5 in TL by late June the following year.
