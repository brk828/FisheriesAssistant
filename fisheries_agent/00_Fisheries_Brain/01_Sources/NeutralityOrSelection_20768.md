## Executive Summary

Philip Hedrick describes a novel bi-allelic selection model discovered while analyzing parent-offspring segregation data at two human [[Major Histocompatibility Complex]] (MHC) loci — **HLA-A** and **HLA-B** — in a South Amerindian population (data from E. L. Black and P. W. Hedrick, unpublished). The model is mathematically unusual: it produces **no net change in allelic frequency** at any starting frequency (a "neutral equilibrium"), yet simultaneously predicts a **minimum mean population fitness** and a **maximum excess of heterozygotes** over Hardy-Weinberg expectations when allele frequencies are equal. This selection appears linked to **maternal-fetal immunological interactions**, wherein homozygous offspring of heterozygous mothers are selected against.

## Background & Description

- **System studied:** Parent-offspring genotype data at two loci of the human MHC — [[HLA-A]] and [[HLA-B]] — in [[South Amerindians]].
- **Observation:** Across nearly all mating types capable of producing homozygous offspring (Table 1), except crosses between homozygous mothers and heterozygous fathers, there was a statistically significant **excess of heterozygotes** relative to Mendelian expectations.
- **Selection coefficient:** A large and significant selection coefficient (**s = 0.46**) was estimated against homozygous offspring.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Selection against homozygous offspring appears specific to **heterozygous mothers**, consistent with an [[MHC-Mediated Maternal-Fetal Interaction]] hypothesis, wherein immunological recognition systems dependent on maternal heterozygosity influence offspring survival.

### The Bi-Allelic Model
- Two alleles, A₁ and A₂, with frequencies *p* and *q*; genotype frequencies P (A₁A₁), H (A₁A₂), Q (A₂A₂).
- Selection acts **only on homozygous progeny born to heterozygous mothers**.
- Mean population fitness: 
  - **w̄ = 1 − sH/2**
- Despite this selection process, the expected change in allele frequency (**Δq**) equals **zero for all values of q** — mathematically equivalent to a strictly [[Neutral Evolution]] model, the foundational assumption of much of molecular evolution theory.

## Key Findings

- **Neutral Equilibrium Under Selection:** Despite operating a real selective process against certain homozygotes, the model produces **no net allele frequency change** — mimicking a purely neutral model at equilibrium.
- **Fitness Minimum at Equal Allele Frequencies:** Mean fitness (w̄) is minimized when p = q (equal allele frequencies) and maximized when q = 0 or 1 — a pattern reminiscent of **selection against heterozygotes**, despite an actual **heterozygote excess**.
- **Maximum Heterozygote Excess:** The fixation index **F = 1 − (Hₑ/2pq)** becomes maximally negative at equal allele frequencies, indicating peak heterozygote excess relative to Hardy-Weinberg expectations.
- **Numerical Example:** At p = q and s = 0.5 → **w̄ = 0.854**, **F = −0.172**.
- **Compensatory Fitness Mechanism:** Heterozygous mothers have reduced average offspring fitness (due to homozygote selection against their progeny) but this is offset by the **higher relative fitness of heterozygous offspring**, preserving the neutral equilibrium.
- **Implications for Multi-Allelic Systems:** Introduction of a third allele via mutation into this two-allele neutral equilibrium confers a **selective advantage** to the new allele. This suggests that in loci with more than two alleles — as is typical for most **MHC genes** — this selection model would maintain **stable multi-allelic polymorphism**, rather than pure neutrality.
- **Novelty:** According to the author, this is the **first described selection model** exhibiting the combined properties of (1) a true neutral equilibrium in allele frequency dynamics, alongside (2) a fitness minimum and (3) a heterozygote excess maximum at equal allele frequencies.
