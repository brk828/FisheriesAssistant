# Gila Topminnow Reintroduction - Bylas Spring Parasite Assessment

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service (Region 2, Arizona Fisheries Resources Office / Fisheries Health Center) dated July 25, 1996. The memo addresses disease and parasite screening protocols required prior to the reintroduction of the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), a federally listed endangered species, into Bylas Spring. Surrogate fish (*[[Gambusia]]* spp., Mosquitofish) native to the spring were examined for pathogens to establish a baseline parasite profile of the habitat prior to stocking the target species.

## Background & Description
*Note: The source document is a regulatory/health memo and does not provide morphometric or phenotypic data for the Gila Topminnow or Gambusia. It focuses exclusively on parasitological health screening.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Bylas Spring, a fragile, likely isolated desert spring system in Arizona—critical habitat type for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Surrogate Species Role:** *Gambusia* (Mosquitofish) were utilized as sentinel/surrogate organisms to assess the existing pathogen load of the spring environment without risking the endangered target species directly.
- **Source Population Constraints:** The recommendation explicitly excludes fish sourced from open, uncontrolled pond environments, citing higher risk of exposure to unknown or additional parasites. This implies a preference for genetically and pathogenically controlled hatchery-reared stock over wild-caught individuals for reintroduction efforts, a common strategy in [[Endangered Species Recovery]] programs.

## Key Findings
- **Parasite Screening Results:** Internal and external examination of surrogate *Gambusia* specimens from Bylas Spring revealed the presence of two parasite taxa:
  - ***Cleidodiscus*** sp.—a monogenean trematode genus commonly infecting the gills of freshwater fish.
  - ***Ambiphrya*** sp.—a peritrich ciliate ectoparasite, closely related to *Trichodina*, commonly found on skin and gills of freshwater fish.
- **Management Recommendation:** Fish designated for reintroduction into Bylas Spring must be **parasite-free**, with the exception of the two organisms already identified as endemic to the spring (*Cleidodiscus* and *Ambiphrya*).
- **Sourcing Protocol:** To ensure this parasite-free status, the memo recommends using fish that have been **spawned and reared in a controlled environment** (e.g., a hatchery/refuge system), explicitly prohibiting the use of fish sourced from open ponds or uncontrolled environments due to elevated risk of introducing novel pathogens into the recipient ecosystem.
- **Future Action:** The Fisheries Health Center (FHC) offered continued support to examine any additional potential donor populations prior to their transfer, underscoring a standard [[Biosecurity Protocol]] for endangered species translocation.
