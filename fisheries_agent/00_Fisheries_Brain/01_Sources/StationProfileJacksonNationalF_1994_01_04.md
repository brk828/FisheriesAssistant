# 1994 Station Profile: Jackson National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #FishHatchery #FisheriesManagement

## Executive Summary
The Jackson National Fish Hatchery (NFH), established in 1958 under the [[Palisades Dam Act]] (64 Stat. 1083, 1950), is a federal mitigation hatchery operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6) in Jackson, Wyoming. Its primary mission is to mitigate the ecological impacts of federal water development projects—chiefly the [[Palisades Reservoir]]—and to support fisheries management across the upper [[Snake River]] basin in Wyoming and Idaho, as well as the [[Wind River Indian Reservation]]. The station produces and distributes approximately 500,000 fish (~70,000 lbs) annually, primarily [[Snake River Cutthroat Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Lake Trout]], across a distribution area of 18,000 square miles encompassing 100,000 acres of lakes and 4,000 miles of streams. Notably, FY1993 marked the final year of the brown trout program at this facility. The hatchery also plays a key role in the [[National Broodstock Program]], maintaining wild Snake River cutthroat trout broodlines and supporting acquisition of a wild [[Lewis Lake]] strain lake trout broodstock for Great Lakes lake trout restoration efforts.

## Background & Description
This station profile is administrative/operational in nature and does not provide direct morphological data on the species reared. However, relevant taxonomic and strain information includes:
- **Snake River Cutthroat Trout** — a wild spring-spawning strain maintained as consecutive-year broodstock classes under the National Broodstock Program; a subspecies of [[Cutthroat Trout]] native to the upper Snake River drainage.
- **Lake Trout** — sourced from a wild [[Lewis Lake]] strain, used to support broodstock development for [[Great Lakes Lake Trout Restoration]].
- **Brown Trout** — non-native salmonid reared for stocking; program discontinued after FY1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution Range:** Fish reared at Jackson NFH are stocked across an 18,000 square mile area spanning Wyoming and Idaho, including 100,000 acres of lakes and 4,000 miles of streams within the upper [[Snake River]] basin.
- **Habitat Types Served:** Federal waters (FWS and other federal lands), state waters, and private waters, indicating a broad multi-jurisdictional stocking program.
- **Water Source:** The hatchery relies on a spring system delivering 800–2,400 gpm, supplemented by 6 wells with pumps and a recirculating water system, indicative of a coldwater habitat suited to salmonid propagation.
- **Ecological Role:** Serves as a mitigation and restoration tool, offsetting habitat loss from the [[Palisades Dam]] and reservoir construction, while also contributing genetic stock to the broader [[Great Lakes Lake Trout Restoration]] initiative via the Lewis Lake lake trout strain.

## Key Findings
- **Authority & Mission:** Established under the Palisades Dam Act (1950) to mitigate impacts of federal water projects, primarily Palisades Reservoir.
- **Species Produced:** [[Snake River Cutthroat Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Lake Trout]] — averaging 500,000 fish (~70,000 lbs) annually.
- **Program Change:** FY1993 was the final year of the brown trout program at Jackson NFH.
- **Broodstock Contributions:** Maintains consecutive-year classes of wild spring-spawning Snake River cutthroat trout broodstock; assists in securing wild Lewis Lake strain lake trout broodstock for Great Lakes restoration.
- **Physical Infrastructure:** 1 combination hatchery/office/public-use building; 21 concrete raceways (8'×100'); 14 concrete hatchery tanks (30'×48'); spring system (800–2,400 gpm); 6 wells; recirculating water system; standby generator.
