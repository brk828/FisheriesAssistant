# New Distributional Records of Illinois Fishes with Additions to the Known Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #cyprinidae #centrarchidae #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This 1988 faunal survey paper reports new distributional records for 12 fish species in Illinois, based on recent field surveys (primarily in southern Illinois) and re-examination of historical fish collections from the late 1930s–1940s housed at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]]. Two species — the [[Taillight Shiner]] (*Notropis maculatus*) and [[Inland Silverside]] (*Menidia beryllina*) — are documented as **new additions to the Illinois state faunal list**, bringing the known total to 202 species (up from 199 reported by Smith 1979). An anomalous record of [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*) from the Mississippi River is also documented, along with range extensions for nine other native species including the [[Spotted Gar]], [[River Chub]], [[Pugnose Minnow]], [[Lake Chubsucker]], [[Trout-Perch]], [[Spotted Sunfish]], [[Bantam Sunfish]], [[Dusky Darter]], and [[Lake Sturgeon]]. The authors recommend conservation status reevaluation for the taillight shiner (endangered), spotted sunfish (reconsideration of threatened status), and inland silverside (exclusion from conservation lists due to lack of a viable native population).

## Background & Description

- **[[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*)**: Single female specimen, 555 mm standard length, containing mature ova — indicating reproductive maturity despite being far outside native range.
- **[[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*)**: Two specimens weighing approximately 18 lbs and 50 lbs; one individual bore a partially destroyed, illegible dorsal fin tag, suggesting prior tagging from an upstream management program.
- **[[Taillight Shiner]] (*Notropis maculatus*)**: Young-of-the-year individuals present at the type locality, confirming local recruitment/reproduction rather than a single vagrant occurrence.
- **[[Lake Chubsucker]] (*Erimyzon sucetta*)**: Distinguished taxonomically from its close relative the creek chubsucker (*Erimyzon oblongus*); historical museum records were found to have been conflated between the two species in prior distributional atlases.
- **[[Pugnose Minnow]] (*Notropis emiliae*)**: Collected in high abundance (up to 40 individuals per site) across lentic and wetland habitats, indicating morphological/ecological adaptability to still-water conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat associations**: Species were documented across a range of lotic and lentic systems — main-stem rivers (Mississippi, Ohio, Wabash), tributary creeks, oxbow lakes, sloughs, swamps, and semi-permanent wetlands, particularly concentrated in the [[Cache River]], [[Ohio River]], and lower [[Wabash River]] drainages of southern Illinois.
- **[[Taillight Shiner]]**: A Coastal Plain species; the Illinois record represents the northernmost known limit of its range and the most upstream occurrence in the Ohio River basin, found in an unnamed wetland near Devil's Washboard, Massac County.
- **[[Inland Silverside]]**: Native occurrence in the Mississippi River near Grand Tower represents the northernmost natural locality recorded; the species has also been deliberately introduced as a forage fish into thermally enriched lakes (Baldwin Lake, Lake of Egypt, Rend Lake) since 1980, where it appears capable of overwintering.
- **[[Bantam Sunfish]] (*Lepomis symmetricus*)** and **[[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]]** (*Elassoma zonatum*): Found syntopically in Horseshoe Lake, representing a new locality record for both species and an extension of the bantam sunfish's range through the Clear Creek drainage.
- **[[Dusky Darter]] (*Percina sciera*)**: Previously thought restricted to the Vermilion, Embarras, and Little Wabash rivers; now documented in the Cache and Ohio rivers, consistent with known distribution across the Ohio River in western Kentucky.
- **[[Trout-Perch]] (*Percopsis omiscomaycus*)**: Historical (1939) records from Big/Little Kincaid Creek (Big Muddy River drainage) indicate a historically more southern range than previously recognized by Smith (1979); sites are now impounded (Lake Kincaid) and likely no longer support the species.
- **[[River Chub]] (*Nocomis micropogon*)**: Little Vermilion River population represents the only known potential breeding population in Illinois, supported by abundant nesting habitat.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new state records**: [[Taillight Shiner]] (*Notropis maculatus*) and [[Inland Silverside]] (*Menidia beryllina*) are formally added to the Illinois fish fauna, raising the state total to 202 species (with the anomalous Atlantic salmon record also factored in).
2. **Taillight Shiner conservation urgency**: Found only at one locality among 22 lakes/wetlands surveyed along the lower Ohio and Wabash rivers; presence of young-of-year suggests active local reproduction, but the population may be at high risk of extirpation — recommended for state endangered status.
3. **Inland Silverside provenance**: The Mississippi River record predates known stocking programs in Illinois, suggesting a natural (non-introduced) origin; however, no additional specimens were found in ten years of subsequent sampling, indicating no viable native population — recommend exclusion from conservation lists.
4. **Atlantic Salmon anomaly**: Over 30 individuals captured by a commercial fisherman near Chester, Illinois; species is native to the North Atlantic and was stocked in Lakes Superior/Michigan in the 1970s. Provenance and survival potential in the Mississippi River remain unknown; not considered part of the established Illinois fauna absent evidence of reproduction.
5. **Range extensions documented for nine species**: [[Lake Sturgeon]], [[Spotted Gar]], [[River Chub]], [[Pugnose Minnow]], [[Lake Chubsucker]], [[Trout-Perch]], [[Spotted Sunfish]], [[Bantam Sunfish]], and [[Dusky Darter]] — several representing first records for the Cache River system or southeastern Illinois generally.
6. **Historical museum collections (UMMZ, 1930s–40s)** proved critical in revealing that some species (e.g., trout-perch, dusky darter) had broader historical ranges in Illinois than documented in Smith's (1979) comprehensive account.
7. **Taxonomic correction**: Prior distribution maps for *Erimyzon sucetta* (Lake Chubsucker) in the Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes were found to conflate records with the co-generic *Erimyzon oblongus* (Creek Chubsucker), necessitating a re-evaluation of the species' true Illinois range.
8. **Conservation recommendations**: The paper explicitly calls for the [[Illinois Endangered Species Protection Board]] and its Technical Advisory Committee on Fishes to reassess the status of the taillight shiner (elevate to endangered), spotted sunfish (reconsider proposed threatened status given new records), and inland silverside (do not list, given lack of a viable native population).
9. **Broader significance**: The study underscores the dynamic nature of the Illinois fish fauna — some species show clear range contraction, others are expanding, and others are being introduced — reinforcing the need for continued ichthyofaunal surveys even in well-studied regions.
