# Bonytail Chub Collection and Transportation Protocol, Lake Mohave (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum transmits a field protocol for collecting wild [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) from [[Lake Mohave]] during the week of May 12–18, 1996. The purpose is to obtain wild fish as potential [[Brood Stock]] for the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Collection is largely carried out by non-Service partners, who are not legally bound to the protocol unless it is written into their endangered species permits. The protocol sets out net and handling rules, holding-tank chemical treatments, and a continuous communication plan among camps, the hatchery, and the fish health biologist.

## Background & Description

The memorandum is addressed to project leaders of the Parker and Arizona Fishery Resource Offices, the project leader of the [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]], and the Regional Geographic Manager (Region 2). It asks recipients to review the protocol and to discuss its procedures with partners so that collected fish survive capture and transport and remain available as future spawners.

The attached Field Protocol for Bonytail Chub Collection and Transportation covers:

- Coordination of transport from collection sites on Lake Mohave to the hatchery.
- Gear restrictions and monitoring schedules.
- Holding-tank preparation and chemical treatments.
- Handling and transport procedures.
- Communication among field crews, the hatchery, and support staff.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses the welfare of a fish that is handled as an endangered or sensitive species. Key stressors and risks identified in the text include:

- **Gear-induced mortality:** Gill nets are explicitly unacceptable for BTC brood stock. Trammel nets must have fine center panels (1–1.5 inches) to avoid gilling fish.
- **Air exposure and delay:** Fish must reach the hatchery soon after capture.
- **Handling injury and infection:** The caudal peduncle is described as especially sensitive to infection, so fish should not be grasped there. Adults retained for transport are not to be weighed, measured, or scanned.
- **Lake holding:** Fish should not be held in the lake in live-cars.

The protocol uses [[Salt Treatment]], [[Furacin]] (nitrofurazone), and [[MS-222]] (tricaine methanesulfonate) as water additives, with stress coat applied to abrasions. These are described as prophylactic and sedative/anesthetic measures to protect fish during holding and transport.

## Key Findings

**Collection methods**
- Gill nets are not acceptable tools for collecting BTC brood stock.
- Trammel nets should have fine center panels of 1–1.5 inches.
- Nets and traps should be checked preferably every 4 hours and at least every 6 hours. If checks cannot be met, nets should be pulled.

**Equipment and preparation**
- A holding (soaking) tank of at least 50 gallons must be prepared before nets are set. The source text reads "b0 gallons," which OCR may have garbled; 50 gallons is the most likely reading.
- Holding water is to be pre-treated with salt (19 g/gal), Furacin (19 mg/gal), and MS-222 (the source gives 95–96 mg/gal in two places, and the third instance is illegible).
- Stress coat is to be applied to abrasions.
- An oxygen bottle with airstone is standard equipment, and oxygen must be running before nets are set.

**Handling and transport**
- Fish should be cut from the net where possible to minimize handling.
- Fish are moved from the lake to holding tanks and transport tanks with soft-mesh dip nets, with hands kept wet.
- Fish are kept covered (in the dark) during transport.
- Adults are not weighed, measured, or scanned before transport to the hatchery.

**Prophylaxis**
- After removal from nets, fish are placed in holding tanks, and prophylactic treatment with salt, Furacin, and MS-222 is applied as described above.
- Premeasured packets of salt and MS-222 are to be supplied by Chuck Minckley and Ronald Major at their visits.

**Coordination and communication**
- [[Chuck Minckley]] coordinates transport from the collection sites to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery. He and [[Ronald Major]] are camped at [[Lake Mohave]], reachable by cell phone.
- Both are to visit each camp on Monday, May 13, 1996, to exchange phone numbers and establish communication procedures.
- Cellular phones are provided at each lakeside camp for 24-hour communication.
- [[Ronald Major]], a fish health biologist, is on site and available by cell phone or in person throughout the week.
- Jim Harrson and John Seals are available by phone during weekday office hours (7:00 A.M.–3:30 P.M.), with an evening contact listed for Jim Harrson.

**Compliance and follow-up**
- Non-Service partners are not bound by this protocol unless it is included as a condition of their endangered species permit. The author expects them to take the necessary precautions if guidance is provided.
- The Service requests comments and critiques on the protocol, and recommendations for future collection efforts, once the collection is complete.
