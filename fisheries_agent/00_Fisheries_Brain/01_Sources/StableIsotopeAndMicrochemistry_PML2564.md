# Stable Isotope and Microchemistry Analyses of Fin Rays to Determine Habitat Use and Movement Patterns of Razorback Sucker in Reach 3

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Bureau of Reclamation report (Portz, Hobbs and Ikemiyagi, August 2010), prepared for the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]], tested whether strontium isotope ratios (⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr) measured by laser ablation in pectoral fin rays could identify the hatchery origin of endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] in the lower [[Colorado River]]. The team first found too little isotopic variation along the reach to map habitat use, then shifted to hatchery-origin classification of 311 field-caught fish collected in 2008 and 2009. The approach was only partly validated: it worked for hatcheries on the river, but was unreliable for hatcheries whose fish showed mixed or misleading signatures. The largest group of fish (about 35–54% depending on the calculation) carried an unknown signature that did not match any recorded hatchery.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally endangered catostomid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Recovery context:** The [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]], a multi-agency effort, relies on hatchery-reared, [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]] (PIT tags) fish for repatriation. Knowing the natal origin of repatriated fish supports recovery and program evaluation.
- **Analytical rationale:** Strontium isotope ratios in fish hard parts reflect ambient water chemistry, which is controlled by bedrock age and mineralogy (via radiogenic decay of ⁸⁷Rb). Fin rays incorporate strontium from water, so they can serve as natural markers of natal habitat, as previously shown for otoliths and for salmonids and [[Delta Smelt]] in California.
- **Study area:** Reach 3 of the lower Colorado River, between Davis Dam and Lake Havasu, including sucker habitats such as Park Moabi, Needles and the Chemehuevi backwaters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Geology:** Bedrock age varies along the river. Neogene granitic bedrock lies upstream of Lake Mead; Paleozoic sedimentary rock occurs above the Grand Canyon; Neogene volcanics and Quaternary sediments occur between Lake Mead and Davis Dam; and Proterozoic granite with Quaternary deposits lies between Davis Dam and Lake Havasu.
- **Hatchery settings:** [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility]] draw on mainstem river water and sit on young bedrock adjacent to the river. [[Bubbling Ponds State Fish Hatchery]] (spring-fed aquifer and well water, Neogene volcanic rock), [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (groundwater, young rock far from the river) and the [[Hualapai Tribal Hatchery]] (older Paleozoic rock) are isolated from the mainstem.
- **Natural variation:** Strontium ratios in the river between Davis and Parker Dams range about 0.7104–0.7108, a baseline against which hatchery signatures were compared.
- **Early finding:** Preliminary sampling of 38 wild fish and 10 hatchery juveniles found little variation along the stretch, so habitat reconstruction was abandoned.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- Pectoral fin rays were clipped (about 1 cm from the base), and fish were measured (TL), weighed and scanned for PIT and wire-coded tags before release.
- Ages were determined by a contract lab. Thin sections (1 mm) were polished, and ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr ratios were measured by laser ablation MC-ICP-MS at the UC Davis Interdisciplinary Center for Plasma Mass Spectrometry (a 60 µm beam at 10 µm/s, with normalization to ⁸⁶Sr/⁸⁸Sr = 0.1194).
- Analytical precision was checked against a modern marine mollusk, giving 0.709190 ± 0.000050 (n = 28), consistent with modern seawater (0.70918).
- Classification used a 95% bootstrapped confidence-interval rule, and paired t-tests, one-way ANOVA and χ² tests compared results with PIT tag records.

**Hatchery signatures (validation)**
- Juvenile fish from three of four hatcheries differed significantly (F = 698.8, df = 2, 21, p < 0.001). [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (0.7107 ± 0.00014) and [[Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility]] (0.7103 ± 0.00013) were statistically similar; [[Bubbling Ponds State Fish Hatchery]] (0.7077 ± 0.00012) and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (0.7076 ± 0.00005) were also similar.
- PIT-tagged known-origin fish: Willow Beach (0.7105) matched juveniles (0.7106), but Bubbling Ponds fish (0.7077) differed strongly from PIT records (0.7105, p < 0.001).

**Field classification (2008–2009)**
- A total of 311 fish were sampled; 123 carried PIT tags. Of these, 110 were recorded as Willow Beach origin (62% of PIT fish), and 188 fish had no hatchery record (unknown).
- Only 56% of test fish with known Willow Beach origin were correctly classified; 33% were assigned to Achii Hanyo, 6% to Emerald Canyon Golf Course Ponds and 1% to unknown.
- Among PIT-tagged fish, about 54% had an unknown signature (0.7094–0.7099), with Willow Beach at 23% and Achii Hanyo at 19%.
- For all 311 fish, the unknown group made up about 35%, Willow Beach about 34%, Achii Hanyo about 23%, and Emerald Canyon about 6%; Dexter, Bubbling Ponds and Hualapai together were under 3%.

**Interpretation and conclusions**
- Strontium fin-ray signatures are useful for hatcheries on the mainstem, but the approach showed limited utility overall. Reworking or re-absorption of fin rays, water-source differences and pond grow-out may shift signatures away from natal values.
- The large unknown-origin group may come from undocumented sources north of Willow Beach or east of Lake Havasu, the [[Bill Williams River]], downstream passage through Davis Dam, or altered Bubbling Ponds or Dexter fish.
- Bubbling Ponds fish appear strongly underrepresented, despite many repatriations in the last 10 years, suggesting a review of hatchery practices.
- Willow Beach and Achii Hanyo hatcheries appear to supply most of the sampled population. Unpublished telemetry data indicate fish from different reach 3 locations congregate at the Needles spawning population.
- The authors recommend further study of hatchery sources upstream of Davis Dam, grow-out ponds in reaches 3 and 4, and spatial and temporal variation in river strontium with flow.
