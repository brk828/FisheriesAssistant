## Executive Summary

The **Mescalero National Fish Hatchery** (Mescalero, New Mexico), established in 1965 under the Appropriations Act of July 26, 1963, is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 2 facility whose primary mission is to supply [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] to the southwestern United States, with 94% of production directed to Indian lands in New Mexico and roughly 6% supporting military installations in Texas and Oklahoma. Beyond routine production, the station plays a conservation role in developing broodstock for the endangered [[Gila Trout]] and the special-concern [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]]. In FY-91, the station hosted approximately 2,000 visitors, including a visit from Secretary of the Interior Manuel Lujan, and undertook a significant broodstock relocation that revealed important husbandry findings related to nitrogen supersaturation.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data on the fish species held at the station. However, an important physiological observation was recorded during the transfer of Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout broodstock from indoor tankhouse conditions to the outdoor covered raceway (C-8):

- **Age-related susceptibility to environmental stress**: Younger fish transitioned to "outside" raceway water with minimal difficulty, while older fish experienced significantly greater physiological strain.
- **Nitrogen supersaturation effects**: Prolonged exposure to nitrogen-supersaturated water caused progressively adverse effects on body tissues, with severity correlating to duration of exposure — indicating a chronic, cumulative pathology (consistent with symptoms of [[Gas Bubble Disease]]) rather than an acute toxic response.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Mescalero NFH functions as an artificial, controlled cold-water salmonid production system rather than a natural ecosystem, but several ecologically relevant details are noted:

- **Habitat types utilized**: Indoor tankhouse rearing units and outdoor covered raceways (e.g., raceway C-8), reflecting a staged approach to acclimating broodstock from protected indoor environments to more naturalistic outdoor flow conditions.
- **Water quality stressor**: Nitrogen supersaturation in the raceway water supply represents an environmental hazard analogous to conditions found below dam spillways or high-head water deliveries, underscoring the importance of degassing infrastructure in hatchery and stocked-stream management.
- **Conservation linkage to wild populations**: The station's broodstock programs for [[Gila Trout]] and [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]] connect hatchery operations directly to the recovery of imperiled wild salmonid populations native to the Southwest, particularly within the [[Rio Grande Basin]] and Gila River drainage.
- **Distribution waters**: Fish were distributed to Federal (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Affairs/BSA-associated) waters, state waters, and private waters across New Mexico and Oklahoma.

## Key Findings

- **Mission focus**: 94% of trout production serves Indian lands in the Southwest; ~6% supplies military installations in Texas and Oklahoma.
- **Conservation role**: Active broodstock development for two at-risk taxa — the federally endangered [[Gila Trout]] and the special-concern [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]].
- **Operational funding trend (1989–1993)**: Operations budget rose from $241.4K (1989 actual) to an estimated $270K (1993); maintenance funding rose sharply from $11.4K (1989) to an estimated $75K (1992–1993), while FTE staffing increased modestly from 5 to 6.
- **Cost-recovery partnership**: The U.S. Army contributed cost-recoverable funding ($14.5K–$22.0K annually, FY89–FY91), reflecting the station's role in supplying fish to military installations.
- **Fish distribution totals**:
  - FY89: 426,360 fish distributed (423,360 to Federal/BSA waters; 3,000 to private waters)
  - FY90: 516,768 fish distributed (511,768 to Federal/BSA waters; 5,000 to private waters)
  - FY91: 493,196 fish distributed (488,196 to Federal/BSA waters; 5,000 to private waters)
  - Note: these totals exclude eggs and fry.
- **Species distributed**: Rainbow Trout and Cutthroat Trout, distributed to New Mexico and Oklahoma.
- **Husbandry insight**: Transition of broodstock from tankhouse to covered raceway freed valuable production space but exposed a vulnerability — nitrogen supersaturation caused cumulative tissue damage, disproportionately affecting older fish compared to younger cohorts, highlighting an age-dependent resilience to environmental water-quality stress in [[Salmonidae]].
- **Public engagement**: ~2,000 visitors in FY-91, including a high-profile visit by Secretary of the Interior Manuel Lujan (May 2, 1991), indicating the station's community and public outreach significance.
