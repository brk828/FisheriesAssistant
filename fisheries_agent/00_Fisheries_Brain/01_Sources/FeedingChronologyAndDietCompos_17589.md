# Feeding Chronology and Diet Composition of Two Darters (Percidae) in the Iroquois River System, Illinois

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This study examined the 24-hour feeding chronology and diet composition of two darters, the [[Least Darter]] (*Etheostoma microperca*) and the [[Banded Darter]] (*E. zonale*), in the [[Iroquois River]] system, Illinois, during 9-10 September 1977. Both species were strictly diurnal feeders, with feeding activity rising after sunrise, peaking around midday to sunset, and declining after sunset. The least darter fed mainly on copepods in the morning and early afternoon, shifting toward chironomids later in the day. The banded darter fed predominantly on chironomids at all hours. The authors interpret these diet differences as a general pattern in which small darters feed chiefly on microcrustaceans and large darters on immature insects.

## Background & Description

Earlier darter feeding studies rarely covered a continuous 24-hour period. Prior work included [[Mathur 1973]] on the [[Blackbanded Darter]] (*Percina nigrofasciata*) in Alabama and [[Schenck and Whiteside 1977]] on the [[Fountain Darter]] (*Etheostoma fonticola*) in Texas. The present study addressed this gap using two darter species from Illinois.

- **Least darter (*E. microperca*):** One of the smallest darters, reaching a maximum of 37 mm SL. Preserved specimens in this study ranged from 17.5-32.7 mm SL and 0.08-0.53 g.
- **Banded darter (*E. zonale*):** A larger species. Preserved specimens ranged from 25.5-49.0 mm SL and 0.28-1.87 g.

Methods followed the feeding chronology approach of [[Darnell and Meierotto 1962]].

**Sampling and laboratory methods:**
- *E. microperca:* 10 specimens collected every 2 hours from 1700 hr on 9 September to 1300 hr on 10 September 1977, from a small tributary of the Iroquois River (the study area of [[Burr and Page 1979]]).
- *E. zonale:* 10 specimens collected every 2 hours from 1800 hr on 9 September to 1400 hr on 10 September 1977, from a large gravel and rubble riffle about 6 km east of Watseka.
- Fish were preserved in 10% formalin, sexed, measured (SL to nearest mm), blotted, and weighed (to 0.01 g). Stomach contents were identified and counted, with partially digested organisms counted as whole. Food weights were estimated from mean weights of whole organisms and summed per stomach.
- Feeding was expressed as number of organisms per stomach and as total stomach-content weight per gram of fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both darters are visual, diurnal predators. The authors note that darters generally appear to have sharp vision and are probably diurnal feeders. Diet shifts were likely linked to the diel activity patterns of their prey, a pattern also reported for other fishes (e.g., [[Keast and Welsh 1968]]).

Comparative data from other percids:
- [[Mathur 1973]] reported a diurnal feeding pattern in *Percina nigrofasciata*, with peaks near 0800 hr and 1700 hr.
- [[Schenck and Whiteside 1977]] concluded that *E. fonticola* fed mainly in daylight, based on empty-stomach frequency among 127 individuals.
- [[Thomas 1970]] suggested daylight feeding in three *Percina* species, though with limited data.
- [[Adamson and Wissing 1977]] considered *E. flabellare*, *E. caeruleum*, and *E. zonale* diurnal, but the authors of this study judged these data inconclusive (18 specimens per species).
- [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*) and [[Eurasian Perch]] (*P. fluviatilis*) are diurnal feeders, while [[Walleye]] and [[Sauger]] (*Stizostedion*) are primarily nocturnal (per [[Thorpe 1977]] and [[Swenson 1977]]).

## Key Findings

**Feeding chronology**
- Both species were diurnal feeders. Feeding activity increased greatly after sunrise and dropped after sunset.
- *E. microperca:* number of organisms per stomach peaked at 1300 hr; stomach-content weight relative to fish weight peaked at 1900 hr (about sunset).
- *E. zonale:* number of organisms per stomach peaked at 1400 hr; stomach-content weight peaked at 1800 hr (sunset).

**Diet composition**
- *E. microperca* fed mainly on copepods (38-76% of food items) in the morning and early afternoon. Copepods declined in late afternoon, evening, and night, with chironomids increasing to offset this. Amphipods appeared only in fish collected between 1500 and 2100 hr.
- *E. zonale* fed heavily on chironomids (59-96% of food items) at all hours. The lowest chironomid proportion occurred at 0200 hr, when ephemeropterans and trichopterans were more prominent.

**Interspecific comparison**
- The contrast between the two species suggests a general darter feeding pattern: small darters (small species or juveniles of larger ones) feed mainly on microcrustaceans, while large darters feed mainly on immature insects. A few species apparently feed mainly on snails.

**Reference data**
- [[Adamson and Wissing 1977]] (N = 39 *E. zonale*) reported chironomids at 63% and simuliids at 26% by weight.

**Conclusion**
- Diel changes in diet composition likely reflect the diel activity patterns of the food organisms themselves.
