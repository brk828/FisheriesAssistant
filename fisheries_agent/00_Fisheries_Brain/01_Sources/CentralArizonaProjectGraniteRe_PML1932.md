# Granite Reef Aqueduct Fishery Investigation: 1986 Annual Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #cyprinidae #canal-fishery

## Executive Summary

This first-year progress report from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] documents a fishery investigation of the [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], a concrete-lined canal carrying up to 3,000 cfs of [[Colorado River]] water from [[Lake Havasu]] toward central Arizona. The study was led by Reclamation with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]], and was prompted by a commitment to monitor fish losses from Lake Havasu and to assess the importation of Colorado River fish into central Arizona through the canal. Sampling in 1986 collected over 2,000 fish representing ten species from Reaches 1 and 3. Threadfin Shad dominated numerically in both reaches, while the largemouth and striped bass populations in Reach 1 appeared established. The report is preliminary and recommends continued sampling.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] was authorized in 1968. The Granite Reef Aqueduct portion was nearing full operational status at the time of the report. The [[Environmental Impact Statement]] for the [[Lake Havasu Pumping Plant]] committed Reclamation to monitor fish losses from Lake Havasu, and this was later broadened to cover canal fish transport. Pre-operational work on Lake Havasu (1983–1985) included limnological studies by Reclamation and fishery work by the Fish and Wildlife Service, whose report status was unknown.

The canal is divided into reaches:

- **Reach 1:** Water is pumped from Lake Havasu through about 7 miles of tunnel under the Buckskin Mountains. The first 5,550 ft has a 24-ft bottom width and a maximum depth of 18 ft. The following 17-mile section widens to an 80-ft bottom and stores roughly 41,000 acre-feet. It is the lowest-velocity section and was chosen as the test site for sampling methods.
- **Reach 2:** Begins at the [[Bouse Hills Pumping Plant]] outfall and runs about 35 miles to the forebay of the [[Little Harquahala Pumping Plant]].
- **Reach 3:** The Little Harquahala section, lifted onward down the system.

All remaining reaches have a 24-ft bottom width, roughly 18-ft depth, and 1½:1 side slopes, with maximum water velocity exceeding 3 ft/s at full operation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Large canals are difficult to sample because of high velocities, steep banks, depth, and poor access. A custom [[Canal Elevator]] built from tubular steel and operated with an automobile winch allowed boats and equipment to be lowered into and raised from the concrete channel, and was described as essential to sampling.

Sampling used [[Electrofishing]], [[Gill Netting]], [[Fyke Net]]s, and [[Seining]], often combined (gill nets as block nets during electrofishing). Sampling was semiannual, focused on spring and fall. Reach 1 was sampled in June and October 1986; Reach 3 in October 1986. Fish were measured (total length to the nearest millimeter; weights in grams, or converted from pounds), marked by fin clipping, and released when possible. In October, Reach 3 was dewatered, killing all fish in the downstream section that drained into the Little Harquahala forebay.

Water quality measured in the Little Harquahala forebay on October 23, 1986 showed temperature falling from 19.16 °C at the surface to 18.75 °C at the bottom, dissolved oxygen falling from 1.27 mg/L to 0.57 mg/L, and conductivity near 0.88 µmhos/cm. The report notes this was the first and only time dissolved oxygen appeared to be a limiting factor, and that fish were observed surfacing with Threadfin Shad dying first.

## Key Findings

**Sampling results**

- Over 2,000 fish of ten species were collected from Reaches 1 and 3 across four field efforts.
- Reach 1 (June 1986): 291 fish were electrofished in 1,676 seconds (5.76 seconds per fish); no fish were caught in the fyke net.
- Reach 1 (October 1986, partial dewatering): 597 fish were electrofished in 20,960 seconds (35.11 seconds per fish), and 1,217 fish were collected from the Little Harquahala forebay in the Reach 3 effort.
- Threadfin Shad dominated biomass and numbers in both reaches. Largemouth and striped bass made up about 75% of the non-shad catch in Reach 1 but under 7% in Reach 3.
- No striped bass were found in Reach 3 despite more than 1,200 fish handled, suggesting very low numbers if present.
- Sunfishes (Bluegill, Green Sunfish, Redear Sunfish) made up 88% of the Reach 3 sample but under 10% of Reach 1. Green and Redear Sunfish were rare or absent in Reach 1 despite having to pass through it.
- Only three Channel Catfish were collected across the entire effort, a low count compared with Coachella Canal studies in southern California, where channel catfish made up 97% of 3,100 fish.
- Three Razorback Sucker (about 500 mm, in excellent health) were collected, one in Reach 1 and two in Reach 3 (reported as one in each reach). Specimens were sent to Arizona State University for age determination.

**Population estimates (Reach 1, October 1986)**

- Petersen mark-recapture estimates were made for largemouth bass, bluegill, striped bass, and carp, separately for the upper and lower sections.
- Estimated biomass suggested fish biomass may already exceed 8 g/m², within the range reported for similar southern California canals; the authors considered this conservative.

**Condition and growth**

- Condition (K) factors were generally high. Largemouth bass in Reach 1 (K = 1.43 in June) and bluegill (K = 2.24) compared favorably with values from [[Lake Havasu]] and [[Alamo Lake]].
- Length-frequency analysis suggests most fish sampled were in the third year class or younger, consistent with the canal having held water for about three years and fish entering mainly at early life stages.

**Entrainment and predation**

- At least ten fish species have entered the system; the authors infer susceptibility to entrainment and adaptability to canal habitat.
- Large numbers of sunfish were observed inside the pump intakes during SCUBA inspections, suggesting that adults may be more susceptible to entrainment than mid-channel species.
- Predation by large Largemouth and Striped Bass may be significant for forage species such as Threadfin Shad.

**Recommendations**

- Continue sampling for at least two more years, with the highest effort during water outages.
- Focus fall and spring sampling on Reaches 1 and 3, and sample other reaches during fall 1987.
- Examine fish transport, and expand egg and larval investigations at Lake Havasu and Bouse Hills pumping plants (started spring 1987).
- Test experimental netting at the outlets of the Lake Havasu and Bouse Hills pumping plants to determine the degree of adult fish passage.
