# Green River Fishery Survey, Utah (1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, Division of Sport Fisheries, Albuquerque) fishery management report describes a six-day float survey of the lower [[Green River]] in April 1962. The survey covered roughly 173 river miles from Jensen to Green River, Utah. Its purpose was to document the fish fauna of this relatively inaccessible reach in connection with the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] and the [[Flaming Gorge]] fish eradication project. Sampling was hampered by high turbidity and debris. The most productive methods were hook and line and set lines on the main river, and [[Rotenone]] on smaller tributaries. The document also includes a May 18, 1962 transmittal memo and a photo caption of a [[Humpback Sucker]].

## Background & Description

- **Survey dates:** April 13 to April 18, 1962 (6-day float period).
- **Reach:** Lower Green River from Jensen, Utah, to Green River, Utah, about 173 river miles.
- **Purpose:** Obtain information on fish fauna in a relatively inaccessible stretch, linked to the Colorado River program and the Flaming Gorge fish eradication project.
- **Survey party:**
  - Reed and Pike, boat guide operators, with two rubber boats and motors (hired).
  - Vern Bressler, hatchery biologist, with float-boat experience on the Snake River in Wyoming.
  - Tom Chapoose, [[Ute Tribe]] Game Warden, representing the tribe.
- **Reservation context:** The reach passes the [[Uintah-Ouray Indian Reservation]], which lies on the left side of the river for about 62 river miles.
- **Specimen handling:** Field identifications were quick and may be inaccurate. Specimens were preserved in 10 percent formalin for confirmation by Dr. Donald R. Franklin, Unit Leader, Cooperative Fishery Unit, [[Utah State University]], Logan, Utah.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water conditions**

- The Green River was quite turbid, with a [[Secchi Disk]] reading near 3 inches, and debris was heavy.
- Most major tributaries in the section were also turbid and high.
- Fish sampling was difficult and at times impossible.

**Sampling methods and results**

| Method | Outcome reported |
|---|---|
| Hook and line, set lines | Only practical and successful methods on the main river |
| [[Gill Net]] sets (main river and tributary mouths) | Frequently drastic results: one net buried in 3+ feet of silt at the [[Duchesne River]] mouth; another near [[Tabyago Creek]] filled with moss and debris; others poor |
| [[Rotenone]] with blocking nets | Best results on smaller tributaries, applied by drip method |

**Locations checked (about 24 mouths and lower reaches)**

- Several sites were **dry** (Jack Creek, Cedar Ridge Canyon, Flat Creek Canyon, Rattlesnake Creek, Sand Wash).
- Rotenone gave **good** results at Rock Creek, Florence Creek and Range Creek.
- Hook and line gave **good** results at the Green River dam area and **fair** results on the main river near the Main River site.
- Most other tributaries, including [[Ashley Creek]], [[White River]], Willow Creek, Chandler Creek, Coal Creek and the [[Price River]] (very high, very turbid), were either not attempted or gave poor results.

## Key Findings

**Species recorded (quick field classification, 9 taxa)**

1. Flannelmouth Sucker (*Catostomus latipinnis*): preserved specimens.
2. Northern Bluehead Sucker (*Pantosteus delphinus*): preserved specimens.
3. Mountain Sucker: preserved specimens.
4. Humpback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*): preserved specimens.
5. Channel Catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*): preserved specimens.
6. Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*): preserved specimens.
7. Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): one specimen type escaped hook and line; others not preserved.
8. Green River Speckled Dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*): preserved specimens.
9. Blue Catfish (*Ictalurus furcatus*), identification marked uncertain (?).

Most species were preserved, with some entries marked as not preserved. Final identifications were pending confirmation by Dr. Franklin.

**Operational conclusions**

- Gill nets were largely unsuccessful in this turbid, debris-laden river.
- Rotenone was effective on clear-water tributary mouths but produced poor results in some turbid streams.
- Hook and line was the main viable method on the mainstem.

**Administrative items**

- The document includes a May 18, 1962 transmittal memo from the Division of Sport Fisheries, Albuquerque.
- Copies went to the Washington office (2), Byrn (CRSP), the Ute Fish and Game department at Fort Duchesne, Utah, and a regional reviewer.
- A photo caption notes a Humpback Sucker "entering the canyon country about 35 river miles below the town of Ouray."
