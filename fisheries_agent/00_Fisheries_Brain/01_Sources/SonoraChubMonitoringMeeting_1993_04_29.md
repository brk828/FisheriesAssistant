## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) internal memorandum summarizing an interagency meeting on the recovery and monitoring of the [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), a federally listed cyprinid endemic to the Sycamore Canyon drainage in Arizona. Attendees included representatives from Arizona State University, the U.S. Forest Service (Nogales Ranger District), the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), and the Service. Discussion centered on implementation of the species' recovery plan, threats from military training exercises and grazing-related habitat degradation, and the design of a long-term population and habitat monitoring protocol. No morphological or taxonomic data are presented; the note is management- and monitoring-focused rather than descriptive biology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** [[Sonora Chub]] populations occupy pools within [[Sycamore Creek]] (Sycamore Canyon, within the Coronado National Forest / Bear Valley Allotment), a desert stream system subject to strong seasonal drying.
- **Refuge habitat:** [[Hank and Yank Tank]], an artificial stock tank, functions as a secondary refuge population but is characterized by **low genetic diversity** and vulnerability to unauthorized transplant introductions (an entomologist reportedly moved fish into the tank during a creek drying event). Recent flood damage reportedly compromised part of the tank's containing slope.
- **Habitat dynamics:** Concerns were raised about pool variability from year to year, overall system hydrological dynamics, and the effects of **sedimentation from livestock grazing** within the Bear Valley Allotment on available pool habitat.
- **Anthropogenic stressors:**
  - Military training/maneuvers (Department of Defense, Joint Task Force Six Mission, March 1993) involving daily patrols and exercises within Sycamore Canyon under a temporary Forest Service special use permit; concerns over compliance with the military's Biological Evaluation (BE).
  - Off-road vehicle use permitted by the Forest Service up to 300 feet off established roads, posing potential habitat disturbance risk.
  - Grazing-related sedimentation degrading pool habitat quality.
- **Ecosystem health indicator work:** Richard Mierhoff (Arizona DEQ) has been evaluating aquatic ecosystem health via macroinvertebrate community sampling, with Sycamore Canyon as one of the assessed sites — a potential complementary dataset for the [[Sonora Chub]] recovery program.
- **Historical population extremity:** J. Johnson reportedly considered the species "extinct" in 1969 after failing to detect any chub during a single day of sampling — illustrating the species' patchy detectability and the risk of false-negative surveys in low-density years.

## Key Findings
- A **~30-year historical field dataset** on Sonora chub populations exists (compiled by W.L. Minckley), including annual summary reports submitted to AGFD; however, availability of these reports at AGFD was uncertain, and obtaining this baseline dataset was identified as a priority.
- No population estimate protocol currently exists; there was debate over whether formal **population estimates** are feasible/useful versus simpler **presence/absence** or **young-of-year presence** monitoring as a proxy for reproductive success and population persistence.
- A proposed **linear habitat survey** of Sycamore Creek (feasible with 2 people and a hip chain over a few days) was discussed as a near-term action, alongside prior habitat evaluation mapping completed by Jeanette Carpenter (via [[Carpenter and Maughn Habitat Map]]).
- Open monitoring design questions included: optimal survey timing (proposed: June, during typical dry conditions), survey frequency (proposed: annual for first 5 years), and which habitat parameters to measure (e.g., substrate composition, cover availability).
- **Action items assigned:**
  - Debra Bills: draft letter to Forest Supervisor requesting Service participation in the updated Bear Valley Allotment Management Plan (due 1994 revision); contact Carpenter/Maughn for habitat map; contact Mierhoff for macroinvertebrate/ecosystem health data from Sycamore Canyon.
  - Kirk Young (AGFD): draft the fish section of the monitoring plan.
  - Jerry Stefferud (Forest Service): draft the habitat section of the monitoring plan by May 1.
  - Final monitoring plan draft to be sent to W.L. Minckley for review.
- The tension between treating [[Hank and Yank Tank]] as a genetic/population **refuge** versus a **liability** (due to low diversity and uncontrolled transplant risk) was flagged as an unresolved issue for the next Sonora Chub Recovery Team meeting.
