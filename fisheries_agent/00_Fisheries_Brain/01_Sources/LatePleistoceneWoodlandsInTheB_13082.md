# Late Pleistocene Woodlands in the Bolson de Mapimi: A Refugium for the Chihuahuan Desert Biota?

#paleobotany #cupressaceae #pinaceae

## Executive Summary

This short paper by [[Thomas R. Van Devender]] and [[Tony L. Burgess]] (*Quaternary Research*, 1985) uses plant macrofossils from [[Packrat Midden]] deposits to reconstruct vegetation in the southern [[Chihuahuan Desert]] during the [[Late Wisconsin]]. Two [[Radiocarbon Dating|radiocarbon-dated]] middens, at 12,280 ± 345 and 12,700 ± 165 yr B.P., record junipers and [[Pinus remota]] (papershell pinyon) woodland extending into desert lowlands of Durango and Coahuila at about 26° N. The authors conclude that the [[Bolson de Mapimi]] was not a geographic refugium unaffected by glacial climates. Instead, woodlands expanded into the lowlands under an equable climate, and many desert plants and animals persisted in situ as members of that woodland.

## Background & Description

Packrat (*Neotoma*) middens are hard, dry deposits in rock shelters that preserve identifiable plant and animal remains and can be radiocarbon dated. Earlier midden work in the region documented woodland and forest communities before about 11,000 yr B.P., with modern desertscrub forming in the Holocene. In the northern Chihuahuan Desert, including the [[Sacramento Mountains]], [[Hueco Mountains]] and [[Big Bend]] areas, modern desertscrub is estimated to have formed less than 4,500 years ago.

The study extends this record south to 26° N with two sites:

- **[[Sierra de la Misericordia]], Durango** (25°56' N; 1,250 m): a limestone sierra on the southwestern edge of the Bolson de Mapimi. The midden at 12,280 ± 345 yr B.P. is an average of samples A-1895 and A-1896.
- **[[Puerto de Ventanillas]], Coahuila** (26°02' N; 1,370 m): a limestone canyon on the north side of Laguna Mayran playa, about 43 km NE of San Pedro de las Colonias. The midden at 12,700 ± 165 yr B.P. (WK-165, on *Juniperus*) is the second dated sample.

The Bolson de Mapimi is a large physiographic area of internally drained playa basins. Its vegetation near the sites is creosote bush–lechuguilla desertscrub with many endemic succulents.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Modern vegetation gradients.** Chihuahuan desertscrub reaches 600 m along the Rio Grande in Trans-Pecos Texas and 1,200 m in the Bolson de Mapimi. It grades upward into desert-grassland (1,280–1,830 m), downward into Tamaulipan thornscrub (460–760 m), and upward into pine–oak woodland or pine forest (above 1,830–2,200 m). Succulents increase relative to grasses south of 30° N, reflecting a more tropical arid climate.

**Inferred Late Wisconsin climate.** The authors propose an equable regime with:
- Cooler summers and reduced summer monsoons, with some spring precipitation.
- Winter temperatures no colder than today, and less frequent Arctic incursions ("nortes").
- More frequent Pacific winter storms, producing a biseasonal precipitation regime.
- Playas and marshes holding water, suggested by the American coot humerus and cattail (*Typha domingensis*) pollen.

**Biogeographic context.** The Bolson de Mapimi sits at the dry end of a moisture gradient from [[Cuatro Cienegas]] (185 km NE). Earlier pollen work at Cuatro Cienegas, by [[Meyer (1973)]], showed little woodland change over 20,000 years. The authors argue this record is too conservative, while [[Morafka (1977)]] had proposed the Bolson as a glacial refugium with minimal woodland encroachment.

## Key Findings

**1. Woodland expansion into the lowlands.** Both middens record woodland taxa. The Sierra de la Misericordia midden is dominated by juniper and *Pinus remota*, and the Puerto de Ventanillas midden reflects a more xeric juniper woodland.

- Cupressaceae (probably *Juniperus*) pollen: 74.9% (SM) and 96.1% (PV).
- *Pinus* pollen: 7.5% (SM) and 0.3% (PV).

**2. Plant richness.** The midden assemblages yielded 36 kinds of plants in total: 14 at SM and 32 at PV. Modern comparison collections yielded 12 species at SM and 28 at PV (modern N = 34). Of the long-lived perennials, 45.5–50.0% were succulents. Of these, 40.0–66.7% lived within 50 m of the site and 70–100% within 1 km. Most identified succulents are endemic to the Chihuahuan Desert in a broad sense.

**3. Taxonomic composition.** Key identifications include:
- *Juniperus* cf. *saltillensis* (Saltillo juniper), restricted to the bordering woodlands of the Chihuahuan Desert.
- *Pinus remota*, also restricted to these bordering woodlands.
- *Agave* remains tentatively assigned to *A. parrasana*, possibly *A. parryi* or *A. havardiana*.
- *Buchloe dactyloides* (buffalo grass). Its presence at PV indicates greater summer/fall precipitation than today.
- *Atriplex canescens* (fourwing saltbush). It was the only tree or shrub recovered that still grows within the study area.

**4. Long-distance relatives.** The nearest populations of the other woody taxa lie 24–580 km away in nearly all directions, including subtropical shrubs to the northeast and southeast. None of the woody plants are endemic to the Chihuahuan Desert.

**5. Fauna.** The Puerto de Ventanillas midden yielded a humerus of *Fulica americana* (American coot). Reptile remains included *Crotalus lepidus*, *C.* cf. *scutulatus*, an unidentified *Crotalus* sp., and *Sceloporus merriami* (Merriam's spiny lizard). The paper's animal records are limited to these few specimens and are not analyzed further.

**6. Refugium conclusion.** The authors conclude the Bolson de Mapimi "was clearly not a refugium" in the sense of an area where desert communities stayed unchanged. Rather, many desert plants and animals lived continuously as members of equable woodland during glacial ages and as desertscrub during interglacials. The authors also note that the Bolson midden at 13,000–12,000 yr B.P. probably represents full-glacial conditions. Midden chronologies from the northern Chihuahuan Desert show little change in pinyon–juniper–oak woodland from 22,000 to 11,000 yr B.P.

**7. Challenge to earlier refugium models.** The results do not support the proposal by [[Wells and Hunziker (1976)]] that frequent winter freezes displaced xerophytes to Ice Age refugia at 18–21° N in Puebla, Hidalgo and Querétaro. The authors cite modern equable woodlands near San Roberto, Nuevo León (24°42' N), as analogues for the Late Wisconsin communities.

**8. Evolutionary implication.** The authors link equable climates, low extinction rates and rapid glacial–interglacial fluctuations to species accumulation at lower latitudes. They estimate 15–20 glacial–interglacial cycles over the last two million years of the Pleistocene. Extinction risk for isolated northern populations is greatest during climatic transitions and decreases southward, so evolutionary rates may be higher at lower latitudes.
