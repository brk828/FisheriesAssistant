# Electrofishing Results on Lake Mohave for Razorback Suckers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This field report documents a cooperative broodstock collection effort by university, state, and federal biologists targeting spawning aggregations of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in Lake Mohave. Conducted over the week of January 12–16, the operation combined boat-based electrofishing and trammel netting around the Cottonwood East Area, Arizona Bay, Levi Cove, and Airport Cove. A total of 94 razorback suckers were captured via electrofishing/dip-netting (36 females, 58 males transported live, plus additional fish noted in surveys), and 84 additional razorback suckers were recorded via trammel net data supplied by Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University). Fish were transported live in lake water to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico to establish a captive broodstock population, with all individuals reportedly surviving transport in good condition.

## Background & Description
- **Target species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]].
- Sex ratio of transported broodstock: 36 females : 58 males (skewed male-heavy; excess males were deliberately released in the field to balance the captured sample).
- **Incidental catch — notable specimen:** A smaller male [[Cutthroat Trout]] weighed 954 grams and measured 442 mm in length; two other cutthroat trout captured were larger. This capture represented the first documented record of this species in the survey area/program.
- Other species observed in the electrical field (not targeted for capture): [[Green Sunfish]], [[Carp]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Rainbow Trout]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Lake Mohave, a reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]], with key sampling zones in Arizona Bay, Levi Cove, Airport Cove, and the Cottonwood East Area shoreline.
- **Spawning behavior:** Preliminary visual surveys on January 12 identified multiple spawning aggregations of razorback suckers along coves and adjoining shoreline, ranging from solitary individuals to a group of approximately 200 fish — indicative of concentrated winter/early-spring spawning activity in shallow nearshore habitat.
- **Water temperature during spawning/capture:** 9.5°C to 11.0°C.
- **Diel activity pattern:** Razorback suckers were substantially more accessible to capture at night, when fish moved closer to shore, allowing boats to approach without spooking them. Most fish were captured after dark using boat-mounted lights.
- **Co-occurring fish community:** The trammel net catch (550–670 m of net set around the area) revealed a mixed assemblage including introduced/non-native species — [[Carp]] (77), [[Largemouth Bass]] (61), [[Rainbow Trout]] (14), [[Cutthroat Trout]] (3), [[Bluegill]] (2) — alongside the target [[Razorback Sucker]] (84), for a total catch of 253 fish. This highlights the ecological context of razorback sucker decline amid non-native competitors/predators in Lake Mohave.
- Nets were also set with intent to capture [[Bonytail Chub]], another endangered Colorado River native, though none were reported in the catch data.

## Key Findings
- **Broodstock collection success:** 94 adult razorback suckers (36 F, 58 M) were successfully collected and transported live to Dexter National Fish Hatchery for captive broodstock development, arriving in good condition despite being held/transported in lake-sourced water.
- **Electrofishing equipment specifications:** Boat-mounted setup using a Coffelt VVP-10 unit powered by a Kohler 10 hp, 5,000-watt generator; two bow-mounted anodes (~3.3 m extension) and two midship cathodes; mounted on a 16-ft flat-bottom Mon-Ark boat with 40 hp Johnson outboard and remote steering/throttle control for forward-facing operation.
- **Effective electrofishing settings:**
  - Pulse width: 40–60%
  - Pulse rate: 80–120 pulses/second
  - DC pulsed voltage: 240–250 volts
  - Amperage: 9–11 A
- **Shock threshold and fish welfare concern:** Razorback suckers were highly susceptible to electroshock, but voltages below 230V failed to stun them effectively. A critical safety/mortality concern was identified: fish remaining in the electrical field longer than 15 seconds without the operator pulsing the foot switch risked severe physiological damage. The report recommends a controlled comparison of mortality rates between electroshocked and net-captured fish (via marking and holding) to evaluate this risk.
- **Nocturnal capture efficiency:** Night surveys using boat-mounted lights were markedly more productive than daytime electrofishing, attributed to fish moving into shallower, nearshore areas after dark and reduced spook response, enabling closer boat approach.
- **Sampling effort timeline:** Multiple work sessions were conducted on the 13th (1930–1130 hrs, 1345–1559 hrs, 2130–2345 hrs) and 14th (1930–2300 hrs), with the majority of razorback suckers captured during evening/night sessions.
- **Species management practice:** Field team practiced selective release of surplus males during electrofishing to avoid over-representation of males in the broodstock sample, indicating active sex-ratio management in the field.
- **First species record:** Capture of [[Cutthroat Trout]] in the trammel nets marked the first recorded occurrence of this species during this monitoring effort in Lake Mohave.
