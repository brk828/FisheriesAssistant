# What Is It? Pectinatella magnifica in Alabama Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bryozoa #freshwater-ecology #invertebrate

## Executive Summary

This article describes [[Pectinatella magnifica]], a colonial freshwater bryozoan commonly found in Alabama ponds and reservoir backwaters. Often mistaken for fish eggs or freshwater sponges, these gelatinous masses consist of thousands of tiny filter-feeding animals that secrete clear jelly and attach to submerged wood. The author addresses public concerns about their impact on fisheries, concluding that bryozoans are likely beneficial rather than harmful to pond ecosystems due to their support of midge larvae populations that serve as fish food.

## Background & Description

[[Pectinatella magnifica]] forms brown-coated masses of stiff jelly attached to sticks, stumps, and twigs in ponds and backwaters. Individual bryozoans are located on the outside of the jelly mass and give it brown coloration. The jelly itself is crystal clear when secreted but becomes colonized by algae (turning green) and bacteria (turning red).

Each tiny white spot on a colony represents a hundred or more individual animals. Individual bryozoans possess a mouth with brownish-red lips surrounded by thread-like tentacles arranged in two rows on a horseshoe-shaped base. The tentacles bear cilia that create water currents to sweep food particles toward the mouth. When irritated, individuals withdraw into a leathery protective sheath.

Colonies can grow to a yard or more across by late summer. As colonies enlarge, they frequently trap gas bubbles and float to the surface. Portions exposed to air and direct sunlight are killed, with the jelly drying to form tough dome-shaped white shells protruding above water.

Reproduction occurs through two mechanisms: (1) budding within colonies for growth, and (2) production of statoblasts for new colony formation. Statoblasts are flat reproductive packages with double-hooked spines along their edges that aid in dispersal, similar to cockleburs. These structures are produced in prodigious numbers, sometimes forming windrows along pond margins in fall. Statoblasts can overwinter and germinate the following spring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Pectinatella magnifica]] inhabits freshwater ponds and reservoir backwaters in [[Lee County]], Alabama, and the broader [[T.V.A.]] area. The organisms attach to submerged woody substrates including sticks, stumps, and twigs.

Bryozoans are filter feeders consuming microscopic plants and other small food particles drifting in the water. They selectively accept or reject particles swept toward their mouths by ciliary currents.

The colonies support substantial populations of midge larvae ([[Chironomidae]]). One mass the size of a quart jar contained over three hundred midge larvae—production exceeding that of an equal amount of pond bottom. These red "worms" (approximately one-fourth inch long) live in silken cases on or just within the jelly surface. The larvae become available as fish food when they leave cases to feed and when they develop into pupae and emerge as adult insects.

Small fish are frequently observed among the jelly masses, suggesting the bryozoans provide foraging habitat.

## Key Findings

- [[Pectinatella magnifica]] colonies are commonly mistaken for fish eggs (particularly crappie eggs) or freshwater sponges by fishermen and the public
- The organisms are harmless to humans; they produce slime when touched but neither sting nor bite and can be handled safely
- Colonies support high densities of midge larvae (over 300 larvae per quart-sized mass), exceeding production from equivalent pond bottom area
- Midge larvae associated with bryozoan colonies serve as food for bream and other small fish
- Small fish frequently forage among the jelly masses
- Despite becoming unsightly in late summer when colonies reach a yard or more in diameter, there is no evidence that bryozoans are harmful to pond ecosystems
- The author concludes bryozoans are likely beneficial to fisheries rather than detrimental, contrary to public concerns
