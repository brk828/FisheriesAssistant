## Executive Summary

This document is a critical scientific commentary by [[James E. Deacon]] on the Virgin River Habitat Conservation and Management Plan, addressed to the [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]]. Rather than presenting new morphological or taxonomic data, the letter is an expert critique of the methodology used to justify proposed flow reductions and water quality changes in the [[Virgin River]], focusing on three federally-listed native fishes: [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin Chub]], and [[Virgin Spinedace]]. Deacon's central argument is that a physical habitat model (depth/velocity, developed by [[Thom Hardy and Associates]]) has been misapplied as a proxy for actual habitat suitability and fish abundance, and he calls for empirical, data-driven adaptive management instead of reliance on unvalidated predictive modeling.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide morphological descriptions of the species discussed. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **River segment focus**: The critical reach discussed spans from [[Pah Tempe Spring]] through [[Quail Creek]] to the [[Washington Fields Diversion]], with flow measurement recommended near [[Hurricane Bridge]].
- **Flow regime**: The Plan proposed reducing winter flows to ~50 CFS based on the Hardy depth/velocity model. Deacon argues this conflates "preferred" physical conditions with actual habitat availability and fish abundance — a critical distinction, since **preference is not abundance**.
- **Water chemistry (TDS) as a niche driver**: [[Pah Tempe Spring]] is a major source of total dissolved solids (TDS) into the Virgin River. Native fishes in this reach have evolved under Pah Tempe-influenced water chemistry conditions. Removing this spring input would lower TDS — beneficial for culinary/irrigation use, but of uncertain or possibly negative consequence for native fish habitat.
- **Species-specific predicted responses to Pah Tempe Spring removal**:
  - [[Virgin Spinedace]]: population size between Pah Tempe Spring and Washington Fields diversion likely to *increase*.
  - [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin Chub]]: population size unlikely to increase, and may *decrease*, in the same reach.
  - Discharge of cooled Pah Tempe Spring water below Washington Fields Diversion may create additional habitat for Woundfin and Virgin Chub — but this outcome is uncertain.
- **Historical natural experiment**: An increased Pah Tempe Spring flow event circa 1985 raised river TDS, offering an underutilized historical dataset on fish response to water quality shifts.
- **Hydrological connectivity**: The author stresses that flow must be guaranteed continuously from Hurricane Bridge to Washington Fields Diversion — measuring flow only at the diversion point does not protect the intervening river habitat used by native fish populations.

## Key Findings

1. **Habitat modeling misuse**: Depth/velocity models (Thom Hardy and Associates) characterize physical channel conditions, not full habitat suitability. Habitat is defined by the sum of biotic and abiotic characteristics affecting a species — not solely depth/velocity. No causal relationship has been established between fish abundance and depth/velocity "preference" metrics in the Virgin River.
2. **Evidence of population decline / "take"**: Deacon's own preliminary analysis suggests native fish populations in the [[Pah Tempe Spring]]–[[Quail Creek]] segment have declined under current operational flow conditions, implying ongoing "taking" of endangered species under the Endangered Species Act.
3. **Burden of proof reversed**: The Plan assumes lowering TDS via Pah Tempe Spring removal automatically benefits native fish habitat; Deacon insists the burden of proof lies with plan proponents to demonstrate ecological (not just human-use) benefit.
4. **Recommended empirical test**: Proposes an experimental minimum flow of 86 CFS from Hurricane Bridge to Quail Creek during spring–fall 1995 (feasible via the existing hydroelectric plant) to directly test the hypothesis that Quail Creek project operations have damaged native fish populations upstream.
5. **Underutilized data resources**: The [[Virgin River Fishes Data Base]] and studies by [[Terry Hickman]] contain fish population structure and abundance data across multiple river points that could resolve many of the Plan's unsupported assumptions but have not yet been analyzed for this purpose.
6. **Monitoring must be adaptive**: A monitoring program is supported in principle, but only has conservation value if paired with an adaptive management mechanism enabling real management responses to monitoring results.
7. **Flow measurement location matters**: Measuring compliance flows at the Washington Fields Diversion does not protect fish habitat upstream (Hurricane Bridge to the diversion); flow should be measured near Hurricane Bridge with safeguards ensuring it reaches downstream reaches, consistent with the existing non-jeopardy opinion for [[Quail Creek]].
