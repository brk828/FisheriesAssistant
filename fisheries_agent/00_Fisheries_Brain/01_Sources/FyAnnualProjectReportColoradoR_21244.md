# Management of Upper Colorado River Basin Fish Collections

#ichthyology #museum-collections #data-management #recovery-program

## Executive Summary

This FY 1993 annual report documents the second year of a three-year project (1992-1994) to reorganize, catalogue, and manage the massive holdings of preserved fish specimens from the Upper Colorado River Basin housed at the Larval Fish Laboratory, Colorado State University. The collection contains over 30,000-35,000 vials and jars (representing over one million specimens dating to 1976), primarily eggs, larvae, and early juveniles deposited by various agencies including [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], and [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]. The project aims to establish proper documentation, ensure permanency, provide online Internet access to the catalogue, and link the collection to a formal museum. By FY 1993, 4,032 specimen lots had been catalogued, with substantial progress made on computerization and network connectivity, though the project remains behind schedule.

## Background & Description

The [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at [[Colorado State University]] serves as the primary depository for preserved fish eggs, larvae, and early juveniles from the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], with holdings growing rapidly. These collections represent irreplaceable historical databases for specimen- and collection-specific information spanning nearly two decades. The specimens serve multiple purposes beyond traditional taxonomic verification: they enable retrospective analyses of community structure changes, size distributions, food habits, predation, condition, health, and parasite susceptibility without requiring new field collections.

Critically, past collections may contain unrecognized specimens of endangered species that were not identified when originally processed due to lack of diagnostic criteria. Examples include [[Gila]] and sucker larvae (especially [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and possible hybrids) that might be re-examined for [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] using recently developed taxonomic criteria. A decade prior, similar re-examination allowed [[Colorado Squawfish]] larvae to be identified among specimens previously categorized as probable Gila in late 1970s collections.

The project addresses RIPRAP Task V (monitoring populations and habitat to support recovery actions) and Task V.A.2 (interagency data management program), recognizing that preserved collections constitute primary databases for historical records that cannot be altered except by destruction and provide sources of new information as analytical methods advance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collections represent aquatic communities throughout the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], documenting fish assemblages and early life stages across multiple years and locations. The specimens provide baseline data for assessing temporal changes in community structure and species associations within this river system. The preservation methods allow for future analyses of skeletal structures such as otoliths to document age and life history characteristics of individual specimens.

The project operates within the broader [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], which coordinates efforts to recover endangered fish species while managing water resources. The institutional context shifted significantly during FY 1993 when the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service Field Station]] and [[National Ecology Research Center]] in Fort Collins transferred to the [[National Biological Survey]] (NBS), with collections relocating to the Albuquerque Field Station and association with the [[Museum of Southwestern Biology]] (MSB).

## Key Findings

**Institutional Developments:**
- The [[US Fish and Wildlife Service Field Station]] collections transferred to [[National Biological Survey]] and relocated to Albuquerque, with plans for cataloguing and housing under the [[Museum of Southwestern Biology]]
- A new [[Colorado Alliance of Biodiversity Collections]] (CABC) emerged as a mechanism to functionally unite natural history collections in Colorado, providing potential framework for ensuring collection permanency and online access
- Key CABC participants include representatives from [[University of Colorado Museum]], [[Colorado State University]], [[Denver Museum of Natural History]], and [[Nature Conservancy Colorado Natural Heritage Program]]

**Technical Infrastructure:**
- Ethernet controller card installed, connecting cataloguing computer to both College of Natural Resources and Department of Fishery and Wildlife Biology networks
- Internet access established for worldwide computer communications
- MUSE collection management program accessible from any Laboratory computer
- Tape backup system and laser printer installed for labels, catalogue data, loan invoices, and reports
- Investigation of University's "Gopher" host on Internet as freely accessed platform for collection catalogue

**Cataloguing Progress:**
- 4,032 specimen lots catalogued by end of FY 1993, covering much of 1991-1992 collections and some from 1990 and 1993
- Project behind schedule but expected to nearly double catalogued lots by late winter
- Approximately 30,000 additional lots remain to be catalogued for completion in final project year
- Procedures for cataloguing continue to be streamlined; documentation delayed until spring 1994

**Data Management Challenges:**
- Recovery Program's overall collection database remains unintegrated
- Concerns expressed about releasing some data to individuals or agencies outside Recovery Program
- Arrangements for transfer of specific collection locality data not yet completed
- Time requirements for cataloguing not yet analyzed; cost estimates dependent on this information

**Budget:**
- FY 1993 funds provided: $25,000
- Funds expended: $25,000
- No discrepancy between provided and expended funds

**Recommendations:**
- Additional support recommended if collections remain uncatalogued after third year
- Future sample processing funds should be increased to cover additional costs of collection cataloguing and maintenance
- Preserved larger fishes from Recovery Program projects should continue to be deposited with the relocated Field Station collection now associated with [[Museum of Southwestern Biology]]
