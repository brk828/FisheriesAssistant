## Executive Summary
This memorandum documents an interagency fish conservation action: the transfer of 3,208 [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to the [[Lake Mead Fish Hatchery]] on October 25, 1993. The fish were subsequently distributed among multiple research and reintroduction efforts, including tagging studies, an experimental stream channel study at the [[University of Nevada, Las Vegas]] (UNLV), and river releases into the [[Virgin River]] to support population monitoring and interstate conservation coordination with [[Utah Division of Natural Resources]].

## Background & Description
- **Species:** [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), a small imperiled cyprinid endemic to the Virgin River and historically the lower Colorado River Basin.
- **Source population:** Hatchery-reared individuals from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH), a federal facility specializing in propagation of endangered southwestern fishes.
- **Transfer logistics:** Fish were shipped via Show Low, Arizona en route to the [[Lake Mead Fish Hatchery]] (LMFH) in Nevada, with zero mortality recorded on both legs of transport.
- **Quantitative receipt data:**
  - Total fish received: 3,208
  - Density: 300 fish per pound
  - Total weight: 10.7 lbs
  - Receipt number: #94-04

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target habitat:** The [[Virgin River]], a tributary system critical to the remaining wild range of the Woundfin, spanning parts of Nevada, Arizona, and Utah.
- **Release sites:** Marked fish were released at two locations to enable comparative survival/movement assessment:
  - Below the **Mesquite Bridge**
  - Below the **Riverside Bridge**
- **Conservation collaboration:** Distribution of fish reflects a multi-state, multi-institutional recovery effort involving:
  - Nevada Division of Wildlife (NDOW)
  - University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV) — experimental stream channel study
  - Utah Division of Natural Resources — parallel studies with the remaining fish stock
- **Monitoring plan:** Post-release surveys of the Virgin River were planned to assess movement and survival, requiring specialized tag-detection equipment for any future native fish survey work in the area.

## Key Findings
- **Total fish distributed within Nevada:** 1,550 (remainder sent to Utah for similar studies).
- **Tagging/marking breakdown:**
  - 50 fish → UNLV experimental stream channel study
  - 1,000 fish → tagged with 1 mm binary-coded magnetic wire tags at the base of the dorsal spine (tags virtually invisible externally)
  - 500 fish → fluorescent dye-marked (pink) with corresponding dorsal spine clip
  - 15 fish (10 wire-tagged, 5 dye-marked) → held in aquaria to evaluate **tagging survival** as a control group
- **Dye retention observation:** Early results indicated dye-marked fish retained pink coloration well on fins and opercles, suggesting effective visual mark longevity.
- **Detection methodology:**
  - Magnetic wire tags detectable via [[Northwest Marine Technology]] wand or comparable metal detector.
  - Dye marks detectable using black/ultraviolet light.
- **Equipment logistics:** NDOW indicated capacity to supply detection equipment for future native fish surveys in the Virgin River with several weeks' advance notice.
- **Zero mortality** was recorded throughout the entire transport process from DNFH to LMFH, indicating effective hatchery-to-hatchery transfer protocols for this sensitive species.
