# Sex Ratios in the Subterranean Amphipod Genus Stygonectes (Gammaridae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: The source is a study of subterranean amphipods, not fish. The tag line above follows the required format, but the taxon is an amphipod crustacean.*

## Executive Summary

This short research note reports sex ratios in populations of the subterranean amphipod genus [[Stygonectes]] collected from seeps, springs, drains and caves across eastern and central North America and Texas. Females predominate in most collections. The bias is strongest in cave populations of species known only from caves (S. spinatus, S. emarginatus and S. russelli), where males are very rare. The authors tentatively propose that [[Parthenogenesis]] occurs in low-density cave populations, particularly in [[Court Street Cave]] in West Virginia.

## Background & Description

- The genus [[Stygonectes]] (family [[Gammaridae]]) inhabits a range of subsurface habitats, from groundwater seeps a few miles from the Atlantic coast to caves in the [[Appalachian Mountains]] and [[Ozark Mountains]] (Holsinger, 1967).
- Cave populations are typically low in density, whereas seep populations may be quite dense.
- The study was part of a continuing ecological survey of the aquatic cave fauna of the [[Greenbrier Valley]], West Virginia (Culver, 1967), where males of S. spinatus and S. emarginatus were noticed to be rare.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Sampling covered non-cave collections with more than 20 individuals and cave collections with 14 or more individuals, plus small collections of S. spinatus from Court Street Cave.
- Habitats sampled include seeps, springs, a drain, a seep bog and several caves, including [[Organ Cave]], [[Onesquethaw Cave]], [[Reftons Cave]], [[Balcones Sink]], [[Stowers Cave]] and [[Snellings Cave]].
- Localities span Mississippi, Arkansas, New York, Missouri, Virginia, Pennsylvania, Washington D.C., Texas and West Virginia.
- Table 1 lists 31 sex-ratio collections across 10 species. Ratios are expressed as males/females, with P values from a one-sided sign test of a 50:50 null hypothesis.
- Sign test P values are given per collection. Ratios below 1 mean a female bias; values near 0 indicate strong female dominance.

## Key Findings

- **Female predominance is general.** Females outnumbered males in the majority of collections from all habitat types.
- **Cave-restricted species show a strong male deficit.** Cave collections of species known exclusively from caves (S. spinatus, S. emarginatus, S. russelli) have very few males. Their sex ratios differ significantly from non-cave collections (P < 0.001, Wilcoxon's t-test, Bradley, 1968).
- **The bias is not seen in cave records of non-cave species.** Cave collections of species found mainly in non-cave habitats (S. pizzinii, S. allegheniensis) do not show the strong male reduction.
- **Non-cave female bias is modest.** The slight female excess in non-cave collections may reflect differential mortality of the sexes (Hynes, 1955), but this could not be confirmed because periodic data for a non-cave population are unavailable.
- **Court Street Cave shows a persistent aberration.** Repeated sampling of S. spinatus at Court Street Cave across the year (June 1967 to October 1968) consistently shows strongly female-biased ratios. Examples include 1 male to 10 females on 17 February 1968, and 1 male to 10 females on 2 October 1968. The authors consider the bias permanent.
- **Parthenogenesis is proposed as a tentative explanation.** The authors tentatively conclude that some form of [[Parthenogenesis]] occurs in Court Street Cave in response to low population density. They suggest a similar process may operate in other cave populations, though data are incomplete.
- **Sampling limitation.** Several collections are small, and the cave data for some species rely on few samples, so the conclusions are described as preliminary.
