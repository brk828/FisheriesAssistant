# Reproduction by Humpback Chub in a Warm Spring Along the Colorado River in Grand Canyon, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #Gila-cypha

## Executive Summary

This study documents the first evidence of mainstem reproduction by the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in Grand Canyon, independent of the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) spawning aggregation. On July 14, 1994, researchers observed ~100 young humpback chub in the thermal plume of a warm shoreline spring ("Spring No. 5" / "Diagonal Spring") at river kilometer (RK) 49.7, part of a cluster of eight springs associated with the [[Fence Fault]] and Eminence Graben. Fourteen captured post-larvae (mean 24 mm TL, range 18-31 mm TL) were estimated at 36 days old, indicating successful egg incubation and larval survival in the 21.5°C spring plume despite surrounding cold (11°C) mainstem water from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] releases. The findings demonstrate that a persistent, genetically distinct relict mainstem spawning aggregation exists in Grand Canyon, and the authors recommend a 16-km extension of designated critical habitat to protect the Fence Fault springs.

## Background & Description

- **Species**: *Gila cypha* — large-bodied river cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin.
- **Post-larvae captured (July 1994)**: n = 14; mean total length (TL) = 24 mm; range = 18-31 mm TL.
- **Estimated age of post-larvae**: ~36 days, based on morphometric characteristics and a growth curve developed by Muth (1990) for LCR-derived brood stock reared at 18-23°C.
- **Adult humpback chub (Fence Fault aggregation)**: 330-460 mm TL (n = 26 captured 1992-93; total 30 individuals across study period).
- **Laboratory developmental benchmarks** (Hamman 1982):
  - Hatching TL: 6.7-7.4 mm
  - TL doubled within 21-28 days post-hatch
  - TL at 56 days post-hatch: 36.9-47.5 mm
- **Spawning condition indicators observed in wild adults**: nuptial tubercles, breeding coloration, expressed gametes (milting males, tubercled/spent females).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat context**: The [[Colorado River]] in Grand Canyon is thermally altered by cold hypolimnetic releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]], transforming the pre-dam seasonal temperature range (2-26°C) to a narrow post-dam range (7-10°C), largely precluding successful mainstem reproduction for most native fishes.

**Spring characteristics (Spring No. 5 / "Diagonal Spring")**:
- Location: West bank of Colorado River, RK 49.7 (RM 30.8), base of limestone shelf
- Source: Mooney Falls Member of the Redwall Limestone, structurally controlled by the Fence Fault
- Source temperature: constant 21.5°C (71°F)
- Estimated discharge: 56-85 L/s (900 gal/min measured in 1979)
- Conductivity: 353 µS/cm
- Dissolved oxygen: 6.6 mg/L
- pH: 7.24
- Water chemistry (1979): 3.28 meq/L total cations, 3.56 meq/L total anions — indicating no significant change over 15 years (1979-1994)
- Thermal plume used by fish: 16-19°C, extending ~10 m from source before blending with mainstem (11°C)
- Largest spring in the Fence Fault complex: Spring No. 2, ~410 L/s (6,500 gal/min)

**Hydrological dynamics**:
- Mainstem flows during observation: 340-566 m³/s, inundating the spring eye by 0.5-1.5 m and creating a recirculating eddy that entrained the warm plume
- Spring exposed (no inundation) at mainstem flow ≈283 m³/s
- At mainstem flow ~425 m³/s: temperature at 2 m and 3 m from spring source = 15°C and 12°C, respectively

**Microhabitat use**: Young fish retreated into cracks, crevices among boulders and limestone bedrock, and sparse overhanging vegetation within 1.5 m of the spring source, remaining within the thermal plume.

**Co-occurring/competing species observed in plume**:
- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) — adults observed, possibly also depositing eggs
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) — adults observed, possible competitor/predator
- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) — noted as a predator risk in discussion

**Other native species mentioned for comparative mainstem spawning surveys**:
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*)
- [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)

**Population structure**: Total estimated 52 adults (Chao M estimator) in the RK 48-50 reach; 24 of 30 marked adults (80%) captured specifically in the Spring No. 5 plume. No evidence of immigration/emigration among marked fish, indicating a discrete, persistent local aggregation. This was the only location where humpback chub were captured across 117 km between Glen Canyon Dam and RK 92.

## Key Findings

1. **First documented mainstem spawning evidence in Grand Canyon**: The Fence Fault springs represent the only confirmed site of successful humpback chub mainstem reproduction in Grand Canyon, independent of the LCR population.

2. **Thermal requirements for reproduction** (Hamman 1982; Marsh 1985):
   - Highest hatching success: 19-20°C (Hamman) / 20°C, 60% success (Marsh)
   - Highest larval survival: 21-22°C
   - Incubation duration: 102-146 h (21-22°C) up to 340-475 h (12-13°C)
   - Egg survival: 79% (21-22°C), 84% (19-20°C), 62% (16-17°C), 12% (12-13°C)
   - Swim-up fry survival: 99% (21-22°C) down to 15% (12-13°C)
   - All embryos died at 5°C, 10°C, and 30°C (Marsh 1985)

3. **Spawning phenology**: Eleven adults found in spawning condition across May 1993-June 1995 (tubercled/milting males, tubercled/spent females), despite the mainstem temperature varying only 2.5°C annually (8.5-11°C) — suggesting small temperature increases and photoperiod, not absolute temperature, cue gonadal development.

4. **Historical context**: Average monthly river temperature at Lees Ferry last exceeded 16°C in July 1970, suggesting this may be the last year mainstem conditions broadly supported spawning success — implying the Fence Fault aggregation's adults could be relict fish nearly 25 years old or more.

5. **Transport hypothesis for downstream juveniles**: Previously documented young humpback chub found far downstream (e.g., RK 71.3 backwater; Suttkus and Clemmer 1977; AZGFD unpublished data) likely originated as drift from Fence Fault spring spawning rather than the LCR, since juvenile *Gila cypha* have poor swimming ability especially in cold water (sustained 0.51 m/s for 85 min at 20°C vs. only 2 min at 14°C; Bulkley et al. 1982).

6. **Survival constraints**: Cold mainstem temperatures, unstable habitat from fluctuating hydropower releases, and predation (rainbow trout, brown trout, common carp) likely limit survival and recruitment of young to adulthood — no subadults were found in the aggregation, suggesting a recruitment bottleneck.

7. **Critical habitat recommendation**: Extend designated [[Critical Habitat]] 16 km upstream (from RM 34 to RM 24) to encompass all eight Fence Fault springs, since this represents the only known relict mainstem spawning stock in Grand Canyon.

8. **Management implications**: Findings support consideration of a selective withdrawal structure (multilevel penstock modification) at Glen Canyon Dam to warm mainstem releases and potentially enhance spawning success; alternatively, gamete collection for a hatchery refugium is proposed to preserve this genetic stock until conditions improve.
