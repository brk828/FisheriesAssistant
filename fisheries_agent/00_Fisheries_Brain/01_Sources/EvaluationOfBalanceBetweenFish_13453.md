# Evaluation of Balance between Fishes and Available Fish Foods in Multispecies Fish Culture Ponds in Taiwan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #aquaculture #limnology #Polyculture

## Executive Summary

This 1970 study by Y.A. Tang quantitatively evaluates the ecological "balance" between stocked fish populations and their available natural food resources (plankton, macrophytes, benthos, nekton) in two contrasting freshwater impoundment systems in Taiwan: the intensively managed, organically fertilized **Tainan Fishpond** (6 ha) and the largely unfertilized **Taoyuan Irrigation Reservoirs** (99 reservoirs, 697 ha total). Using a [[Multispecies Fish Culture|multispecies (polyculture)]] system rooted in traditional Chinese pond management, six ecologically distinct fish groups (planktophagic, detritophagic, macrophytophagic, benthophagic, and nektophagic) were stocked to exploit complementary food niches. The study introduces quantitative ratios (Weight ratios [Wf:Wb] and Weight-percentage ratios [Ef:Eb]) to assess whether fish consumption rates matched the growth/replenishment rates of their respective food bases. Findings show that heavy organic fertilization and feeding in the Tainan pond substantially increased fish-food biota (~558%) and shifted the balance toward benthophagic and planktophagic fish, while in the unfertilized reservoirs, the natural standing crop of biota (particularly macrophyte complexes) sustained a different equilibrium. The work provides one of the earliest quantitative frameworks for assessing carrying capacity and ecological niche partitioning in polyculture systems.

## Background & Description

- **System studied**: [[Multispecies Fish Culture]] (aka polyculture), a traditional Chinese aquaculture philosophy of harmonizing man, matter, and nature by stocking fish of different food habits together to maximize utilization of pond food niches.
- **Key cultured species** (the "Chinese carps") introduced widely across Asia, Africa, Europe, and the Americas:
  - [[Silver Carp]] (*Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*) — planktophagic
  - [[Bighead Carp]] (*Aristichthys nobilis*) — zooplanktophagic
  - [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) — macrophytophagic
  - [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) — benthophagic
  - [[Black Carp]] (*Mylopharyngodon piceus*) — benthophagic
  - [[Grey Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) — detritophagic (phytoplankton-detritus feeder)
  - [[Sea Perch]] (*Lateolabrax japonicus*) — nektophagic (piscivorous)

### Study Sites
1. **Tainan Fishpond** (southwest Taiwan)
   - 6.0 hectares, alluvial sandy loam soils, groundwater-fed.
   - Depth: 0.5–1.0 m (Feb–May dry season) to 2.0–3.0 m (rest of year).
   - Water chemistry: hard water, pH 8.4–8.8 (pre-dawn).
   - Temperature range: 9–32°C annually; below 20°C only Dec–Mar.
   - Heavily managed: organic fertilization (rice bran, chicken manure, pig manure, night soil) and supplemental feeding, centuries-old traditional practices.
   - Annual manure application: 12,600 kg dry matter/ha (267 kg N, 196 kg P, 153 kg K).

2. **Taoyuan Irrigation Reservoirs** (northwest Taiwan)
   - Part of the Tachun Irrigation System (241 reservoirs total, 2,112 ha); 99 reservoirs (697 ha) used for fish culture.
   - Individual reservoir size: 3.5–22.0 ha.
   - Reddish-brown clay loam to light clay soils; fed via canal system from the River Tamshui.
   - Depth: 0.5–4.0 m, fluctuating with rice irrigation demand.
   - Water chemistry: soft water, pH 6.0–7.8 (pre-dawn).
   - Temperature range: 7–31°C annually; below 20°C Nov–Apr.
   - Minimal management: no manure/feed in most reservoirs due to frequent water outflow; managed by retired army veterans since 1957.

### Methods
- **Plankton**: Kemmerer water sampler; Foerst centrifuge (15,000 rpm) separation; dry weight via incineration loss; wet weight assumed at 10× dry organic matter (90% water content assumption), following [[Juday 1926]] method.
- **Zooplankton**: filtered through 20-mesh silk bolting cloth.
- **Benthos**: Ekman dredge (378 samples in Tainan pond; 216 in Taoyuan reservoirs).
- **Macrophytes**: manual collection + area survey (annual, Sept for pond, Aug for reservoirs).
- **Nekton**: measured by draining impoundments at harvest.
- **Fish stocking**: fingerlings 3–9 cm TL stocked Feb–March (piscivores after April); harvested Dec–Jan via draining.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The study frames pond ecosystems as having discrete **ecological niches** (plankton, macrophyte, benthic, nektonic) that can be exploited by ecologically differentiated fish species — directly citing [[Yashouv 1966]]'s theory that fertilization/feeding enrich food niches, but only balanced polyculture stocking can realize full carrying capacity.
- **Mortality rates** (11-month growing period) differed sharply between sites, reflecting environmental harshness/predation pressure in unfertilized reservoirs vs. managed pond:

| Species | Tainan Pond Mortality (%) | Taoyuan Reservoirs Mortality (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Silver carp | 14 | 20 |
| Grey mullet | 33 | 60–82 |
| Bighead carp | 16 | 26 |
| Grass carp | 10 | 20 |
| Black carp | — | 50 |
| Common carp | 39 | 60 |
| Sea perch | 23 | 75 |

- **Organic loading pathways**: Tang identifies three routes by which added organic matter enters the food web: (1) direct consumption by omnivorous/forage fish and invertebrates, (2) microbial decomposition feeding zooplankton, (3) nutrient release (C, N, P) fueling phytoplankton growth — functioning ecologically similar to dead phytoplankton input.
- **Algal bloom risk**: Overabundance of undesirable phytoplankton (e.g., *Microcystis*, Euglininae) can cause "autoshedding" — self-shading that reduces photosynthesis in lower water layers and depletes dissolved oxygen through decay of surface scums, a risk mitigated by adequate planktophagic fish grazing.
- **Interspecies facilitation**: Grey mullet (detritophagic) was shown to scavenge bottom sediments stirred up by common carp feeding activity, mitigating the "muddy water" problem classically associated with carp in warm-water pond management ([[Swingle 1949]]).
- Common carp additionally benefits **primary production** via nutrient recycling from sediment disturbance, despite turbidity costs.

## Key Findings

### Standing Crop & Fish Yield (Table 4/5 summary)
- **Tainan Pond** (fertilized/fed): mean total biota standing crop ≈ 7,829 kg/ha; total fish yield mean ≈ 7,057 kg/ha.
- **Taoyuan Reservoirs** (unfertilized): mean total biota ≈ 1,190 kg/ha; total fish yield mean ≈ 424 kg/ha.
- Organic fertilization increased fish-food biota standing crop by approximately **558%** relative to unfertilized reservoirs.

### Weight Ratios (Wf:Wb) — kg fish per kg standing food biota (Table 6)
| Fish-food biota | Fish utilizing | Tainan Pond | Taoyuan Reservoirs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plankton | Silver carp, bighead carp, grey mullet | 0.81 | 0.43 |
| Macrophytes | Grass carp | 2.24 | 0.19 |
| Benthos | Common carp, black carp | 2.51 | 0.44 |
| Nekton | Sea perch | 1.98 | 0.38 |
| Total biota | All species | 0.92 | 0.36 |

### Weight-Percentage Ratios (Ef:Eb) — Table 6
| Fish-food biota | Tainan Pond | Taoyuan Reservoirs |
|---|---|---|
| Plankton | 0.88 | 1.22 |
| Macrophytes | 2.40 | 0.53 |
| Benthos | 2.65 | 1.23 |
| Nekton | 2.09 | 1.07 |
| Total biota | 1.00 | 1.00 |

- A hypothetical "perfect balance" would yield Ef:Eb = 1.0 across all groups; deviations reflect differing nutritive values of food types and effects of supplemental feeding.
- In irrigation reservoirs, **Ep:Eb for plankton and benthos** were statistically significantly different from 1.0 (P=0.04 and P=0.03 respectively, 5% level).
- In the Tainan pond, only the **plankton ratio (Ep1:Ef1 = 0.88)** was significantly different from 1.0 (P=0.05).

### Feed Conversion Estimates (Table 7)
- Estimated fish production directly attributable to organic feeding/manuring in Tainan Pond: **3,887 kg fish** from direct feed utilization vs. **3,400 kg fish** from natural fish-food biota consumption (roughly balanced split).
- **Conversion ratio of organic matter added to fish produced by direct feeding**: **3.2:1** (12,600 kg dry organic matter → ~3,887 kg fish).
- Comparative literature conversion ratios: napier grass (*Pennisetum purpureum*) to grass carp = 48:1 ([[Hickling 1962]], Malacca); plankton algae to milkfish = 13:1 ([[Tang & Chen 1966]]).

### Ecological Niche (E-value) Comparisons — Biota vs. Fish (Table 5)
| Component | Tainan Pond Biota E (%) | Tainan Pond Fish E (%) | Reservoirs Biota E (%) | Reservoirs Fish E (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plankton/Planktophagic | 92.7 | 81.6 | 58.6 | 71.7 |
| Macrophytes/Macrophytophagic | 1.5 | 3.6 | 32.0 | 17.2 |
| Benthos/Benthophagic | 4.7 | 12.5 | 6.5 | 8.0 |
| Nekton/Nektophagic | 1.1 | 2.3 | 2.9 | 3.1 |

- Under high fertilization (Tainan), the macrophytophagic fish E-value dropped **13.6 points** relative to reservoirs, while planktophagic and benthophagic E-values rose by **10.0** and **4.5 points**, respectively — indicating that enriched conditions shifted fish community composition/food utilization patterns.

### Feed Quality Effects
- Fish fed exclusively on raw manures (in tank experiments) failed to grow normally — mirrored in [[Milkfish]] (*Chanos chanos*) feeding trials ([[Tang 1967]]) — suggesting organic wastes alone are poor-quality feed; a mixture with natural food organisms is necessary for good growth.
- High-quality commercial feeds (as in [[Stevenson 1966]]'s Arkansas grass carp experiments) can reduce interspecific competition for natural food biota, promoting facultative feeding and lowering food-biota utilization efficiency — a contrast to the low-quality-feed, high-competition dynamic observed in the Tainan pond.

### Methodological Note
- Ecological classification of fish by food habit was validated via stomach content analysis: cross-feeding (e.g., benthophagic fish consuming plant detritus, macrophytophagic fish consuming insect larvae) was consistently under 10%, and silver carp zooplankton consumption was under 7% of total plankton intake — supporting the accuracy of the six-group classification scheme (error margin <10%).
