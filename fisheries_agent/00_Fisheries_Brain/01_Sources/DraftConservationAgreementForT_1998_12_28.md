# Pecos Pupfish (Cyprinodon pecosensis) — Draft Conservation Agreement

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This document is a regulatory/administrative notice from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), published in the Federal Register on December 28, 1998, announcing the reopening of the public comment period on a proposal to list the **[[Pecos Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*) as an endangered species under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**. The notice also announces availability of a **Draft Conservation Agreement**, developed collaboratively by the FWS, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, New Mexico State Parks Department, Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, and the Bureau of Reclamation. The Agreement aims to address the primary threats to the species — small isolated populations and hybridization risk with the non-native **[[Sheepshead Minnow]]** (*Cyprinodon variegatus*) — as an alternative to federal listing. No original morphological or field ecological data are presented; the document is policy/administrative in nature but contains species-relevant conservation and threat information.

No morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan, sexual dimorphism) are provided in this source. The document does not describe physical characteristics of *Cyprinodon pecosensis* or *Cyprinodon variegatus*; it focuses exclusively on regulatory status and conservation planning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** The Pecos Pupfish occupies small, isolated aquatic habitats within the **[[Pecos River]]** drainage system in New Mexico and Texas — consistent with the genus's typical preference for spring-fed pools, saline/alkaline waters, and isolated desert aquatic refugia.
- **Population structure:** Described as existing in **"small, isolated populations,"** a structural vulnerability that elevates extinction risk from stochastic events, habitat loss, and genetic isolation.
- **Interspecific threat — hybridization:** A central ecological concern is potential **hybridization** with the introduced **[[Sheepshead Minnow]]** (*Cyprinodon variegatus*), a nonnative congener likely introduced via baitfish releases. Hybridization threatens the genetic integrity of pure *C. pecosensis* populations, a common conservation concern among **[[Cyprinodon]]** species with restricted, fragmented ranges (cf. other endangered pupfishes such as the **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]** and **[[Comanche Springs Pupfish]]**).
- **Anthropogenic pressures:** Baitfish release practices are identified as a vector for nonnative species introduction, indicating that regional fishing/baitfish regulations directly intersect with the species' ecological risk profile.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory timeline:** The Pecos Pupfish was proposed for listing as endangered on **January 30, 1998** (63 FR 4608); a public hearing was held in **Carlsbad, New Mexico on April 9, 1998**; the original comment period ran **January 30–November 20, 1998**, and was reopened through **January 27, 1999**.
- **Primary identified threats:**
  1. Small, isolated population sizes limiting resilience and genetic diversity.
  2. Risk of hybridization with the nonnative **Sheepshead Minnow** (*Cyprinodon variegatus*), which threatens the species' genetic purity.
- **Conservation Agreement commitments** (by signatory State/Federal agencies):
  - Protect known **extant populations** of pure Pecos Pupfish.
  - **Expand distribution** of the species within its native range by establishing new populations.
  - **Prohibit use of Sheepshead Minnow as baitfish** through revisions to baitfish regulations in both **New Mexico** and **Texas**, directly targeting the hybridization vector.
- **Regulatory significance:** The FWS indicated that if these voluntary agency commitments adequately mitigate the identified threats, formal **ESA listing may not be required** — an example of a **Candidate Conservation Agreement** used as a pre-listing regulatory tool.
- **Collaborating entities:** U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, New Mexico State Parks Department, Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, and the Bureau of Reclamation.
- **Document authorship/authority:** Prepared under authority of the **Endangered Species Act (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.)**; primary author Jennifer Fowler-Propst, New Mexico Ecological Services Field Office.
