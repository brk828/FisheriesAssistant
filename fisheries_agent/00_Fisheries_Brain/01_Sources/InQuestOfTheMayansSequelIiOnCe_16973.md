# Fresh-water Fishes from British Honduras

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Dorosomidae #Characidae #Central-America

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic study documents a small collection of Isospondylous and Characoid fishes from [[British Honduras]] (modern [[Belize]]) and neighboring [[Guatemala]], a poorly known Central American region. The collection includes representatives from two families: [[Dorosomidae]] (gizzard shads) and [[Characidae]] (characins). Notable findings include the first record of [[Brycon guatemalensis]] from British Honduras, morphological analysis suggesting [[Hyphessobrycon milleri]] may represent the adult stage of [[Hyphessobrycon compressus]], and documentation of a potentially new subspecies of [[Dorosoma]]. The study emphasizes the biogeographical pattern of characoid fishes penetrating Central America from South America via the [[Isthmus of Panama]], with species diversity decreasing northward.

## Background & Description

**Family Dorosomidae:**
The [[Dorosomidae]] constitute a small family of mostly marine fishes closely related to [[Clupeidae]]. Distinguishing characters include stomach structure, absence of lateral line, small inferior toothless mouth, strongly compressed belly forming a keel armed with bony serrae, and a filiform last dorsal fin ray similar to [[Tarpon]] and [[Megalops]].

**Dorosoma mexicanum (Günther):**
- Sample: 3 specimens, largest 135 mm standard length (S.L.)
- Location: Vicinity of Belize (river, brackish water)
- Known distribution: East coast of Mexico
- Morphometrics: Depth 2.75 in S.L.; head 4.1 in S.L.
- Meristics: Dorsal rays 12 + filament; anal rays 24-25; serrae 27, with 10 behind ventrals
- Note: Literature reports 14 dorsal rays and 9 serrae behind ventrals

**Dorosoma species (undescribed):**
- Sample: 1 specimen, 121 mm S.L., sympatric with D. mexicanum
- Morphology: Strongly inferior mouth, short maxillary
- Comparison with [[Dorosoma cepedianum]]: Longer anal fin (36 rays vs. 30-33), somewhat longer body (2.9 vs. 2.5 in S.L.), longer head (4 vs. 4.33 in S.L.)
- Taxonomic status: May represent new subspecies; affinities with [[Dorosoma cepedianum exile]] from Texas require investigation

**Family Characidae:**
Characoid fishes penetrated Central America late in Earth's history from the southeast via the [[Isthmus of Panama]]. The isthmus acts as a biogeographical filter, resulting in decreasing species abundance northward. British Honduras appears poor in characoid species diversity.

**Brycon guatemalensis Regan:**
- Sample: 13 juvenile specimens, largest approximately 40 mm S.L.
- Location: [[Punta Gorda]]
- Meristics: ~60 longitudinal scales; anal fin equals head length, iv, 31; pectorals reaching ventrals
- Dentition: Premaxillary teeth in 4 rows (7-4-1-6 on each side); maxillary teeth approximately 8
- Coloration: Unpaired fins (including adipose) salmon colored
- Significance: Apparently first record from British Honduras

**Astyanax fasciatus aeneus (Günther):**
- Sample: 9 specimens (largest 77.6 mm S.L.) from vicinity of Belize; 1 specimen (37.7 mm S.L.) from Punta Gorda; 1 specimen (53.5 mm S.L.) from arroyo Ixlu, Guatemala
- Distribution: Two northern forms ([[Astyanax fasciatus aeneus]] and [[Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus]]) show clinal distribution along Central America with possible isolation mechanisms creating sibling species
- Meristics: Anal rays iii or iv, 22-24; maxillary not quite reaching middle of eye
- Dentition: Mandibulary teeth gradually diminishing in size from frontal to lateral, without abrupt size reduction
- Coloration: Dorsal and caudal fins orange, caudal base yellow; ventrals and beginning of anal cherry-red
- Taxonomic assessment: Close to aeneus-type (already recorded from British Honduras) but with some mexicanus features, notably maxillary length and relatively low anal ray count

**Hyphessobrycon compressus aff. (Meek):**
- Sample: 9 specimens (largest 28.6 mm S.L.) from arroyo Ixlu, Guatemala (24 km west of Flores); 1 juvenile from vicinity of Belize
- Taxonomic significance: [[Hyphessobrycon compressus]] is type-species of genus [[Hyphessobrycon]]
- Previous records: Known only from types (Prov. Vera Cruz, Mexico)
- Related form: [[Hyphessobrycon milleri]] from nearby locality, differing in dorsal fin color pattern and suborbital width

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The biogeographical context is central to understanding this fauna. Characoid fishes represent a South American lineage that invaded Central America relatively recently in geological time, migrating northward through the [[Isthmus of Panama]]. This land bridge functions as an ecological and dispersal filter, progressively reducing species diversity with increasing latitude. British Honduras represents a northern frontier for this fauna, exhibiting characteristically low species richness compared to South American source regions.

The collection sites span both coastal brackish water environments (vicinity of Belize) and inland freshwater systems ([[Punta Gorda]], arroyo Ixlu). The sympatric occurrence of two [[Dorosoma]] species near Belize suggests niche partitioning or temporal separation in these transitional habitats.

The presence of juvenile [[Brycon guatemalensis]] at Punta Gorda indicates successful reproduction in British Honduras waters, though this represents a range extension for the species. Similarly, the distribution of [[Astyanax fasciatus]] populations across the region demonstrates the complex interplay between clinal variation and potential reproductive isolation in Central American freshwater systems.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic and Systematic Contributions:**

1. **Dorosoma taxonomy:** Documentation of a potentially undescribed subspecies of [[Dorosoma]] sympatric with [[Dorosoma mexicanum]] near Belize, distinguished by anal ray count (36 vs. 30-33), body proportions, and head length. Relationship to [[Dorosoma cepedianum exile]] requires further investigation.

2. **Hyphessobrycon systematic hypothesis:** Detailed morphological comparison suggests [[Hyphessobrycon milleri]] may represent the adult stage of a local population of [[Hyphessobrycon compressus]] rather than a distinct species. Evidence includes:
   - Present specimens from near milleri type locality possess compressus dorsal pattern and narrow suborbital
   - Positive allometry observed in maxillary and third suborbital size
   - These characters are diagnostic for milleri vs. compressus
   - Hypothesis requires verification with samples from Los Amates, Guatemala (milleri type locality)

3. **Genus Hyphessobrycon structure:** The genus divides into two groups:
   - Large, possibly polyphyletic group of generalized species (callistus-group) centered in Amazon basin
   - Small Central American group (compressus and milleri) representing specialized northern offshoot from primitive southern forms (e.g., [[Hyphessobrycon bentosi]], [[Hyphessobrycon rosaceus]], certain [[Megalamphodus]])
   - Central American group shows evolutionary trends: increased scale and tooth numbers, reduced predorsal scalation and circumorbitals

**Biogeographical Records:**

4. **Range extension:** First record of [[Brycon guatemalensis]] from British Honduras, extending known distribution northward.

5. **Astyanax variation:** Documentation of intermediate populations between [[Astyanax fasciatus aeneus]] and [[Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus]], supporting clinal distribution hypothesis while noting potential for localized reproductive isolation and sibling species formation.

**Morphological Data:**

Comprehensive morphometric and meristic data provided for all species, including detailed comparison table for [[Hyphessobrycon compressus]] complex showing variation in:
- Standard length/depth ratios (2.6-2.95)
- Anal ray counts (iii, 21-25)
- Longitudinal scale counts (42-49)
- Dentition patterns
- Suborbital proportions
- Dorsal fin pigmentation patterns

The study emphasizes the value of examining geographical variation in widespread species and the importance of adequate sampling across type localities for resolving taxonomic controversies in Central American freshwater fish fauna.
