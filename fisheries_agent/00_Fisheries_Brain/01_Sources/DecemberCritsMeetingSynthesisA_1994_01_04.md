# 3 December 1993 CRiTS Meeting Synthesis and Next Meeting

#ichthyology #river-ecology #Colorado-River-Trophic-Studies

## Executive Summary
This document is a National Park Service memorandum summarizing the 3 December 1993 meeting of the **[[Colorado River Trophic Studies (CRiTS)]]** group — a consortium of hydrologists and aquatic biologists studying water quality and trophic dynamics in the dam-regulated **[[Colorado River]]** system through **[[Grand Canyon National Park]]**, upstream and downstream of **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]**. Rather than presenting new empirical findings, the memo outlines a draft synthesis document integrating results from the **[[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)]]** Program (Phases I and II) and the ongoing **[[Interim Flows]]** period, treated as a test of ecosystem-structure hypotheses. The synthesis framework spans reservoir limnology (**[[Lake Powell]]**), downstream water quality, light attenuation, benthic ecology, and backwater habitats — with explicit linkages to fish trophic ecology, particularly the role of **[[Backwater Habitats]]** as rearing refuges for young-of-year (YOY) native fish and as arenas of native–nonnative competition. The document also previews a planned **1995 experimental flood** (habitat-building flow) requiring baseline data and risk assessment for potentially affected resources, including fish populations.

## Background & Description
The source is a programmatic/administrative synthesis outline and does not report morphological or anatomical data on any fish species. No length, weight, meristic, or morphometric data are presented. Fish are referenced only functionally — by life stage (YOY vs. mature) and origin (native vs. non-native) — in the context of habitat use, not morphology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although fish-specific ecological data are limited, the outline situates fish trophic ecology within a multi-tiered aquatic food web framework:

- **Primary production base**: Attached algae (*[[Cladophora]]*, epiphytes, *Oscillatoria*) and phytoplankton form the foundation of the [[Benthic Ecology|benthic food web]], varying with turbidity, exposure, depth, and temperature.
- **Secondary production**: Macroinvertebrates such as *[[Gammarus]]* (an amphipod) and chironomids link primary production to higher trophic levels, including fish.
- **Drift dynamics**: Particulate organic matter (POM), zooplankton, and invertebrate drift respond to flow fluctuations (including experimental flooding) and change longitudinally from the tailwaters near Glen Canyon Dam downstream to Lake Mead.
- **Backwater habitats** (tributary mouths and main-channel backwaters) are identified as critical fish habitat:
  - Serve as **feeding areas** for both YOY and mature fish.
  - Function as **refuges for YOY native fish**, with explicit concern over **competition between native and non-native species** in these habitats.
  - Support water-quality and productivity gradients (temperature, nutrient retention, floc/detritus retention) that shape food availability for fish.
  - Also used by invertebrates, amphibians, avifauna (including the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher), and beaver, indicating backwaters as multi-trophic-level hotspots.
- **Light and turbidity regime**: Sediment concentration and dissolved/particulate organic matter strongly attenuate light in the river corridor, indirectly constraining primary productivity and, by extension, the food base available to fish.
- **Flow regulation effects**: Managed flow parameters (minimum/maximum discharge, ramping rates, seasonality, day-vs-night low water, and flooding) are recognized as master variables controlling benthic production, drift, backwater formation, and ultimately fish habitat quality and trophic support.
- **Reservoir–river linkage**: **[[Lake Powell]]** water quality, thermal structure, and withdrawal-plume characteristics propagate downstream, with explicit anticipated fisheries effects tied to planned flood releases and multi-level withdrawal management.

## Key Findings
- The CRiTS group proposes a structured synthesis of GCES-era research (Phase I background, Phase II focused studies, Interim Flows as hypothesis-testing period) covering Lake Powell limnology, downstream water quality, light attenuation, benthic ecology, and backwaters.
- **Backwaters** are explicitly flagged as key habitat for **native fish conservation**, especially as YOY refugia, and as sites of **native/non-native fish competition** — a central applied-management concern for the Grand Canyon Colorado River ecosystem.
- A major **experimental controlled flood is planned for late March 1995** as a "habitat-building flow," intended to test system responses; the memo calls for 1994 baseline data collection and **risk assessment for resources potentially affected**, implicitly including fish and their habitats.
- Multiple discipline-specific models are identified as needed but not yet developed, including a **lake food web and productivity model** for Lake Powell, a **channel bottom/margin biogeochemical model**, and models linking light attenuation to primary productivity — all of which bear indirectly on the aquatic food base supporting fish.
- No chapter on **plankton** yet exists in the outline; the 11 March 1994 agenda explicitly raises the open question "Do we need a chapter on plankton?", indicating this trophic component was still unresolved in the synthesis structure.
- Data management concerns (QA/QC, storage formats, interlab calibration) are repeatedly flagged as incomplete across water quality, light, and benthic ecology datasets, underscoring that much of the underlying aquatic monitoring infrastructure (relevant to fish habitat assessment) was still under development as of late 1993.
- Relevant contributing groups/agencies for aquatic and fish-adjacent studies included **[[Arizona Game and Fish (AGF)]]**, **[[BioWest]]**, **[[Northern Arizona University (NAU)]]**, **[[Bureau of Reclamation (BR-GCES)]]**, and NPS Grand Canyon staff.
