# Systematic Studies of Darters of the Subgenus Catonotus (Percidae): Description of a New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

Page and Braasch (1976) revisit the barcheek darters of the subgenus [[Catonotus]] (family [[Percidae]]) in the Cumberland River drainage. The [[Barcheek Darter]], [[Etheostoma obeyense]] Kirsch 1892, had long been treated as a single species, but the authors show that it comprises two morphologically similar species. *E. obeyense* occupies tributaries of the middle [[Cumberland River]], while the newly described [[Slabrock Darter]], [[Etheostoma smithi]], occupies tributaries of the lower Cumberland and lower [[Tennessee River]]. The species are separated by infraorbital canal pore counts, pored lateral line scale counts, dorsal fin ray modes and preorbital length to body depth ratios.

## Background & Description

**Scope and methods.** The study examined meristic counts (lateral scales, pored lateral line scales, fin rays and spines, head canal pores), pigmentation, squamation and about 29 morphometric ratios. Counts followed [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1964), with head canal pores counted on the left side only. Samples were compared by sex, size class, river system and longitude using one-way analysis of variance, with significance set at α = 0.005 for most tests.

**Etheostoma smithi (new species).** The holotype is an adult male of 51.7 mm SL, collected 26 April 1975 from Ferguson Creek, Livingston County, Kentucky. The type series includes 121 paratopotypes, distributed among eight institutions. The material examined spans numerous Cumberland and Tennessee River tributaries. The species is named for Dr. Philip W. Smith of the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]]. Its common name reflects its preference for slabrock habitat.

Diagnostic features include:
- Infraorbital canal with three pores anteriorly and one posteriorly (99% of specimens).
- 10 (rarely nine) preoperculomandibular pores.
- Usually 13 or fewer pored lateral line scales in adults over 35 mm SL.
- Usually 14 dorsal fin rays.
- Bicolored cheek bar, with red and blue pigments on fins and head.

Maximum size is 52 mm SL. Mean PreOL/BD is 0.39.

**Etheostoma obeyense.** Described by [[Kirsch]] (1892) from 210 specimens in four Clinton County, Kentucky tributaries. The authors examined type material and a large series of museum specimens. Diagnostic features include four anterior and usually two posterior infraorbital canal pores, 12 or more pored lateral line scales in adults, and usually 13 dorsal fin rays. It reaches 70 mm SL, and its mean PreOL/BD is 0.44.

**Sexual dimorphism.** Males of *E. smithi* have more dorsal fin rays, a smaller BD/AL ratio and a larger D2FL/QL ratio. In *E. obeyense*, males have smaller PreDL/SL and BD/AL ratios and a larger D2FL/QL ratio.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** *E. smithi* is known from the lower Cumberland River, from near its mouth in western Kentucky to near the Caney Fork system in north-central Tennessee. It also occurs in two lower Tennessee River tributaries, Whiteoak Creek and Hurricane Creek. A large hiatus in its Cumberland distribution extends from Cumberland City to near Nashville. A November 1975 collecting trip to this area failed to find the species. *E. obeyense* is restricted to the middle Cumberland, from the Obey River system east to tributaries of the Big South Fork. The two species are allopatric but share the Cumberland system.

**Habitat.** *E. smithi* inhabits shallow slabrock pools in streams. Its populations are usually small, and collecting is difficult. About 900 specimens were taken from the type locality. *E. obeyense* was most common in bedrock pools, where crevices serve as hiding places, and in slabrock pools in small streams. Earlier reports placed it in pools of 2nd to 4th order streams and over sand in shallow water.

**Geographic variation.** Variation in *E. smithi* shows no clinal pattern. Eight dorsal fin spines occur in 10 of 12 Tennessee River specimens, while 56% of Cumberland specimens have nine. The Stones River sample is distinctive in pigmentation, saddle prominence, and reduced fin ray counts. Richland Creek samples have low pored lateral line and lateral scale counts. In *E. obeyense*, geographic variation is greater but also lacks a consistent pattern.

**Relationships.** No intergradation or east-west cline linking the two species was found. The authors infer that *E. smithi* is derived from *E. obeyense*, based on reduced lateral line pores and smaller maximum size. The closest relatives are the other barcheek darters, [[Etheostoma virgatum]] and [[Etheostoma barbouri]], followed by the nonbarcheek *Catonotus* species *E. kennicotti* and *E. flabellare*.

## Key Findings

- *Etheostoma obeyense* is two species. The earlier single concept included *E. smithi*, which was misidentified in studies by Kuehne and Small (1971) and Page (1975b).
- *E. smithi* is diagnosed by three anterior and one posterior infraorbital canal pores, 10 preoperculomandibular pores, ≤13 pored lateral line scales in adults, and modally 14 dorsal fin rays.
- *E. obeyense* is diagnosed by four anterior and usually two posterior infraorbital canal pores, ≥12 pored lateral line scales in adults, and modally 13 dorsal fin rays.
- Mean PreOL/BD is 0.39 in *E. smithi* versus 0.44 in *E. obeyense*. Preorbital length is significantly shorter in *E. smithi* (α = 0.005).
- Both species show sexual dimorphism in the D2FL/QL ratio. Ontogenetic changes in pored lateral line scale counts and relative head length are shared by all three *Catonotus* species examined.
- Records from the Cumberland and Tennessee systems confirm a limited, fragmented range for *E. smithi*. Its Tennessee presence rests on only two tributaries.
- The authors propose that *E. smithi* is a derivative of *E. obeyense*, with differentiation likely occurring in relatively recent time.
