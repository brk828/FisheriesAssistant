# Growth of Blue Catfish in the Tombigbee River of Alabama

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Ictaluridae #age-growth-studies #population-assessment

## Executive Summary

This study examines the growth, age composition, and mortality rates of [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*) in the upper [[Tombigbee River]] of western Alabama using data collected August 5, 1965. Analysis of 196 fish for length-weight relationships and 125 spine samples for age determination revealed a well-represented population of year classes prior to 1964, with notable absence of 1964-1965 year classes. The population exhibits relatively constant annual growth rates, with weight increments increasing rapidly after fish reach 25 inches total length. Mortality analysis indicates an under-fished population with 39% annual mortality rate.

## Background & Description

[[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*) is one of the most important commercial freshwater fish species in southern waters. The species ranges throughout the [[Mississippi Valley]], north to Canada and south through Eastern Mexico to the Yucatan Peninsula. Its life history is similar to [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), with the distinction that blue catfish prefer larger rivers.

In Alabama, the [[Alabama River]] and [[Black Warrior River]] systems support large populations of blue catfish. Previous work by Swingle (1953) documented E-values (percentage of species weight in total population) of 46.1% for shallow water areas and 48.6% for deep water areas in the Tombigbee River, indicating the species' dominance in this system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area encompassed the [[Tombigbee River]] from Cocharane (Pickens County) to one mile below its junction with the [[Sipsey River]] (Greene County), Alabama. The river drains 5,990 square miles of gray and grayish-brown sandy loam soils in nearly level terrain.

**Hydrological Characteristics:**
- Annual mean discharge: 7,590 cfs
- Maximum discharge: 108,000 cfs
- Minimum discharge: 271 cfs
- High flow period: January through April (usually exceeding 12,000 cfs with surrounding lowlands inundated)
- Low flow period: August, September, and October

**Climate:**
- Largely subtropical
- Average annual precipitation: 52 inches
- Annual average temperature: 64.2°F
- Temperature extremes: -5°F to 105°F

## Key Findings

**Age Composition and Recruitment:**
- 196 fish examined for length-weight data
- 125 spine samples collected for age determination
- 3 spines rejected due to regeneration
- 1964 year class represented by only one specimen
- No fish from 1965 year class captured
- Year classes prior to 1964 well represented
- Age class 0 (young-of-the-year) absent from population at sampling time

**Growth Rates:**
The study employed spine sectioning techniques following Sneed (1951) methodology. Back-calculation correction factor (a) = 1.96 inches. Average calculated total lengths at each age:

| Age | I | II | III | IV | V | VI | VII | VIII | IX | XIV |
|-----|---|----|----|----|----|----|----|------|----|----|
| Length (in) | 4.9 | 8.7 | 13.3 | 17.7 | 20.9 | 24.1 | 27.3 | 31.6 | 37.1 | 42.0 |
| Annual Increment | 4.9 | 3.8 | 4.6 | 4.4 | 3.2 | 3.2 | 3.2 | 4.3 | 5.5 | 1.0 |

Fish grew at relatively constant rates throughout their lifespan. Growth rates in the Tombigbee River were approximately half those observed in the [[Mississippi River Delta]] but closely approximated growth rates in [[Kentucky Lake]], Tennessee.

**Length-Weight Relationship:**
Multiple regression equations were tested. The cubic equation of total length on weight provided the best fit:

Y = 0.152299 - 0.013402X² + 0.000706X³ - 0.5834

Where Y = weight in pounds and X = total length in inches.

The relationship indicates weight increments increase rapidly after fish reach 25 inches total length, suggesting accelerated biomass accumulation in larger individuals.

**Mortality Analysis:**
- Chi-square test determined age group III as first completely recruited year class
- Survival rate (s): 0.64 (Heincke method) and 0.61 (Robson-Chapman method)
- Annual mortality rate (a): 39% (Robson-Chapman method)
- Catch curve analysis revealed rounded, sloping right limb indicating an under-fished population
- Fishing mortality versus natural mortality could not be separated due to lack of catch records

**Population Structure:**
Statistical analysis of age groups showed:
- Decreasing coefficient of variation with age
- Increasing standard deviation with age
- Age groups becoming less skewed and more normally distributed with increasing age

**Management Implications:**
The absence of 1964-1965 year classes raises concerns about recruitment, though the author could not determine the cause from available data. The under-fished status suggests potential for increased harvest without population detriment. This study represents the first step in obtaining information needed for management of blue catfish in the Tombigbee River system.
