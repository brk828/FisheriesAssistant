## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (Bruce R. Schmidt, Chief of Fisheries) to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Region 6 Director, transmitting a formal proposal to introduce **[[Rainbow Smelt]]** (*Osmerus mordax*) into **[[Lake Powell]]** as a forage fish. The stated goal is to remedy a critical forage shortage affecting the reservoir's **[[Striped Bass]]** (*Morone saxatilis*) sport fishery, which had declined from its previously popular status due to problems with the existing **[[Shad]]** forage base. The letter does not present primary morphological or ecological data itself, but documents the interagency review process—via the **[[Colorado River Wildlife Council]]** (CREWC) and its Technical Committee—and raises the central management question of whether smelt introduction poses a risk to endangered native fishes, particularly those under review by the **[[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Team]]**.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The letter is a policy/administrative communication rather than a taxonomic or physiological study. Species referenced (Rainbow Smelt, Striped Bass, Colorado Squawfish/Pikeminnow) are named only in the context of management and food-web roles, not described physically.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Lake Powell]]** is characterized as a large reservoir system within the Colorado River Basin supporting an intensive recreational fishery, with visitor use reportedly exceeding that of Yellowstone National Park.
- The reservoir's forage base, historically dependent on **[[Shad]]** populations, had become critically deficient, undermining the predatory **[[Striped Bass]]** sport fishery.
- **[[Rainbow Smelt]]** were proposed as a supplemental forage species to fill this trophic gap and restore prey availability for striped bass.
- The proposal explicitly acknowledges potential ecological risk: introduced smelt could interact with or threaten **[[Endangered Fishes of the Colorado River Basin]]**, including species managed under the **[[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Team]]** (i.e., *Ptychocheilus lucius*, Colorado pikeminnow/squawfish).
- The **[[Colorado River Wildlife Council]]**, composed of the seven state wildlife agencies within the Colorado Basin, was engaged as an interagency body with expertise spanning both sport fish and native fish management to vet ecological risk prior to broader USFWS review.

## Key Findings
- A proposal for introducing **[[Rainbow Smelt]]** into **[[Lake Powell]]** had been in development for approximately three years (as of 1989) as the most feasible solution to the reservoir's forage shortage.
- The **[[Colorado River Wildlife Council]]** Technical Committee recommended acceptance of the proposal in spring 1989; the full Council reviewed it in July 1989 and directed staff to engage other involved agencies (including USFWS).
- The Utah Division of Wildlife Resources requested that USFWS Region 6, and specifically the **[[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Team]]**, provide technical review to determine whether rainbow smelt introduction poses a likely threat to endangered native fishes in the Colorado River system.
- A stated dual management objective was articulated: (1) improve forage availability to support the **[[Striped Bass]]** sport fishery, and (2) ensure no likely decline in native/endangered fish species as a result of the introduction.
- A meeting was scheduled for September 29, 1989, in Salt Lake City to present background and rationale for the proposal to USFWS staff, marking a formal step toward interagency technical review rather than a final decision.
- The letter underscores the socioeconomic importance of the Lake Powell sport fishery (citing visitor numbers exceeding Yellowstone National Park) as a driver for pursuing forage-base intervention, while simultaneously recognizing the conservation value of native Colorado River fishes.
