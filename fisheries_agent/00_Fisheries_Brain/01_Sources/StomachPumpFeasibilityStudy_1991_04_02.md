## Executive Summary
This document is an internal administrative memorandum from BIO/WEST, Inc. (Rich Valdez, Principal Investigator for Mainstem [[Humpback Chub]] Studies) rather than a primary data report. It announces the suspension of a planned **Stomach Pump Feasibility Study**, which had been scheduled for April 10–15, 1991 at the **Dexter National Fish Hatchery**. The stated reason for the hold is concern raised over the use of hatchery-reared **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*) as test subjects for the invasive sampling methodology. No morphological, dietary, or ecological data were collected or reported in this document; it is purely a project-management communication distributed to a wide network of federal, state, and academic collaborators involved in Colorado River native fish conservation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Study Species Context**: The intended subjects were captive **bonytail** (*Gila elegans*) held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico, a facility used for conservation propagation of endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **Broader Program**: The feasibility study was nested within a larger **Mainstem Humpback Chub Studies** research program, indicating the ultimate goal was to validate a non-lethal dietary sampling technique (gastric/stomach lavage) for use on wild [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the Colorado River mainstem, likely within the Grand Canyon reach (implied by GCES — Glen Canyon Environmental Studies — recipients).
- **Habitat Note**: No wild habitat data are given; the only setting referenced is the artificial hatchery pond environment at Dexter, contrasted implicitly with the natural riverine habitat of the target wild populations.

## Key Findings
- The **Stomach Pump Feasibility Study**, planned for April 10–15, 1991 at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], was placed **on hold** prior to execution.
- The hold was triggered by **concerns over using bonytail** — a federally protected, endangered species — as subjects for testing an invasive/semi-invasive stomach-pumping (gastric lavage) methodology.
- The author (Rich Valdez) indicated that further guidance would follow **consultation with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service**, suggesting regulatory or animal-welfare compliance issues under the Endangered Species Act framework.
- The memo's broad distribution list (BOR, GCES, ASU, FWS, AGF, CDOW personnel) indicates this was a **multi-agency collaborative research effort** on [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]], with stomach content sampling intended to inform diet studies supporting management of [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail]] populations.
- **No feasibility data, results, or methodological validation** were produced or reported as a result of this cancellation — the study's outcome remains an open question in this record.
