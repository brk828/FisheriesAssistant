## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 research proposal (memo + draft study plan) from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Arizona Fishery Resources Office, submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program. The proposal addresses a methodological gap: during GCES Phase II, habitat evaluation of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) used FWS-specific methods, producing a habitat "model" for the species. However, mainstem Colorado River and tributary-confluence habitat surveys conducted separately by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGF) and Biowest used incompatible methodologies. The proposal calls for superimposing FWS's LCR-based habitat sampling protocol onto AGF's FY96 interim monitoring trips in order to generate a directly comparable mainstem/tributary-confluence dataset. The ultimate ecological goal is to assess whether mainstem habitat in Grand Canyon could support a second, self-sustaining spawning population of humpback chub, as mandated by the Glen Canyon Dam Biological Opinion Reasonable and Prudent Alternative.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary population stronghold**: The largest known [[Humpback Chub]] metapopulation resides in the lower 14 km of the [[Little Colorado River]] (Kaeding and Zimmerman, 1983), estimated at **4,000–6,000 subadult-adult fish** (Douglas, 1992).
- **Life history stages studied**: Habitat use patterns were characterized in the LCR for post-larval young-of-year, juvenile, subadult, adult, and spawning adult humpback chub (Gorman, 1994).
- **Candidate secondary habitats**:
  - [[Havasu Creek]] was tentatively identified as the most suitable tributary for establishing a secondary spawning population, though its carrying capacity is much smaller — estimated at **several hundred adult fish** within the 4–5 km reach below [[Beaver Falls]] inside Grand Canyon National Park boundaries.
  - The **mainstem Colorado River** (from the [[Paria River]] to [[Diamond Creek]]) was identified during GCES Phase II as a candidate site for a second spawning population and was formally incorporated into the Glen Canyon Dam Biological Opinion's Reasonable and Prudent Alternative.
- **Habitat types assessed**: backwaters, eddy complexes, and tributary mouths/confluences along the mainstem.
- **Sampling gear** used in interim monitoring: seines, trammel nets, hoopnets, and electrofishing gear.
- **Habitat measurement approach**: FWS employed a systematic grid-point method to characterize available habitat at sample locations, and separately measured habitat within grids where fish were actually captured, allowing fish abundance to be related to specific habitat characteristics and enabling detection of sampling bias.

## Key Findings

- A significant methodological discordance existed between FWS's LCR-derived habitat model and the mainstem/tributary-confluence habitat data collected by AGF and Biowest during GCES Phase II, precluding direct comparison.
- The proposal's core recommendation: apply FWS's LCR habitat evaluation methodology to Phase II mainstem and tributary confluence sample sites during FY96 interim monitoring (three 18-day mainstem trips in spring, summer, fall; 2–4 LCR confluence trips April–May) to create a comparable dataset.
- This unified dataset would permit (1) assessment of mainstem habitat suitability for various humpback chub life-history stages relative to the LCR model, and (2) retrospective synthesis of previously incompatible Phase II habitat data.
- No additional FY96 funding or logistic support was requested; the work was designed to be embedded within AGF's existing contracted monitoring effort in partnership with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
- Deliverables timeline: field trip reports throughout FY96; annual report to Bureau of Reclamation (BOR) and National Park Service (NPS) by 15 October 1996; data archived by 31 December 1996 (per GCES metadata standards); Final Report evaluating mainstem habitat suitability and methodology recommendations by 15 October 1997.
- The analysis was intended to directly inform adaptive management decisions regarding Glen Canyon Dam operations and the feasibility of establishing a second spawning population of [[Humpback Chub]] as required under the [[Glen Canyon Dam Biological Opinion]].
