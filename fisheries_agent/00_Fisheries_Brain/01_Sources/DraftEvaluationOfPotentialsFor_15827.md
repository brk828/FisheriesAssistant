# Proposal for Field Ecology Summer Program at Sierra Ancha Field Station

#ichthyology #marine-biology #field-ecology #conservation-biology #education #aquatic-entomology

## Executive Summary

This 1994 memorandum from [[W. L. Minckley]] to [[Arizona State University]] administration proposes establishing a formal summer field ecology program at the [[Sierra Ancha Field Station]]. The proposal addresses nationwide reductions in field training opportunities during the 1970s and responds to renewed public interest in ecology and conservation. The program would offer upper-division and graduate courses in ecology, utilizing the station's facilities at 5,600 feet elevation within [[Tonto National Forest]], approximately 140 miles from ASU's Tempe campus. The station provides access to diverse biological communities ranging from [[Upper Sonoran Desert]] to montane coniferous forests, with capacity for 36 students plus faculty and support staff.

## Background & Description

The [[Sierra Ancha Field Station]] was constructed by the [[U.S. Forest Service]] in the 1930s as a watershed research base. Facilities include four cabins (accommodating 6-16 persons each), a dormitory (12-16 persons), mess hall, and laboratory/classroom (maximum 18 students). The station provides cots, mattresses, propane appliances, and basic utilities including an approved water supply and modern waste disposal. Rates in 1993 were $9.50 per night for ASU affiliates and $12.00 for non-affiliates. The location offers proximity to [[Salt River Wilderness]] areas and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]'s Three-Bar Wildlife Management Area, with biological communities spanning elevations from less than 3,000 feet to greater than 7,000 feet.

Historical usage (1988-93) totaled 7,797 person-nights, with ASU academic units accounting for 3,061 person-nights, other educational institutions 1,645, and non-profit groups 2,716. The station has hosted classes in biology, botany, zoology, engineering, mathematics, creative writing, and history.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal emphasizes Arizona's diverse landscapes under federal and state jurisdiction as a "vast and dynamic stage" for studying natural ecosystems and human interactions. The station's location provides access to multiple habitat types within 10 miles, enabling comprehensive field studies across ecological gradients. The document notes that "only remnants of original conditions remain" due to rapid environmental change, making field-based education in remote locations increasingly valuable for providing historical perspective.

The 1990s context shows rising demand for ecology education: graduate applications in ecology/behavior increased from 60 (1990/91) to 108 (1993/94), while [[Conservation Biology]] majors grew from 56 to 96 students. A multidisciplinary Ecology Concentration already existed with 44 faculty across 11 academic units, with nearly half committed to the proposed program.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure**: The proposal recommends offering 12 credit hours per session, comprising two field classes (12 students each) and two lecture/laboratory classes (up to 18 students each). Students must enroll for six credit hours through various combinations. Optimal enrollment of 36 students would generate $43,020 in revenue (tuition at $95/credit hour, room and board at $15/day for 35 days, laboratory fees at $20/week).

**Innovative Staffing Model**: Faculty compensation proposed at "honorarium rate" of $500 per lecture period (day) plus travel, housing, and subsistence, rather than conventional summer session rates. This approach would "circumvent potential monetary problems during initiation" and allow maximum staffing flexibility. Four teaching assistants per session would receive half-time compensation for 60 contact hours plus general station assistance.

**Course Offerings**: Immediately available courses include:

- **Field Classes**: [[Techniques in Natural Resource Management]] (Zol 410), [[Field Zoology]] (Zol 420), [[Research Techniques in Animal Behavior]] (Zol 431), [[Intro. Ecol. Lab.]] (Bio 321), [[Quantitative Ecology]] (Bio 526)

- **Lecture/Laboratory with Field Trips**: [[Entomology]] (Zol 354), [[Population & Community Ecology]] (Zol 423), [[Animal Ecology]] (Zol 425), [[Aquatic Insects]], [[Ornithology]] (Zol 471), [[Mammalogy]] (Zol 472), [[Ichthyology]] (Zol 473), [[Herpetology]] (Zol 474), [[Natural History of Arizona]] (Bio 300), [[Biology and Conservation of Wildlife Resources]] (Bio 411), [[Biology and Management of Aquatic Resources]] (Bio 413)

**Financial Projections**: At maximum enrollment (36 students), estimated expenses total $43,425 with deficit of -$405. At minimum enrollment (24 students), revenue drops to $28,680 with deficit of -$11,190, requiring subsidies for administration and operations. High-side estimates include cook ($500/week), assistant ($250/week), vehicle leases (4 at $300/week), and subsistence ($4.00/day/person).

**Detailed Syllabi**: The proposal includes complete syllabi for multiple courses:

- **[[Herpetology]]** (Dr. Thomas R. Jones): Covers systematics, biogeography, morphology, ecology of amphibians and reptiles with emphasis on Arizona species. Assessment includes unit exam (100 points), laboratory practical (100 points), and independent research project (200 points).

- **[[Advanced Limnology]]** ([[Plankton Ecology]]): Five-week course emphasizing taxonomy, ecology, and evolutionary biology of freshwater plankton, particularly [[phytoplankton]] and [[crustacean zooplankton]]. Includes quantitative/qualitative sampling, taxonomic identification, and experimental field approaches.

- **[[Ornithology]]**: Five-week course covering evolutionary origin, major families, structure/function, and annual cycles of birds. Field projects focus on birds of chaparral, pine/oak woodland, [[Sonoran Desert]], and montane coniferous forests.

- **[[Aquatic Entomology]]**: Five-week course covering aquatic habitats, life history, functional morphology, systematics and ecology of aquatic insects. Requires alcoholic collection of 50 correctly identified specimens (generally to genus level) representing seven primary aquatic insect orders, both larval and adult stages.

- **[[Mammalogy]]**: Covers systematics, morphology, evolution, physiology, ecology, and ethology of mammals. Laboratory includes sampling techniques, identification, skull/skeleton work, and field projects. Grading: lecture exams (350 points), lab practicals and field projects (300 points).

**Strategic Alignment**: The program addresses four ASU Strategic Planning Committee directions (May 1994): improving undergraduate education, improving graduate education and research, nurturing multidisciplinary strengths, and shaping enrollment profiles. The proposal notes compatibility with existing undergraduate initiatives (Hughes, MARC & ECOREU programs) and plans for an [[Arizona Museum of Natural History and Culture]], comparable to the [[American Museum of Natural History]]'s [[Southwestern Research Station]] at Portal, Arizona.

**Implementation Timeline**: If approved, the program must start early summer 1994 for summer 1995 offering. Required positions include part-time Director and Administrative Assistant for planning, staffing, advertisement, and enrollment during the academic year, ideally through released time for current faculty/staff with program funding.
