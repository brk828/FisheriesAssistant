# Suitability of Reclaimed Wastewater for a Sport Fishery: Channel Catfish, Rainbow Trout and Tilapia Hybrids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae #salmonidae #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This thesis, from a Department of Biological Sciences, evaluated whether [[Reclaimed Wastewater]] treated by [[Sand Filtration]] (tertiary treatment) could support a [[Sport Fishery]]. Three fish were tested: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Tilapia Hybrids]]. The study aimed to establish survival, growth, stocking schedules and maximum stocking densities in an unusually productive environment. Survival was below 1% in five trout experiments and one catfish experiment, mainly because of low sunrise dissolved oxygen caused by dense phytoplankton blooms. Where fish survived, production was high. The author concluded that the water, though highly productive, cannot be expected to support a dependable fishery because unfavorable oxygen conditions are likely to recur.

## Background & Description

The title page is partly illegible in the source scan, and the thesis is available only as its abstract. The study investigated:

- **Water source:** domestic wastewater reclaimed by sand filtration.
- **Species:** Channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*), rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdneri*) and tilapia hybrids. The abstract does not specify the tilapia parental species.
- **Objectives:** survival rates, growth rates, stocking schedules and maximum stocking densities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Oxygen:** Low sunrise [[Dissolved Oxygen]] levels, caused by respiration of dense [[Phytoplankton Blooms]], were the main cause of mortality.
- **Nutrients:** Average inflow [[Orthophosphate]] concentration was high, at 2 mg/l, and helped stimulate the blooms.
- **Food web:** [[Chironomus Larvae]] were the predominant food organism. They made up 90% of an estimated annual [[Benthic Production]] of 1,160 kg per hectare. [[Zooplankton]] were abundant but were not an important fish food because of their small size.
- **Primary productivity:** Phytoplankton productivity averaged 10.2 gm O2 per m3 per day.

## Key Findings

- **Survival:** Less than 1% survival occurred in five trout experiments and one catfish experiment.
- **Mortality driver:** Low sunrise oxygen tensions, from phytoplankton respiration, were an important cause of death. Phytoplankton growth was partly fuelled by high inflow orthophosphate (average 2 mg/l).
- **Production when fish survived:** Total yields of acceptable Channel catfish and Tilapia were 383 and 397 kg per hectare, respectively.
- **Food base:** Chironomus larvae comprised 90% of an estimated annual benthic production of 1,160 kg per hectare. Zooplankton, despite their abundance, were not a major food source because of their small size.
- **Productivity:** Average phytoplankton productivity was 10.2 gm O2 per m3 per day.
- **Conclusion:** Although highly productive, these waters cannot be expected to support a dependable fishery, because unfavorable oxygen conditions are likely to recur.
