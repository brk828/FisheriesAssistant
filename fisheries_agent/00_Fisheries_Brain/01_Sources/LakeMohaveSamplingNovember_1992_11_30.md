# Lake Mohave Sampling Report — 22-28 November 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #freshwater-fish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
A collaborative 7-day trammel-net survey of [[Lake Mohave]] was conducted by Arizona State University, Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to monitor fish community composition and assess the status of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] population. Six trammel nets were fished for 24-hour periods at depths of 1-6 m along shoreline and point habitats from Cottonwood Cove to Carp Cove. A total of 258 fish across eight species were captured, with [[Striped Bass]] and [[Common Carp]] dominating the catch. Twenty-seven adult Razorback Suckers were processed (measured, weighed, PIT tagged) as part of ongoing long-term population monitoring, with plans for future larval/embryo translocation to establish a refuge population in Yuma Cove.

## Background & Description

### Razorback Sucker (n = 27 adults)
| Sex | n | Mean TL (cm) ± SD | TL Range (cm) | Mean Weight (g) ± SD | Weight Range (g) |
|-----|---|--------------------|----------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Male | 8 | 56.2 ± 2.4 | 53.0–60.0 | 2531 ± 574 | 1750–3500 |
| Female | 19 | 61.6 ± 3.5 | 55.7–66.6 | 3005 ± 503 | 2000–3800 |

- Females were significantly larger in both length and weight than males, consistent with known sexual dimorphism in [[Razorback Sucker]].
- Six of eight males exhibited nuptial tuberculation; two of these were determined to be reproductively "ripe" (spawning-ready).
- No females captured were in ripe reproductive condition, suggesting sampling occurred prior to peak female spawning readiness.

### Striped Bass
- Most individuals captured were approximately 30 cm TL, representing a strong smaller size class.
- A subset of larger specimens reached up to 80 cm TL.
- Stomach content analysis of larger individuals revealed exclusive predation on [[Rainbow Trout]], indicating significant piscivory on stocked salmonids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat sampled**: Littoral shoreline and point habitats at 1-6 m depth, spanning from northern Cottonwood Cove to Carp Cove within [[Lake Mohave]], a mainstem Colorado River reservoir.
- **Community structure**: The catch included both native (Razorback Sucker) and non-native/introduced species (Striped Bass, Carp, Channel Catfish, Rainbow Trout, Largemouth Bass, Bluegill, Green Sunfish), reflecting a heavily altered reservoir fish assemblage typical of the lower Colorado River system.
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: Large Striped Bass functioning as apex piscivores, preying on stocked Rainbow Trout — indicating potential competitive/predatory pressure on other fish species including juvenile native fishes.
- **Conservation context**: The presence of PIT- and Floy-tagged recaptures indicates an active long-term monitoring/tagging program for [[Razorback Sucker]], a federally listed endangered species endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Management activity**: Plans noted for translocating razorback sucker embryos/larvae from Arizona Bay adults into the Yuma Cove backwater as a conservation/refuge strategy, under the Native Fish Work Group.

## Key Findings

- **Catch composition**: 258 total fish captured across 8 species over 60.12 units of net effort (CPE in fish/100 m²/24-hr).

| Species | Number | Percent | CPE |
|---------|--------|---------|-----|
| Rainbow Trout | 11 | 4.26% | 0.183 |
| Common Carp | 77 | 29.84% | 1.280 |
| Razorback Sucker | 27 | 10.47% | 0.449 |
| Channel Catfish | 20 | 7.75% | 0.333 |
| Largemouth Bass | 10 | 3.88% | 0.166 |
| Bluegill | 13 | 5.04% | 0.216 |
| Green Sunfish | 2 | 0.78% | 0.033 |
| Striped Bass | 98 | 37.98% | 1.630 |
| **Totals** | **258** | **100.00%** | **4.290** |

- **Striped Bass dominance**: Striped Bass comprised 37.98% of total catch (98 individuals), notably more abundant than in previous sampling efforts — a potential concern for native fish conservation given documented predation on other reservoir fish.
- **Razorback Sucker recaptures**: Of 27 adults captured, 4 were long-term recaptures from prior tagging efforts (2 PIT-tagged, 2 Floy-tagged), and 1 individual was both captured and recaptured within the same sampling trip, demonstrating repeat catchability and effective tag retention.
- **Tagging protocol**: All newly captured Razorback Suckers lacking tags were scanned, PIT tagged, and released — consistent with standardized long-term population monitoring methodology for this species.
- **Future sampling**: A follow-up visit under the [[Native Fish Work Group]] was scheduled for February 1993 to conduct the Yuma Cove larval/embryo transfer, with the next full lake sampling planned for 13-20 March 1993, coordinated by USFWS (Parker, AZ office).
- **Data gap**: Determination of original long-term tagging/capture data for recaptured individuals was still in process at time of reporting.
