# Identification, Distribution and Incidence of the Razorback × Flannelmouth Sucker Hybrid in the Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #hybridization #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This paper identifies a previously misidentified hybrid catostomid from [[Lake Mohave]] as a cross between the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*). The author corrects earlier identifications that suggested hybridization with [[Utah Sucker]] or [[White Sucker]], and documents the widespread occurrence of this hybrid combination throughout the middle and upper [[Colorado River Basin]]. Morphological analysis indicates the [[Virgin River]] population of flannelmouth suckers is the most likely parental stock. The study reveals that hybridization incidence varies from 5-20% in upper river collections and up to 75% at some sites below [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. River modifications, particularly dam construction, appear to increase hybridization rates. The author warns that proposed reintroductions of razorback suckers may result in further hybridization with local catostomid populations, potentially threatening the genetic integrity of this endangered species.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is characterized by a pronounced nuchal (dorsal) keel, subterminal mouth, relatively broad caudal peduncle, typically 15-16 dorsal fin rays, and an average of 77 lateral line scales in [[Lake Mohave]] populations. The [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) exhibits regional morphological variation, with the [[Virgin River]] population distinguished by a narrow caudal peduncle, approximately 15 dorsal fin rays (higher than other populations at 10.9-12.5), and lower lateral line scale counts than other populations.

Hybrids between these species display intermediate morphology: narrower caudal peduncles than razorback suckers, 15-16 dorsal fin rays, and slightly elevated lateral line scale counts. Two distinct hybrid morphs occur in [[Lake Mohave]], possibly representing reciprocal crosses. One morph resembles a razorback sucker with a poorly developed keel; the other appears more similar to flannelmouth sucker but possesses a moderately well-developed keel. The nuchal keel alone is unreliable for identification, as some hybrids exhibit pronounced keels. Distinctive labial and oral morphologies provide the most reliable diagnostic characters.

The author reinterprets a 1952 photograph by Douglas of a supposed "razorback sucker" from [[Lake Havasu]] as a hybrid, based on flannelmouth-typical snout, distinctly inferior mouth, shallow posterior body, and 86-87 lateral line scales (above razorback mean, near hybrid mean of 85.25).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River Basin]] has undergone extensive modification through dam construction, including [[Hoover Dam]] (creating [[Lake Mohave]]), [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (creating [[Lake Powell]]), [[Davis Dam]] (above [[Lake Havasu]]), and [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. These impoundments have dramatically altered riverine habitats and fish community dynamics.

Prior to impoundment, razorback suckers were common in the Colorado River below Boulder Dam, while flannelmouth suckers were probably present but uncommon. Following impoundment of [[Lake Mohave]], hybridization likely occurred in the first several years, then apparently ceased with the extirpation of flannelmouth suckers from the lake. Only one flannelmouth sucker specimen has ever been collected from Lake Mohave post-impoundment.

In the upper [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], and [[Yampa River]] systems, both parental species persist, and hybrids are present and often common. In tributaries where razorback suckers are rare or absent (San Juan, Gunnison, San Rafael, Dolores rivers), flannelmouth suckers remain abundant, but hybrids have not yet been collected.

The razorback sucker has been extirpated from much of its historic range. Predation upon young fish by non-native species is suggested as the major factor preventing recruitment. The species' life history appears incompatible with current reservoir and modified river conditions.

## Key Findings

**Hybrid Identification and Distribution:**
- Hybrids previously identified as razorback × Utah sucker or razorback × white sucker combinations are correctly identified as razorback × flannelmouth sucker hybrids
- Morphological intermediacy and comparison with Hubbs and Miller (1953) descriptions confirm identification
- [[Virgin River]] flannelmouth sucker population identified as most likely parental stock based on morphological concordance
- Hybrids occur throughout middle and upper Colorado River where razorback suckers are now found
- Earliest hybrid record: Jordan's 1891 description of *Xyrauchen uncompahgre* from [[Uncompahgre River]], Colorado

**Hybridization Incidence:**
- Upper river (Green, Yampa, upper Colorado): 5-20% incidence (hybrids/[hybrids + razorback suckers])
- One collection reported 45% incidence
- Below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] at Colorado-Paria confluence: 75% incidence (3 hybrids, 1 razorback sucker collected; 2 additional razorbacks observed)
- [[Lake Mohave]] upper riverine portion: 10% incidence
- [[Lake Mohave]] lacustrine portions: 0-5% locally, approximately 1% overall
- [[White River]]: 1 hybrid specimen, 0 razorback suckers collected

**Temporal Patterns:**
- Evidence suggests increased hybridization prior to 1950 as indirect result of early river modifications
- Hybridization most intense in mid- to late 1960s following major impoundments (Lake Powell, Flaming Gorge Reservoir)
- Abundance of hybrids declined in recent collections, though this may reflect sampling differences
- [[Lake Mohave]] hybrids likely all very old; greatest incidence probably occurred immediately post-impoundment

**Management Implications:**
- Reintroduction efforts involve larval and fingerling razorback suckers
- Predation by non-native fishes observed on reintroduced fish
- Majority of reintroduced razorback suckers unlikely to survive to reproductive age
- This creates situations with small numbers of adult razorback suckers among abundant catostomid populations
- High probability of hybridization at reintroduction sites containing catostomids, especially *Catostomus* species
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Sonoran Sucker]] present throughout historic razorback sucker range; both known to hybridize with razorback sucker
- Reintroduction sites should be very carefully chosen to minimize hybridization risk
- 1976 translocation: 600 flannelmouth suckers collected from Colorado River near Paria River for release below [[Parker Dam]]; photographs show hybrids present in this collection

**Species Interactions:**
- Razorback × flannelmouth hybrid not collected below [[Lake Havasu]]
- Razorback × [[Sonoran Sucker]] hybrids collected in [[Salt River]] drainage system
- Introgressive hybridization may be occurring in [[Lake Mohave]]
- Growing scarcity of razorback sucker, abundance of flannelmouth sucker, and continued river modifications conducive to continued and possibly increased hybridization
