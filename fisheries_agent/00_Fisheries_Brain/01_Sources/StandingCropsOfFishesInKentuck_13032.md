# Standing Crops of Fishes in Kentucky Farm Ponds

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #centrarchidae #fisheries-management #limnology

## Executive Summary

This study by William R. Turner (Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources) quantified standing fish crops in 22 unfertilized Kentucky farm ponds (1956–1958) dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*). Using a mark-recapture technique (fin-clipping followed by total rotenone eradication), the study calculated standing crops ranging from 73 to 770 lbs/acre (average 385 lbs/acre). Ponds were categorized per [[Swingle 1950]] criteria as either "balanced" (9 ponds, sustainable harvestable yields) or "unbalanced" (13 ponds, stunted/overcrowded populations). Water chemistry (total alkalinity) and soil fertility (available phosphorus and potassium in the watershed) were strongly correlated with total standing crop biomass, highlighting the role of terrestrial nutrient runoff (e.g., pasture/livestock drainage) in freshwater pond productivity.

## Background & Description

While the paper is primarily population-focused rather than morphological, it notes physical changes relevant to sampling methodology:
- **Marking Technique:** Fish were marked by excising a small portion of the lower lobe of the caudal fin—sufficient to be distinguishable but not enough to impede locomotion.
- **Post-mortem Changes:** Citing [[Krumholz 1950]] and [[Larimore 1952]], the study notes that fish weights can change drastically after death, necessitating the use of Day-1 recovery weights as standardized representative weights for fish recovered on subsequent days.
- **Size Class Distinction:** Populations were differentiated by "harvestable-sized" fish versus stunted individuals, particularly evident in [[Bluegill]] populations where unbalanced ponds showed high biomass but low harvestable percentage (indicating small, stunted individuals).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Parameters:**
- **Pond Size:** 0.45 to 1.56 acres (average 0.96 acre).
- **Water Chemistry:** Total alkalinity ranged from 12 to 103 p.p.m.; pH ranged from 6.8 to 9.0.
- **Soil/Watershed Correlation:** Standing crop biomass was closely tied to watershed land use.
    - **Pasture/Barnyard drainage:** Highest productivity due to phosphorus/potassium leaching from manure (indirect fertilization).
    - **Woodland drainage:** Lowest productivity; phosphorus compounds are "bound" by higher plants, reducing bioavailability to the aquatic system.

**Correlations with Standing Crop:**

| Variable | Correlation Coefficient (r) |
|---|---|
| Total Alkalinity | 0.67 |
| Soil Available Phosphorus | 0.72 |
| Soil Available Potassium | 0.44 |

**Species Interactions:**
- [[Bluegill]] numerically and biomass-dominated nearly every pond studied, regardless of balance status.
- [[Green Sunfish]] acted as a significant "forage overcrowder" in unbalanced systems, occurring in 9/13 unbalanced ponds with high standing crop (69 lbs/acre) but negligible harvestable yield (5%), suggesting it out-competes or displaces balanced [[Largemouth Bass]]-[[Bluegill]] dynamics when present in excess.
- Regional trends: Ponds in limestone-based soil regions (Bluegrass, Outer Bluegrass, Pennyroyal) generally exhibited higher standing crops than sandstone/shaly-sandstone regions (Eastern/Western Coalfields), though high intra-regional variability precluded strict geographic generalization (see pond [[WC-2]] outlier).

## Key Findings

1. **Overall Standing Crop:** Average standing crop across 22 ponds was **385 lbs/acre** (range: 73–770 lbs/acre).
2. **Balanced vs. Unbalanced Populations:**
    - **Balanced (9 ponds):** Average crop of 282 lbs/acre; 48% (130 lbs/acre) was harvestable-sized. Average [[F/C Ratio]] (Forage/Carnivore) was 4.6 (range 1.6–8.0).
    - **Unbalanced (13 ponds):** Average crop of 456 lbs/acre; only 15% (70 lbs/acre) was harvestable-sized. Average F/C ratio was 16.6 (range 4.1–37.7), indicating severe overcrowding of forage fish relative to predators.
3. **Species-Specific Crops:**
    - **[[Largemouth Bass]]:** Average 43 lbs/acre overall. Notably, unbalanced populations had a *higher percentage* of harvestable-sized bass (79%) than balanced populations (67%), attributed to a small number of large adult "trophy" fish with poor recruitment of young in unbalanced systems.
    - **[[Bluegill]]:** Average 365 lbs/acre overall. Unbalanced populations held nearly double the bluegill biomass of balanced ponds (410 vs. 224 lbs/acre) but only 12% harvestable size (vs. 42% in balanced), confirming a stunted population structure.
4. **Rotenone Recovery Efficiency:** Average recovery of marked fish across the 3-day sampling window was 73% (Day 1: 20%, Day 2: 44%, Day 3: 9%), used as the basis for extrapolating total standing crop.
5. **Time Since Stocking:** Balanced populations averaged 5 years since stocking; unbalanced populations averaged 4.5 years, suggesting balance status is driven more by habitat/nutrient factors than time elapsed since introduction.
6. **Management Recommendation:** Artificial fertilization (phosphate/carbonate compounds) is recommended for ponds in woodland drainage areas lacking natural nutrient inputs from agricultural land use.
