# Hatchery Spawning and Larval Rearing Update: Spikedace, Loach Minnow, and Roundtail Chub (April 2010)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a short email thread (April 20–22, 2010) among hatchery staff and fisheries scientists about the progress of captive spawning and larval production at the "BP hatchery" in Arizona. The correspondents are [[David Ward]] (Arizona Game and Fish), [[Robert W. Clarkson]] (Bureau of Reclamation), [[Thomas E. Dowling]] (Arizona State University), and [[Tony Robinson]] and [[Bill Stewart]] (copied). The main news is that [[Spikedace]] larvae have been produced in the [[Artificial Streams]], while [[Loach Minnow]] and Eagle Creek [[Roundtail Chub]] have not yet produced larvae. The group discusses whether to try hormone-induced spawning for the roundtail and when fish will be released to the [[Blue River]]. The thread ends without a firm decision.

## Background & Description

The thread concerns fish being propagated for conservation. It covers three groups:

- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*), a small cyprinid. Larvae were found first in the artificial streams and later in the indoor tanks.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*), another small cyprinid. Staff hold broodstock in tanks, including a set labelled for the Blue River.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*), from the [[Eagle Creek]] population. Fish are held in tanks and in a larger pond downstream at the hatchery.

The email also mentions "Aravaipa fish" held with the spikedace in the indoor tanks. The document does not say which species this refers to.

Two systems are compared. The artificial streams recirculate water. The indoor tanks in the building do not, so their water temperature runs a few degrees colder. Staff link this to the slower spawning response in the tanks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** Ward states that tank water is "a few degrees colder" than the recirculating streams because it is not recirculated. He links this to the slower larval timing in the indoor tanks. No specific temperature values are given.
- **Release planning:** Dowling asks when fish are scheduled to go into the [[Blue River]] and whether this affects the spawning decision. The answer is not included in the surviving text.
- **Habitat management:** The thread mentions [[Rotenone Treatment]] and conditions in rocky banks. The text is garbled, so no details can be confirmed.
- **Pond conditions:** No spikedace larvae have yet been found in the outdoor pond. Ward suggests waiting before reassessing.

## Key Findings

1. **Spikedace larval production (artificial streams):** The first larvae were found in the artificial streams in April 2010. Ward reports that staff counted about 30–40 larvae in a single day, and that numbers are "really" increasing.
2. **Spikedace larval production (indoor tanks):** The Aravaipa-group fish in the indoor spikedace tanks were slower to spawn. The first larvae were found on the weekend before April 20, and staff estimated "maybe two to three dozen" larvae at the time of the email. The original text is partly garbled, so "two to three dozen" is a reconstruction.
3. **Outdoor pond:** No larvae were found in the pond. Ward recommends waiting "a little while longer" before reassessing.
4. **Loach minnow:** One tank of Aravaipa-origin loach minnow spawned, but no larvae were seen. No spawning or larvae had been seen in the Blue River loach minnow tanks.
5. **Eagle Creek roundtail chub:** The Eagle Creek fish had not yet spawned or produced larvae. Other roundtail held at the hatchery, including fish in the larger downstream pond, had spawned and produced offspring.
6. **Hormone-induced spawning:** Ward proposes considering hormone injection to induce spawning in the Eagle Creek roundtail. Dowling and Clarkson discuss whether this should be tried, noting that the group should "acknowledge" the current limits and plan follow-up effort. The thread does not record a final decision.
7. **Blue River release timing:** Dowling asks when fish are scheduled to enter the Blue River and whether this affects the spawning decision. No answer is recorded.
8. **Next steps:** Clarkson forwards the thread to Dowling for input. Ward and the group agree to reassess pond spawning and the roundtail spawning effort later.
