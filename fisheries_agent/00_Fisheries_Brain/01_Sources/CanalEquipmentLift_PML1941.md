# Canal Equipment Lift for Granite Reef Aqueduct Fishery Studies

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #fishery-sampling

## Executive Summary

This document is a technical description of a portable boat and equipment lift built by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Reclamation) for use in the [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], a portion of the [[Central Arizona Project]]. Reclamation, with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], is studying the fish community that is establishing itself in the canal. The canal's depth, fast flow, and steep concrete banks make conventional sampling difficult and dangerous, so the lift was designed to let biologists launch boats and lower heavy equipment almost anywhere along the canal. The authors are [[Gordon Mueller]] and [[Howard Carver]]. The document reports no fish species, counts, or survey results.

## Background & Description

- **Project context:** Reclamation is finalizing the Granite Reef Aqueduct, which diverts [[Colorado River]] water from Lake Havasu toward central Arizona through roughly 600 km (380 miles) of concrete canal.
- **Study purpose:** The fishery studies are meant to inform the aquatic resource and future management options for the resource agencies.
- **Access problem:** Water depth, high velocity, and steep canal banks make conventional sampling gear and techniques hard to use and hazardous. No boat access exists, except for craft carried down the bank by hand or placed in the canal by large crane.
- **Design goal:** Build a stable, easily transported lift that compensates for the canal bank slope and lets biologists lower and retrieve large boats and heavy equipment.

**Lift components**

| Component | Description (as stated) |
|---|---|
| Frame | Steel tubular or channel iron, about 5 cm (2 in) section; approx. 2 m long, 1.8 m (5.5 ft) wide, 1.5 m (4.5 ft) high |
| Platform | 2 cm (3/4 in) plywood sheet on the frame, serving as a work platform |
| Rollers | Four sets of roller assemblies taken from a boat trailer, about 13 cm (5 in) |
| Top rails | Wooden planks bolted to the frame top to protect the hull of a trailered boat |
| Weight | 68 kg (150 lb); can be moved by hand |

The lift fits in a pickup and is operated by a bumper-mounted truck winch. The truck parks on the far side of the levee, and the cable attaches to an eye bolt centered on the frame. Light equipment is loaded by hand. A [[Hydraulic Boom Truck]] handles heavier loads, but it cannot extend the roughly 15 m (50 ft) needed to reach the water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document discusses the aquatic setting only in general terms. The canal is lined and concrete-walled, which limits natural bank habitat. Its water depth and high velocity restrict where and how biologists can sample. The fish community is described as "becoming established," which indicates a newly formed assemblage in a constructed waterway. The document gives no species lists, habitat measurements, water chemistry, or population data.

## Key Findings

1. **Access solved for a constructed canal:** The lift allows boat launching and equipment handling "nearly anywhere" along the lined canal, which conventional methods could not do safely.
2. **Economic alternative to cranes:** The lift reduces dependence on large cranes for placing equipment in the canal.
3. **Safety improvement:** Moving equipment by hand down steep banks is replaced by the winch-operated lift, which reduces personal injury risk.
4. **Mobility and portability:** At 68 kg, the lift is transported in a pickup and handled by hand, and the authors describe it as stable and easy to move.
5. **Multiple uses:** Besides launching boats, the lift serves as a docking and working platform.
6. **Limitation:** The hydraulic boom truck cannot reach the water from the bank, since it cannot extend the required 15 m.
7. **Study status:** The fishery community studies are ongoing, and the document reports no fish data or management conclusions.
