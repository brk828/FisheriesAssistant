# Future Gila Work - Interagency Collaboration Proposal

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #systematics

## Executive Summary

This 1986 memorandum from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Upper Colorado Regional Office to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Endangered Species Office proposes collaboration with Dr. W. L. Minckley of [[Arizona State University]] on basin-wide systematic studies of the genus *[[Gila]]*. The proposal emphasizes validating photographic monitoring techniques and resolving taxonomic questions in the [[Gila robusta]] complex through integration of Upper and Lower [[Colorado River Basin]] data. Dr. Minckley's letter details extensive existing datasets, ongoing research programs, and a systematic three-step approach to resolving species identity, hybridization, and genetic swamping questions.

## Background & Description

Dr. Minckley and his research team at [[Arizona State University]] have conducted multi-year studies on the genus *Gila* in the lower [[Colorado River]] basin and [[Rio Yaqui]] of Mexico, now extending into the upper Colorado River and tributaries. The research group includes five committed personnel: Dr. Minckley (systematics, zoogeography, ecology, genetics), Dr. Michael Douglas (biometrics, vertebrate morphology, computer science), Dr. Paul C. Marsh (fisheries biology, species interactions, reintroductions), Dean A. Hendrickson (Ph.D. candidate studying *Agosia* and *Gila* hybridization), and Bruce D. DeMarais (Ph.D. candidate in *Gila* systematics, completed M.S. on morphological variation).

The team has developed four critical datasets: three standard morpho-meristic sets covering more than 2,000 lower-basin fish specimens, and one dataset from hatchery hybridizations of *[[Gila robusta]]*, *[[Gila elegans]]*, and *[[Gila cypha]]* conducted at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and transferred to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Known hybrids have been preserved and subjected to detailed morphological and meristic analysis for comparison with wild-caught fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal addresses the decline of *Gila* species throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]] and emphasizes the mandatory need for basin-wide systematics of the [[Gila robusta]] complex. The research aims to characterize species in existing and predicted habitats through statistical applications including [[Principal Components Analysis]] and estimation of pattern concordance. Ecological data will be integrated with morphological and meristic information to provide comprehensive species characterization.

Related conservation efforts include reintroductions of native fishes: [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the lower Colorado River basin. The research addresses critical management questions including specific identity, hybridization patterns, and genetic swamping in the [[Gila robusta]] complex.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Three-Step Systematic Approach:**

1. **Validation Phase**: Validate photographic data compilation by comparing with meristic and morphometric data from actual specimens. Account for variation introduced by photographic techniques, photographers, and equipment. The Bureau of Reclamation's Basinwide Monitoring photographic techniques have not yet been validated.

2. **Variation Analysis**: Accumulate and examine large photographic datasets for sexual, ontogenetic (size), or ecological variation that might cause aberrations in geographic (presumably genetic) patterns.

3. **Taxonomic Synthesis**: Make firm taxonomic comparisons only after understanding variation patterns, then apply synthetic approaches to determine precise taxonomic relationships.

**Existing Research Foundation:**

The [[Arizona State University]] team has published extensively on *Gila*, with 12 major publications and theses completed between 1967-1986, including:

- Comprehensive morpho-meristic datasets from lower basin specimens
- Taxonomic reviews of the [[Gila robusta]] complex across multiple drainages
- Distribution and abundance data from [[Rio Yaqui]] basin surveys
- Morphological variation studies correlating with geologic history
- Zoogeographic analyses of fossil and recent *Gila* distributions

**Collaborative Resources Needed:**

Access to photographs, field counts, and measurements from Upper Colorado River Fish Project and basin-wide monitoring programs conducted by Harold Tyus and colleagues. Integration of these Upper Basin data with existing Lower Basin datasets should provide adequate coverage to resolve taxonomic questions.

**Complementary Research:**

Electrophoretic studies with Dr. Don Buth ([[University of California Los Angeles]]), mitochondrial DNA analyses, and local morpho-meristic studies by other institutions will add critical information to the overall systematic synthesis.

**Management Recommendation:**

Bob Williams (Interagency Agreement Project Officer) recommends pursuing collaboration with Dr. Minckley through either Interagency Agreements or Coordinating Committee Activities, viewing the basin-wide systematic approach as sound and the provision of Upper Basin materials as a positive step toward resolving *Gila* species questions.
