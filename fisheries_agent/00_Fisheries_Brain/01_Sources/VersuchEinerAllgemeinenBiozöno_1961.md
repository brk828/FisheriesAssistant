# Versuch einer allgemeinen biozönotischen Gliederung der Fließgewässer

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #riverine-biocoenosis

## Executive Summary

This seminal 1961 paper by Joachim Illies proposes a globally applicable biocoenotic (ecological community-based) classification system for running waters (Fließgewässer), introducing the now-foundational terms **[[Rhithron]]** and **[[Potamon]]**. Moving beyond the geographically-limited Central European fish-based nomenclature (Salmonid Region vs. Cyprinid/Barbel Region), Illies argues that these two "Großbiozönosen" (macro-biocoenoses) represent universal ecological zones definable by thermal regime, current velocity, oxygen saturation, and substrate — validated by comparative faunal analysis across four river systems on three continents (Germany, Peru, South Africa). The paper establishes the concept of **[[Isozönose]]** (isocoenosis): taxonomically distinct but ecologically/phylogenetically equivalent communities occurring in physiographically similar habitats worldwide, even absent shared species.

## Background & Description

### Conceptual Framework
Illies builds on the concept of "Isozönose" (Balogh 1958) — the idea that physiographically identical biotopes across the globe host biologically equivalent communities, even without shared species, due to convergent ecological adaptation within related taxonomic groups (genera, subfamilies, families).

### Two Abiotic "General Factors"
The paper models river longitudinal profiles as governed by two inversely-related continuous gradients from source to mouth:
1. **Current velocity** (near-bed flow speed) — highest at source, decreasing downstream
2. **Temperature** (local monthly mean maxima) — lowest at source, increasing downstream

Oxygen content (as "physiological oxygen richness," sensu Ruttner 1940) tracks proportionally with these factors. **Water discharge (Wasserführung)** is a secondary factor, generally inversely related to current velocity but directly dependent on regional precipitation, introducing significant regional variability (e.g., lowland rivers can stagnate in flood or dry up entirely in arid regions).

### Rhithron vs. Potamon: Formal Definitions

**[[Rhithron]]**: The stream section following the spring (Krene) up to a monthly mean temperature amplitude of ~20°C. Characterized by:
- High current velocity, high O₂ saturation (turbulent flow)
- Low water discharge generally
- Substrate: bedrock, boulders, gravel, or fine sand; mud only in flow-protected pockets
- Biota: cold-stenothermic, rheobiontic, polyoxybiontic organisms with strong morphological adaptations to current

**[[Potamon]]**: The section following the Rhithron, with >20°C monthly temperature amplitude (or, in tropics, summer monthly means >20°C). Characterized by:
- Low near-bed current velocity, largely laminar flow
- Oxygen deficits at depth, partial-to-total light extinction, mud deposition
- Substantial seasonal discharge fluctuation
- Substrate: sand and mud, may carry bedload gravel
- Biota: eurythermal or warm-stenothermic, rheotolerant organisms, mostly derived from lineages whose ecological optimum lies in standing water

### Terminological Justification
Illies explicitly rejects the traditional Central European terms **Salmonidenregion** and **Cyprinidenregion** (or "Barbenregion") because:
- They are only meaningful where these specific fish families occur (essentially restricted to the Northern Hemisphere)
- The namesake species (grayling/Äsche and barbel/Barbe) have restricted distributions and are increasingly threatened by human impacts, leading to the absurdity of "Barbenregionen" lacking any actual barbel (e.g., the Elbe river, per Bauch 1953)

### Six-Tier Subdivision System
Following the precedent of lake stratification terminology (Epi-, Meta-, Hypo-Limnion), Illies proposes:

| New Term | Old Central European Term |
|---|---|
| Epirhithron (oSR) | Upper Salmonid Region / upper trout region |
| Metarhithron (mSR) | Middle Salmonid Region / lower trout region |
| Hyporhithron (uSR) | Lower Salmonid Region / grayling (Äschen) region |
| Epipotamon (BaR) | Upper Cyprinid Region / Barbel region |
| Metapotamon | Lower Cyprinid Region / Bream (Brassen/Blei) region |
| Hypopotamon | Ruffe-Flounder (Kaulbarsch-Flunder) Region |

(Note: The tripartite division of Rhithron is empirically well-supported per Illies 1953, 1960; Schmitz 1957. Whether Potamon requires two or three subdivisions is left open for future study.)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### The Rhithron-Potamon Boundary as a Sharp Ecotone
Contrary to the gradual, continuous change in physiographic factors (current, temperature) along a river's course, **faunal turnover is markedly discontinuous**, occurring in concentrated pulses — typically at tributary confluences. The most dramatic of these transitions occurs at the boundary between the Hyporhithron (Äschenregion/grayling region) and Epipotamon (Barbenregion/barbel region).

This transition is not merely qualitative but deeply **quantitative**:
- In the Salmonid region, [[Trichoptera]] and [[Plecoptera]] comprise ~25% of total individual abundance
- In the Barbel region, these groups are largely displaced
- Conversely, [[Isopoda]], [[Nematoda]], [[Hirudinea]] (leeches), and [[Odonata]] become abundant in the Barbel region while being negligible in the Salmonid region
- In the nekton, [[Salmonidae]] are replaced by [[Cyprinidae]] (Müller 1955)

### Rhithrobenthos — Global Taxonomic Composition
Characteristic cold-stenothermic, rheobiontic, polyoxybiontic invertebrate taxa found worldwide in Rhithron zones:
- **Ephemeroptera**: Ecdyonuridae, Ephemerellidae, Leptophlebiidae
- **Plecoptera**: Capniidae, Leuctridae, Nemouridae, Gripopterygidae
- **Diptera**: Blephariceridae, Simuliidae, Podonomidae, Psychodiidae
- **Coleoptera**: Elmidae, Psephenidae, Helodidae, Hydraenidae
- **Trichoptera**: [[Rhyacophilidae]], Odontoceridae, Glossosomatinae, Philopotaminae (excluding *Chimarrha*)
- **Gastropoda**: Ancylidae
- **Hydrachnellae**: Hygrobatidae, Protziidae

**Rhithronekton (fish)** — biogeographically variable but ecologically equivalent (isozönotic):
- Northern latitudes: **[[Salmonidae]]**
- Tropics: numerous endemic families, e.g., **[[Loricariidae]]**
- Southern latitudes: **[[Galaxiidae]]**

**Rhithroplankton**: poorly developed, dominated by Harpacticidae.

### Potamobenthos — Global Taxonomic Composition
Eurythermal/warm-stenothermic, rheotolerant taxa, mostly lineages whose primary radiation occurred in standing water:
- **Ephemeroptera**: Siphlonuridae, Potamanthidae, Polymitarcidae, Caenidae
- **Plecoptera**: Perlodidae, Perlidae (especially Acroneuriinae)
- **Diptera**: Chironomidae, Culicidae, Tabanidae, Stratiomyidae
- **Odonata**: Agrionidae
- **Hemiptera**: Corixidae, Notonectidae
- **Coleoptera**: Haliplidae, Dytiscidae
- **Trichoptera**: Leptoceridae, Hydroptilidae

**Potamonekton (fish)**:
- Northern latitudes: **[[Cyprinidae]]**
- Other regions: numerous endemic families

**Potamoplankton**: richly developed, comprising both true potamic species and washed-in limnoplankton.

### Trichoptera as a Phylogenetic/Ecological Case Study
Following Milne & Milne (1939) and Ross (1956), Illies highlights Trichoptera as exemplifying the isozönose concept: primitive lineages are almost universally "cool adapted" (e.g., **Rhyacophilidae**, cold-stenothermic, globally restricted to Rhithrobenthos), while specialized/derived lineages are "warm adapted," occurring either in Potamobenthos (e.g., *Chimarrha*) or Limnobenthos (e.g., Phryganeidae). This phylogenetic-ecological correlation supports treating Rhithron and Potamon as valid global isozönoses independent of geographic species turnover.

### Biogeographic Modulation of the Rhithron-Potamon Boundary
The elevation/latitude at which the Rhithron-Potamon transition occurs is climate-dependent:
- **Higher latitudes (polar)**: Rhithron dominates almost exclusively; Potamon may be entirely absent; even lowland-originating streams remain truly rhithric. Rhithron-Limnion (lake) differences become minimal due to uniformly cold temperatures (Thienemann 1941).
- **Temperate latitudes**: Classic dual system — lowland rivers are Potamon, mountain streams are Rhithron.
- **Low tropical latitudes**: Even small streams above 1000 m elevation are typically already potamic; true Rhithron only occurs at very high elevations. Additionally, Potamon increasingly resembles Limnion (standing water) communities with increasing equatorial proximity (Borosaneanu 1960).

## Key Findings

1. **Two universal freshwater macro-biocoenoses**: Rhithron and Potamon represent globally valid ecological units, ranking equivalently to spring (Krene) and lake (Limnion) biocoenoses — independent of the geographically restricted Salmonid/Cyprinid fish-based terminology.

2. **Faunal discontinuity exceeds physiographic gradualism**: While abiotic factors (temperature, current) change continuously along a river's course, biotic community turnover is sharply discontinuous, concentrated at specific ecotones — especially the Hyporhithron/Epipotamon boundary.

3. **Isozönose validation across four continents**: Comparative analysis of Fulda-Weser (Germany, 51-53°N), Huallaga-Amazonas (Peru/Brazil, 0-10°S), Chillón (Peru, 10°S), and Great Berg River (South Africa, 33°S) confirmed consistent Rhithron→Potamon zonation despite vastly different taxonomic compositions, geographic settings, and river sizes (Chillón: 120 km; Amazonas system: ~5350 km).

4. **Case study specifics**:
   - **Fulda-Weser**: Rhithron ~35 km (source to Fliede confluence); Epipotamon to ~195 km (Werra confluence); Metapotamon ~360 km; Hypopotamon (Unterweser) ~70 km (Bremen to Bremerhaven).
   - **Huallaga-Amazonas**: Rhithron-Potamon boundary at river-km ~70 (near Ambo, Peru, 2500 m elevation); Hypopotamon extends 780 km; transitions to Epipotamon at Marañón confluence, persisting thousands of km to the coast.
   - **Chillón**: Boundary at ~1500 m elevation (between stations 3-4); Potamon atypical due to evaporation/irrigation water loss, intermittently failing to reach the sea ("verläuft im Sande").
   - **Great Berg River**: Boundary at Station 12 (Wellington, river-km 39); Harrison et al. (1958) explicitly compared their invertebrate Zone III (trout-supporting) to European trout-stream faunas, independently corroborating the Rhithron concept.

5. **Taxonomic groups, not just species, define isozönoses**: Ecological preferences (cold-stenothermy, rheobionty) are conserved at genus/family level (e.g., Rhyacophilidae globally, Salmonidae/Loricariidae/Galaxiidae as biogeographic Rhithronekton equivalents), enabling cross-continental comparison without shared species.

6. **Boundary elevation is climate-dependent**: The Rhithron-Potamon transition rises in elevation with decreasing latitude, collapses toward absence of Potamon at high latitudes, and converges toward Limnion-like conditions in the deep tropics.

7. **Exceptions to the model are acknowledged**: Rhithron can discharge directly to sea without an intervening Potamon; Potamon can arise directly from a lake outflow without a preceding Rhithron; lowland "Bach" (brook) can be physiographically Potamon-like in flow but ecologically classified otherwise.
