## Executive Summary
This is a field trip report documenting a two-day fish community survey conducted along the east end of [[Aravaipa Creek]], a perennial desert stream in the [[Gila River Basin]] (Arizona), tributary to the [[San Pedro River]]. Researchers from Arizona State University sampled six reaches spanning from approximately 2 miles above Cobra Ranch Crossing downstream to Chimney Rock Crossing using two seine sizes (1.83m and 2.74m). The survey targeted the native minnow-sucker assemblage characteristic of Aravaipa Creek, including two federally significant species, [[Meda Fulgida]] (Spikedace) and [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (Loach Minnow). Notably, **no exotic (non-native) fish species were detected** in any of the six sampled reaches, suggesting this reach retained an intact native fish assemblage as of October 1993. Ancillary observations included breeding coloration in two native cyprinids, predatory bird activity, and incidental amphibian (leopard frog) sightings.

## Background & Description
The report does not provide detailed morphometric data on individual specimens, but it references species-diagnostic breeding coloration as a field identification cue:

- **[[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (Loach Minnow)** — observed exhibiting breeding coloration more frequently than [[Rhinichthys Osculus]], suggesting the survey period (late October) overlapped with a late-season reproductive window for this species.
- **[[Rhinichthys Osculus]] (Speckled Dace)** — breeding coloration observed infrequently, less pronounced than in *Tiaroga*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
[[Aravaipa Creek]] at the survey time was characterized by dense in-stream and bank vegetation, providing structurally complex habitat for the native fish assemblage:

- **Riparian/aquatic vegetation**: Nasturtium, spiny rush, cocklebur, burrobrush, *Polygonum*, *Veronica* sp., and Bermuda grass dominated the understory; woody riparian species included *Salix* (willow), *Baccharis*, *Populus* (cottonwood), and sycamore.
- **Mesohabitat sampling**: Six ~200m reaches were sampled, spaced roughly one mile apart, encompassing all available habitat types (riffles, runs, pools) within each reach.
- **Predator-prey interactions**: Great blue herons and a belted kingfisher were observed actively foraging on fish within the sampled reaches, indicating the creek supports piscivorous avian predators reliant on the native minnow/sucker assemblage.
- **Co-occurring herpetofauna**: *Rana pipiens* ssp. (leopard frog complex) were observed along the water's edge in multiple reaches, indicating shared use of near-shore/riparian microhabitat.
- **Community structure**: The assemblage was dominated numerically by *Agosia chrysogaster* and *Meda fulgida* in most reaches, with *Catostomus insignis*, *Pantosteus clarkii*, *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Tiaroga cobitis*, and *Gila robusta* present in lower, variable abundances — consistent with the known native fish guild structure of the Gila River basin headwater streams.

## Key Findings

**Sampling design**: Two seine sizes were used — a 1.83m x 1.22m x 0.32m seine and a 2.74m x 1.52m x 0.64m seine — with every 10th haul recorded for the smaller seine and every 5th haul recorded for the larger seine, likely reflecting subsampling protocol adopted after disagreement between survey leads on methodology.
