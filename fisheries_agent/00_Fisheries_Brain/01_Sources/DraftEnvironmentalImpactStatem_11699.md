# Sanchez Copper Project: Draft Environmental Impact Statement Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #environmental-impact-statement

## Executive Summary

This draft Environmental Impact Statement (EIS), prepared by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Safford District Office with third-party contractor Fletcher Associates, evaluates AZCO Mining Inc.'s proposed [[Sanchez Copper Project]] in Graham County, Arizona. The proposal is an open pit copper mine with [[Heap Leach Processing]] and solvent extraction/electrowinning (SX-EW) recovery, plus a large waste rock dump. The project would disturb approximately 1,407 acres over a 17-year mine life, process about 12 million tons of ore annually (10 million tons per year of ore mining per the Summary and Chapter 2), yield 25,000 tons of copper per year, employ about 210 people with an annual payroll of $8.15 million, and use about 1,350 gpm (2,200 acre-feet per year) of groundwater.

The document is a draft and does not contain fish or aquatic-species survey data. Its aquatic content concerns groundwater and ephemeral surface drainages, with Gila River and Bonita Creek water quality discussed for context. The EIS concludes that most impacts are low to medium, with significant long-term effects from the unreclaimed open pit and the loss of about 900 acres of soil and vegetation productivity.

## Background & Description

**Proponent and lead agency.** AZCO Mining Inc., a Colorado corporation, submitted a Plan of Operations to the BLM on August 2, 1991. The BLM determined that an EIS was required under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA). Comments were directed to Larry Thrasher, BLM project manager.

**Project footprint.** The claim group comprises 307 lode mining claims and 274 millsite claims in the Lone Star Mining District, covering about 6,700 acres. The site lies about 10 miles east-northeast of Safford, on the southeast end of the Gila Mountains.

**Disturbance (Table 2-3).** Open pit 310 acres; waste dump 480; ore heap 535; crushing plant 5; SX-EW plant 8; ponds 15; conveyor 10; shop/office 7; roads 17; miscellaneous 20; total 1,407 acres. Existing disturbance from past exploration and mining is 245 acres.

**Mining and processing.** Conventional open pit mining with rotary blast-hole drilling (8–12 inch holes). The ultimate pit is about 4,000 feet in diameter and roughly 1,200 feet deep, containing 168 million tons of ore, 23 million tons of low-grade material, and 206 million tons of waste rock. Two leach pads with a combined capacity of 191 million tons use dilute sulfuric acid. Copper is recovered by SX-EW into cathodes of about 150 pounds each.

**Alternatives considered.** The No Action alternative; an alternative access route (Options 1 and 2, each about 7 miles from the airport intersection); alternative heap leach pad configurations; alternative groundwater disposal (irrigation, infiltration reservoir, or reinjection); and alternative reclamation (flattening side slopes to 3:1). Eliminated alternatives were [[Underground Mining]], backfilling the pit, milling with tailings, waste rock disposal in Head Canyon, and a heap leach pad south of the pit.

**Permits required (Table 1-1).** Authorizing agencies include the BLM (Plan of Operations, NEPA, NHPA, AIRFA, ESA compliance), the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] (Section 404 dredge and fill permit), the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] (Aquifer Protection Permit, air quality permits), the [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]] (dam safety, well permits), the Arizona State Historic Preservation Office, the Arizona Department of Agriculture (native plant law), and Graham County (site and septic permits).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Climate.** Semi-arid high desert. The Safford annual mean temperature is 64.1°F, with a July high mean of 85.0°F and a January low of 44.2°F. Annual precipitation averages 8.66 inches (1931–1960 normals). The 100-year, 24-hour rainfall is 3.85 inches. Growing season is 195 days. The annual mean wind speed is about 6 mph, with winds from the east through southeast accounting for over 43% of observations.

**Geology.** The Sanchez deposit is a disseminated, low-sulfide [[Porphyry Copper Deposit]] in andesites intruded by monzonite porphyry. Weathering produced an oxide zone 1,000 to 1,500 feet thick overlying a mixed ore zone. Leach pad terraces are caliche-cemented gravels over lacustrine clays. The site lies in a low-seismic-risk setting (USGS: about a 10% chance of exceeding 10% of gravity acceleration in 250 years).

**Groundwater (the aquatic system of most relevance).** The Safford Basin has two aquifers:
- A **shallow upper alluvial aquifer** in flood plain and stream channel deposits, hydraulically connected to the Gila River, used for irrigation and of good quality (TDS roughly 480–1,544 ppm).
- A **deep artesian aquifer** in basal [[Gila Conglomerate]], separated by lacustrine clays, which is roughly ten times more saline and of marginal to poor irrigation quality (TDS 2,045–5,098 ppm).

Static levels in the lower aquifer range from about 130 to 400 feet below ground. Regional irrigation pumping was 119,566 acre-feet in 1990. Table 3-4 lists more than 60 registered wells, and Table 3-5 summarizes water quality for selected wells.

**Surface water.** The [[Gila River]] flows east to west through Safford Valley. About 43,000 gpm (69,999 acre-feet) was diverted to the San Jose Canal in 1990. The City of Safford draws from [[Bonita Creek]] (appropriation 3,240,000 gpd). All project-area drainages are ephemeral and flow south to the Gila River. The project will not use surface water from Bonita Creek or the Gila River. Peak flows for the 100-year, 6-hour storm were estimated at 325 cfs (initial pad), 339 cfs (future pad), 447 cfs (open pit watershed), and 946 cfs (waste rock watershed).

**Wetlands and aquatic biota.** There are no wetlands or permanent water on the site. Numerous washes are classified as "Waters of the U.S." by the Army Corps of Engineers but lack sufficient water to sustain wetlands. The EIS notes that sensitive fish, including [[Gila Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], may occur in the Gila Box Riparian NCA about three miles upstream. Downstream distribution is restricted by agricultural diversions, and washes draining the project lie between the first and second diversions. No sensitive fish occur downstream of the project above San Carlos Reservoir.

**Vegetation.** Sonoran desert scrub dominated by creosote bush, with desert washes supporting the most diverse habitat. Twelve threatened or endangered plant species were screened; the nightblooming cereus is present, but none were found in the project area's habitats. Several state-protected cacti are present.

**Wildlife.** Relatively depauperate Sonoran fauna. The most significant element is a maternity colony of [[California Leaf-Nosed Bat]] (estimated 150 to 200 individuals on October 29, 1991) in two historic mine adits, with a cave myotis also present. Javelina herds range over the project area, and about 50% of one herd's forage area would be covered by the waste dump. No federal or state endangered species are known to occupy the site.

**Land use and culture.** The project is in a Visual Resource Management Class IV area (scenic quality "C"). Grazing allotments include 770 AUMs (Lone Star) and 168 AUMs (Sanchez). The SWCA surveys recorded 36 sites (35 prehistoric, 1 historic) in or near disturbance areas.

**Socioeconomics.** Graham County had a 1990 population of 26,554 and an unemployment rate of 7.4%. Agriculture, government, and retail dominate the economy. Estimated annual revenue to state and local governments from the project is detailed in the tax tables.

**Transportation.** Access follows existing paved roads and bridges over the Gila River. Projected traffic increases by 118 vehicles per day, including about 79 worker vehicles. The Solomon Bridge has load limits, so large trucks must use the 8th Avenue (Safford) Bridge.

## Key Findings

- **Air quality:** Particulate matter is the principal air impact. Modeled maximum PM10 (24-hour) at the off-site location is 144 µg/m³ including background, against a federal standard of 150 µg/m³. Annual PM10 is 31 µg/m³ off-site against 50 µg/m³. Annual NO2 is 33 µg/m³ against 100 µg/m³. Impacts are below standards, and the project is rated low to medium, long term.
- **Groundwater:** Water use averages 1,350 gpm (range 1,000–2,000 gpm). The pit is estimated to intercept less than the amount needed for processing and dust control (potential inflow 200–500 gpm). Interceptor wells and a cutoff wall would limit seepage. Waste rock sulfide content is under 0.10%, and leach tests found only arsenic detectable (0.003–0.01 mg/L, below the 0.05 mg/L drinking water standard). Impacts are rated low to medium, with no significant cumulative adverse effects.
- **Heap leach containment:** Pads use at least 18 inches of compacted clay (minimum permeability 10⁻⁷ cm/sec) plus HDPE liners. Ponds are double-lined with leak detection, and overflow ponds hold 21 million gallons. Design storm is the 100-year, 24-hour event.
- **Surface water:** Impacts are rated low to medium, with no discharge from processing facilities. Diversion ditches and sedimentation ponds control runoff. Cumulative impacts from the [[Phelps Dodge Lone Star Deposit]] could be significant to surface water quality, as it is assumed to be roughly 10 times the Sanchez scale.
- **Soils and vegetation:** 507 acres will be revegetated. The pit (310 acres), heap slopes (134 acres), and dump slopes (456 acres) will not be, for a permanent loss of about 900 acres of soil and vegetation productivity. Cumulative soil loss could reach about 10,000 acres long term (medium impact).
- **Wildlife:** Long-term habitat loss of 1,407 acres is locally severe but biologically insignificant given surrounding habitat. Bat roosts will be displaced, and adit sealing is proposed at an appropriate time of year.
- **Significant impacts:** The EIS identifies a significant, long-term adverse impact from the unbackfilled pit. Backfilling was rejected because the ore and waste "swell" (about 25%), and the estimated $400 million cost would make the project uneconomic.
- **Socioeconomics:** Beneficial, with 210 new jobs, an $8.15 million payroll, and local hiring and training. The eliminated alternative of underground mining was judged technically infeasible.
- **Draft status:** This document is a draft; the BLM invited public comment. Public scoping drew 37 comment letters and oral comments from eleven people, and a public information meeting drew more than 75 attendees on August 28, 1991.
