# Green River Flow Recommendations: Non-native Fish Management

## Executive Summary
This FY92 Annual Report (Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program, Project #18-38) documents research on non-native fish management in the Green River, Utah, with a primary focus on assessing the ecological impact of introduced [[Northern Pike]] on the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (young-of-year, YOY). The study, led by Leo Lantsch (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources) and Todd Crowl (Utah State University), combined radio telemetry, movement analysis, and bioenergetics modeling to characterize pike habitat use, seasonal congregation patterns, and prey consumption. Findings indicate that while pike predation pressure during summer months is comparable to other non-native predators, spring congregation behavior in predictable locations (notably Ashley Creek) creates a management opportunity for targeted removal efforts.

## Background & Description
- **Northern Pike (Esox lucius)**: Data collected included standard length and weight measurements for all 49 captured individuals; no detailed morphometric breakdown provided in this report.
- Twenty-nine pike were surgically implanted with internal radio transmitters (Advanced Telemetry Systems [ATS], 1-year battery duration) featuring external antennae; remaining fish were externally tagged with Floy tags.
- No morphological data reported for [[Colorado Squawfish]] beyond reference to "YOY" (young-of-year) life stage as the vulnerable cohort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use**: [[Northern Pike]] in the Green River exhibit a low-energy behavioral strategy:
  - Daylight hours: protected side channels and areas contiguous with sand bars, low water velocity.
  - Nighttime hours: deep, protected areas with little or no flow.
- **Seasonal congregation**: Pike aggregate in spring (post-peak flows) near Jensen, Utah, particularly around **Ashley Creek** — 38 of 49 captured fish originated from this vicinity, suggesting a critical bottleneck habitat for management intervention.
- **Movement patterns**: Following release, tagged pike dispersed both upstream and downstream; one individual reached the [[Yampa River]] confluence 42 days post-release, another reached Sand Wash within 53 days. Movement bursts were generally downstream-oriented, consistent with an energy-minimization foraging strategy.
- **Trophic interactions**: Pike are implicated as potential predators of [[Colorado Squawfish]] YOY, a federally endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] endemic. The study sought to quantify this predation risk relative to other non-native predatory fish in the system.
- **Study reach**: Green River between the [[Yampa River]] and [[Duchesne River]] confluences, sampled via float surveys from Split Mountain to Sand Wash.

## Key Findings
- **Capture effort**: 49 Northern Pike captured via electrofishing and seining in spring 1992; 29 radio-tagged, remainder Floy-tagged.
- **Bioenergetics estimate**: Calculated energy expenditure indicates daily prey rations of only **1–4 fish per day** are sufficient to account for observed pike growth rates — suggesting relatively low individual predation pressure.
- **Predation risk assessment**: Data do not suggest pike pose a disproportionately greater threat to native fishes during summer months compared to other non-native predators already established in the system.
- **Management implication**: Despite modest per-capita predation impact, **spring congregation behavior** (particularly at Ashley Creek) presents a high-efficiency removal opportunity — an intensive 1–2 week harvest effort during this window could reduce the local "Ouray" pike population by an estimated **>50%**.
- **Literature synthesis**: Over 7,000 published articles identified on non-native fishes in the drainage; 200+ specifically addressing selective removal/control techniques, under review for feasibility analysis (Task 3).
- **Budget status**: $57,000 allocated, $37,000 expended at reporting time; program on track, consistent with fiscal year timing.
- **Data submission status**: On track, no outstanding recommendations issued in this report cycle.
