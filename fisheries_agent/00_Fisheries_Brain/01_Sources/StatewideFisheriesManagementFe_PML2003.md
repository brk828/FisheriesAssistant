# Colorado River Fisheries Job Progress Reports, Laughlin Lagoon (1986–1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This series of Nevada Division of Wildlife Federal Aid job reports (Job 305, Statewide Fisheries Management, Colorado River) documents fish sampling in the [[Laughlin Lagoon]], a backwater of the [[Colorado River]] below [[Davis Dam]]. The reports cover 1986 through 1993 and were written mainly by biologist Mike Burrell. Sampling used overnight [[Gill Netting]] and, in 1986, [[Beach Seine]] hauls. The work tracked the dominance of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] in the lagoon, and it recorded natural reproduction of largemouth bass in 1986. Electrofishing and [[Creel Census]] work was repeatedly planned but often not completed. Each report recommended continued and expanded sampling.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** About 12 miles of the Colorado River below Davis Dam form the boundary between Arizona and Nevada. The river is swift, cool, and has highly variable flow controlled by Davis Dam releases. The bottom is rubble, gravel, and sand. The 1986 report gives a release water temperature of 59°F year-round.
- **Lagoons:** Several backwater lagoons were created on the Nevada side by [[Training Dikes]], which line nearly the entire Nevada reach. The Laughlin Lagoon is the largest and became contested because of proposed development.
- **Fish community:** The fishery depends mainly on [[Striped Bass]] and stocked [[Rainbow Trout]]. Largemouth bass, bluegill, green sunfish, and channel catfish are of lesser importance. The native [[Razorback Sucker]] and Flannelmouth Sucker are present. [[Colorado River Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] were no longer known from this section.
- **Striped bass:** Spring and early summer runs move about 40 miles upstream from [[Lake Havasu]] to spawn. Fish over 20 pounds were common in the late 1970s but were rare by the 1980s.
- **Trout management:** Rainbow trout fingerlings are stocked from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Trout grew well on [[Simuliid Larvae]], but a larval control program begun in 1984 to reduce a local nuisance may be harming the trout fishery.
- **Development pressure:** Growth at Laughlin, Nevada, and Bullhead City, Arizona, was expected to increase fisheries management problems.
- **Regulation:** The razorback sucker is federally threatened and state-listed as rare.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology:** Fluctuating releases from Davis Dam raise and lower water levels 4 to 8 feet in a 24-hour period. The 1990 report describes the Laughlin Lagoon as mud-flat, with water level changes that make night work hazardous.
- **Water conditions:** Water temperature, runoff, and levels differed considerably between years. The 1986 report notes high runoff and levels in the early years and a return to normal flows in later years.
- **Hazards and logistics:** Swift currents, rapidly changing water levels, and night operations made sampling dangerous. Equipment problems and time limits repeatedly prevented planned electrofishing and creel surveys.
- **Agency coordination:** Technical assistance went to the [[Native Fish Work Group]], the Lake Mohave Native Fish Work Group, the Lower Colorado River Management Work Group, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and the Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Council.

## Key Findings

### 1986 Completion Report (F-20-22)
- **April gill netting:** Carp were the most abundant species, followed by largemouth bass, razorback sucker, bluegill, channel catfish, and an unidentified sucker.
- **June gill netting (2 net nights):** Channel catfish were the most abundant. Carp, sunfish, threadfin shad, largemouth bass, razorback sucker, and striped bass followed.
- **Beach seining (June 19, three hauls):** Largemouth bass reproduction was documented. The total was 250 largemouth bass, 226 of them from a single haul in the northwest corner of the lagoon. The report notes this as evidence that the lagoon is an important [[Largemouth Bass]] spawning area.
- **Recommendations (1987):** Enlist law enforcement staff to collect creel data, and continue an annual survey of the Laughlin Lagoon.

### 1990 Report (F-20-26)
- **Sampling:** Two net nights in November.
- **Results:** Flannelmouth sucker made up 50% of the catch and 39% of the biomass, with an average length of 314 mm. Carp, rainbow trout, and largemouth bass were also taken.
- **Recommendations:** Begin routine creel census, and make no regulatory changes.

### 1991 Report (F-20-27)
- **Sampling:** Two net nights on December 13, 1991, catching 20 fish in total.
- **Flannelmouth sucker:** 50% of the catch and 87% of the biomass. Lengths ranged from 300 to 560 mm, averaging 384 mm, up from 314 mm in 1990.
- **Other species:** Bluegill made up about 35% of the catch (figure as scanned) and 11% of the biomass, with an average length of 148 mm. One largemouth bass (190 mm), one rainbow trout (170 mm), and one carp (102 mm) were each 5% of the catch.
- **Recommendations:** Expand fish sampling as time allows.

### 1992 Report (F-20-28)
- **April 4 (one overnight set):** 15 flannelmouth suckers, 4 carp, and 1 largemouth bass. Flannelmouth made up most of the biomass, averaged 17.7 inches, and showed at least three age classes in length-frequency analysis.
- **December 11 (one gill net set):** 3 largemouth bass (average 11.6 inches), 1 flannelmouth sucker (7.2 inches, likely a young fish), 1 bluegill (7.5 inches), and 1 yellow bullhead (12.2 inches). Largemouth bass made up 75% of the biomass, followed by yellow bullhead (14%), bluegill (8%), and flannelmouth sucker (3%).
- **Constraints:** Scheduled electrofishing could not be completed because of equipment problems. No creel census was conducted, and no anglers were contacted.
- **Recommendations:** Continue sampling and schedule a random creel census to establish baseline angler use patterns.

### 1993 Report (F-20-29)
- **March 26 (one gill net set):** 15 flannelmouth suckers (55% of the catch, 68% of the biomass; average 477 mm), 4 carp (15%; 25% of biomass), 5 threadfin shad (19%), 2 bluegill (7%), and 1 largemouth bass (4%; 5.9% of biomass).
- **December 1 (two gill net sets):** 4 flannelmouth suckers (17%; 43% of biomass), 10 threadfin shad (42%), 3 bluegill (12%; 11% of biomass), and 7 largemouth bass (29%; 38% of biomass). No carp were caught.
- **Trends (1990–1993):** Largemouth bass and flannelmouth sucker were the most consistently caught species in the Laughlin Lagoon. Catch rates for most species did not change significantly, though biomass shifted.
- **Recommendations:** Continue fish sampling and expand it as time allows.

### Cross-Report Conclusions
- Flannelmouth sucker dominated the lagoon catch by number and biomass in most years.
- Largemouth bass were consistently present, and documented spawning occurred in 1986.
- Electrofishing, creel census, and angler surveys were recommended in nearly every report but were rarely completed, leaving the fishery's angler use and harvest largely unmeasured.
