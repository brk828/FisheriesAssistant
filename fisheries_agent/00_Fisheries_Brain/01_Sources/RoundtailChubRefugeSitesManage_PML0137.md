# Roundtail Chub Refuge Sites Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #roundtail-chub

## Executive Summary

This draft management plan, prepared by Arizona Game and Fish Department staff, sets out how [[Roundtail Chub]] will be secured at refuge sites. The refuges would supply fish for supplemental stocking into the source locality and unoccupied habitats within its drainage, and would give private pond owners and outreach programs a source of the fish. The plan covers population selection, collection and transport, quarantine and parasite treatment, brood stock maintenance, and offspring management. It is marked "DRAFT - Do not distribute."

## Background & Description

The plan's stated goal is to:

- Secure roundtail chub populations at refuge sites.
- Supplement the source locality and unoccupied habitats within its drainage.
- Provide landowners with fish for private tanks and ponds within the drainage's historical occupancy range.
- Support outreach.

The document is a management protocol rather than a research study. It does not report field surveys or new population data. Its main technical basis is a parasite survey of the [[Verde River]] (1996), published in the *North American Journal of Fisheries Management* (Robinson et al. 1998). That survey examined [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and roundtail chub. Its table lists percent occurrence of protozoan, platyhelminth, crustacean, and annelid parasites, with a reported total of 313 fish (as read from OCR, which is garbled in places).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population selection and genetic policy**

- If enough individuals can be obtained, each refuge population will derive from a single [[Panmictic Population]].
- Fish from that population may be returned only to the original locality or to unoccupied habitats within the source drainage. They may not be mixed with other populations.
- This restriction stays in force until genetic and ecological data suggest otherwise.
- If the target number cannot be reached, populations may be combined on a case-by-case basis.

**Collection and transport**

- The initial pond population should be at least 100 chub from adult and age-1 classes.
- Brood fish over 100 mm total length are to be tagged with [[Pit Tagging]]. Fish under 100 mm may receive [[Coded Wire Tagging]].
- Only the first fish captured are to be taken, and fish with visible deformities or untreatable pathogens (for example, yellow grub) are excluded.
- Hauling tanks must hold pathogen-free, oxygenated water with 0.6% unionized NaCl and Stress Coat® at the manufacturer's dose. Tanks should be free of sharp or porous objects and insulated, with backup oxygen on hand.
- Captured fish go to [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]] or another suitable facility if treatment is needed.
- Temperature acclimation is required if the hauling tank and source water differ by more than 3 °C.
- Field data and permits must travel with the fish.
- The vehicle stops after the first half hour and hourly thereafter to check fish health, temperature, and oxygen.
- On arrival, fish are counted and measured for length and weight to support future growth estimates.

**Quarantine and treatment**

Populations scheduled for collection receive a fish health assessment using standard "blue-book" methods, with exceptions assessed case by case. Fish that die in transit are sent to a fish health laboratory for pathogen inspection. Treatments are matched to the pathogen groups in the Verde River survey:

- **Protozoans and trematodes:** [[Formalin Treatment]] at 100 ppm for one hour, increased gradually toward 167 ppm as tolerance allows, for 3 to 5 consecutive days. Fish are then checked visually and by skin scrapes.
- **Copepods and Piscicolidae (leeches):** Salt baths for one hour, starting at 1‰ and increasing to 3.5‰ as tolerated. Adult parasites are also removed physically over several weeks, matching the parasite life cycle. The plan notes that salt concentrations needed to kill these parasites may be too high for the fish.
- **Cestodes:** [[Praziquantel Treatment]] at 6 ppm, above the usual 0.67 ppm for 24 hours, because recent research suggests higher doses may be needed for Asian tapeworm. One treatment is planned.
- **Internal bacterial infections:** Oxytetracycline (TM) in feed or bath.
- **External bacterial infections:** Chloramine-T at 10 ppm for one hour.

**Brood stock management**

- Fish identity is checked by multiple staff before tagging or stocking.
- Fin clips are taken for genetic tissue samples to measure heterozygosity and set a baseline for genetic integrity.
- At least 20% of brood stock (or 50 fish) is replaced every 3 years, using wild adults where available to limit [[Inbreeding]].
- Replaced brood fish may be used for supplemental stocking.

**Offspring (F1+) management**

- Remove as many offspring as possible to reduce inbreeding.
- Every 3 years, run population estimates and remove up to 80% of F1+ offspring.
- Removed fish can go to supplemental stocking, private ponds, or outreach.
- If heterozygosity is judged unsuitable for wild stocking, the strategy is to be reevaluated case by case.
- Every 10 to 12 years, if source populations exist, all fish are removed from refugia and the refuge is restarted.

## Key Findings

- The plan's core rule is to keep each refuge population genetically distinct, with no mixing of populations or transfers outside the original locality and its drainage, until new genetic or ecological data are available.
- The minimum starting population is 100 chub from adult and age-1 classes.
- Tagging depends on size: [[Pit Tagging]] for fish over 100 mm, [[Coded Wire Tagging]] for smaller fish.
- Transport is designed to protect fish, with 0.6% salt, oxygen, temperature control within 3 °C, and scheduled health checks.
- Pathogen treatment follows the 1996 Verde River survey. Formalin, salt baths, Praziquantel (6 ppm), oxytetracycline, and Chloramine-T are each matched to a parasite group.
- Brood stock is replaced by at least 20% (or 50 fish) every 3 years, and up to 80% of F1+ offspring are removed every 3 years, to limit inbreeding.
- Genetic monitoring through fin-clip heterozygosity sets the baseline and guides future management.
- The refuge is to be renewed every 10 to 12 years, if source populations allow.
