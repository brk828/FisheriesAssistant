# Biological Opinion on the Reintroduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish into Three Tributaries of the Agua Fria River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) biological opinion, dated November 24, 1998 (consultation number 2-21-99-F431), evaluates a joint proposal by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Phoenix Field Office and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) to reintroduce the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] into three perennial tributaries of the Agua Fria River in Yavapai County, Arizona. The Service concluded that the action, as proposed, is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of either species and does not affect designated critical habitat. An incidental take statement sets a cap of no more than 25 percent mortality of each species captured, stocked, held, or transported, and requires monitoring, reporting, and reinitiation if take limits are exceeded.

## Background & Description

**Species status.** The Gila topminnow was listed as endangered without critical habitat in 1967. It was once among the commonest fishes of the Gila River Basin but has been extirpated from most of its range through habitat destruction and predation or competition from nonnative fishes, especially [[Mosquitofish]]. The desert pupfish was listed as endangered with critical habitat in 1986. Historically it occurred across the Gila River basin, lower Colorado River, and Mexican basins, and approximately 13 natural populations persist, nine of them in Mexico.

**Proposed action.** Each of the three sites would receive a minimum of 500 individuals of each species:
- Lousy Canyon (T. 9 N., R. 3 E., Sec. 5)
- An unnamed tributary to Larry Creek (T. 9 N., R. 3 E., Sec. 9)
- Silver Creek (T. 10 N., R. 3 E., Sec. 11)

Desert pupfish would come from the Santa Clara Slough stock held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], New Mexico. Gila topminnow donors are Coal Mine Canyon, Fresno Canyon, and Lower Sonoita Creek, all in the same drainage. Collections would occur in May–June, before the monsoon season. Supplemental stocking would continue until self-sustaining populations are established or monitoring shows a site cannot support one.

**Ongoing land uses continued under the action:**
- Livestock grazing on the Horseshoe, Cross Y, and Copper Creek allotments at current levels.
- Two vehicle crossings on Silver Creek (one unimproved, one on the heavily used [[Bloody Basin Road]]).
- Periodic [[Prescribed Burning]] in adjacent semidesert grassland watersheds, coordinated by the Tonto National Forest and BLM.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat requirements.** Gila topminnow need unpolluted water with wide tolerance of temperature, pH, and salinity; shallow water with abundant aquatic vegetation; channel morphology resistant to scouring; freedom from nonnative competitors and predators; and slow currents over soft bottoms. Recorded tolerances include temperatures of 43–97°F, pH 6.6–8.9, and salinities approaching seawater. Desert pupfish tolerate temperatures up to 113°F, dissolved oxygen as low as 0.1–0.4 mg/l, and salinities up to 68 g/l.

**Study area.** All three sites lie in the Agua Fria Grasslands ecosystem, with upland vegetation dominated by tobosa grass, curly mesquite, and red brome, and riparian corridors of velvet ash, cottonwood, and willow.
- **Silver Creek:** A roughly four-mile perennial reach (elevation 3,760 to 3,320 feet) with a five-foot waterfall that blocks upstream fish movement. Gila chub occur above the barrier; green sunfish, fathead minnow, longfin dace, and Gila chub occur below it.
- **Lousy Canyon:** A roughly one-mile reach (3,080 to 2,600 feet) with a 30-foot waterfall. Gila chub were reintroduced there in 1995.
- **Larry Creek tributary:** A roughly 0.6-mile reach (3,200 to 2,860 feet), shallower and less diverse than the other two. Gila chub were reintroduced in 1995.

**Barriers and threats.** The Agua Fria River downstream is occupied by green sunfish and mosquitofish, which act as a biological barrier preventing establishment beyond the expected dispersal area. Historic populations were lost through the spread of exotic fishes and drought-driven decline of isolated populations. The nearest natural populations are at AD Wash, Tule Creek, and possibly Cow Creek, which drain into [[Lake Pleasant]] and are isolated by its predator population.

**Land management designations.** The Larry Creek tributary lies within the Larry Canyon Area of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC), focused on riparian protection. Lousy Canyon lies within the Perry Mesa ACEC, focused on cultural resources. The Agua Fria River is proposed as a wild reach under the Arizona Statewide Wild and Scenic Rivers process.

**Riparian condition (1998 assessment).** Most of Lousy Canyon and the Larry Creek tributary were rated Proper Functioning Condition. Silver Creek was mixed: about one mile in proper condition, about three miles Functioning at Risk with an upward trend, and the upper Tonto reach in proper condition. Livestock do not use Lousy Canyon or the Larry Creek tributary because of terrain.

## Key Findings

**Effects assessment**
- Stocked fish are expected to suffer mortality from [[Gila Chub]] and other native predators at all three sites. Predation by nonnative fish is expected if fish disperse downstream past natural barriers.
- Silver Creek road crossings are unlikely to harm fish; the upper bedrock crossing is unlikely to be occupied, and the lower crossing is normally dry and lies about two miles below expected dispersal limits.
- Winter livestock use of Silver Creek (two months, dispersed) is not expected to cause measurable harm. Trampling or ingestion risk is considered minimal.
- Prescribed burns may cause short-term sediment influx after heavy rain, but unburned canyon buffers should largely prevent sediment reaching water. Long-term effects are expected to improve watershed function. No adverse impacts have been observed since burning began.
- Recreation is light and largely dispersed, with an immeasurable effect expected on the species.
- Private lands are downstream of all sites; fish reaching the Agua Fria mainstem are expected to perish, so no effects on private lands are anticipated.
- Both species historically co-occurred in the Gila system. Gila topminnow use the upper water column while desert pupfish use mid-column and bottom, and sites should support large numbers of both.

**Cumulative effects:** No future non-Federal actions reasonably certain to occur were identified that would harm the species. Future Federal actions require separate consultation.

**Conclusion:** The Service found that the anticipated losses are small relative to the expected population. Both species have high reproductive potential, and established populations should absorb the anticipated take with minimal impact.

**Incidental take statement**
- Take is anticipated through direct mortality during capture, transport, holding, and stocking, and through harassment, harm, and kill from livestock grazing, recreation, road use, and prescribed burning.
- Mortality must not exceed 25 percent of each species captured, stocked, held, or transported in any given action, including supplemental stocking.
- Surrogate triggers include a noticeable fish kill, streambank alteration above 10 percent, woody riparian seedling browsing above 30 percent, or loss of bank cover from causes other than natural wildfire or flood.
- Take is difficult to detect because of the fishes' small size, complex habitat, and seasonal population fluctuations.

**Reasonable and prudent measures and terms**
1. Notify the Service before stocking.
2. At Silver Creek, limit grazing and recreation so that under 10 percent of streambanks are altered, bank cover is not lost, and browsing of woody seedlings stays below 30 percent.
3. Limit prescribed burns to no more than one-half of each watershed in any two-year period, with repeat treatment at intervals longer than two years.
4. Monitor vegetation and streambanks at Silver Creek annually using BLM methods.
5. Monitor both fish populations and aquatic habitat variables at least annually with AGFD and the Service.
6. Monitor for fish kills after the first runoff event following prescribed fires.
7. Submit an annual report covering monitoring results, all incidental take, and implementation of terms for grazing, recreation, roads, and burns.

**Conservation recommendations**
- BLM could assist AGFD in monitoring extant populations and identify additional reintroduction sites.
- BLM could formalize a fire management protocol protecting reintroduction and native sites on public land.

**Procedures for dead or injured specimens:** Report findings to Service Law Enforcement within three working days and provide written notice within five calendar days, including a photograph. Specimens should go to an institution holding appropriate permits.

**Reinitiation:** Consultation must be reinitiated if take exceeds the stated limit, if new information reveals unconsidered effects, if the action is modified, or if a new species is listed or critical habitat designated that may be affected.
