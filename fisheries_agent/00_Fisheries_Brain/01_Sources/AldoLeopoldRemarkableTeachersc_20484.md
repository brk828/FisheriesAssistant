# Aldo Leopold: Remarkable Teacher-Scholar

#wildlife-ecology #environmental-ethics #land-ethic

## Executive Summary

This review essay, by [[Edward Schriver]] of the University of Maine, surveys five books on [[Aldo Leopold]] published in 1987-1988, along with a short group of natural-history titles. The essay argues that environmental problems persist because society lacks a sound land philosophy, and that Leopold's [[Land Ethic]] offers one. The reviewed works are [[Curt Meine]]'s full-length biography, two books centred on Leopold as a teacher ([[Robert A. McCabe]]'s memoir and a symposium volume), and two collections of essays, one of them a [[Companion to A Sand County Almanac]]. The reviewer concludes that none of the books is flawless or complete, but that together they introduce readers to a scholar, teacher and person of wide influence.

## Background & Description

The essay opens by noting that most Americans know little of Leopold's concepts of a land ethic and a "living land community" in which humans coexist with other creatures. The reviewer therefore treats the books as a way for scholars and the public to appreciate Leopold's achievement.

The five reviewed books are:

- **Aldo Leopold: His Life and Work** by [[Curt Meine]] (University of Wisconsin Press, 1988; xv + 638 pp.; $29.50). A full-length, though not final, biography covering Leopold's childhood, education, years in the [[Forest Service]], family life, professorship at the [[University of Wisconsin]], clash with Wisconsin hunters over deer management, and scholarly fame.
- **Aldo Leopold: The Professor** by [[Robert A. McCabe]] (Rusty Rock Press, 1987; xii + 172 pp.; $29.95). A personal account by a former student and later colleague, covering Leopold's department, teaching, his shack on the Sand County land, his work as a Wisconsin commissioner, hunting, writing and death.
- **Aldo Leopold: Mentor**, edited by Richard E. McCabe (Department of Wildlife Ecology, University of Wisconsin, 1988; iii + 126 pp.; paper $6.00). Proceedings of the Aldo Leopold Centennial Symposium held in Madison, April 23-24, 1987, containing reminiscences by Leopold's graduate students.
- **Aldo Leopold: The Man and His Legacy**, edited by Thomas Tanner (Soil Conservation Society of America, 1987; xv + 175 pp.; paper $10.00). Papers from the Aldo Leopold Centennial Celebration at Iowa State University, October 1986. Topics include the evolution of the land ethic, Leopold's ideas on agriculture, North American deer ecology, and family reminiscences.
- **Companion to A Sand County Almanac**, edited by [[J. Baird Callicott]] (University of Wisconsin Press, 1987; x + 308 pp.; $22.50). A more scholarly collection on the writing and drafts of [[A Sand County Almanac]] and its influence on American thought since 1948.

Four contributors (Susan Flader, J. Baird Callicott, Roderick Nash and Curt Meine) appear in both the Tanner and Callicott volumes. The reviewer describes Tanner's collection as more informal and Callicott's as more scholarly.

The essay also reviews a second group of titles under "A Natural History Bookshelf," including works on Martha Maxwell, the North American seal fishery, Wilbur Day, the economic and social value of wildlife, American wildlife symbolism, bird-watching, and extinction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The essay frames Leopold's work as a response to ecological damage caused by a "tunnel vision" that ignores environmental effects that do not harm people directly. Leopold's central claim, as paraphrased in the review, is that the trouble lies in human intellect developing faster than morals. The reviewer quotes Leopold: "His machines get away from him. He is still the 'Fool with a gun.'"

Several topics bear directly on wildlife and aquatic ecology:

- **Predators and "varmints."** Meine's biography examines Leopold's changing view of wolves and other varmints. The review highlights the episode in which Leopold and a crew shot at a wolf pack from a rimrock above a river. The old wolf, mortally wounded, seized Leopold's rifle butt in its teeth, and Leopold later described the "fierce green fire" in its eyes. [[Susan Flader]]'s *Thinking Like A Mountain* (1974) is recommended as a companion account of this episode.
- **Hunting.** Meine quotes Leopold's view that hunting was "an active participation in the drama of life, to be conducted in a civil manner."
- **Deer management.** Leopold's clash with Wisconsin hunters over deer management is noted as a major adult-life episode.
- **Aquatic habitat.** [[Edwin P. Pister]], a fishery biologist, contributed an article in the Tanner volume, "A Pilgrim's Progress from Group A to Group B," which the reviewer singles out as one of the most rewarding pieces in either book. The essay describes Leopold's Wisconsin haunts, including the [[Wisconsin River]] and Crane Marsh.

## Key Findings

- **Leopold's land ethic is still under-applied.** The reviewer argues that Leopold's ideas have "not been widely translated into policies," largely because most Americans are unaware of them.
- **Multiple perspectives on one life.** The five books together offer a full biography, personal memoirs, student reminiscences, and two scholarly collections, giving readers several angles on Leopold's life and thought.
- **Teaching as a moral practice.** The McCabe books present Leopold as a model of a teacher whose influence "does not stop at the lecture hall door." A symposium reminiscence describes his relationships with students as characterized by "kindness" (p. 78).
- **Respect for predators.** The wolf episode is presented as the event that shaped Leopold's later ecological attitude toward predators.
- **Human limits on wisdom.** Leopold's view that moral development must keep pace with technical power is presented as central to the current environmental crisis.
- **Strength of the collections.** The reviewer judges Tanner's volume more informal and Callicott's more scholarly. Pister's fishery essay is singled out as the most memorable single piece.
- **Overall assessment.** The reviewer concludes that "No one of these books is flawless or complete," but that each offers "significant insights into the environmental crisis" and introduces readers to a profound scholar and caring teacher.
