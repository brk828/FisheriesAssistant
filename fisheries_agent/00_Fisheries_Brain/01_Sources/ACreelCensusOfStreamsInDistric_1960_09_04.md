## Executive Summary

This 1960 completion report by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] presents a **creel census** of two trout stream sections on the [[Black River]] drainage in Arizona District I: the **East Fork** (near Diamond Rock Campground, censused 2 June–12 July) and the **West Fork** (near West Fork Campground, censused 16 July–4 September). The study quantified angler effort, catch rates, and tagged-fish returns to evaluate the effectiveness of catchable trout stocking programs and to generate stocking recommendations. Both ~1.5-mile stream sections received comparable total angling pressure (~60–66 angler-hours/mile/day), but differed substantially in catch rate, average trip length, and total tag return, prompting a broader discussion of the relationship between angler success and the recreational value of stocked streams.

## Background & Description

This report is a **fisheries management creel census**, not a morphological or taxonomic study. No species-level identification, meristic data, or morphological descriptions of the trout are provided in the source text. Fish are referred to generically as "trout" and "tagged fish," consistent with hatchery-reared **catchable-size stocked trout** typical of Arizona's put-and-take management programs (likely [[Rainbow Trout]] and/or [[Brown Trout]], though species is not specified in the original text).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Cold, flowing montane stream habitat characteristic of the [[Black River]] drainage, divided into two managed reaches — [[East Fork Black River]] and [[West Fork Black River]].
- **Stocking-supported population:** Both stream sections function as **put-and-take fisheries**, with trout populations artificially maintained via periodic stocking (~170 lb per stocking event on East Fork; variable 150–350 lb events on West Fork) rather than sustained by natural recruitment alone.
- **Movement behavior:** Weir traps installed on the West Fork after tagging revealed limited fish movement — only **3% of tagged fish moved downstream** out of the census area, and just **two trout** were recorded moving upstream into the traps, suggesting most stocked trout remained localized near the stocking site during the study period.
- **Angler-fish interaction as ecological driver:** The stream reaches function as recreational fisheries where angler pressure (measured via use counts and time-of-day sampling, see [[Figure 2]] and [[Figure 3]]) is a key structuring factor on population dynamics of the stocked cohort.

## Key Findings

**Catch and Effort Metrics**

| Metric | East Fork | West Fork |
|---|---|---|
| Census period | 2 Jun – 12 Jul 1960 | 16 Jul – 4 Sep 1960 |
| Catch per man-hour | 1.1 trout/hr | 0.4 trout/hr |
| Catch per angler trip | 2.65 trout | 2.1 trout |
| Average angler day (hours) | 2.49 hrs | 5.27 hrs |
| Fishing pressure | ~60 angler-hrs/mile/day | ~66 angler-hrs/mile/day |
| Total anglers checked | 607 | 633 |
| Total fish checked | 1,607 | 1,327 |
| % successful anglers | 72% | 58% |

**Tagging Study**

- **East Fork:** 694 tagged fish stocked (2 June); total tag return (collected + voluntary) = **362 fish (52%)**. Actual creel return likely higher due to unreturned tags.
- **West Fork:** 697 tagged fish stocked (16 July); tag return = **40%**, similarly considered an underestimate of true creel return.
- Census was conducted on **alternate days only**, meaning reported tag-return percentages substantially underrepresent total actual return to the creel.

**Fish Movement (Weir Trap Data, West Fork)**

- Only **3%** of tagged fish emigrated downstream out of the census area.
- Essentially no upstream movement detected (2 trout total in upstream trap), indicating strong retention of stocked fish near release points.

**Management Relationship — Success vs. Angler Effort**

- Despite East Fork having nearly **3× higher catch rate** than West Fork (1.1 vs. 0.4 fish/hr), total fishing **pressure was nearly identical** between the two areas (~60–66 hrs/mile/day).
- West Fork anglers fished **more than twice as long per trip** (5.27 hrs vs. 2.49 hrs) despite lower success, suggesting that **catch rate alone does not determine recreational attractiveness** of a stream — a moderate success level (~0.5 fish/hr) can sustain comparable recreational demand to a high-success area (~1 fish/hr).
- No obvious environmental or habitat difference between the two reaches was identified to explain the discrepancy in average angler-day length.

**Management Recommendations**

- Stock **600–700 lbs/week** in East Fork.
- Stock **350–400 lbs/week** in West Fork.
- Conduct **periodic monitoring** of fishing pressure and success to dynamically adjust stocking rates to match angler demand, rather than applying a fixed stocking schedule.
