# Some Notes on Fish Scales: Taxonomic Keys and Evolutionary Inferences from Scale Morphology

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pleuronectidae #soleidae #gadidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1911 paper by T. D. A. Cockerell presents preliminary notes from a larger study of fish scale morphology, based on material from the [[Bureau Of Fisheries]] stations at [[Woods Hole]] and the Bureau's Washington collections. It offers dichotomous keys for the scales of three groups: [[Flat Fishes]] (Soleidae and Pleuronectidae), [[Gadidae]] and allies including [[Macrouridae]], and selected [[Cyprinidae]] from North American and Mexican waters. The author uses scale structure to infer phylogenetic relationships, most notably concluding that the soles are not derived from the ordinary flounders. This conclusion is supported by an independent anatomical study by [[G. H. Parker]] on the optic chiasma.

## Background & Description

Cockerell had continued his work on fish scales at Woods Hole through Bureau of Fisheries support and acknowledged assistance from Dr. F. B. Sumner, Dr. Hugh M. Smith, and Dr. B. W. Evermann. He describes the notes as preliminary, written before he knew how much material he would obtain. A detailed, illustrated report was promised but not included here.

Scale characters used in the keys include:

- **Ctenoid versus cycloid margins**: [[Ctenoid Scales]] bear marginal teeth; [[Cycloid Scales]] have smooth margins.
- **Apical field**: the outer zone with teeth or spines.
- **Basal radii and circuli**: radiating grooves and concentric growth ridges near the nucleus.
- **Nucleus position**: central or subbasal.
- **Lateral areas and pitting**: sculpture on the sides of the scale.
- **[[Latinucleate Scales]]**: scales with a broadly modified nuclear region, which the author attributes to regeneration after loss of the original scales.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The taxa span a range of habitats. The flatfishes and gadids are largely marine, several recorded from the Gulf Stream, Woods Hole waters, and Pacific stations such as Albatross Station 2405, 2414, 2426, 2546, and Magdalena Bay. Deep-sea macrourids were collected at about 39°53′ N, 70°9′ W. Cyprinids are freshwater fishes from rivers and drainages in Idaho (Snake River), Oregon (Siuslaw River), Virginia (Roanoke), Colorado (Chiquito River), and Guanajuato, Mexico. The author notes that Lepidomeda scales differ from those of Gila, which helps separate the two in a region where juvenile Gila occur.

## Key Findings

### 1. Flatfish scale keys (Soleidae and Pleuronectidae)

The flatfish table is divided into three groups:

- **Group A (Soleidae, ctenoid on both sides):** includes *Achirus lineatus*, *Solea solea*, and *Symphurus piger*. *Achirus* is considered the most modified of the three, and the genera represent the subfamilies Achirinae, Soleinae, and Cynoglossinae.
- **Group B (Pleuronectidae, ctenoid above and cycloid below):** includes about ten species, including *Pseudopleuronectes americanus*, *Limanda ferruginea*, and *Hippoglossoides platessoides*. The author notes that the last two are nearly alike.
- **Group C (cycloid on both sides):** includes *Glyptocephalus cynoglossus*, *Lophopsetta maculata*, and *Paralichthys oblongus*. *Glyptocephalus* has the most degenerate scales in the whole series. The author reports that some Achirinae he did not examine are scaleless.

### 2. Evolutionary inference: the soles are not derived from flounders

Cockerell argues that flatfishes descend from fishes with ctenoid scales, and that cycloid scales arose by loss of the ctenoid character. Because a fully lost character is unlikely to return in its original form, he concludes that the Soleidae are not derived from ordinary Pleuronectidae. Instead, they likely descend from an ancient, possibly extinct, flatfish type. The living *Ancylopsetta*, which has ctenoid scales on both sides and is close to *Paralichthys*, is noted as a partial exception.

### 3. Independent support from Parker (1908)

Cockerell reports that [[G. H. Parker]] independently showed, in his 1908 paper in the *Bulletin of the Museum of Comparative Zoology*, that the optic chiasma of Soleidae resembles that of ordinary teleosts, while that of Pleuronectidae is modified. Parker concluded that "the Soleidae are not degraded Pleuronectidae, but degenerate descendants of primitive flat-fishes." Parker examined 19 Pleuronectid genera, but not *Ancylopsetta*.

### 4. Gadid and allied scale keys

The table separates three groups:

- **Group A (Macrouridae, with long apical spines):** *Macrourus bairdii*, *Macrourus* sp., *Coryphaenoides rupestris*, and *Coelorhynchus caribaeus*. The author notes that these scales must be called ctenoid, but their margins are not dentate, except slightly in *Macrourus* sp.
- **Group B (cycloid, without spines, lacking radii):** includes *Enchelyopus cimbrius* (with a median transverse furrow), *Merluccius bilinearis* (with finely pitted surfaces above the nucleus), and *Urophycis regius*.
- **Group C (cycloid, with radii):** includes *Brosme brosme* (very long and narrow scales, about 4 mm by 1.3 mm), *Melanogrammus aeglefinus* (about 7–8 mm by 4–5 mm), *Gadus callarias* and *Microgadus tomcod* (intermediate, 5–8 mm), and *Pollachius virens* (smallest, about 2 mm).

### 5. Phylogenetic inference from gadid scales

The author finds that the macrourid scales, despite differences in shape and spines, resemble those of Merlucciidae and Phycidae in having angled circuli and no radii. He suggests that a macrourid-like ancestor may have given rise to the *Merluccius*, *Urophycis*, and *Enchelyopus* lines. In contrast, the Gadinae and Brosminae scales differ fundamentally from the others and probably have a separate origin. Gadinae and Brosminae scales are essentially alike in structure, although the author found nearly all of his *Brosme* scales to be latinucleate.

### 6. Cyprinid species descriptions

Cockerell describes five North American and Mexican cyprinids:

1. ***Algansea sallaei***: subquadrate scales, about 2 mm long, with basal radii. The author notes that this is one of the few American cyprinids with basal radii.
2. ***Mylocheilus caurinus***: large scales (about 5.5 mm long, 5 mm broad), with no basal radii and about 25 apical radii.
3. ***Richardsonius siuslawi***: typical of the subgenus *Richardsonius*, with about 18 apical radii.
4. ***Exoglossum maxillingua***: scales about 2.16–2.34 mm long, with about 20 apical radii. Structure is close to *Mylocheilus*.
5. ***Lepidomeda vittata***: transversely oval scales with about 7 strong apical radii and no basal radii. These differ from those of *Gila*.

### 7. Terminology and dimorphism

The author notes that the latinucleate condition is common among fish scales. He interprets it as a regeneration response following loss of the original scales, rather than a distinct taxonomic character.
