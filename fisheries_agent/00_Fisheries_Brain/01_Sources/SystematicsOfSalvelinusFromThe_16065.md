# Systematics of Salvelinus from the North Pacific Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This chapter, by T. M. Cavender, presents a morphological comparison of charrs (genus [[Salvelinus]]) from the North Pacific basin, with emphasis on the newly redefined [[Bull Trout]] (*S. confluentus*). The study compares Pacific taxa with North American and Eurasian relatives, including [[Lake Trout]] (*S. namaycush*), [[Brook Trout]] (*S. fontinalis*) and [[Arctic Charr]] (*S. alpinus*). Characters examined include scale and lateral line structure, infraorbital bones, head form, osteology (premaxilla, supraethmoid, vomer, hyomandibula), gill rakers and meristic counts. The author argues that the origin of the genus predates the [[Pleistocene]] and that the current distribution of Pacific charrs was established millions of years before the first ice age.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** The taxonomy of North Pacific *Salvelinus* was described as poorly understood, partly because of limited preserved material and language barriers between English-speaking and Japanese or Russian ichthyologists.
- **Distribution of [[Dolly Varden]] (*S. malma*):** Spans the North Pacific arc from the Sea of Japan and Kamchatka across the [[Aleutian Islands]] to southern Alaska and northern California, and north to the Arctic coast. It is generally anadromous, with many inland populations.
- **Specimen base:** Material came mainly from the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ), the National Museum of Natural Sciences, Canada (NMC), the United States National Museum (USNM) and the Ohio State University Museum of Zoology (OSUM). Specimen lists cover Japanese, Korean, Kamchatkan, Siberian, Alaskan, Canadian, Greenlandic, Icelandic, Norwegian, Swiss and North American collections. Exact lots are listed in the source and are not reproduced here.
- **Methods:** Counts and measurements follow [[Cavender 1978]]. Line drawings were made with a Wild stereoscope and camera lucida. Meristic data are summarized as means and ranges only.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Feeding and habitat adaptation:** Subterminal mouths and lateral eye positions in *S. malma* and *S. alpinus* are interpreted as adaptations for feeding off the bottom. Dorsal eye positions in *S. namaycush* and *S. confluentus*, which rest on the bottom and ambush prey, are interpreted as benthic adaptations. The lateral eye and compressed body of *S. leucomaenis* are associated with probable pelagic habits.
- **Gill raker function:** Two adaptive types are recognized. The toothed type in piscivorous species grasps large, struggling prey. The toothless, long-tapered type in *S. malma* and *S. alpinus* handles small invertebrates such as crustaceans and insects.
- **Biogeography and evolution:** Repeated evolutionary trends occur on both sides of the Pacific and Atlantic. Reduction in pyloric caeca and development of subterminal mouths appear independently in different regions.
- **Glaciation:** Glacial events strongly influenced the modern ranges of *S. malma* and *S. alpinus*, but the author argues that the basic distribution of the genus predates the glaciations.
- **Hybridization:** Enlarged mandibular pores and high pyloric caecum counts in *S. alpinus aureolus* from [[Sunapee Lake]], New Hampshire, are interpreted as evidence of hybridization with introduced *S. namaycush*.

## Key Findings

**Generic definition**
- *Salvelinus* is defined by minute scales (at least 195 in a lateral series, usually over 200) and modified lateral line scales that appear as ossified tubes. These are shared by all members, including *S. namaycush*.
- Five infraorbital bones (versus six in other salmonines) and two epurals are considered derived characters. Six infraorbitals occur as a variation.
- The shape of infraorbital 2 is diagnostic for *S. fontinalis*.

**Head and morphology**
- Two groups are recognized by jaw length. Lower-jawed forms include *S. malma* and some *S. alpinus*.
- *S. profundus* (Bodensee) has the most subterminal mouth. *S. malma* and *S. alpinus* have a sloping, gabled skull roof.
- Head size, as a proportion of standard length, falls into three groups: large (*S. namaycush*, *S. confluentus*, *S. fontinalis*), small (*S. malma*, *S. alpinus*), and intermediate (*S. pluvius*, *S. imbrius*, *S. leucomaenis*).

**Osteology**
- The supraethmoid is elongated in *S. namaycush*, *S. confluentus* and *S. fontinalis*, and short and rounded in *S. alpinus* and some *S. malma*. The supraethmoid reliably separates *S. confluentus* from *S. malma*.
- The hyomandibula groups *S. alpinus* and *S. malma* (adductor ridge, plesiomorphic) against *S. namaycush*, *S. confluentus*, *S. fontinalis* and *S. pluvius* (broad concave face).
- Vomerine tooth crest is best developed in *S. namaycush* and reduced through *S. alpinus*, *S. fontinalis* and *S. malma*.

**Gill rakers**
- Toothed marginal rakers occur in *S. confluentus*, *S. namaycush*, *S. fontinalis*, *S. leucomaenis*, *S. pluvius* and *S. imbrius*.
- *S. malma* and *S. alpinus* lack marginal teeth. Their rakers are a derived state, and the variation within them is slight.

**Conclusions**
1. *Salvelinus* contains at least seven fully differentiated species: *S. namaycush*, *S. fontinalis*, *S. confluentus*, *S. alpinus*, *S. malma*, *S. leucomaenis* and *S. pluvius*. *S. imbrius* and *S. profundus* may also merit species status, but samples were inadequate.
2. *Salvelinus* shares features with [[Hucho]] and [[Brachymystax]], including a short vomer and elongate supraethmoid. The genera may share a common North Pacific ancestor.
3. The genus originated well before the Pleistocene. The oldest known fossil, dated to about ten million years, comes from northern Nevada. An earlier salmonine fossil, *Eosalmo driftwoodensis*, is from British Columbia (about fifty million years old).
4. *S. confluentus* and *S. malma* are not each other's closest relatives. *S. malma* is sister to *S. alpinus*, and *S. confluentus* may be sister to that pair.
5. *S. pluvius* is closely related to *S. leucomaenis*. The separation of *S. imbrius* from *S. leucomaenis* is doubtful.
6. *S. fontinalis* is distinct. Its supraethmoid resembles that of *S. namaycush*, and its skull roof resembles that of *S. alpinus*.
7. Northern and southern eastern Pacific *S. malma* differ in vertebral and gill raker counts. Western Pacific *S. malma* does not align clearly with either.
8. *S. alpinus* combines high gill raker and pyloric caeca counts. Alaskan *S. alpinus* differs from Greenland fish in head size, caeca and vertebral number.
9. The lack of marginal gill raker teeth separates *S. alpinus* and *S. malma* from the other species.
10. Breeding males of *S. alpinus*, *S. malma*, *S. fontinalis* and probably *S. pluvius japonicus* show marked skull modification. *S. leucomaenis* and *S. namaycush* show little or none.
11. The "primitive" versus "derived" interpretation depends on the character. Low vertebral numbers are considered primitive for salmonids, while high numbers in *S. confluentus* are considered derived. High pyloric caeca numbers are considered primitive, and reduction is an evolutionary trend.
12. Several characters point to parallel evolution. Pyloric caeca reduction and subterminal mouths arose independently on both sides of the Pacific and Atlantic.
