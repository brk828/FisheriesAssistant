# Limnological Survey of Nevada Spring Systems Research Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #desert-ecology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This research proposal by James Everett Deacon (Associate Professor, Nevada Southern University) seeks funding to conduct comprehensive limnological surveys of spring systems in Nevada, particularly the [[Pluvial White River System]], [[Moapa Valley]], and [[Ash Meadows]]. The proposal addresses critical gaps in water chemistry data and ecological information for these rapidly disappearing desert spring habitats. Budget requested: $975.00 for principal investigator time and student assistants (20 hours/week for 12 weeks at $1.50/hour). The study aims to document baseline conditions before further development and water table lowering alter these unique aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

Previous work on Nevada spring systems has been conducted by [[Clark Hubbs]] and colleagues (1922-1964), [[La Rivers]] (1962), and [[Deacon]] et al. However, comprehensive limnological data remains incomplete. La Rivers (1962) provided information on waters of [[Ash Meadows]], [[Moapa Valley]], and [[White River Valley]]. Extensive work on insect fauna has been published in numerous papers (1947-1956), but invertebrate checklists remain extremely incomplete. No meaningful limnological comparisons can be made among springs due to sketchy or completely absent data.

The [[White River Valley System]] may connect with the [[Pluvial White River System]]. Survey locations include several springs in the Pluvial White River System, multiple sites along [[Moapa River]], and at least ten additional springs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

These desert spring systems are rapidly being altered by:
- Development for irrigation
- Commercial uses
- Lowering of water table

The springs support endemic fish species and unique invertebrate communities adapted to thermal conditions. Previous research has documented thermophilous animals and their tolerance of high temperatures and low dissolved oxygen. The fauna includes endemic cyprinid fishes and specialized invertebrate taxa.

Water chemistry parameters to be measured include: alkalinity, calcium, chlorine, copper, fluoride, hardness, magnesium hardness, hydrogen sulfide, iron, nitrate, nitrite, oxygen, pH, phosphate (ortho and meta), silica, sulfate, turbidity, and temperature. Analysis of principal cations will utilize atomic absorption spectrophotometry.
