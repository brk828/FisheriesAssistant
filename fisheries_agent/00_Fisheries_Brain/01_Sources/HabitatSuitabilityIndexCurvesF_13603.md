# Habitat Suitability Index Curves for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This final report (BIO/WEST, Inc., prepared for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], June 1987) presents interim [[Habitat Suitability Index]] (HSI) curves for depth, velocity, substrate and habitat type for three endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin (UCRB): [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The curves were built for use with the [[Physical Habitat Simulation System]] (PHABSIM) within the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM). The [[Bonytail Chub]] was excluded for lack of data.

The work assimilated about 100,000 database records from 99 studies (1964–1986) by 17 principal investigators. Two expert workshops, run by consensus, reduced 291 data partitions to 56 preliminary curve sets and then to 18 final curve sets covering 32 size categories or life functions. The Service has not endorsed the curves; they are explicitly interim and are expected to be refined as research continues.

## Background & Description

The report was requested because the 1984 FWS curves were criticized for lack of expert interaction, weak documentation, and use of only 1979–1984 FWS data. This project therefore pooled data from FWS, the Colorado Water Congress, Bureau of Reclamation, Colorado Division of Wildlife and Utah Division of Wildlife Resources.

Habitat Suitability Index curves give relative utilization from 0.0 (nonuse) to 1.0 (optimal) for variables such as depth, velocity, substrate, cover and temperature. The project followed six tasks:

- **Task A – Database assimilation:** [[FWS Database]] and [[CWC Database]] were merged; only rare-fish records entered the working database (~8,100 records). Summary tables (108) were produced.
- **Task B – Workshop #1 (Salt Lake City, January 5–9, 1987):** 17 attendees, including five species experts. Nine criteria (life stage, river, strata, year, gear, time of year, time of day, sample design, habitat parameters) were used to partition data.
- **Task C – Draft report:** statistical pooling via [[Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test]] (depth, velocity) and [[Chi-Square Test]] (substrate); curves fitted with exponential polynomial, exponential density and generalized Poisson models.
- **Task D – Workshop #2 (Fort Collins, March 30–April 3, 1987):** 16 attendees, including six experts. Consensus was defined as four of six experts agreeing.
- **Tasks E–F:** final curve sets, documentation, constraints and recommendations.

Table 1 schedule: database summaries completed December 12, 1986; final report June 19, 1987.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Data coverage and gaps.** Most records came from March–October; no winter data existed. No spawning-habitat data existed for razorback sucker or humpback chub. Temperature was rarely recorded, and cover was almost never recorded. Depth, velocity and substrate were the only consistently available variables. Substrate was classified on a modified [[Wentworth Scale]].

**Habitat types.** Habitat codes included backwater, eddy, embayment, concavity, pool, shoreline, run, riffle, rapid, isolated pool and gravel pit. Several curve sets were dominated by backwaters or eddies, which motivated the [[General Habitat Constraint]].

**Sampling biases.** Radiotelemetry reached only about 6 m of water depth in Black Rocks (maximum depth 18 m), so deep habitat was undersampled. Conventional gears (electrofishing, gill and trammel nets, seines) tended to capture fish in shallow shorelines.

**Key habitat sites.** [[Black Rocks]] (Colorado River), [[Westwater Canyon]], [[Cataract Canyon]], [[Yampa Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]] were the main humpback chub strongholds. Spawning sites for Colorado squawfish were confirmed only in the Yampa and Green rivers.

**Non-native species.** The report notes about 40 introduced fish species in the upper basin and flags competition, predation and disease as unassessed risks.

## Key Findings

**Methodological outcomes**
- 291 data partitions identified at Workshop #1; 56 preliminary curve sets from them; 18 final curve sets (32 size categories or life functions) at Workshop #2.
- Category 1 (judgment) and Category 2 (utilization) labels were dropped for the final curves because nearly all were modified by expert judgment.
- Radiotelemetry data were preferred over conventional gears for adult Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker after statistical tests showed significant gear differences.
- A "left tail rule" set depth and velocity curves to intercept the x-axis 0.1 ft or ft/s below the observed minimum.

**Final curve sets by species**

*Colorado squawfish (curve sets 1–12):*
- Size 1 (<25 mm): pooled from all rivers, confidence A; sampled with seines July–October.
- Size 2 (25–149 mm): pooled Colorado, Green and Yampa rivers, confidence A; sampled March–November. 85.47% of samples came from backwaters, triggering the General Habitat Constraint.
- Size 3 (150–299 mm): sample size 77 for depth, confidence C+.
- Size 4 (300–399 mm): sample size 76 for depth, confidence C+.
- Size 5 (400+ mm, nonspawning adults): separate radiotelemetry curves for Green River (n=698, A), Colorado River April–June (n=120, A) and July–November (n=89, A), Yampa River April–June (n=69, C) and July–September (n=121, B), and White River (n=149, C). Duchesne River uses Green River curves (C).
- Staging and deposition (spawning): Green and Yampa pooled data (confidence B for staging, C– for deposition); Colorado River application rated D.

*Humpback chub (curve sets 13–16):*
- Size 1 (<21 mm): depth and velocity borrowed from Colorado squawfish size 1 (confidence D); substrate and habitat not developed.
- Size 2 (21–74 mm): Colorado and Green rivers pooled, confidence C; 48.53% backwater samples.
- Sizes 3 & 4: separate Green River (C) and Colorado River (C) curve sets.
- Size 5 (260+ mm, adults): pooled three rivers across all gears, confidence B (n=286 fish).
- Spawning: no curves; adult curves assumed to cover spawning needs.

*Razorback sucker (curve sets 1, 17, 18):*
- Size 1: borrowed from Colorado squawfish size 1 (confidence D–).
- Sizes 2 and 3: no curves; experts assumed Colorado squawfish young and juvenile curves would cover them.
- Size 4 (400+ mm): Green River radiotelemetry only, split May–June (n=15, C) and July–October (n=18, C); applied to Colorado and Yampa rivers with lower confidence.
- Spawning: no curves; radiotracking planned for spring 1987.

**Data gaps identified by experts**
- Winter habitat (November–April) for all three species.
- Spawning areas for humpback chub and razorback sucker.
- Reliable field and lab identification of larval and juvenile humpback chub, which were confused with other Gila species.
- Passage and migration requirements across dams and diversions.
- Cover and temperature requirements; no temperature curves were produced.
- Secondary substrate, which the database recorded but the analysis did not use.

**General Habitat Constraint.** Curve sets should not be used with current PHABSIM analysis if: (1) 25% or more of the data came from backwaters, concavities, isolated pools, embayments or gravel pits; (2) 50% or more came from eddies; or (3) 60% or more came from combined categories 1 and 2. Eight of the 18 curve sets (sets 1–3, 6, 8, 11, 13, 14 and the constrained categories) fall under the constraint.

**Recommendations**
1. Refine PHABSIM habitat simulation so it can resolve the habitats used by the rare fishes; joint work by species experts and hydrologists was urged.
2. Refine temperature models to daily resolution, calibrated for each major river.
3. Consider secondary substrate in future curve development.
4. Centralize the basin database with annual updates and standardized formats.

**Project conclusions.** The FWS Region 6 letter accompanying the report states the curves may need refinement before reliable PHABSIM outputs, and that they had not yet been tested against PHABSIM sites. Prospective users should consult the Colorado River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator.
