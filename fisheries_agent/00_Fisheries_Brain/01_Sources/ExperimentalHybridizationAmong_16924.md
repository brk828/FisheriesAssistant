# Experimental Hybridization Among Three Coregonine Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #coregonine

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by [[E. T. Garside]] and [[W. J. Christie]] tested reciprocal and diallel crosses among three coregonine fishes: [[Lake Whitefish]] (*Coregonus clupeaformis*), [[Cisco]] (*Leucichthys artedii*, treated as *Coregonus artedii* in the footnote) and [[Round Whitefish]] (*Prosopium cylindraceum*). The work was originally meant to test whether egg (yolk) size relates to metameric differentiation in embryos. Excessive mortality in rearing prevented meristic and morphometric analysis, so the report is mainly a description of gross embryology, mortality and abnormalities.

Three of the nine crosses hatched: [[Coregonus × Leucichthys]], [[Coregonus × Prosopium]] and [[Leucichthys × Coregonus]]. The authors describe the Coregonus × Prosopium hatch as the first known successful hybridization between the genera *Coregonus* and *Prosopium*. The other three crosses involving *Prosopium* or *Leucichthys × Prosopium* were invariably deformed and died before hatching.

## Background & Description

Coregonine fishes are northern freshwater whitefishes and ciscoes. The study compares eggs of three species that differ in size, which the authors used to test [[Egg Size and Embryonic Differentiation]] following [[Garside and Fry (1959)]].

Notes on the species and nomenclature:

- **Lake Whitefish** (*Coregonus clupeaformis*, Mitchill): netted a week before spawning from the Bay of Quinte.
- **Cisco** (*Leucichthys artedii*, LeSueur): netted with the lake whitefish; the authors state that *Leucichthys* equals *Coregonus* under the 1960 American Fisheries Society name list.
- **Round Whitefish** (*Prosopium cylindraceum*, Pallas): 26 live individuals were trucked from Dorcas Bay, Bruce County, to the [[Glenora Fisheries Station]] on 27 November 1959.

Sperm and eggs were stripped on 28 November 1959. The lake whitefish sperm × cisco egg cross was made the next day. Eggs were mixed into wet humus to reduce clumping from adhesive mucus. Each of the nine crosses was incubated in three vessels, with several thousand eggs per cross. The incubation apparatus is a miniature version of the jar hatchery used for adhesive eggs. It uses 50 × 400 mm test tubes fed from a 40-litre constant-level reservoir, giving gentle agitation.

Water came from Lake-on-the-Mountain, a natural reservoir on the height of land south of the station. The station could not control temperature, so eggs experienced ambient surface lake temperatures (Table 1: 10-day means from 3.5 °C in late November to 5.6 °C in late April, with winter means near 1 °C).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors frame the study in terms of reproductive isolation and phylogeny. They cite [[Moenkhaus (1910)]] as making the first experimental hybridization between two coregonines, the reciprocal whitefish × cisco crosses, which produced viable progeny. [[Hubbs (1955)]] reported that these crosses occur, though infrequently, in nature. The round whitefish had not been crossed with other coregonids in the laboratory or in nature before this study.

Egg-size effects are discussed in relation to salmonid crosses, where progeny from the smaller-egg cross are often less viable. The authors note this is "by no means a rule" for fish hybrids, citing [[Hubbs and Drewry (1959)]].

Post-hatch conditions were also an environmental limit. Epilimnion water in Lake-on-the-Mountain reached 25 °C in June 1960, killing most remaining fry. Surviving fry were moved to tanks supplied with hypolimnion water pumped from Adolphus Reach, Bay of Quinte.

## Key Findings

### Crosses and hatching

- Fertilization succeeded in all nine diallel crosses. The three parental controls (C × C, L × L, P × P) developed normally and hatched.
- Three hybrid crosses hatched: Coregonus × Leucichthys, Coregonus × Prosopium and Leucichthys × Coregonus.
- Three hybrid crosses failed to complete development: Leucichthys × Prosopium, Prosopium × Coregonus and Prosopium × Leucichthys. All of their embryos died before the expected hatching time.
- The Coregonus × Prosopium hatch is described as the first known successful hybridization between *Coregonus* and *Prosopium*.

### Developmental timing (Table 2)

- In the non-hatching lots (L × P, P × C, P × L), development was delayed, beginning at closure of the blastopore, and there was considerable delay before the stages preceding die-off.
- Hatching lots showed no appreciable delay.
- Median hatching days ranged from 138 to 142 across the lots reported.

- Abnormality incidence strongly tracks viability: the non-viable crosses had the highest deformity rates.
- All living embryos collected after the 28th day in P × C and P × L were deformed. The 20th-day threshold applies to the L × P lot.
- Infertile eggs were excluded from mortality counts.

### Abnormalities

- **Coregonus × Leucichthys:** only three deformed embryos (one microcephalic, two with truncated bodies).
- **Coregonus × Prosopium:** only one of 553 live embryos deformed.
- **Leucichthys × Coregonus:** no deformed embryos.
- **Leucichthys × Prosopium:** fin fold absent; failure of development posterior to the anus; microphthalmia, anophthalmia and microcephaly; development arrested after eye pigmentation, about 10 days later than controls. The last embryos died on day 107.
- **Prosopium × Coregonus:** truncated bodies, imperfect caudal peduncles, microphthalmia and microcephaly. No survivors past day 124.
- **Prosopium × Leucichthys:** microphthalmia, microcephaly and truncated bodies; no development past eye pigmentation; all embryos dead by day 81.

### Post-hatch rearing and survivors

- Early diet was brine shrimp (*Artemia* nauplii), then finely ground raw beef liver. Large particles fouled the gills and kept opercula from closing, causing high mortality. Commercially cooked, strained liver solved this.
- Survivors on 1 October 1960: L × L, 1; C × L, 6; L × C, 6; C × P, 3. Control lots of lake whitefish and round whitefish were lost during the summer.

### Interpretation

- Reciprocal incompatibility in the Prosopium × Leucichthys pair (L × P also arrested) suggests "fundamental disturbances in the processes of cell and organ differentiation" rather than an egg-size effect alone.
- The Coregonus × Prosopium success and the failure of its reciprocal (P × C) might suggest a size effect, but the arrest pattern in P × C matches the other *Prosopium* failures, so the authors do not attribute it to size.
- Germ compatibility may relate to phylogenetic distance, in line with [[Hubbs and Drewry's Hypothesis]]: fertile F1 hybrids come from intrageneric crosses, and F1 hybrids that can be reared indefinitely but never mature come from intergeneric crosses. The authors did not test this with the present data.
- Planned follow-up for 1961 included long-term rearing and crosses among F1 hybrid progeny.
