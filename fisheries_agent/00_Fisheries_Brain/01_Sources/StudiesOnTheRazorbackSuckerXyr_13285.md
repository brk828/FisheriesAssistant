# Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) Life History Study Proposal, 1974

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1974 revised research proposal, submitted by the [[Lower Colorado River Basin Research Laboratory]] at [[Arizona State University]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Endangered Species Program, Region 2), requests $10,000 to study the life history of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]]. The work would focus on wild fish in [[Lake Mohave]], supplemented by hatchery stock from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and the [[Page Springs Hatchery]]. The stated goal is ecological data for a management plan that would allow the species to become self-sustaining in nature and be removed from the endangered species list. The proposal covers the period 18 June 1974 to 17 June 1975, with a suggested multi-year extension of up to five years or more.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker was once widely distributed in most larger streams of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. From about the 1930s it declined rapidly across much of its range. It became extinct in the [[Gila River]] system in the 1950s. Remnant populations persist in mainstem impoundments of the Colorado River. Much smaller populations occur in flowing reaches of the Colorado River below [[Lee's Ferry]] and in the mainstem and major tributaries north of the Arizona–Utah border.

The document describes the species as unique and endemic to the basin. It is a long-lived fish that appears to grow slowly to maturity. The proposal notes that its taxonomy and distribution are well documented in the literature, and that the bibliography contains about 80 references, mostly 19th- and 20th-century faunal surveys and regional fish checklists for the Colorado, Gila, and Great Basin drainages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal identifies several poorly known aspects of the species' biology that can be studied efficiently because hatchery stock is available:

- **Early life stages:** embryology and post-embryonic development, using hatchery juveniles and preserved material where possible.
- **Sex ratios:** the proportion of sexes in young fish and how it may change with age.
- **[[Sexual Dimorphism]]:** external secondary sexual characteristics, with the goal of a reliable sexing criterion that does not require sacrificing fish.
- **[[Fecundity]] and fertility:** of sexually mature fish across the age structure of the population.
- **Reproduction:** spawning requirements, periodicity, and detailed breeding behavior.
- **Diet:** foods and feeding habits relative to food availability for adults and young.
- **Distribution:** in the lower Colorado River, with emphasis on Lake Mohave, which seemingly holds the largest known population.
- **Population structure:** size and age structure, estimated mainly by indirect methods.

Field methods proposed include [[SCUBA Diving]], night observation with lights, [[Electrofishing]], seines, underwater photography, and minimal netting to limit post-capture mortality. The proposal also plans to use sonar to track razorback schools and possibly to help assess stocks in Lake Mohave.

## Key Findings

**Study plan and sampling limits**
- A literature review on taxonomy, distribution, and life history was "essentially finished" at the time of writing.
- Wild adult sacrifice is limited to a maximum of **50 adult wild razorback suckers per year** for analyses. Fish killed inadvertently during collection would be allowed. The proposal asks for no comparable limit on sacrifice of hatchery-reared fish.

**Budget (10-month period, $10,000 total)**

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Graduate research assistant and undergraduate hourly assistants (including 1.0% workman's compensation) | $6,000.00 |
| Operations (expendable supplies and equipment upkeep) | $800.00 |
| Travel and per diem | $1,200.00 |
| Capital (one 220-volt generator for electrofishing) | $600.00 |
| Indirect costs (about 15% of direct costs, less capital) | $1,400.00 |
| **Total** | **$10,000.00** |

The indirect cost rate follows the Region 2 limit of no more than 15% of direct costs (less capital items) for projects funded at $10,000 per year or less.

**Contract terms**
- Period: 18 June 1974 to 17 June 1975.
- Multi-year: a project of up to five years, or longer, is suggested, with funding negotiated annually or within a predetermined time frame.
- Payment: reimbursement quarterly on university billing. Payment for the final month is withheld until the final report is received. The final report and payment are due within three months after the contract ends.
- Reporting: quarterly reports are due within 15 days after each quarter of the annual study.
- Publication: data are to be cleared for open-literature publication within a negotiated period of no more than six months after the final report is transmitted.

**Overall conclusion:** The proposal presents a targeted, low-impact program to fill specific gaps in razorback sucker life history knowledge. Its purpose is to support a self-sustaining management plan for a species that has lost most of its historic range.
