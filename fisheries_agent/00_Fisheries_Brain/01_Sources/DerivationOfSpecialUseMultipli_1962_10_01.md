## Executive Summary

This document is not a taxonomic or morphological study of a fish species; rather, it is a **National Park Service administrative memorandum** from Lake Mead National Recreation Area (LMNRA) describing a statistical method for estimating recreational fishing pressure and other special uses (boating, swimming, skiing, camping, picnicking) from traffic counter data. It also includes **visitor-use statistics for December 1962** and **hatchery fish stocking requirement tables** for 1963–1964 from the **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery**, which supplied game fish to Lower Colorado River management areas (Needles to Davis Dam, Lower Mohave Lake, Eldorado Canyon to Hoover Dam, and Glen Canyon Tailwaters). While no morphological or taxonomic fish data are given, the report is highly relevant to understanding historical **fisheries management, stocking effort, and angler-use intensity** on the [[Lower Colorado River]] and [[Lake Mead]]/[[Lake Mohave]] system.

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic data on fish species are provided in this source. The document exclusively addresses **visitor-use statistics** and **hatchery stocking poundage**, not species-level biological descriptions. Fish are referenced generically as "Fishermen" (visitor use category) or as bulk stocking weights (lbs.) delivered from the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While explicit habitat/niche data are absent, the document implies a managed put-and-take fishery across several distinct hydrological zones of the Lower Colorado River system, each treated as a separate "Management Area" for stocking purposes:

- **Area 1 — Needles to Davis Dam**: Riverine stretch below Davis Dam, likely warmwater habitat.
- **Area 2 — Lower Mohave Lake**: Reservoir habitat behind Davis Dam ([[Lake Mohave]]).
- **Area 3 — Eldorado Canyon to Hoover Dam**: Riverine/reservoir transition zone near [[Hoover Dam]] tailwaters.
- **Area 4 — Glen Canyon Tailwaters**: Coldwater tailwater fishery below Glen Canyon Dam, consistent with stocking patterns favoring coldwater species (e.g., rainbow trout) in tailwater reaches.

Recreational fishing effort is also broken down spatially and seasonally across NPS developed areas within [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]]: [[Boulder Beach]], [[Las Vegas Wash]], [[Overton]], [[Temple Bar]], [[Katherine]], [[Cottonwood Cove]], and [[Willow Beach]] — each showing distinct seasonal fishing-use multipliers and visitor origin patterns (Arizona, California, Nevada, Utah, Other).

## Key Findings

### Multiplier Methodology

A statistical formula was developed to convert mechanical traffic-counter readings into estimates of "Special Use" (fishermen, boaters, skiers, swimmers, campers, picnickers, cabin-sitters) using weighted weekday/weekend sampling:

$$C = \frac{[a/A \times (B/7)] + 2[b/B \times (B/7)]}{3}$$

(formula reconstructed from garbled OCR; weekday weighted once, weekend weighted twice, divided by 3 to normalize to a 7-day week).

Worked example: Temple Bar, October — weekday count (37 cars, 50 anglers) and weekend count (85 cars, 41 anglers) yielded a derived multiplier of **C = 1.102** fishermen per car for that month.

- Temple Bar and Cottonwood Cove showed the highest fishermen-per-car ratios, suggesting these access points served disproportionately angler-focused visitor bases.
- Fishermen use peaked in different months by district (e.g., Temple Bar peaked in September–October; Cottonwood Cove peaked April–October), indicating seasonal variability likely tied to species spawning/feeding behavior, water temperature, and holiday travel patterns.

### Visitor Origin Patterns

- Nevada residents dominated visitation at most districts (up to 73% at Las Vegas Wash), while California residents were more prevalent at Cottonwood Cove (54% average) and Willow Beach.
- Arizona-origin visitors were most represented at Temple Bar (35%) and Katherine (42%), consistent with proximity to Arizona access roads.

### December 1962 Visitor & Fisheries Use Statistics

- Total recreational visitors (December 1962): **164,475**; Year-to-date: **2,688,745**.
- **Fishermen (December 1962): 20,975**; Year-to-date: **394,485**.
- Boats launched: 5,335 (month); 170,295 (YTD).
- Notable month-over-month decline (−17.9%) but year-over-year increase (+52.8%) in total recreational visitation, reflecting growing postwar recreational demand on the reservoir system.

- The marked increase in total requested stocking poundage from 1963 to 1964 (+29,000 lbs., ~16%) suggests rising angler demand and/or expanding put-and-take management, particularly at **Lower Mohave Lake**, which saw the largest single-quarter increase (Q1: 17,000 → 35,000 lbs.).
- The introduction of **Glen Canyon Tailwaters** as a stocking area in both years indicates active management of a coldwater tailwater fishery, likely supporting introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] populations below Glen Canyon Dam, consistent with known regional stocking practices of that era.

### Interpretive Note

This document underscores mid-20th-century fisheries management practice on the Lower Colorado River: reliance on **traffic-based statistical sampling** rather than direct creel surveys to estimate angler pressure, and coordinated **interstate hatchery stocking** (Arizona, California, Nevada) to sustain a heavily used, artificially maintained recreational fishery across a hydrologically fragmented reservoir/tailwater system.
