# Aravaipa Creek Seine Monitoring Data, 25–26 June 1994

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This interagency memorandum from [[Arizona State University]] (Minckley, Velasco and Timmons, Department of Zoology) transmits seine-sampling and habitat data for [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, collected 25–26 June 1994. Sampling covered east-end and west-end stream reaches, including canyon-bound sections, and recorded habitat composition, vegetation cover, water temperature, and fish species presence. Recipients included the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], and [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Several native and listed-status cyprinids and catostomids were captured, including breeding [[Loach Minnow]] and tuberculate [[Gila Mountain Sucker]] adults, alongside non-native species.

## Background & Description

The memo covers one field event in a single creek system, so it is a short monitoring record rather than a full study. Fish were collected with a 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot [[Seine Sampling]] net over 200 m reaches. Collectors were Velasco and Timmons of Arizona State University. Each station was described by percent habitat type (run, riffle, pool), mean width and depth, and percent cover of emergent vegetation, submergent vegetation, and overstory.

Station locations were:
- **East end, mainstream:** Station 1, 200 m reach starting 400 m upstream of the TNC guest house; Station 2, starting at Chimney Rock and extending 200 m downstream.
- **Canyon-bound:** Station 3, 200 m upstream of the WA parking area near the Aravaipa Canyon trailhead (labelled "electric closure fence" in source); Station 5, from the west-end WA gauge for 200 m.
- **West end:** Station 1, from the White's Ranch hay barn for 200 m downstream; Station 2, from the Sycamore Tree picnic site for 200 m downstream.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat characteristics**

- East-end Station 1 was 80% run, 15% riffle and 5% pool, with mean width 1.5 m and mean depth 0.4 m. Emergent vegetation was 80%, submergent 5%, overstory under 5%.
- East-end Station 2 was 65% run, 30% riffle and 5% pool, with mean width 3 m. Emergent vegetation was 65%, submergent 10%, overstory under 50%.
- Canyon-bound Station 3 was 45% run, 45% riffle and 10% pool, with mean width 4 m and mean depth 0.3 m. Emergent vegetation was 20%, submergent 5%, overstory 40%.
- Canyon-bound Station 5 was 90% run and 10% riffle, with mean width 4.5 m. Emergent vegetation and submergent vegetation were under 5%, overstory 10%.
- West-end Station 1 was 70% riffle, 25% run and 5% pool, with mean width 5.5 m. Emergent vegetation was under 5%, submergent under 5%, overstory under 5%.
- West-end Station 2 was 50% riffle and 50% run, with mean width 6 m. Emergent vegetation was under 5%, submergent 5%, overstory under 5%.

Some depth values in the source are garbled by OCR and are not reported here.

**Weather and temperature**

June conditions were very hot and moderately breezy. Daytime air temperatures at the east end averaged 35 °C (low 20 °C, high 40 °C). West-end temperatures averaged 42 °C (low 32 °C, high 48 °C).

**Channel change and invasive vegetation**

[[Watercress]] (*Nasturtium* sp.) was predominant at east-end sites, narrowing the stream channel to under 1 m at the Chimney Rock and east-end guest house sites. The White's Ranch site had aggraded noticeably since the prior year's sampling; a 2 m deep plug of sand and gravel had filled most of the embedded boulder habitat.

**Access**

A new gate at the Brandenburg campsite now limits west-end vehicle access; only local residents may drive beyond it.

**Vertebrate observations (non-fish)**

- Two [[Zone-Tailed Hawk]] (*Buteo albonotatus*) at the east-end guest house site.
- A congregation of 9–10 herons or egrets (taxon garbled in OCR) fishing near Geldmacher's crossing, west end.
- A [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense*) downstream of the Normandy Ranch site.
- A [[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]] (*Coccyzus americanus*) near the Normandy Ranch site.
- Purple martins (*Progne subis*, inferred from OCR) at the west end.
- A [[Spiny Softshell Turtle]] (*Apalone spinifera*) in a pool created by the White's Ranch irrigation diversion.
- Fireflies (Lampyridae) active in the evenings at Turkey Creek (east end) and Aravaipa Ranch (west end).

## Key Findings

**Species presence and reproduction**

- Listed or regionally sensitive natives were present. [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] ([[Loach Minnow]]) was frequently captured in breeding coloration, and apparent breeding pairs were noted at the east end.
- [[Pantosteus clarkii]] adults were collected at both ends of the creek, and several east-end adults were still tuberculate, indicating breeding condition. Tuberculate adults were occasionally collected at both east- and west-end sites.
- [[Meda Fulgida]] ([[Spikedace]]) was present in numerous individuals with brassy coloration at the east end.
- Other species coded in the source include [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (Longfin Dace), [[Rhinichthys Osculus]] (Speckled Dace), and [[Gila Robusta]] (Roundtail Chub). Non-native species coded include [[Gambusia Affinis]] (Western Mosquitofish), [[Lepomis Cyanellus]] (Green Sunfish), [[Micropterus Salmoides]] ([[Largemouth Bass]]), and [[Pimephales Promelas]] (Fathead Minnow).

The source provides a species code key, but several codes are garbled by OCR, so the complete species list and per-station catch counts cannot be reconstructed reliably from this memo.

**Management implications stated in the memo**

- Habitat degradation at the east end, driven by [[Watercress]] encroachment and sediment aggradation, narrowed the channel and reduced habitat in some reaches.
- Extreme summer temperatures and restricted west-end access are relevant to ongoing monitoring logistics.
- The memo recommends continued monitoring of the same stations to track sediment changes and fish populations.
