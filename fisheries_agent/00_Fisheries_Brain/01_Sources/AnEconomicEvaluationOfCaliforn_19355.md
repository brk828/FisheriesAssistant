# Economic Evaluation of California's Sport Fisheries (1955 Survey)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-economics

## Executive Summary

This paper, by John Mahoney of the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (Inland Fisheries Branch), reports a statewide economic survey of [[Sport Fishery Economics|sport fishing]] in California for the year 1955. Data were collected by a [[Mailed Questionnaire]] sent to a random sample of [[Angling License]] buyers, with the goal of estimating total angling expenditures, the split between [[Saltwater Angling]] and [[Freshwater Angling]], and average daily and annual spending per angler. The survey was modeled on the [[National Survey of Fishing and Hunting]] (1955). Estimated total angling expenditure was $92,200,000 for saltwater and $226,884,935 for freshwater. No species-level biological data are reported.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** Economic values are used to justify fishery resources when they conflict with other land and water uses. The author notes that expenditure figures are only a fraction of total fishery worth, but they indicate the economic status of a fishery relative to other activities.

**Prior work.** Earlier evaluations existed for specific fisheries (Pelgen, 1955, covering [[Striped Bass]], [[Salmon]] and [[Steelhead Trout]] in California) and for watersheds, but no statewide estimate existed for hunting or fishing in California.

**Sampling frame.**
- Names were drawn from [[Angling License]] stub books in the five administrative regions of the Department of Fish and Game.
- 17,500 stubs were drawn; every fifth was retained for this survey.
- Sampled: buyers of resident citizen licenses ($3) and 3-day Pacific Ocean special licenses ($1).
- Not sampled: persons under 16, ocean anglers fishing from a public pier (no license required), and nonresident and alien license buyers.

**Table 1: 1955 license sales and sample**

| Category | Licenses sold | Number sampled |
|---|---|---|
| Resident citizen | 1,241,321 | 3,289 |
| 3-day Pacific Ocean special | 43,961 | 71 |
| All licenses | 1,303,085 | 3,360 |

**Mailing procedure.**
- Questionnaires (Forms A-1 and A-2) were mailed beginning February 1, 1956, with a franked return envelope.
- A postal card reminder (Form A-3) followed one week later.
- On March 11, a follow-up letter and duplicate questionnaire (Form A-4) went to nonrespondents.
- Questionnaires received through April 1 were used.

**Table 2: Questionnaire returns**

| Group | Mailed | Usable returns | Usable return rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resident citizen | 3,289 | 1,962 | 59.6% |
| 3-day special | 71 | 25 | Not legible in source (OCR) |

- Returns before the follow-up letter made up 67.9% of resident returns; returns after it made up 32.1%.
- Unusable returns totaled 15.0% of resident returns. These included 116 respondents (5%) who bought a license but did not fish.
- The overall final return rate was reported as 70.5%.

**Table 3: Angling effort, resident-citizen licensees**

| Category | Respondents | % of respondents | Projected anglers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fished both fresh and salt water | 836 | 42.6% | 502,362 |
| Fished fresh water only | 897 | 45.7% | 538,920 |
| Fished salt water only | 229 | 11.7% | 137,973 |

- The estimated 62,066 licensees who bought a license but did not fish were deducted from the resident total.

**Expenditure calculation.**
- Total fishery expenditure = estimated participants × mean annual expenditure per angler.
- Daily expenditure = mean annual expenditure ÷ mean days fished annually.
- Expenditures of shared-fishery anglers were split between salt and fresh water in proportion to reported days.
- Automobile costs were estimated at 7.5 cents per mile.
- Nonrespondents were assigned the expenditures of late (follow-up) respondents, following [[Nonresponse Bias]] logic from Wallace (1956).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is an economic survey and contains no species population, habitat, water chemistry, or ecosystem data.

- **Water classification:** San Francisco Bay and all other waters inside the Golden Gate were classed as fresh water for survey purposes. Other bays counted as salt water.
- **Taxa mentioned as harvested:** Clams, crabs, abalones, lobsters, frogs and "all kinds of fish" were included in the activity scope of the questionnaire. No taxon-specific catch data are given.
- **Fishing settings:** The text describes a wide range of gear and vessels on California waters, from cane poles to seagoing yachts. Pacific Ocean special licenses applied to ocean anglers.
- **Related literature:** Cited studies cover [[Maryland Tidewater Sport Fishery]], [[British Columbia Sport Fishery]], [[Madison River Creel Census]] (Montana), and Oregon salmon and steelhead values.

## Key Findings

**Statewide expenditures (1955)**

- **Saltwater angling:** An estimated $92,200,000 was spent. This comprised $90,637,000 by resident-citizen licensees and $1,500,000 by 3-day special licensees.
- **Freshwater angling:** An estimated $226,884,935 was spent statewide.

**Per-angler averages**

| Fishery | Mean annual | Mean daily | Mean days fished |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saltwater (resident citizen) | $141.54 | $12.51 | 11 |
| Freshwater (resident citizen) | $217.89 | $14.27 | 15 |
| 3-day special (saltwater) | $36.16 (for 3 days) | — | 3 |

- Saltwater annual expenditures ranged from less than $1 to $2,600 (resident citizens) and from $1 to $156 (3-day specials).
- Freshwater annual expenditures ranged from less than $1 to $13,000.

**Distribution of spending (Table 7, resident-citizen anglers)**

| Category | Freshwater total ($000s) | Freshwater % | Saltwater total ($000s) | Saltwater % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transportation | $65,797 | 29.0% | $23,838 | 26.3% |
| Food | $53,772 | 23.7% | $18,399 | 20.3% |
| General purpose equipment | $36,755 | 16.2% | $10,151 | 11.2% |
| Fishing equipment | $24,050 | 10.6% | $10,423 | 11.5% |

- Transportation and food were the largest expenditure categories in both fisheries.
- Freshwater fishing equipment per angler was $23.10; saltwater was $16.28.

**Methodological conclusions**

- The author chose a mailed questionnaire over personal interviews to reduce cost, and relied on a vigorous follow-up.
- Nonrespondents likely spent less than respondents (per Wallace, 1956). Assigning them follow-up-group values may still leave some upward bias, which the author acknowledges is unmeasured.
- Only 1955 expenditures were counted. Equipment amortization was dropped to simplify editing.
- Data depend on respondent memory and may be affected by prestige and recall bias (per Atwood, 1956).

**Survey limitations (from source)**

- Expenditure figures are described as a fraction of total fishery worth, not total value.
- Some table values are garbled in the source scan; the 3-day return rate and some Table 4 and Table 5 entries could not be confirmed and are omitted or flagged here.
