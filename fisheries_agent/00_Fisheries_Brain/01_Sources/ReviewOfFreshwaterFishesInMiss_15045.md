# Freshwater Fishes in Mississippi — Review and Taxonomic Corrections

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepisosteidae #cyprinidae #petromyzontidae

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but a critical **book review** by Royal D. Suttkus (Tulane University) of Fannye A. Cook's *Freshwater Fishes of Mississippi* (1959). The review identifies taxonomic errors, misidentifications, distributional omissions, and factual exaggerations in the original monograph, particularly concerning [[Gars]] (Lepisosteidae), [[Lampreys]] (Petromyzontidae), and several [[Cyprinidae|minnow]] and [[Percidae|darter]] species. The review serves as an important corrective annotation to Cook's original taxonomic and distributional claims for Mississippi's freshwater ichthyofauna, with emphasis on the Pearl River drainage and Gulf Coast river systems.

## Background & Description
Key morphological and meristic corrections raised in the review:

- **Lateral line scale counts (Gars)**: Cook's key correctly lists 59–64 lateral line scales for [[Lepisosteus platostomus]] (shortnose gar). However, specimens reported in the text (14 from the Pearl River, scale counts 53–56) and in Table I (three specimens, counts 53, 55, 56) fall well outside this range, indicating these were misidentified and are actually [[Lepisosteus oculatus]] (spotted gar), not *L. platostomus*.
- **Lamprey mouth structure**: Cook's description of the lamprey mouth as having a "suctorial mouth without functional jaws" is flagged as ambiguous — the reviewer recommends removing the qualifier "functional," since lampreys lack jaws entirely, not merely functional ones.
- **Exaggerated size claims**: Cook's reported spotted gar size of 7–8 feet and 300+ pounds is dismissed as "preposterous" and unsupported by verifiable data; an 8-foot specimen claim was based only on a photograph, not a physical specimen.
- **Shortnose gar maximum length**: A claimed maximum length of 2–3 ft for *L. platostomus* is presented in the original text without clear attribution ("it is claimed by some"), which the reviewer criticizes as unsubstantiated.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Several habitat and behavioral claims in the original work are contested:

- **Ammocoetes (larval lampreys)**: Cook states ammocoetes live in "mud of quiet backwaters." Suttkus disputes this, asserting that ammocoetes almost invariably inhabit **silt pockets or bars downstream of logs, rocks, or bank projections** within or along the main channel — a product of eddy currents that also concentrate food for these filter-feeders.
- **Longnose gar habitat**: [[Lepisosteus osseus]] (longnose gar) is described in the original text as preferring quiet water; the reviewer counters that this species commonly inhabits **river channel currents**, not just quiet lake environments.
- **Hybognathus habitat partitioning**: Cook claims *Hybognathus nuchalis* and *Hybognathus hayi* share the same habits and environmental preferences. The reviewer, citing Kemp and Hubbs (1954), notes that while the two species occasionally co-occur, they **typically occupy distinct habitats**.
- **Heterandria formosa distribution**: Considered likely an **introduced species** in areas around Pearlington, Mississippi, and westward into Louisiana (New Orleans, beyond Raceland), rather than a native population.

## Key Findings
### Taxonomic and Nomenclatural Corrections
- **Lepisosteus oculatus** Winchell, 1864 is the valid name and should be used instead of *L. productus* Cope, 1865.
- **Lepisosteus chaseii**: The original type specimen (Wailes' collection) is likely lost, and the published description is inadequate for species-level determination. The name cannot currently be validly assigned to any synonymy — though if the specimen were rediscovered and confirmed as spotted gar, *L. chaseii* could take priority over *L. oculatus*.
- **Notropis roseus** should be corrected to **[[Notropis texanus]]** (per Suttkus, 1958, Copeia).
- Misspellings flagged: *L. platostemus* → *L. platostomus*; *chasii* → *chaseii*; *Opsopoedus* → *Opsopoeodus*; *Lepomis cynellus* → *[[Lepomis cyanellus]]*.
- Acknowledgment error: Dr. Ernest A. Lachner (not Dr. Karl F. Lagler) should be credited with identifying Hybopsis specimens.

### Distributional Records (Additions/Corrections to Cook 1959)
- **[[Notropis camurus]]**: occurs in the upper Pearl River system.
- **[[Hiodon tergisus]]** (mooneye): occurs in the Pearl River.
- **[[Hybopsis aestivalis]]**: common in the Pearl River; also occurs in Mobile Bay drainage, Escambia River, and Choctawhatchee River systems.
- **[[Ammocrypta asprella]]**: common species in the Pearl River.
- **[[Moxostoma carinatum]]** (river redhorse): occurs in the Pearl River and drainages to the east (per Robins and Raney, 1957).
- **[[Etheostoma histrio]]**: common in the Pearl River, ranging eastward to the Escambia River.
- **Lepisosteus platostomus**: No authenticated records exist from the Pearl River despite Cook's claims; 12 years of the reviewer's collecting confirmed its absence there.

### Overall Assessment
Despite the noted errors, the reviewer concludes that *Freshwater Fishes of Mississippi* is "a fine compilation" of value to both laymen and students, given the limited material examined. However, the author is criticized for not consulting the extensive Mississippi fish holdings in the **Tulane University fish collection**, which likely could have resolved many of the identified distributional and taxonomic discrepancies.
