# Analytical Request - Little Colorado River Contaminant Study

#ichthyology #environmental-contaminants #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #toxicology

## Executive Summary

This document is an analytical request memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Ecological Services office dated June 16, 1988, requesting contaminant analysis of fish tissue and bottom sediment samples from the [[Little Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The study aims to determine ambient levels of organochlorine pesticides, trace metals, and radionuclides in [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), as well as sediment samples. The request stems from concerns following a 1979 uranium mill spill at Church Rock, New Mexico, which released 94 million gallons of contaminated effluent into the Rio Puerco, a major tributary of the Little Colorado River.

## Background & Description

The study lot consists of six samples from the [[Little Colorado River]] above its confluence with the [[Colorado River]]: four fish composite samples (two [[Humpback Chub]] composites and two [[Channel Catfish]] composites) and two bottom sediment samples. The analytical request was submitted under FY88, Modification 04 to Interagency Agreement No. 4-AA-40-01860 (Work Item 03) with the Bureau of Reclamation Salt Lake City District.

Sample inventory:
- Sample 88AZLCRO1: [[Humpback Chub]] composite, 2812g
- Sample 88AZLCRO2: [[Humpback Chub]] composite, 1718g
- Sample 88AZLCRO3: [[Channel Catfish]] composite, 4299g
- Sample 88AZLCRO4: [[Channel Catfish]] composite, 1991g
- Sample 88AZLCRO5: Sediment, 597g
- Sample 88AZLCRO6: Sediment, 587g

The contamination concern dates to July 1979 when the United Nuclear Corporation's uranium mill at Church Rock, New Mexico experienced a catastrophic failure, releasing 94 million gallons of highly contaminated effluent and 1,100 tons of slurry into the [[Rio Puerco]], a major tributary of the [[Little Colorado River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Little Colorado River]] is a significant tributary of the [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The 1979 uranium mill spill at Church Rock represents one of the largest releases of radioactive material in U.S. history, with potential long-term impacts on aquatic ecosystems downstream. The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is an endemic cyprinid of conservation concern in the Colorado River system, making contaminant monitoring particularly important for this species.

The study involves multiple agencies and institutions: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Los Alamos National Laboratory, Arizona State University, Arizona Game and Fish Department, National Park Service, and U.S. Geological Survey. This multi-agency coordination reflects the complexity and importance of monitoring contaminants in this system.

## Key Findings

**Analytical Protocols:**
- All samples to be analyzed for full organochlorine (OC) scan, including total PCBs and dicofol
- Trace metal analysis: arsenic and selenium by Atomic Absorption Hydride, mercury by Cold Vapor Reduction, all other metals by Inductively Coupled Plasma Atomic Emission Spectroscopy following Type B Preconcentration (pH 6)
- Results to be reported in µg/g (ppm), wet weight, with sample weight, percent moisture, and percent lipid

**Special Procedures for Humpback Chub (Sample 88AZLCRO2):**
- [[Otolith]] extraction for age determination: all six calcareous otoliths (sagitta, lapillus, and asteriscus) to be excised from each specimen in the composite
- Otoliths located in sacculi at the base of semicircular canals, posterolaterally to the cranium
- Otoliths to be arranged and separated by individual specimen, matched to Total Length and Weight data
- Otoliths to be maintained at room temperature in glass vials and shipped to Dr. W.L. Minckley, Arizona State University

**Biochemical Analysis Tissues:**
- Brain tissue (1g minimum) and one eyeball to be excised from each [[Humpback Chub]] specimen in Sample 88AZLCRO2
- Tissues for protein electrophoresis and mitochondrial DNA analysis
- Tissues to be maintained separately, frozen, and express mailed to Arizona State University

**Radionuclide Analysis:**
- Remaining sample aliquots (minimum 200g) from Sample 88AZLCRO2 ([[Humpback Chub]]) and 88AZLCRO4 ([[Channel Catfish]]) to be forwarded to Los Alamos National Laboratory
- Department of Energy to conduct radionuclide chemistry analysis at Los Alamos facility
