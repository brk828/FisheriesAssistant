# Occurrence of the Red Pike-Conger, Cynoponticus coniceps, in the Gulf of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Muraenesocidae #Anguilliformes #range-extension

## Executive Summary

This report documents the first occurrence of the [[Red Pike-Conger]] (*Cynoponticus coniceps*) in the upper [[Gulf of California]], representing a significant northward range extension of approximately 1,055 km from previously known distributions. Two specimens were captured near [[El Desemboque]], Sonora, Mexico in January 1984 at 33 m depth on sandy bottom. The species, a benthic piscivorous fish reaching 1 m length, was previously known from [[Mazatlan]] and [[Cabo San Lucas]] south to Colombia. This record may represent a case of geographical disjunction similar to patterns observed in other [[Anguilliformes]] in the eastern Pacific.

## Background & Description

The [[Red Pike-Conger]] (*Cynoponticus coniceps*), also known locally as "morenocio bio-bio" or "culebra blanca," is a member of the family [[Muraenesocidae]]. The species is characterized by:

**Morphological Features:**
- Body robust and moderately elongate with compressed tail
- Large mouth with upper jaw extending well beyond eye
- Anterior nostril short and tubular; posterior nostril at eye level
- Large, inferior, oblique gill cleft
- Vomer with median row of approximately 15 very large, compressed teeth
- Teeth angular, directed backwards with nick on posterior edge
- 1-2 anterior teeth slightly tricuspidate
- Pectoral fins more than 1/2 head length

**Specimen Measurements (n=2):**
- Total length: 550-553 mm
- Head length: 74.1-77.9 mm
- Trunk length: 150-151 mm
- Caudal length: 332-333 mm
- Preorbital distance: 17.3-18.8 mm
- Interorbital distance: 11.5-12.0 mm
- Eye diameter: 8.6-9.0 mm
- Upper jaw length: 31.1-33.0 mm

**Coloration (preserved):**
Coppery brown, darker on dorsum; dorsal and anal fins edged with black; pectoral fins black.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics:**
The [[Red Pike-Conger]] is a benthic, piscivorous species inhabiting warm, shallow coastal waters at depths of 10-100 m. The species shows preference for sand and mud bottoms. The specimens from [[El Desemboque]] were captured at 33 m depth on sandy substrate, consistent with known habitat preferences.

**Collection Location:**
- Site: Near [[El Desemboque]], between [[Santo Tomas]] and [[Cabo Tepoca]]
- Coordinates: 30°34'N, 113°01'W
- Date: 26 January 1984
- Depth: 33 m
- Substrate: Sandy bottom
- Collection method: Commercial shrimp net

**Biogeographic Context:**
The occurrence in the upper [[Gulf of California]] may represent geographical disjunction similar to patterns documented for other conger-like eels ([[Anguilliformes]]) in the eastern Pacific region. This pattern suggests complex distributional dynamics in the region.

## Key Findings

**Range Extension:**
- **Northernmost Record:** These specimens represent the northernmost occurrence of *Cynoponticus coniceps* and the first record from the upper [[Gulf of California]]
- **Extension Distance:** Approximately 1,055 km north of previously reported range
- **Previous Distribution:** [[Mazatlan]], Sinaloa (type locality) and [[Cabo San Lucas]], Baja California Sur, Mexico, south to Colombia coast

**Taxonomic Verification:**
Specimens identified using diagnostic characteristics from Jordan and Evermann (1896) and Smith (1995), with detailed morphometric and meristic data confirming species identification.

**Specimen Deposition:**
- UANL-13370: Facultad de Ciencias Biológicas, Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León
- UABC-0010: Facultad de Ciencias, Universidad Autónoma de Baja California

**Biogeographic Implications:**
The record suggests possible geographical disjunction in distribution, a pattern observed in other [[Anguilliformes]] taxa in the eastern Pacific. This finding contributes to understanding of fish biogeography in the [[Gulf of California]] and highlights the need for continued survey work in the region to document species distributions and potential range shifts.
