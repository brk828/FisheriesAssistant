# Cui-ui Recovery Status and Listing Review (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1995 fax memorandum from Tom Strekal reports an April 5, 1995 meeting between [[Cui-ui]] recovery team representatives and [[Mike Spear]], Regional Director of the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) in Portland. The team discussed the listing status of the [[Cui-ui]], an endangered sucker of [[Pyramid Lake]]. The Regional Director agreed with the recovery team that biological and hydrologic revisions should precede any revision of the [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]]. FWS will not change the listing status at this time. Any future action depends on securing recovery benefits and on a future plan revision.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] is an endangered fish that spawns in the [[Truckee River]] and lives in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The document is a correspondence item and does not give morphological or life-history descriptions. It summarizes the species' recovery criteria and population counts from the [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]] (Second Revision, 1992).

The memo lists the recipients: W. L. Minckley, G. Gary Scoppettone, Gary Vinyard, Paul Wagner, James Cooper, James Curran, and Chester Buchanan. Chet Buchanan and John Doebel attended the meeting with Strekal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery criteria focus on water supply and habitat:

- **Water inflow:** Additional Truckee River inflow to Pyramid Lake of 45,000 acre-feet, or equivalent benefit, at a minimum rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year, is required for [[Reclassification]]. For [[Delisting]], 65,000 acre-feet beyond that amount is required, equivalent to 110,000 acre-feet in total.
- **Water quality:** Lake and river water quality standards must be met for 15 years before delisting.
- **Habitat:** The lower [[Truckee River]] floodplain must be rehabilitated.
- **Passage:** The [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and [[Derby Dam Fish Ladder]] must pass at least 300,000 adult cui-ui in a spawning run.
- **Refuge:** A [[Hatchery Refuge]] for brood stock must be established to protect against catastrophic events.

The memo notes that the water requirement is central to the recovery objectives. Objectives 1 and 2 are related, because water supports survival.

## Key Findings

**Recovery criteria (Second Revision, 1992):**

- **Reclassification (endangered to threatened):** Requires a 0.85 probability of persistence for 200 years, the water inflow above, and stable or increasing adult and juvenile numbers over the previous 15 years. It could be completed within 11 years if efforts began by 1994.
- **Delisting:** Requires a 0.95 probability of persistence for 200 years, the larger water benefit, stable or increasing numbers, water quality compliance, floodplain rehabilitation, fish passage, secured facility maintenance, and a hatchery refuge. It could be completed within 24 years if efforts began by 1994.

**Status against criteria:**

- Immediate reclassification is not possible, because none of the three reclassification criteria has been met. No additional water or equivalent benefit has been secured for Pyramid Lake.
- The adult population has changed sharply. Reported adult counts are:

| Year | Adult cui-ui (reported) |
|---|---|
| 1989 | 200,000 |
| 1992 | 1,400,000 |
| 1993 | 700,000 |
| 1994 | <700,000 (projected) |

- Delisting remains a future action.

**Proposed Third Revision:**

- The plan would keep the stochastic methodology but update models with new biological and hydrologic data, operating assumptions, and water management (flow regimes).
- Reclassification changes: modify Objective 2 (water requirement), and clarify Objective 3 (definitions and reference period).
- Delisting changes: modify Objective 2, clarify Objectives 3, 4 (water quality reference period), and 6 (unimpeded passage for all potential spawners), and add an objective on the significance of genetics for facility management.

**Recovery programs in progress** (several years needed for completion):

- [[Riparian Habitat Restoration]] on the lower Truckee River
- Improvement of delta passage
- Negotiation of the [[Truckee River Operating Agreement]] (TROA)
- A water quality agreement
- [[Water Rights Acquisition]]

Many recovery tasks are authorized by [[Public Law 101-618]]. The TROA Draft Environmental Impact Statement (DEIS) is expected to provide considerable socioeconomic data on water acquisition and storage and management changes in the Truckee River basin, relevant to a revised recovery plan.

**Outcome of the April 5, 1995 meeting:** FWS will not pursue a change in listing status for the cui-ui at this time. Any such action is conditional on securing benefits and on a future revision of the plan.
