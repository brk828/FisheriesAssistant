# Early Development of the Hitch, *Lavinia exilicauda*, of Clear Lake, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1965 study by Camm Swift documents, for the first time in detail, the complete early ontogeny of the [[Hitch]] (*[[Lavinia exilicauda]]*), a cyprinid endemic to California, from fertilization through the juvenile stage. Eggs were collected from spawning adults in Clear Lake tributaries (Seigler Canyon Creek, 1962; an unnamed rain-swollen ditch near Lakeport, 1963) and reared under controlled laboratory conditions (~55–74°F). The 1963 cohort, reared at a stable ~61°F, provided the primary developmental timeline reported here. Hatching occurred at approximately 7 days (Balinsky stage 26) and free-swimming/feeding larvae emerged by day 14 — notably faster than the 10- and 20-day estimates previously reported by [[Murphy 1948]] for wild Clear Lake hitch. Full ossification of fin rays was achieved by day 65. The study also establishes a key taxonomic character: a large melanophore spot at the caudal fin base, distinguishing young hitch from young [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) and [[Venus Roach]] (*Hesperoleucus venustus*), both of which possess only a small caudal spot.

## Background & Description

### Eggs
- Freshly stripped eggs: pale orange, becoming light yellow ~20 minutes post-fertilization as the vitelline membrane lifts.
- Fertilized egg diameter: 2.00–2.25 mm.
- Yolk sphere diameter: 1.25 mm.
- **Demersal, nonadhesive** — heavier than water, do not stick to substrate or each other.
- No oil globules present (a distinguishing feature from many other cyprinids).

### Larval and Juvenile Development
- **Hatching (Day 7, stage 26):** Prolarva 6 mm long; motionless unless disturbed, then swims erratically ("tadpole-like").
- **Day 8:** Dorsal/ventral pigment lines extend to caudal peduncle; eyes dark grey.
- **Day 10:** Prolarvae 7 mm; upper/lower jaw bars visible; eyes black with visible lenses; spontaneous swimming and jaw movement begin; pectoral fins as small paddles.
- **Day 11:** 6–8 melanophores along lateral pigment line.
- **Day 14:** Air sac (single-lobed) functional with air, enabling upright swimming/resting; strong jaw/feeding movements; larvae become free-swimming and begin feeding.
- **Day 15:** Gill filament buds on four gill bars; liver encroaching on yolk sac.
- **Day 20:** Yolk sac fully absorbed — marks onset of **postlarval stage**.
- **Day 21:** Cleithrum visible at pectoral fin origin.
- **Day 22:** Filamentous lateral-line organs visible (45X).
- **Day 26:** Eyes iridescent gold with black pupils (adult condition); 2–4 melanophores on lip tips; melanophore concentration ventral to tail.
- **Day 29:** All gill bars pigmented; lateral pigment line has 10–14 melanophores.
- **Day 32:** Postlarvae 8.5 mm long; gut constricted at future pelvic fin site; liver obscures anterior 1/4 of gut; silvery guanophores on stomach/intestine; pectoral fins pigmented.
- **Day 36:** Dorsal fin outline present; 8–10 ossified caudal rays; melanophore concentration forms distinct spot at caudal fin base; melanophores migrate onto caudal rays.
- **Day 41:** Anal and dorsal fins each have 1–4 melanophores.
- **Day 46:** Bilobed air sac (anterior lobe ~1/3 volume); 8–10 dorsal, 6–8 anal, 16–18 caudal ossified rays; guanophore patch on opercles.
- **Day 58:** Juveniles reach 13 mm.
- **Day 65:** Full complement of ossified fin rays; gill filaments pigmented; lateral-line organs still visible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Clear Lake, Lake County, California, and its tributary streams; broader native range includes the Pajaro-Salinas, Sacramento–San Joaquin, and Russian River systems.
- **Spawning behavior:** Unlike most cyprinids, the hitch requires **flowing water** for spawning, laying demersal, nonadhesive eggs — a notable departure from typical cyprinid reproductive strategy ([[Murphy 1948]]). [[Kimsey 1960]] additionally documented spawning in wave-washed lakeshore gravel, indicating some plasticity in spawning substrate.
- **Taxonomic context:** The hitch is part of a species complex with described geographic subspecies structure — *L. e. harengus* (Pajaro-Salinas, slender-bodied) vs. *L. e. exilicauda* (Sacramento–San Joaquin). The Clear Lake population is provisionally suggested (per J.D. Hopkirk, pers. comm.) to represent a third distinct geographic subspecies, pending further study.
- **Thermal regime:** Natural spawning water temperature recorded at 55°F; laboratory rearing conducted between 60–74°F, with growth beyond free-swimming stage lagging behind wild-stock growth reported by Murphy (1948), suggesting temperature-dependent developmental plasticity.

## Key Findings
- **Faster development than previously assumed:** Hatching at 7 days and free-swimming/feeding larvae at 14 days — roughly half the durations (10 and 20 days) estimated by [[Murphy 1948]] at comparable temperatures.
- **Diagnostic pigmentation:** A large melanophore spot at the caudal fin base is diagnostic for young hitch, distinguishing them from young [[Common Carp]] and [[Venus Roach]], which show only a small caudal spot — useful for field/lab identification of larval cyprinids in Clear Lake.
- **Developmental staging concordance:** Hitch development closely follows the general cyprinid staging scheme of [[Balinsky 1948]] through stage 43; later stages could not be confirmed because scale formation was never observed in laboratory-reared specimens.
- **Reproductive novelty:** Confirms hitch as an atypical cyprinid in requiring flowing water for successful (demersal, nonadhesive) egg deposition, contrasting with the adhesive/lentic egg strategies common in the family.
- **No oil globules in eggs:** A morphological feature that may hold systematic or ecological significance relative to other cyprinids.
- **First complete developmental atlas:** Provides the first descriptive and illustrated account of hitch ontogeny from fertilization to juvenile stage, filling a gap left by Murphy's (1948) focus on post-juvenile age and growth.
