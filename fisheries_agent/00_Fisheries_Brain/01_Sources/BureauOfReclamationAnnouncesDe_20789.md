# Bureau of Reclamation Announces Delay in Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement Release

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #dam-impacts #Colorado-River

## Executive Summary

The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] announced a three-month delay in releasing the draft Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, originally scheduled for August 1993. The delay resulted from ongoing discussions with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] concerning endangered fish species protection measures in [[Glen Canyon]] and [[Grand Canyon]]. The draft EIS public comment period was rescheduled for late 1993, with final completion expected by October 1994. [[Interim Operating Criteria]] implemented November 1, 1991, designed to reduce adverse impacts on downstream resources, would continue during the delay.

## Background & Description

In July 1989, the Secretary of the Interior directed [[Reclamation]] to prepare an EIS examining effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on downstream environmental and ecological resources in [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]] and [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The project involved multiple cooperating agencies including [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[National Park Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], and several tribal nations (Havasupai, Hopi, Hualapai, San Juan Southern Piute, Pueblo of Zuni, Southern Utah Paiute Consortium, Kaibab Band, Paiute Indian Tribe of Utah, Shivwits Band, and Navajo Nation).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses endangered fish species below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] in the [[Colorado River]] system. Upper Colorado Regional Director Roland Robison indicated that [[Reclamation]] and [[FWS]] reached agreement on acceptable measures to protect endangered fish species in [[Glen Canyon]] and [[Grand Canyon]]. The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program supported various scientific researchers working in [[Grand Canyon]] to inform operational criteria development.

[[Interim Operating Criteria]] were developed jointly by cooperating agencies with input from scientific researchers and reviewed under [[National Environmental Policy Act]] requirements. These criteria aimed to reduce adverse impacts on downstream resources while the comprehensive EIS process continued.

## Key Findings

- Draft EIS release delayed approximately 3 months from August 1993 to late 1993
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] expected to complete draft Biological Opinion on preferred alternative operational criteria
- Public hearings scheduled for early 1994 in San Francisco, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Flagstaff, Salt Lake City, Denver, and Washington DC
- Final EIS completion scheduled for October 1994
- [[Interim Operating Criteria]] implemented November 1, 1991, to remain in effect until Record of Decision
- Agreement reached between [[Reclamation]] and [[FWS]] on endangered species protection measures
