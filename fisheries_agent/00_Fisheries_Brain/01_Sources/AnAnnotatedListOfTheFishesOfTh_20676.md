# Annotated List of the Fishes of the Upper Des Moines River Basin in Iowa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1949 paper by Harry M. Harrison, published in the *Proceedings of the Iowa Academy of Science* (Vol. 56), presents an annotated checklist of the fishes of the Upper [[Des Moines River]] watershed in Iowa. The list was compiled from field surveys in 1946–1948 and updated against the earlier records of [[Samuel E. Meek]] (1892) and Call (1892). It documents 82 species in 14 families. Of the 59 species Meek recorded, 11 were no longer found, and 22 new species were added. The author attributes these shifts to changes in species composition over the intervening decades.

## Background & Description

The authors of earlier lists were [[Samuel E. Meek]] (1892), whose report covered the fishes of Iowa, and Call (1892), who listed fishes near Des Moines. A paper by Starrett (1949) on the fishes of [[Boone County]] was in press at the time. The study area was limited to the upper part of the drainage between the Iowa-Minnesota line and the Polk-Warren County line.

The new list draws on:
- 316 collections from the river and its tributaries, taken at 90 sampling stations.
- 29 collections from 12 lakes, made in 1946–1948 with records from E. T. Rose.
- Survey records from [[Carl L. Hubbs]], E. B. Speaker and others.

Species identifications were made by Hubbs (for the Hubbs, Salyer and Speaker specimens), E. T. Rose (for lake specimens), and the author (for river specimens). Voucher specimens are held by the [[Iowa State Conservation Commission]].

Sampling used a range of gear across open water and all habitat types:
- [[Linear Seine]]s with bar mesh of 1/4 inch to 1 inch, from 6 to 500 feet long.
- [[Hoop Net]]s, pond nets and wooden basket traps.
- Observations at four dam sites fitted with modified [[Denil Fishway]]s.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species list is organized by habitat preference, which the annotations describe in detail:

- **Swift, gravel- or rubble-bottomed streams:** [[Hog Sucker]] (common in swift water), [[Stonecat]] (prefers swift water over gravel and rock), and [[Western Blacknose Dace]] (common at steep-gradient, rubble-bottom stations).
- **Larger rivers:** [[Silver Chub]] (taken only in larger rivers), [[Slenderhead Darter]] (evenly distributed in larger rivers) and [[Iowa Darter]].
- **Lakes:** [[Black Hawk Lake]] and [[Storm Lake]] hold abundant sport and forage species, including [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Largemouth Black Bass]] and [[Yellow Perch]]. Several small-stream minnows are rare or absent in lakes.
- **Dams and fishways:** Four dam sites with modified Denil fishways were surveyed, providing a notable habitat type for migratory and upstream-moving fish.

The author also records population trends across the survey period. Several species appear to be declining, including the [[Topeka Shiner]] (common in 1946, rare by 1948), the [[Brassy Minnow]] (common in 1946, rare by 1948) and the [[Northern Longnose Chub]] (abundant in 1946, rare by fall 1948). Other species are increasing or have a localized presence. [[Yellow Bass]] was becoming common in North Twin Lake.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 82 species in 14 families are known from the Upper Des Moines River drainage. Of these, 70 persist to the time of the study.
- **Changes since Meek (1892):** Meek listed 59 species. Eleven of these are no longer found, and 22 species not recorded by Meek were added.
- **Most abundant river minnows:**
  - [[Spotfin Shiner]] is the most abundant river minnow and occurs at all stream stations.
  - [[Sand Shiner]] is second in abundance in streams.
- **Widespread and abundant species:**
  - [[European Carp]] is abundant in all waters.
  - [[Bigmouth Buffalo]] is common in lakes and larger streams.
  - [[Gizzard Shad]] is abundant in Black Hawk Lake, with only a few records from Storm Lake and the Raccoon River.
  - [[Northern Fathead Minnow]] is abundant in all lakes, ponds and bayous.
- **Rare or sporadic species:**
  - [[Black Buffalo]] was taken only three times.
  - [[Spotted Chub]] was taken once, with only two individuals secured.
  - Several species, including [[Chestnut Lamprey]], [[Northern Longnose Gar]] and [[River Redhorse]], were reported by Meek but not collected in 1946–1948.
- **Declining species:** Topeka Shiner, Brassy Minnow and Northern Longnose Chub became rare by 1948.
- **Sampling scope:** Collections were made throughout the year and across all habitat types. Each of the 316 river collections and 29 lake collections was used to build the contemporary list.
- **Taxonomic arrangement:** Families and nomenclature follow Bailey (manuscript). Specimen identifications rely on Hubbs, Rose and the author.
