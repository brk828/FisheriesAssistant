# Ichthyology of the United States and Mexican Boundary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This is the fish section of the report of the [[United States and Mexican Boundary Survey]], written by [[Charles Girard]], M.D., and published under Lt. Col. W. H. Emory, U.S. Commissioner. It presents taxonomic descriptions of fishes collected during the survey, mainly from 1851 to 1855, across the [[Rio Grande]] basin, the Texas Gulf coast and interior Texas, and several Mexican and southwestern US drainages. Each species gets a diagnostic description (meristic fin formulas, body proportions, scale counts, coloration), figured plates of the fish and its scales, and a list of specimens with catalogue numbers, locality, collector and preservation.

The text is organized by family. Many entries are fully described; others are noted only briefly, with the authors deferring descriptions to later work because of space limits.

## Background & Description

- **Author and context:** Girard wrote the section as part of the survey's natural history reporting. Specimens were gathered by survey personnel including Maj. Emory, Col. J. D. Graham, Dr. C. B. Kennerly, John H. Clark, G. Wurdemann, Lt. D. N. Couch, Capt. Van Vliet and A. Schott.
- **Format of species accounts:** Each entry gives a generic and specific diagnosis, a fin formula (for example "D X, 13; A III, 11; C 4,7,9,8,1,3; V I,6; P 16"), body proportions, scale structure and counts, color in life or alcohol, and figure references (plates I to XLI).
- **Taxonomic approach:** Girard grouped taxa by morphology, such as the pharyngeal teeth in Cyprinidae-like forms and the pectinate or ctenoid scales in perches and drums. He recognized the need for anatomical study to define family limits, especially for the Scomberesocidae and Cyprinidae.
- **Scope:** The report describes numerous species across many families, including perches, sunfishes, drums, croakers, minnows, suckers, killifishes, gobies, blennies, flatfishes, catfishes and eels. Several taxa are described as new in the 1856 to 1859 publications cited within the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range:** Specimens came from fresh, brackish and marine waters. Localities include the [[Rio Grande]] mouth (Rio Bravo), the [[Rio Nueces]], Rio Cibolo, Brazos Santiago, [[Indianola Texas]], St. Joseph's Island, Galveston, Dry Creek near Victoria, the Devil's River, Rio San Pedro, Rio Sonoita, the [[Gila River]] and [[Colorado River]] basins, and Chihuahua drainages.
- **Freshwater endemism:** Girard notes that the [[Etheostomidae]] (darters) are restricted to fresh waters of the northern western hemisphere and that their natural affinities need future anatomical work.
- **Biogeographic notes:** The Scomberesocidae and Sciaenidae sections note a mix of Gulf coastal and marine taxa. The [[Cyprinodontidae]] are described as typical of brackish waters in the warm temperate zone.
- **Taxonomic synonymy in text:** Girard reports that [[Pomotis]] cyanellus was judged identical to P. fallax, and that P. aquilensis and P. fallax are both highly polymorphic.

## Key Findings

1. **Diplites nuecensis (Percidae)** is closer to D. fasciatus than D. salmoides. Its mouth is deeply cleft and the lower jaw projects, and the species is figured in Plate I.
2. **Pomotis group (sunfishes):** Four species are separated mainly by head and eye size, opercular flap development and preopercle serration. P. speciosus, P. heros, P. aquilensis and P. fallax are each described with full fin formulas and scale descriptions.
3. **Pomotis fallax** is highly variable. Girard judged P. cyanellus identical to it after examining more specimens, and gave figures of the main forms on Plate X of the survey reports.
4. **Cyprinids and suckers:** Numerous species of [[Cyprinella]], [[Dionda]], [[Campostoma]], [[Moxostoma]], [[Minomus]], [[Agosia]] and [[Gila]] are described, many with short diagnoses and several new names. Gila elegans and G. grahami are compared in detail.
5. **Killifishes and livebearers:** Cyprinodon elegans, C. gibbosus, C. bovinus and C. maculatus are distinguished partly by sexual dimorphism in fin shape. Girard states that Poecilia has priority over Mollienesia, so the species formerly placed in Mollienesia belong to Poecilia.
6. **Atheriniformes and Poeciliidae:** [[Gambusia]] (G. nobilis, G. affinis, G. patruelis) and [[Limia]] are separated by fin placement and mouth teeth.
7. **Marine and estuarine taxa:** Drums (Sciaenidae), croakers, pompanos and jacks (Carangidae), mullets (Mugil berlandieri) and threadfins (Polynemus octonemus) are recorded from the Gulf coast, with fin formulas and scale descriptions.
8. **Catfishes:** Pimelodus afinis and P. vulpes are described with morphological notes on dorsal and adipose fins; Arius and Ailurichthys are noted from marine and brackish waters.
9. **Eels:** Anguilla tyrannus and Neomuraena nigromarginata are described from coastal waters, with Neoconger mucronatus noted from Saint Joseph's Island.
10. **Specimen record:** Collections were numerous and multi-site, and each species entry includes a catalogue table. Girard explicitly deferred some families (for example, the Sciaenidae and Atherinidae) for lack of space or materials, and noted that several species remained to be described.
