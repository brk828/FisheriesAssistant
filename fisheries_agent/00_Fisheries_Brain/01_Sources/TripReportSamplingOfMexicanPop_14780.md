# Trip Report: Sampling of Mexican Populations of Desert Pupfish (November 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #field-survey

## Executive Summary

This trip report documents a November 10-12, 1990 field survey of known [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) populations in the [[Colorado River Delta]] region of Sonora and Baja California, Mexico. Conducted by Sally Stefferud (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) and Jerry Stefferud (Tonto National Forest/Desert Fishes Recovery Team), the survey aimed to familiarize personnel with pupfish habitats. Casual dipnet sampling without collection permits revealed encouraging pupfish presence at multiple sites, despite competition from invasive species including [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Sailfin Molly]] (*Poecilia latipinnis*), and [[Tilapia]]. The survey found pupfish at four of five sites examined, with particularly abundant populations in drainage canals near [[Cerro Prieto]].

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) is a small cyprinodontid fish native to the [[Colorado River Delta]] and associated aquatic habitats in northwestern Mexico. This survey revisited localities previously sampled by Hendrickson and Varela-Romero (1989) to assess current population status and habitat conditions. Sampling was conducted using dipnets only, with no specimens collected due to lack of Mexican collection permits.

The survey covered five primary locations: [[Santa Clara Slough]] (Sanchez Taboada), [[El Doctor]] (two sites), [[Cerro Prieto]] marsh, [[Laguna Salada]], and agricultural canals south of Cerro Prieto. These sites represent diverse habitat types including concrete-lined canals with vegetated margins, geothermal spring pools, cattail marshes, salt-pan pools, and agricultural drains.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River Delta]] region provides critical habitat for [[Desert Pupfish]], though populations face significant pressure from invasive species. At all sites surveyed, non-native fishes were abundant, particularly [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Sailfin Molly]] (*Poecilia latipinnis*), [[Tilapia]] sp., [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), and [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*).

Habitat characteristics varied considerably across sites. At [[El Doctor]], a geothermal spring pool approximately 30 feet wide by 50 feet long with depths exceeding 5 feet provided warm water habitat. The pool was surrounded by impenetrable [[Tamarisk]] thicket except at one access point, with limited [[Cattail]] growth in shallow edges. Associated salt-pan pools at the embankment base contained shallow water with inundated saltgrass mats where pupfish were easily captured.

The [[Cerro Prieto]] marsh is extensively modified, bermed into rectangular ponds with little vegetation in upper portions and salt encrustation along shorelines. Water temperature measured 68°F with clear reddish coloration. The relationship between water distribution and the adjacent geothermal power plant remains unclear.

[[Laguna Salada]] was completely dry during the survey, contrasting with 1987 conditions when Hendrickson and Varela found water and fish populations. Windrows of dead fish from the 1987 drying event remained visible, with salt-preserved specimens still identifiable, primarily [[Carp]].

Agricultural canals south of [[Cerro Prieto]] were concrete-lined with limited habitat, though associated drains provided suitable pupfish habitat with silty clay bottoms, shallow water, moderate current (2-3 cubic feet per second), and vegetation cover.

## Key Findings

**Santa Clara Slough (Sanchez Taboada):**
- Concrete-lined canal with weedy shallows containing [[Tamarisk]] and [[Cattail]]
- Only [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Sailfin Molly]] found in canal proper
- At marsh locality 12 (Hendrickson and Varela 1989): one juvenile [[Desert Pupfish]] found in shallow water among saltgrass, with large numbers of [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Sailfin Molly]]
- No pupfish found in the slough itself beyond concrete channel end
- 1987 comparison: pupfish comprised only 1% of 833 fish collected

**El Doctor - Spring Pool (Locality 6):**
- Geothermal spring pool 30 feet wide × 50 feet long, center depth >5 feet
- [[Tilapia]], [[Mosquitofish]], and [[Sailfin Molly]] abundant; large Tilapia schools at surface
- One [[Desert Pupfish]] found during 15 minutes sampling effort
- Associated salt-pan pools at embankment base: pupfish easily caught by netting through inundated saltgrass mats
- 1987 comparison: pupfish comprised 7.1% of 241 fish collected

**El Doctor - North Site (Locality 10, Highway 40 km 70):**
- Small spring complex with excavated ponds (livestock use), extensive marshy areas with saltgrass, [[Cattail]], [[Tamarisk]], and scattered [[Phragmites]]
- Extensive salt-pan pools on delta mud-flats
- Three [[Desert Pupfish]] caught in 10 minutes: 2 individuals <5 mm, 1 individual >30 mm
- Pupfish found in shallow pond with large cattail growth, among cattail bases
- [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Sailfin Molly]] also present
- 1987 comparison: pupfish comprised 5.1% of 195 fish collected

**Cerro Prieto Marsh:**
- Sampled only at uppermost end below Cerro Prieto hill
- Water temperature: 68°F, clear reddish color
- No fish found at this sampling location
- Marsh is very large, bermed into rectangular ponds with little vegetation in upper portion and salt encrustation along shoreline
- Note: Hendrickson and Varela found pupfish in 1987 but sampled different area

**Laguna Salada:**
- Completely dry at northeast end near [[El Oasis]]
- No water visible anywhere in laguna from vantage points
- Windrows of dead fish from 1987 drying event still evident; salt-preserved specimens broken up and chewed by animals
- Only identifiable species: [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- Recent off-road-vehicle activity noted (20-foot wide churned track)

**Canals South of Cerro Prieto (Near Locality 18):**
- Concrete-lined canals with little habitat
- Small side canal: [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*)
- Drain crossing under canal: many [[Desert Pupfish]] of all sizes
- Dipnetting along edges around vegetation/cover almost always produced ≥1 pupfish per net haul
- No other fish species found in drain
- Silty clay bottom, shallow water, moderate current (2-3 cubic feet per second inflow from south)
- Water too murky for visual observation
- 1987 comparison: pupfish comprised only 2.3% of 2,583 fish at this general site

**Overall Assessment:**
The survey team was "quite encouraged" by findings. [[Desert Pupfish]] were found more readily than expected based on 1987 data, when Hendrickson and Varela-Romero documented low pupfish percentages at these sites. The distribution of [[Desert Pupfish]] in the [[Colorado River Delta]] region appears not to have changed considerably since 1987, with the exception of [[Laguna Salada]]. The drying of Laguna Salada is described as a cyclical phenomenon, with pupfish populations likely to return when the laguna refills.
