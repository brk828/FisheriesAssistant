# Razorback Sucker Larval Spotlight Sampling Procedure (1987 Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1987 interagency memorandum from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] sets out a field protocol for sampling [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) larvae at stocking sites in Region 5. The method uses a spotlight to attract larvae at night, captures them with nets, preserves a capped sample, and records site conditions for each 10-minute station. The memo was written by Dan Langhorst, identified as a Razorback Sucker Consultant, to Jim St. Amant, Associate Fisheries Biologist, Region 5.

## Background & Description

The memo is a procedural directive rather than a research report. It is dated March 25, 1987, and carries the subject line "Q-Beam Lighting Procedure for Razorback Sucker Larvae." The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a native catostomid of the [[Colorado River Basin]], and the protocol relates to monitoring larvae released or present at managed stocking sites.

The memo lists eleven numbered steps, summarized here:

1. Begin spotlighting one hour after sunset.
2. Anchor the boat in the sampling area, at predetermined stations mapped for each stocking site.
3. Direct the light into a roughly 2 m² area adjacent to the boat for 10 minutes.
4. Capture all larvae attracted to the light with a small, fine-mesh aquarium net.
5. Place the captured larvae in a shallow pan of water, using an inverted dip-net, so they can swim free.
6. Capture all larvae seen during the 10-minute period.
7. Preserve at least 30 larvae per 10-minute period. If more than 30 are present, release the excess into the water.
8. Preserve larvae in 10% [[Formalin]] solution.
9. Place preserved larvae in tagged vials using an eyedropper, with tags inside and outside the vial.
10. Record data on a data sheet, including stocking site, sampling station, cover-vegetation, water depth, water temperature, number captured, date, and time (military).
11. After each 10-minute period, move to a new sampling station and repeat.

The memo attaches a data sheet, which is not reproduced in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The protocol targets the early life stages of a fish found in riverine and reservoir habitats. Larvae are sampled in shallow water near shore, where cover and vegetation are recorded. This allows comparison of larval abundance across habitat types, including water depth and temperature, at each [[Stocking Site]]. Because capture is nocturnal and light-attracted, the counts represent relative abundance for the sampled area and time, not total population size.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling design:** Each stocking site has predetermined sampling stations, and the number of stations varies with site size. The exact range is not legible in the scanned copy.
- **Capture effort:** Each station is sampled for 10 minutes using a light focused on roughly 2 m² of water.
- **Sample cap:** Up to 30 larvae are preserved per 10-minute period. Excess larvae above 30 are released alive.
- **Preservation:** Larvae are fixed in 10% formalin and stored in tagged vials with date, military time, and capture location.
- **Data recorded:** Stocking site, sampling station, cover-vegetation, water depth, water temperature, number captured, date, and time.
- **Operational timing:** Sampling begins one hour after sunset and proceeds in consecutive 10-minute station rotations.
