## Executive Summary
This document is a 1986 memorandum from W.L. Minckley organizing a **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** meeting at the Sierra Ancha Experiment Station, Arizona. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, it outlines an agenda for reviewing and drafting federal recovery plans for several imperiled desert fish taxa native to the [[Gila River Basin]] and [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage. Key agenda items include technical review of the **Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan**, preliminary review of the **Gila ditaenia (Sonora Chub) Recovery Plan**, management of non-native **Cyprinodon macularius (Desert Pupfish)** populations outside their native range, non-native fish removal from stock tanks in the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed, and initial planning for recovery plans covering three additional cyprinid species. A field trip to observe re-introduction sites for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] was tentatively planned for the meeting's conclusion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The memorandum implicitly addresses several ecological and biogeographic concerns central to desert fish conservation:

- **Native range integrity**: Concern is raised over **non-native populations of *Cyprinodon macularius*** occurring in the **upper [[San Pedro River]]** and **[[Santa Cruz River]]** basins — drainages outside the species' historic native range — highlighting risks of competitive displacement or hybridization with native fish assemblages.
- **Habitat disturbance from anthropogenic water sources**: Stock tanks within the **[[Aravaipa Creek]]** watershed are identified as reservoirs for non-native fish that may threaten native aquatic communities, prompting proposed removal actions.
- **Transboundary drainage assessment**: The status of the **[[Rio Magdalena]]** drainage (a tributary system relevant to *Gila ditaenia*) is discussed both within the U.S. and via a proposed field survey in Mexico, reflecting the transboundary ecological range of the species.
- **Re-introduction ecology**: The planned site visit to **[[Cherry Creek]]** and the **[[Salt River]]** pertains to active re-introduction programs for the **Razorback Sucker** and **Colorado Pikeminnow**, both large-river desert fishes extirpated from much of their historic range due to damming, water diversion, and non-native species introductions.

## Key Findings
- The meeting served as a coordination point for **multiple simultaneous federal recovery planning efforts** for southwestern desert fishes under what would become Endangered Species Act recovery frameworks.
- A **page-by-page technical review** of the draft **Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan** was scheduled, indicating this plan was in an advanced drafting stage by late 1986.
- The **Gila ditaenia (Sonora Chub) Recovery Plan** was in early/preliminary drafting stages, with an associated proposal to survey the **Rio Magdalena drainage in Mexico** — underscoring the binational conservation scope required for this species.
- Non-native **Cyprinodon macularius** populations were recognized as a management problem in basins where they are **not historically native**, requiring dedicated policy recommendations.
- Non-native fish removal from **stock tanks** was identified as a specific, actionable habitat management measure within the Aravaipa Creek watershed.
- Recovery plan preparation was formally initiated for three additional imperiled cyprinids: **[[Meda Fulgida]]**, **[[Tiaroga Cobitis]]**, and **[[Lepidomeda Vittata]]**.
- The team planned a **field verification component**, visiting active reintroduction sites for **Razorback Sucker** and **Colorado Pikeminnow** at **Cherry Creek** and the **Salt River**, contingent on meeting agenda time constraints.
