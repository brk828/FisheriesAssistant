# Effects of Salinity on Preferred and Lethal Temperatures of the Blackchin Tilapia, *Sarotherodon melanotheron*

## Executive Summary
This study (Stauffer, Vann, and Hocutt, 1984) investigated how acclimation salinity (0, 15, and 30 ‰) interacts with temperature to affect the behavioral preference and thermal tolerance limits of the [[Blackchin Tilapia]] (*Sarotherodon melanotheron*), an exotic [[Cichlidae|cichlid]] established in Florida estuaries. Acclimation salinity had **no significant effect** on final preferred temperature (33.5°C), but acclimation temperature did significantly affect preference. Critically, salinity strongly influenced **lethal temperature tolerance**: fish acclimated to 15 ‰ salinity survived exposure to 15°C, while fish acclimated to 0 ‰ and 30 ‰ salinity died at the same temperature, supporting a hypothesis of reduced osmoregulatory stress at intermediate ("near-isosmotic") salinities. The thermal zone of tolerance in freshwater was determined to be 17–35°C, independent of acclimation temperature. The authors conclude that *S. melanotheron* is relatively stenothermal in freshwater but euryhaline, and its thermal tolerance may expand in brackish water, suggesting potential for further range expansion in North America.

## Background & Description

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range**: Brackish coastal/estuarine waters from central [[Liberia]] to southern [[Cameroon]], West Africa.
- **Introduced range**: Established in eastern estuarine waters of [[Tampa Bay]], Florida, from the Alafia River to Lithia Spring (Hensley and Courtenay, 1980).
- **Salinity tolerance**: Euryhaline; tested across 0, 15, and 30 ‰ salinities.
- **Osmotic physiology hypothesis**: The species is proposed to be near-isosmotic with its environment at ~15 ‰ salinity, analogous to findings in *Tilapia nilotica* (body fluid tonicity of 11.6 ‰; Farmer and Beamish, 1969). This reduces energetic cost of osmoregulation, freeing metabolic resources to combat thermal stress — consistent with a corollary of **[[Shelford's Law of Tolerance]]**.
- **Field observations**: Specimens (misidentified as *T. heudeloti*) were documented surviving in Tampa Bay at water temperatures as low as 9.6°C (Springer and Finucane, 1963), suggesting short-term survival outside laboratory-defined tolerance zones is possible in situ.
- **Comparative species**: Behavior and thermal preference patterns compared to [[Oreochromis Niloticus]] (*Tilapia nilotica*, Beamish, 1970) and [[Oreochromis Mossambicus]] (Smit et al., 1981), which also exhibits osmoregulatory collapse near 15°C.
- **Invasion ecology relevance**: Considered alongside other introduced cichlids (e.g., [[Oreochromis Aurea]]/Tilapia aurea) known to seek thermal refuge in power plant effluents during cold periods (Buntz and Manooch, 1969; Nobel et al., 1976).

## Key Findings

### Temperature Preference
- **Final preferred temperature**: 33.5°C (independent of salinity).
- Acclimation **temperature** had a significant effect on preferred temperature (Two-way ANOVA, P < 0.05), but acclimation **salinity** did not (P > 0.05), nor did the temperature × salinity interaction.
- Highest preferred temperatures occurred near 25°C acclimation across all salinities.
- Lowest preferred temperature recorded: 20.68°C, in fish acclimated to 15°C at 15 ‰ salinity (this treatment was excluded from the ANOVA due to unequal sample design).
- Fish could not be reliably acclimated to 15°C at 0 ‰ or 30 ‰ salinity — mortality occurred within six days, indicating severe thermal-osmotic stress.

### Lethal Temperature Tolerance
- **Upper lethal temperature**: 37°C (consistent across salinities, with slight reduction in survival at 30 ‰).
- **Lower lethal temperature** (fish acclimated to 25°C):
  - 0 ‰ salinity: 15°C (lethal)
  - 15 ‰ salinity: **15°C survived at 100%** — notably lower lethal tolerance
  - 30 ‰ salinity: 15°C (lethal)
- Complete mortality (0% survival) occurred at 9, 11, and 13°C across all salinities, and at 39–41°C across all salinities.
- **Thermal zone of tolerance in freshwater**: 17–35°C, not dependent on acclimation temperature (tested at 17, 25, 35, and 37°C acclimation).
- **Resistance time** (time-to-death) did vary with acclimation temperature, even though the ultimate lethal thresholds did not — fish acclimated to 17°C survived 15°C exposure for 4–7 days before death, longer than implied by prior single-point lethal temperature estimates.

### Comparison to Prior Literature
- Shafland and Pestrak (1982) reported a lower lethal threshold of 10–11°C for *S. melanotheron* using a 1°C/day cooling protocol — likely an overestimate of tolerance because their fish were not held long enough at 15°C to reveal delayed mortality (resistance-time effect).
- Findings are consistent with osmoregulatory collapse observed in *[[Oreochromis Mossambicus]]* at 15°C (Smit et al., 1981).

### Ecological/Management Implications
- Cool temperature (not salinity per se) appears to be the primary limiting factor on *S. melanotheron* dispersal.
- Because the species is euryhaline and shows salinity-dependent thermal tolerance, it may be able to disperse **further northward in brackish water** than in either freshwater or full-strength marine environments.
- The species can withstand temperatures outside its defined "zone of tolerance" for short durations, and behaviorally selects temperatures well within tolerance limits when given the choice (via preference behavior) — suggesting potential for further **range expansion in North America**, particularly in estuarine corridors or near thermal refugia (e.g., power plant effluents).
