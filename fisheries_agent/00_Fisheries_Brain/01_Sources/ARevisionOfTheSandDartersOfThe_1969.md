# A Revision of the Sand Darters of the Subgenus Ammocrypta (Pisces, Percidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #Ammocrypta

## Executive Summary
This dissertation (Williams, 1969, University of Alabama) presents a comprehensive systematic revision of the [[Sand Darters]] within the subgenus **Ammocrypta**, genus *Ammocrypta*, family [[Percidae]]. The study analyzed seventeen taxonomic characters across a large sample size (~900 *A. beanii*, 500 *A. clara*, 700 *A. vivax*, 1,000 *A. pellucida*; total ~3,100 specimens) to resolve taxonomy, zoogeography, and intraspecific variation. The work formally describes a new subspecies of *A. beanii* and a new subspecies of *A. pellucida*, clarifies long-standing confusion between *A. pellucida* and *A. vivax*, and establishes two distinct species groups within the subgenus: the **beanii group** (*A. beanii*, *A. clara*) and the **pellucida group** (*A. pellucida*, *A. vivax*). Distribution maps and morphological illustrations of male specimens accompany the diagnoses.

## Background & Description
The subgenus [[Ammocrypta]] is diagnosed by inhabiting sandy-bottomed streams, with species differentiated by squamation, pigmentation, fin banding, breeding tubercles, vertebral counts, and preopercular/canal pore characters.

### Ammocrypta beanii (Bean's Sand Darter)
- Dark blotch on anterior spines of first dorsal fin
- Median band on anal and soft dorsal fins
- Belongs to the **beanii group** (see group characters below)

### Ammocrypta beanii vermiculata (new subspecies)
- Distinguished from *A. b. beanii* by a **dark marginal and submarginal band on the median fins** (rather than blotch + median band pattern)
- Described from Perdido, Escambia, and Choctawhatchee Bay drainages (western Florida, southeastern Alabama)

### Ammocrypta clara (Western Sand Darter)
- Strong opercular spine present
- Absence of blotches or bands of pigment in the fins
- Well-developed tubercles on pelvic, anal, and caudal fins
- Belongs to the **beanii group**

### Ammocrypta pellucida (Eastern Sand Darter) — nominate subspecies (*A. p. pellucida*)
- Most variable species in the subgenus; variation appears random, with **no clinal or geographic trends**
- Belongs to the **pellucida group**

### Ammocrypta pellucida (new subspecies, Mobile Basin Coastal Plain)
- Distinguished from *A. vivax* by:
  - Absence of dark bands on median fins
  - Absence of breeding tubercles on the anal fin
- Distinguished from nominate *A. p. pellucida* by:
  - More complete squamation of the body
  - Pigmented tip of snout
  - Usually 42 vertebrae
  - First interneural spine positioned between neural spines of the 7th and 8th vertebrae

### Ammocrypta vivax (Scaly Sand Darter)
- Most completely scaled species in the subgenus
- Dark bands present on first and second dorsal fins, and usually the caudal fin
- Long axis of lateral blotches vertically oriented
- Breeding tubercles present on pelvic and anal fins
- Belongs to the **pellucida group**

### Diagnostic Group Characters

| Character | Beanii Group (*A. beanii*, *A. clara*) | Pellucida Group (*A. pellucida*, *A. vivax*) |
|---|---|---|
| Body scalation | Almost naked | Partially to almost completely scaled |
| Transverse scale rows | 3–6 | 9–15 |
| Posterior margin of preopercle | Entire | Usually serrate |
| Preoperculomandibular canal pores | Usually 8 or 9 | Usually 10 |
| Vertebrae | Usually 40 | Usually 42 or 43 |
| Pelvic fin pigmentation | Unpigmented | (not unpigmented; banding present in *A. vivax*) |

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All members of subgenus [[Ammocrypta]] are psammophilic (sand-dwelling) [[Darters]], typically inhabiting sandy-bottomed streams and rivers, exhibiting characteristic **burying behavior** in substrate.
- Habitat range spans the **Gulf Coastal Plain**, the **Mississippi Valley**, and **Great Lakes drainages**.
- **A. b. beanii**: Mobile Basin west to the Big Black River (Mississippi River tributary); disjunct population in the Hatchie River (southwestern Tennessee), interpreted as a result of stream capture from the upper Tombigbee River drainage.
- **A. b. vermiculata**: Perdido, Escambia, and Choctawhatchee Bay drainages (western Florida, southeastern Alabama).
- **A. clara**: Moderate to large rivers throughout the Mississippi Valley, excluding the Tennessee, Missouri, and Arkansas drainages; also occurs in the Sabine and Neches Rivers along the Gulf Coast. Dispersed into the Waupaca River (Lake Michigan tributary), likely via the Fox-Wisconsin Canal — an example of anthropogenic/canal-mediated range expansion.
- **A. p. pellucida**: Widespread throughout most of the Ohio Basin; southern Lake Huron, Lake St. Clair, Lake Erie; below Lake Ontario in the St. Lawrence River drainage.
- **A. pellucida (new subspecies)**: Coastal Plain drainages of the Mobile Basin; previously confused taxonomically with *A. vivax*.
- **A. vivax**: Primarily a Coastal Plain species; center of distribution in the central Mississippi embayment. Range extends from the San Jacinto River (eastern Texas) north to the St. Francis River drainage (southeastern Missouri). Eastern Mississippi River tributaries: Hatchie, Yazoo, Big Black, and Bayou Pierre River drainages. East of the Mississippi River: Pearl, Biloxi Bay, and Pascagoula Bay drainages along the Gulf Coast.

## Key Findings
- The subgenus [[Ammocrypta]] comprises **four species** inhabiting sandy-bottomed streams of the Gulf Coastal Plain, Mississippi Valley, and Great Lakes drainages.
- Two distinct **species groups** are recognized within the subgenus: the **beanii group** (*A. beanii*, *A. clara*) and the **pellucida group** (*A. pellucida*, *A. vivax*), differentiated by squamation, preopercle margin, canal pore counts, vertebral counts, and pelvic fin pigmentation.
- **No extant intermediate forms** exist between the two species groups, and the relationship between them is not considered extremely close.
- A **new subspecies of A. beanii** (from Perdido, Escambia, and Choctawhatchee Bay drainages) is described, distinguished by median fin banding pattern.
- A **new subspecies of A. pellucida** (Mobile Basin Coastal Plain) is described, resolving prior taxonomic confusion with *A. vivax*.
- *A. clara*'s presence in the Waupaca River (Lake Michigan tributary) is attributed to dispersal through the **Fox-Wisconsin Canal**, an example of human-mediated interbasin transfer.
- The disjunct Hatchie River population of *A. b. beanii* is explained by **stream capture** from the upper Tombigbee River drainage, a zoogeographic mechanism for range disjunction.
- *A. pellucida* exhibits the highest intraspecific variation among the four species, but this variation is **random rather than clinal or geographic**.
- Diagnostic characters separating *A. pellucida* from *A. vivax* include presence/absence of dark median fin bands and breeding tubercles on the anal fin.
