## Executive Summary

The Erwin National Fish Hatchery (Erwin NFH), located in Unicoi County, Tennessee, is a federally operated broodstock station managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Established under the 1894 Appropriation Act and physically founded in 1897, the facility serves as a cornerstone of the **National Broodstock Program**, specializing in the production and distribution of genetically sound [[Rainbow Trout]] eggs (green and eyed) rather than raising fish to catchable size for direct stocking. The station supplies State and Federal hatcheries, Indian reservations, research institutions, universities, and foreign governments, and acts as a critical genetic backup facility to safeguard [[Rainbow Trout]] broodstocks maintained at other national facilities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Erwin NFH does not describe a wild ecological niche but rather an artificial, controlled propagation environment designed to mimic conditions suitable for salmonid egg development and broodstock maintenance:

- **Facility Type:** Rainbow trout broodstock station (egg production, not primarily a grow-out/stocking hatchery)
- **Physical Infrastructure:**
  - Hatchery/Office/Visitor Center Complex
  - 24 outside raceways
  - Aeration house
  - 3 pump houses
  - Egg incubation system (newly fabricated)
  - 4 spawning shelters
  - Feed storage/shop house, equipment storage, oil/paint house, generator house
  - Heritage Museum and Picnic Shelter (public education/interpretive component)
- **Distribution Range:** Eggs and fish distributed to [[North Carolina]] and [[Tennessee]] state systems, in addition to broader federal, tribal, and international recipients.
- **Role in Broader System:** Functions as a genetic reservoir and backup node within the National Broodstock Program, reducing risk of genetic loss at primary broodstock stations through strain redundancy.

## Key Findings

- **Historical Establishment:** Founded in 1897 on 11.83 acres; formally authorized under the 1894 Appropriation Act (27 Stat. 362; 28 Stat. 387).
- **Primary Mission:** Production and distribution of genetically sound green and eyed [[Rainbow Trout]] eggs rather than juvenile/catchable fish stocking.
- **Strain Diversity:** Maintains five distinct genetic lines (Eagle Lake, Arlee, Shasta, Fish Lake, Erwin/Arlee Backcross), supporting genetic diversity and backup redundancy across the national hatchery system.
- **Infrastructure Modernization:** Recent upgrades include two new kiosks, sealcoating of asphalt areas, a new egg incubation system, and four new spawning shelters.
- **Funding Trends (1991–1994):**
  - Operations budget increased steadily from $209.0K (1991) to an estimated $239.5K (1994).
  - Maintenance funding declined from $89.0K (1991) to $32.0K (1993).
  - Staffing (FTEs) remained relatively stable, ranging from 3.5–4.0 full-time equivalents.
- **Production Output Trends:**
  - **Fish numbers distributed** declined sharply from 153,273 (1990) to 110,000 (1991) to 53,915 (1992).
  - **Egg numbers distributed** rose from 13,326,991 (1990) to 15,400,000 (1991), with 1992 egg data incomplete in the source.
  - This trend suggests an operational shift toward greater emphasis on egg distribution over live fish distribution, consistent with the station's core mission as an egg-production broodstock facility.
- **Distribution Network:** Primary state recipients identified are [[North Carolina]] and [[Tennessee]], with [[Rainbow Trout]] as the sole distributed species noted in the summary.
