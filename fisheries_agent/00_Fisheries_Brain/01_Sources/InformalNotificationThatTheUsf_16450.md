## Executive Summary
This document is a 1987 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) regarding the conservation status of the [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*). The letter serves as informal notification that the Service intended to propose reclassifying the species from **Endangered** to **Threatened** under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, based on data suggesting recovery efforts had been successful. The correspondence solicits informal scientific comment from a recognized expert prior to formal rulemaking and Federal Register publication.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or physical descriptive data for *Salmo gilae* are provided in this source. The document is administrative/regulatory in nature rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Implied to be restricted, isolated montane freshwater stream systems (consistent with the historical range of [[Gila Trout]] in the [[Gila River]] drainage of New Mexico/Arizona).
- **Population Status**: Ten total populations referenced as being "successfully secured":
  - **5 original (native) populations**
  - **5 introduced (translocated/refugial) populations**
- **Conservation Context**: The species' recovery is attributed to a "concerted recovery effort over the past decade" (i.e., roughly 1977–1987), suggesting active management including population introductions to new or restored habitat as a hedge against extirpation of native stocks.
- **Proposed Management Tool**: The letter notes that special regulations (paired with New Mexico state regulations) would allow **limited angling take**, intended both as a demonstration of the species' value as a **sport fish** and as a mechanism to further support recovery (e.g., via increased public/political support for conservation).

## Key Findings
- **Status Change Proposal**: USFWS proposed reclassifying [[Gila Trout]] from **Endangered** to **Threatened** status, reflecting improved population security since original listing.
- **Recovery Metric**: Success benchmark cited was the securing of 10 distinct populations (5 original + 5 introduced), an early example of using population redundancy (native + refugial/introduced stocks) as a recovery criterion in salmonid conservation.
- **Regulatory Mechanism**: Reclassification to Threatened would permit **limited take** under combined federal special regulations and New Mexico state regulations — an early application of using regulated sport-fishing as an incentive-based conservation strategy for a rare salmonid.
- **Expert Consultation**: The Service explicitly sought input from [[W.L. Minckley]], a leading ichthyologist on southwestern U.S. fishes, indicating reliance on external taxonomic/ecological expertise to inform the rulemaking process.
- **Procedural Note**: This letter predates formal public comment; it functioned as a pre-proposal solicitation for status assessment data (threats, distribution, planned activities) ahead of the formal Federal Register listing/reclassification action.
- **Historical Significance**: Reflects a documented case study in ESA-driven adaptive management for a narrow-range endemic salmonid, illustrating the practical link between population monitoring data and legal status determinations in the late 1980s.
