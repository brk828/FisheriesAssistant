## Executive Summary
This document is a formal letter from the **Desert Fishes Council** to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, dated June 27, 1986, opposing or expressing serious concern over the decision to open the **Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge** to hunting activities before a comprehensive ecosystem management plan was in place. The letter is not a primary scientific research paper but rather a conservation policy communication referencing the **[[Devils Hole Decision]]**, a landmark legal case that protected the aquifer system feeding **[[Devils Hole]]** — the sole natural habitat of the critically endangered **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*). The letter underscores the ecological sensitivity of the Ash Meadows aquifer-spring ecosystem and calls for funding and implementation of a protective management plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The letter references the **[[Ash Meadows Ecosystem]]**, a unique desert spring and aquifer complex in Nevada supporting multiple **[[Ash Meadows Endemic Fishes]]** and other specialized desert aquatic biota.
- The **aquifer system supplying [[Devils Hole]]** is identified as the critical hydrological feature requiring legal and physical protection, as it directly sustains the isolated population of the **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]**, an extreme habitat specialist confined to a single limestone cavern pool.
- The refuge itself is described as harboring a "component biota" implying a broader assemblage of desert-adapted aquatic and terrestrial species dependent on spring-fed wetland habitats within the **[[Mojave Desert]]** region.
- Ecological concerns center on disturbance from **hunting activities** potentially disrupting a fragile, hydrologically isolated ecosystem lacking a finalized management plan.

## Key Findings
- The **Desert Fishes Council**, a body of approximately 350 scientists, agency personnel, and conservationists, played an active role in the **[[Devils Hole Decision]]**, which established legal protection for the aquifer system critical to Devils Hole.
- Council members contributed to the effort that led to the designation of **[[Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge]]**.
- The Council formally expressed "serious misgivings" about opening the refuge to hunting without a prior management plan ensuring ecosystem protection.
- The letter requests two actions from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service:
  1. Manage any hunting activity in a way that safeguards the ecosystem and its component species.
  2. Fund and implement, "at the earliest possible date," a comprehensive management plan for the refuge.
- This document serves as a historical record of conservation advocacy tied to the protection of the **[[Ash Meadows Ecosystem]]** and its endemic aquatic fauna, situating it within the broader context of U.S. desert fish conservation policy in the 1980s.
