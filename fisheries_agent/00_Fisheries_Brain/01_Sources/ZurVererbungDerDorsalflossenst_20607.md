# Inheritance of Dorsal Fin Ray Number in Mollienesia Hybrids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 study by Johannes Horst Schröder, published in the *Zeitschrift für zoologische Systematik und Evolutionsforschung*, tests whether the [[Dorsal Fin Ray Count]] in [[Mollienesia]] hybrids is inherited polygenically, as previously shown for vicariant [[Limia]] species by Breider (1936). Counts were made on 1,259 fish from parental stocks, intraspecific crosses, and interspecific and intergeneric crosses, all reared under constant conditions. The author concludes that the trait is polygenic with freely combinable factors, and that the number of genes involved in Mollienesia is probably smaller (about 10 to 20) than in Limia.

## Background & Description

- **Study question:** Whether the dorsal fin ray count (DFZ), a [[Meristic Characters|meristic character]] that varies within genotypic limits under temperature and salinity, follows the same polygenic inheritance in Mollienesia as in Limia.
- **Earlier work:** Schmidt (1919–1921) on [[Lebistes Reticulatus]] and [[Zoarces Viviparus]]; Breider (1936) on Limia hybrids; Schröder (1964) on Mollienesia stocks.
- **Material:** 1,259 fish counted under a binocular or hand lens after anaesthesia with [[MS-222 Anesthetic]] (about 0.1% solution).
- **Parental stocks:** [[Mollienesia Sphenops]], [[Mollienesia Velifera]], [[Mollienesia Latipinna]] (from the Berlin Aquarium), and [[Lebistes Reticulatus]] (from inbred lines at the Hamburg institute).
- **Rearing conditions:** Water at (24 ± 2) °C annual mean, moderately hard (DH 14°), pH 7, with sea salt added to about 0.1% (slightly brackish). Broods were reared separately.
- **Crossing methods:** Natural pairing and [[Artificial Insemination]] of anaesthetized fish, following Zander (1961).
- **Nomenclature:** F1 and F2 generations, backcrosses (e.g. sphen/sphen-vel), and colour classes I to IV (wild grey, weakly spotted, strongly spotted, uniform black).
- **Expected means:** Arithmetic and geometric means of parental values were calculated as the theoretical expectation for hybrid progeny.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The paper is a laboratory genetics study. It does not examine natural populations, field habitats, or food webs, so ecological context is limited.
- Environmental modification of DFZ by temperature and salinity is acknowledged in the introduction, but the experiments were run under fixed conditions to reduce this effect.
- Heredity is treated as a species- and stock-level trait. Parental stock differences are described as resembling those between subspecies.

## Key Findings

**1. Parental ranges (dorsal fin rays)**
- [[Lebistes Reticulatus]]: 6–7
- [[Mollienesia Sphenops]]: 8–11 (Liberty-type mean 9.75 ± 0.07, n = 99)
- [[Mollienesia Latipinna]]: 13–15 (mean 13.83 ± 0.31, n = 6)
- [[Mollienesia Velifera]]: 17–19 (mean 17.73 ± 0.11, n = 37)
- Sexes did not differ significantly within any species. The difference between the two latipinna means was attributed to chance (P = 0.67).

**2. Intraspecific sphenops hybrids**
- Liberty-type sphenops (colour class I) differs significantly from classes IIa and IV (P < 0.0002), and it behaves like a true subspecies.
- Observed means for the class II hybrid generation (9.15) and the class I stock (9.75) differ significantly (P < 0.0002).
- Observed means for hybrid classes IIb and IIIb match arithmetic expectations (P = 0.90).

**3. Interspecific Mollienesia hybrids**
- **Velifera × latipinna:** F1 mean 15.11 ± 0.07 (n = 85), range 14–16. The distribution is shifted toward fewer rays than the arithmetic expectation (15.78), a "left shift."
- **Sphenops × velifera:** F1 hybrids show a narrower range (13–14) than either parent. F2 generations have means around 12.4–12.5, below the expected 13.74 (arithmetic). The two reciprocal F1 means do not differ (P = 0.63), so plasmatic (cytoplasmic) inheritance is unlikely.
- **Backcrosses:** Backcrosses to sphenops or velifera show the same deviations and fall 1–2 classes narrower than F2.
- **Sphenops × latipinna:** A right shift appears in some crosses, but sample sizes are too small for firm conclusions.
- **Lebistes × Mollienesia (sterile F1):** A left shift appears only in Leb × latipinna (9.70 vs 10.34 expected); the Leb × velifera mean (10.64) falls between the parental distributions.

**4. Statistical summary**
- In Mollienesia, about 60% of hybrid generations fall outside the 3-sigma confidence interval for the expected mean, compared with about 50% in Limia.
- Deviations from expectation are roughly ten times larger in Mollienesia than in Limia.
- The observed mean is always below the arithmetic expectation in Mollienesia, and the geometric mean fits sphenops-derived crosses better.

**5. Genetic interpretation**
- A seven-dominant-gene model for sphenops DFZ fits the F2 of (sphen × vel)² well (χ² = 5.48, P = 0.49, Table 5).
- The F1 of sphen × vel cannot be explained by a comparably simple model. The author proposes additive genes of equal effect, possibly 10 to 20 loci, with possible epistasis or isophane polygeny.
- Limia shows purely intermediate inheritance with narrow F2 variation, suggesting a larger number of genes.
- The larger DFZ in Mollienesia is probably recessive or hypostatic.
- The modal count of 8 to 10 rays is proposed as the ancestral condition for primitive Poeciliidae.

**6. Conclusions**
- DFZ is inherited polygenically in Mollienesia, like in Limia, with freely combinable factors.
- Deviations from normal distribution in F2 can be explained by dominance of sphenops genes or additive gene action.
- Mollienesia likely has fewer controlling factors (10–20) than Limia.
