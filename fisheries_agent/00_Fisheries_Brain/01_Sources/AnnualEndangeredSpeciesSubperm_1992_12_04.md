# Annual Endangered Species Subpermit Report (1992) — Tucson BLM

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1992 memorandum from the Bureau of Land Management (Tucson Resource Area Office) to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service documents annual monitoring activities for three federally listed fish species in southeastern Arizona: the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Gila Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Monitoring efforts focused on [[Cienega Creek]], [[Bonita Creek]], and [[Big Spring]]. The report confirms successful population sampling of topminnow and chub populations in Cienega Creek, a null detection of topminnow at Big Spring, and a failure to detect any Razorback Sucker in Bonita Creek despite intensive electroshocking effort. No listed bat species were encountered during concurrent inventories.

## Background & Description
The source document is a regulatory/administrative field report and does not provide morphological descriptions of the species involved. Morphological identification was presumably conducted via standard field taxonomic keys during collection, distinguishing:
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis*) — small-bodied livebearer (Poeciliidae), collected via seining, consistent with its preference for shallow, low-velocity marginal habitats.
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*) — cyprinid minnow, collected via backpack electroshocking, consistent with occupation of deeper pool habitats.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-bodied catostomid, notable for a pronounced dorsal nuchal hump (not described in this text but relevant to species identity); targeted in Bonita Creek electroshocking survey.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Cienega Creek]]** (Pima County, AZ): Supports sympatric populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Gila Chub]], sampled across 6 and 4 locations respectively. This creek functions as a critical refugium habitat within the Gila River drainage.
- **[[Big Spring]]** (Graham County, AZ): A spring-fed habitat surveyed visually for Gila Topminnow; represents a marginal or potentially extirpated site for this species.
- **[[Bonita Creek]]**: Lower 3-mile reach surveyed for Razorback Sucker via electroshocking; this species typically requires larger river/reservoir habitats, suggesting Bonita Creek may represent peripheral, transient, or unsuitable habitat for this taxon.
- Sampling methodology varied by species' habitat preference: seining for topminnow (shallow margins), backpack electroshocking for chub and sucker (pools, deeper runs).

## Key Findings
- **9,182 individual [[Gila Topminnow]]** were collected (and released alive) from 6 sites along Cienega Creek during the AZGFD "fall fish count" (Oct 27–Nov 10, 1992).
- **63 individual [[Gila Chub]]** were collected from 4 sites along Cienega Creek during the same survey period.
- **Zero [[Gila Topminnow]]** were visually observed at [[Big Spring]] in October 1992, indicating possible local absence or very low detectability.
- **Zero [[Razorback Sucker]]** were collected from the lower 3 miles of [[Bonita Creek]] despite intensive backpack electroshocking effort in June–July 1992, suggesting absence, extremely low density, or unsuitable habitat conditions.
- No listed bat species were encountered during concurrent inventory efforts (noted as a negative result outside primary fish focus).
- All captured topminnow and chub were returned live to Cienega Creek, indicating a catch-and-release monitoring protocol consistent with endangered species handling permits.
