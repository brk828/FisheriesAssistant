# Aquaculture in Fresh Waters: A List of Selected Reference Books and Monographs, 1951-1984

#ichthyology #aquaculture #freshwater-systems #bibliography

## Executive Summary

This FAO Fisheries Circular (No. 724 Revision 2) is a comprehensive bibliographic compilation of 255 selected reference books and monographs on freshwater aquaculture published between 1951 and 1984. Compiled by André G. Coche of the [[Inland Water Resources and Aquaculture Service]], the document serves as a reference tool for students, researchers, field workers, and laymen seeking published information on freshwater aquaculture. The list emphasizes recent publications (most less than ten years old at time of compilation) still available from publishers or technical bookstores. While predominantly English-language sources, the bibliography includes major French, German, Italian, and Spanish titles. The document features chronological organization by publication year, with alphabetical ordering by author within each year, supplemented by comprehensive author, taxonomic, geographic, and subject indexes.

## Background & Description

The circular was prepared in August 1984 as part of a series of FAO publications documenting aquaculture literature. It complements three related FAO circulars: (i) Aquaculture in marine waters (FAO Fish.Circ. 723 Rev.2, 1984), (ii) A list of FAO publications related to aquaculture, 1966-1982 (FAO Fish.Circ. 744, 1982), and (iii) List of serials, newsletters, bibliographies and meeting proceedings related to aquaculture (FAO Fish.Circ. 758, 1983). The document is structured with a main reference list spanning pages 5-21, followed by four specialized indexes: Author Index (p. 19), Geographic Index (p. 23), Taxonomic Index (p. 25), and Subject Index (p. 27). Each reference entry includes author(s), publication year, title, publisher location, publisher name, and pagination. Many entries note translations or multiple editions in different languages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography encompasses freshwater aquaculture across diverse ecological contexts and geographic regions. Coverage includes extensive aquaculture systems (polyculture in ponds, rice-field aquaculture, floodplain management), intensive commercial operations (cage culture, recirculation systems, hatcheries), and integrated systems (animal-fish farming, waste recycling). Environmental topics addressed include water quality management, pollution control, heated effluent utilization from power plants, biological control of reservoirs, and aquatic vegetation management. The taxonomic scope is broad, covering major cultured species including [[Cyprinus carpio]] (common carp), [[Chinese Carps]], [[Salmonids]], [[Tilapias]], [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish), [[Anguilla]] species (eels), [[Macrobrachium rosenbergii]] (freshwater prawns), and [[Crayfish]]. Geographic coverage is global, with particular emphasis on North America, Europe, Asia (especially Japan, China, India, Philippines, Thailand), and developing regions in Africa and Latin America.

## Key Findings

The bibliography reveals several key patterns in freshwater aquaculture literature from 1951-1984:

**Taxonomic Focus**: The most extensively documented species are [[Cyprinus carpio]] (common carp) with 52 references, [[Salmonids]] with 48 references, [[Tilapias]] with 29 references, [[Anguilla]] species with 27 references, and [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish) with 18 references. This reflects both commercial importance and research investment in these taxa.

**Subject Distribution**: The subject index identifies dominant themes including general aquaculture management (45 references), diseases and health (42 references), nutrition and feeding (35 references), hatchery operations (20 references), water quality (28 references), and culture techniques for specific species. Emerging topics include waste heat utilization (8 references), integrated farming systems (7 references), and aquaculture economics (15 references).

**Geographic Patterns**: North American literature dominates with 35 USA-specific and 8 Canada-specific references. European coverage includes 18 references for Western Europe and 8 for Germany. Asian aquaculture is well-represented with 15 Japan-specific, 8 India-specific, and 7 Philippines-specific references. This distribution reflects both research capacity and aquaculture industry development.

**Multilingual Resources**: While English predominates, the bibliography includes 28 French titles, 18 German titles, 15 Spanish titles, and 8 Italian titles, facilitating access for non-English-speaking practitioners. Many works exist in multiple language editions (e.g., Huet's "Traité de pisciculture" in French, English, and Spanish).

**Practical Orientation**: The collection balances scientific research with practical application, including extension manuals (e.g., INADES-Formation fish farming course, Yancey's warmwater fish culture manual), construction guides (e.g., pond building, hatchery design), and disease diagnosis handbooks. This reflects the document's intended audience of both researchers and field practitioners.

**Historical Development**: The temporal distribution shows accelerating publication rates, with 15 titles from 1951-1960, 35 from 1961-1970, 85 from 1971-1980, and 120 from 1981-1984, indicating rapid expansion of aquaculture knowledge and practice during this period.

**Interdisciplinary Coverage**: Beyond fish culture, the bibliography encompasses related fields including fish physiology (Hoar and Randall's 9-volume series), aquatic ecology, water chemistry, genetics and breeding, parasitology, nutrition, and socio-economics, reflecting aquaculture's multidisciplinary nature.

The document notes plans for subsequent revisions to maintain currency and solicits user feedback for improvements, demonstrating FAO's commitment to ongoing information dissemination in support of global aquaculture development.
