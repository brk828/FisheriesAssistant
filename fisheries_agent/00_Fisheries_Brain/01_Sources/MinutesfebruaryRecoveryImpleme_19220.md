# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee Meeting Minutes – February 21, 1990

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-program #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

These minutes document the February 21, 1990 meeting of the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]], which oversees recovery efforts for four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Key topics included the impending listing of the [[Razorback Sucker]], progress on water rights acquisition, [[Section 7 Consultation]] activities (especially [[Flaming Gorge]] operations and [[Animas-La Plata Project]]), propagation and genetics management planning, and integration of [[San Juan River]] recovery efforts. The committee approved a water rights acquisition strategy, extended the comment period for [[Flaming Gorge]] technical reports to April 15, 1990, and accepted a proposal for a [[San Juan River]] fishery plan to be completed by July 1, 1990.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] was established to recover four endangered cyprinid and catostomid fishes endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The program involves federal agencies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Land Management]]), state agencies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming), water users, environmental groups, and power utilities. The February 1990 meeting addressed program structure, research priorities, water management, and regulatory compliance under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

**Species Status (as of February 1990):**

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]**: Relatively stable in the [[Green River]], though reproduction was poor in the upper Green and good in the lower Green in 1989 (potential relation to oil spill unknown). Few individuals in the [[San Juan River]]; continued decline evident in the mainstem [[Colorado River]].
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: No evidence of reproductive success in wild populations. Propagated fish have been stocked in the upper [[Green River]]. Proposed for federal listing with publication in the Federal Register expected June–July 1990.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: Very rare. The Service directed that any captured bonytails be brought into refugia.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: Doing well in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Blackrocks]] area of the [[Colorado River]]; status uncertain or poor elsewhere.

**Program Structure:**

The program operates through a tiered committee structure: the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]] (policy level), [[Management Group]] (coordination), [[Technical Group]] (scientific review), and various subcommittees (Sediment, Information and Education, Propagation, Water Rights Acquisition). Recent criticism of the [[Technical Group]] focused on alleged political influence, level of technical expertise, and overlap with the [[Management Group]]. Lee Mills prepared a comparative report on other basin-wide fishery programs, concluding the program structure is generally sound but with room for improvement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangered fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]] evolved in a highly variable, turbid, warm-water riverine system. Habitat alterations—including flow regulation by dams (e.g., [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Ruedi Reservoir]]), water depletions for agriculture and municipal use, sediment reduction, and introduction of nonnative fishes—have severely impacted native fish populations. Key ecological concerns include:

- **Flow Regime Alteration**: Dams have changed natural hydrographs, reducing peak flows and altering temperature regimes. The [[15-Mile Reach]] of the [[Colorado River]] is a critical habitat area where flow management (via releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]]) is being implemented to benefit endangered fishes.
- **Sediment Dynamics**: The [[Sediment Subcommittee]] finalized a discussion paper prioritizing sediment-related research. Investigations on the [[Yampa River]] showed minimal sediment impact to date, prompting cost-benefit evaluation of future sediment research.
- **Nonnative Species**: Nonnative fish predation and competition are major threats. The Service is negotiating agreements with Utah and Wyoming to control nonnative fish stocking. Utah requested that discussions include the proposed introduction of rainbow smelt in [[Lake Powell]]. The Service has limited authority to require states to consult on stocking unless federal funds are involved.
- **Water Rights and Instream Flow**: Securing water rights for instream flows is critical. The [[Water Rights Acquisition Subcommittee]] developed a status report and recommendations, including evaluation of conditional water rights on the [[Little Snake River]] and [[Yampa River]] (Cross Mountain), and water availability studies for the [[15-Mile Reach]].

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker Listing:**

- Proposed rule expected in Federal Register June–July 1990, with 1-year comment period.
- Conference reports required when a project "would likely jeopardize" a proposed species (vs. "may affect" for listed species).
- Projects already consulted on would not necessarily require reinitiation of [[Section 7 Consultation]] unless continuing federal involvement exists.

**Section 7 Consultations:**

- Multiple consultations involving water depletions were completed or ongoing (see Attachment 4 for detailed list).
- [[Flaming Gorge]] technical study reports (basis for biological opinion) under review; comment period extended from 30 to 60 days (to April 15, 1990) due to workload on [[Animas-La Plata]] opinion.
- [[Muddy Creek Biological Opinion]] completed (7,716 acre-feet depletion, $80,324 in recovery funds).
- [[Sandstone Reservoir]] consultation ongoing (30,600 acre-feet depletion, $318,546 in recovery funds).

**Water Rights Acquisition:**

- The [[Water Rights Acquisition Subcommittee]] report was approved with revisions. Key recommendations:
  - Secure services of a water rights engineer and appraiser (funded via Section 7 funds, implemented by September 30, 1990).
  - Evaluate conditional water rights on the [[Little Snake River]].
  - Conduct water availability study for the [[15-Mile Reach]] after defining critical assumptions related to [[Orchard Mesa Check]] and conditional water rights.
  - Not actively pursue purchase or donation of conditional water rights until certain issues are resolved.
- [[The Nature Conservancy]] is negotiating water rights on [[Cross Mountain]] ([[Yampa River]]) and lower [[Little Snake River]].
- Congress allocated $1 million for water rights acquisition (held by Service Finance Center until expended).

**Propagation and Genetics Management:**

- Some populations ([[Colorado Squawfish]] in mainstem [[Colorado River]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) are so imperiled that natural reproduction cannot sustain them.
- The [[Propagation/Genetics Management Plan]] calls for broad genetic surveys, development of refugia, and prioritization of populations.
- Short-term fish needs data transmitted to lower basin production programs for 1990–1991.
- Long-term needs require a multipurpose facility integrating refugia/production, research and development, and propagation coordination.
- [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] [[Hatchery Feasibility Study]] is coordinated with the Propagation Plan; three consultants short-listed, contract expected mid- to late April 1990, completion December 1991.

**San Juan River:**

- Considerable controversy arose over a proposed "San Juan River Management Plan" at a January 1990 meeting in Durango. Water users objected to the plan's scope and policy implications.
- The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] proposed to deliver a "San Juan River Fishery Plan" by July 1, 1990, as the final product of the study funded by the [[Recovery Implementation Program]]. This plan would identify major problems, needs, and limiting factors for endangered fishes, and prioritize future work.
- An expanded group of governmental and water user representatives would then integrate competing uses into an overall management approach similar to the [[Recovery Implementation Program]].
- The committee accepted this proposal. Future considerations will determine whether the [[San Juan River]] effort should be incorporated into the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] and the source of future funding.
- New Mexico's interest in joining the program is unknown; the committee agreed no additional program funding for the [[San Juan River]] would be supported unless it is incorporated into the program with additional funds.

**Biology Researchers Advisory Group:**

- Researchers requested greater input into the program, specifically in developing research recommendations and priorities.
- The proposed group's primary function (providing recommendations on research and priorities to the [[Technical Group]]) raised concerns about conflict of interest, as group members submit proposals for funding.
- The committee could not formally recognize the group due to potential conflict of interest and violation of federal procurement regulations.
- The group was encouraged to operate informally to provide input to the [[Technical Group]].
- The [[Management Group]] will consider the concept of researchers' input along with other program organization/structure issues.

**FY-1989/1990/1991 Work Plan:**

- FY-1989 Annual Report summarizing project results to be completed and distributed in late March 1990.
- FY-1990 projects generally on track and within budget.
- FY-1991 depletion charge adjusted to $10.91 per acre-foot (based on 4.8% Consumer Price Index for 1989).
- Multiyear funding policy approved: funds allocated for each year of a multiyear project provided funds are available, annual reports are timely, and the project is on track. Initial proposals must clearly identify study length, goals, objectives, workplan, schedules, deliverables, and detailed cost breakdown. Continuation of funding depends on achievement of previous year's goals. Projects longer than 3.5 years require more complete interim reports.
- Draft 5-Year Road Map distributed for review; comments due March 2, 1990. [[Technical Group]] will develop FY-1991 research priorities at March 13–14 meeting. Final document for committee approval at next meeting (August 1990).
- Section 7 funds status: FWS account balance $501,125 (as of February 2, 1990); [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] account $42,096.80 plus interest.
- Funding for the program is in the President's 1991 budget.
- Committee agreed to send representatives to Washington in early summer (not during budget process) to maintain contact with new budget examiner.

**Information and Education:**

- FY-1990 activities include Recovery Program Newsletter, full-color poster, special section in Colorado fishing regulations brochure, scripted slide show, and media relations plan/kit.
- Possible FY-1991 projects: informational wallet cards, interpretive displays, video, joint Colorado/Utah Project Wild program.

**Next Meeting and River Trip:**

- River trip: August 23–25, 1990, [[Blackrocks]]/[[Westwater]] area. Key locals and Washington staffers to be invited. Service sponsoring.
