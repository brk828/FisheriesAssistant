## Executive Summary

This landmark 1953 study by H. S. Swingle used **emulsifiable rotenone sampling** (1 p.p.m.) to characterize fish populations in large Alabama rivers (Coosa, Tallapoosa, Black Warrior, Tombigbee, Tensaw) and their downstream impoundments (Lay Lake, Jordan Lake, Lake Martin). The work aimed to determine whether population dynamics concepts developed for [[Farm Pond Fish Populations]] (predator-prey balance, standing crop, harvestable percentages) apply equally to riverine and reservoir systems.

Key conclusions: (1) large piscivorous catfishes (channel, blue, flathead) function as the dominant predator ("C") guild in rivers, analogous to bass in ponds; (2) river populations show balance metrics (Aτ, F/C) similar to well-managed ponds; (3) river impoundment triggers major shifts in species composition, favoring [[Gizzard Shad]] and centrarchids over [[Freshwater Drum]] and catfish; (4) pollution destabilizes population balance, though effects on total standing crop can be either an increase (organic enrichment/fertilization effect) or a decrease (toxic effect), depending on the pollutant source.

## Morphological / Population Metrics Characteristics

Rather than individual organismal morphology, this study defines population-level indices used to characterize fish communities:

- **Standing Crop** — pounds of fish per acre recovered by rotenone sampling.
- **Aτ value** — percentage by weight of "harvestable" fish in the total population (species- and size-specific minimum harvestable lengths given in Table 1, e.g., Largemouth Bass ≥ ~10 in, Blue Catfish ≥ ~16–20 in, Gizzard Shad ≥ ~9–10 in).
  - Aτ < 40 → unsatisfactory balance
  - Aτ < 33 → unbalanced population
- **F/C ratio** — ratio of forage fish weight (F) to piscivorous fish weight (C). Large catfishes (channel, blue, flathead) ≥16 inches were classified as the principal "C" (piscivorous) species in river systems, replacing the centrarchid-dominated predator role seen in ponds.
- **E value ("environmental value")** — percentage by weight that a given species contributes to the total sampled population; used to track species composition shifts across sites (river vs. impoundment; polluted vs. unpolluted).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**River populations:**
- Dominated by [[Blue Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Freshwater Drum]], and [[Buffalo (Fish)]]; sport fishes (bass, crappie) were minor (<5% of biomass).
- **Freshwater Drum** favored swift-water reaches; **blue catfish, channel catfish, and buffalo** favored deep, quiet waters.
- **Gizzard Shad** were minor in fast currents but dominant in quiet backwaters/cutoffs (e.g., above navigation locks on the Black Warrior, Tombigbee cutoffs).
- **Common Carp** absent from deep/swift rivers but a major component of shallow, muddy Tallapoosa River habitat.
- Standing crops ranged widely: 24.0–482.3 lb/acre depending on river/impoundment and habitat type (cove vs. open water vs. shallow/swift vs. deep).

**Post-impoundment shifts (Coosa River → Lay Lake → Jordan Lake):**
- **[[Gizzard Shad]]**: E value rose from 0.3–1.5 (river) to 27.9–58.3% (impoundments); favored coves over open water.
- **[[Freshwater Drum]]**: E value fell from 33.8–74.2 (river) to 0.1–12.8% (impoundments); favored open water over coves.
- **Catfishes**: slight decline in the upstream reservoir (Lay Lake), sharp decline in the downstream reservoir (Jordan Lake) — possibly reflecting a need for flowing water for reproduction.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]**: absent from main river channel (likely present in tributaries); E values of 3.7–9.5% in impoundments, higher in coves.
- **[[Spotted Bass]]**: relatively stable abundance between river and reservoir.
- **[[Bluegill]]**: increased from unimportant in-river to E = 2.6–14.5% in reservoirs.
- **Crappies**: absent in river, 0.1–3.3% in impoundments.

**Tallapoosa River / Lake Martin comparison** (notable for **absence** of gizzard shad, blue catfish, and freshwater drum):
- Combined predator/game species (largemouth bass, spotted bass, white bass, crappie) reached 24.6–27.9% of Lake Martin's population — much higher than the 6.2–27.7% range seen in gizzard-shad-dominated Coosa impoundments — suggesting gizzard shad presence may suppress relative game-fish biomass proportion.
- **Common Carp** spiked immediately after impoundment (1926) then declined over 24–26 years to ≤4.1% of the population.
- **Largemouth Bass**: E = 3.5 (river) → 3.0–14.4% (impoundment).
- **Bluegill**: E = 3.7 (river) → 11.9–39.5% (impoundment).

**Pollution effects:**
- *Coosa River* (industrial/domestic pollution near Gadsden, AL): unpolluted area — 85.6 lb/acre, F/C = 2.4, Aτ = 82.6 (balanced); polluted area (5 mi downstream) — 8.3 lb/acre, F/C = 62.7, Aτ = 11.7 (severely unbalanced, reduced standing crop).
- *Black Warrior River* (sampled 100 mi below a severe oxygen-depletion pollution event): standing crop increased to 1,069.9 lb/acre (apparent fertilizing effect of sewage-driven plankton bloom), but F/C = 21.2 and Aτ = 27.9 indicated an unbalanced, low-yield population despite high biomass.
- Chemical water-quality analyses (pH, O₂, CO₂) alone were insufficient to detect biological pollution effects; rotenone-based population sampling revealed impacts even when chemistry indicated "recovery."

## Key Findings

1. **Rotenone sampling** (1 p.p.m. emulsifiable formulation, applied via pump/hose) is a workable, if imperfect, method for quantifying large-river and reservoir fish populations; sampling underestimates large evasive fish and small (<2 in) fish, and can kill fish outside the nominal sample area due to downstream drift of poisoned water.
2. Standing crop estimates from rivers (e.g., 45.7–1,544.6 lb/acre) were comparable in magnitude to those from managed/fertilized ponds (~40–1,080 lb/acre), validating cross-system comparison of population indices.
3. Large piscivorous catfishes (channel, blue, flathead ≥16 in) occupy the "C" (predator) niche in rivers analogous to [[Largemouth Bass]] in ponds — a key reinterpretation of trophic structure in riverine systems.
4. Aτ values in unpolluted, un-impounded rivers (70.5–89.0) matched or exceeded desirable pond benchmarks, indicating that natural river populations can be inherently well-balanced.
5. Impoundment produces predictable, directional shifts in species composition — favoring lentic/quiet-water species (gizzard shad, bluegill, largemouth bass, crappie) at the expense of riverine/lotic specialists (freshwater drum, catfishes).
6. Pollution consistently degrades population balance (elevated F/C, depressed Aτ), but its effect on total biomass is context-dependent: it can **increase** standing crop via nutrient enrichment (Black Warrior) or **decrease** it via toxicity/habitat degradation (Coosa), meaning biomass alone is an unreliable pollution indicator — balance indices are more diagnostic.
7. The presence/absence of [[Gizzard Shad]] as a forage base appears inversely related to the relative proportion of predator/game species biomass in reservoirs (Tallapoosa/Lake Martin vs. Coosa impoundments).
