# Roosevelt Diversion Dam Removal Environmental Assessment Correspondence

#ichthyology #environmental-impact #native-fish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This March 7, 1988 correspondence from SWCA, Inc. Environmental Consultants to Dr. W. L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] outlines an environmental investigation for the [[Salt River Project]] regarding the proposed removal of [[Roosevelt Diversion Dam]]. The assessment focuses on four primary concerns: impacts on foraging [[Bald Eagle]]s, indigenous native fish populations, reintroduced or experimental endangered fish populations in the [[Upper Salt River]], and sport fisheries. SWCA planned intensive electrofishing and netting surveys above and below the diversion dam, followed by an onsite workshop on March 17, 1988.

## Background & Description

SWCA, Inc. was retained by the [[Salt River Project]] to investigate potential environmental effects of removing the [[Roosevelt Diversion Dam]] on the [[Salt River]] in Arizona. The project manager, Mark W. Larson, coordinated the assessment from the Phoenix office. Dr. W. L. Minckley, a recognized expert from the Department of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]], was invited to participate as a consultant with a $500.00 honorarium to cover expenses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Roosevelt Diversion Dam]] site supports multiple ecological values requiring assessment. The area provides foraging habitat for [[Bald Eagle]]s and contains indigenous populations of native fishes. The [[Upper Salt River]] also hosts reintroduced or experimental populations of endangered fish species, alongside established sport fisheries. The dam's presence creates distinct upstream and downstream aquatic habitats that would be affected by removal.

## Key Findings

The assessment identified four areas of potential environmental concern related to dam removal:

1. **Impacts on foraging bald eagles** - potential effects on raptor foraging opportunities
2. **Impacts on indigenous populations of native fishes** - effects on resident native fish communities
3. **Adverse effects on reintroduced or experimental populations of endangered fishes** in the [[Upper Salt River]] - risks to conservation efforts for threatened species
4. **Adverse effects on sport fisheries** - impacts on recreational fishing resources

**Planned Methodology**: SWCA planned to assimilate data from ongoing and recently completed studies over a two-week period, followed by intensive electrofishing and netting surveys above and below the diversion dam to characterize fish communities and determine the nature and extent of existing fisheries.

**Workshop**: An onsite Roosevelt Diversion Dam Removal Workshop was scheduled for March 17, 1988 at 1:00 P.M. at the diversion dam site, bringing together representatives from resource agencies, [[Salt River Project]], and SWCA biologists to discuss the proposed removal.
