# Comments on Proposed Critical Habitat Designation for the Humpback Chub in the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #ConservationPolicy #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document consists of two public comment letters authored by field biologist David R. Van Haverbeke, submitted to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regarding the 1993 proposed critical habitat designation for four endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes under 50 CFR Part 17. The core argument advocates for a **substantial expansion** of proposed critical habitat for the [[Humpback Chub]] beyond the initially proposed 8 miles of the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), citing the LCR's role as the basin's primary spawning ground and one of the few self-sustaining populations of the species. The letters raise concerns regarding inadequate evaluation of anthropogenic threats (water diversion, impoundments, uranium extraction), the ecological necessity of natural flood regimes, and the exclusion of key Grand Canyon side tributaries (Shinumo, Havasu, Kanab, Bright Angel, Paria, Spencer Creeks) from proposed critical habitat. Field observations on spawning phenology, non-native predation, and travertine dynamics are presented as evidence for expanded protections.

## Background & Description

Limited direct morphological data is provided, as this is a policy/advocacy document rather than a taxonomic study. Noted observations include:

- **Juvenile Humpback Chub** (captured in [[Shinumo Creek]], 11 September 1991): total length range of **72–110 mm**, captured via minnow traps; likely young-of-year individuals.
- **Small chub** up to **4 inches (~10 cm)** were found in the stomachs of [[Rainbow Trout]] captured in the mainstem Colorado River near the LCR confluence, indicating a vulnerable juvenile size class subject to predation.
- Reference to **razorback sucker** hybrids/pure individuals in the Grand Canyon exhibiting **diminished keels**, suggestive of possible hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]].
- Reference to potential **introgression/hybridization** between [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Roundtail Chub]] in upper basin populations (citing Bruce DeMarais' dissertation and Gila taxonomy studies), implying the LCR population may represent the last genetically pure stock.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary Habitat:** The Little Colorado River is characterized as the humpback chub's most productive spawning ground and supports one of the few self-sustaining populations in the Colorado River Basin.
- **Hydrology:** The species has evolved under a historically fluctuating hydrologic regime; seasonal flooding is hypothesized to be critical for:
  - Scouring travertine and marl deposits to maintain clean gravel/sand spawning substrates.
  - Knocking down travertine barrier dams that could otherwise block fish passage.
  - Creating turbidity that provides refuge from sight-predators (e.g., [[Rainbow Trout]]) at the LCR/mainstem confluence.
  - Supplying sediment for downstream beach-building in Grand Canyon.
- **Spawning Behavior:** 
  - Ripe (reproductive) fish are captured from **March onward** (revising earlier assumption of May–July onset), based on year-round sampling by Arizona State University (A.S.U.).
  - Chub migrate into the LCR to spawn on the **descending hydrograph** of spring flows, typically when discharge recedes to approximately **2,500–3,000 cfs**.
  - Spawning is linked to water temperatures approaching **26°C**, consistent with findings from Karp and Tyus (1990).
- **Threats:**
  - Non-native fish expansion (killifish, red shiner, fathead minnow, common carp) potentially favored by reduced flood disturbance.
  - External **copepod parasite infestation** hypothesized to increase during base (low, clear "blue water") flow periods relative to turbid flood periods.
  - Predation by introduced [[Brown Trout]] implicated in likely historical decline/extirpation of humpback chub from [[Bright Angel Creek]] and potential future threat to the [[Shinumo Creek]] population.
  - Water diversion (e.g., Phelps-Dodge diversion from East Clear Creek to East Verde River), headwater impoundments (Blue Ridge, Lower/Upper Lake Mary, Chevelon, Clear Creek, Springerville reservoirs), and proposed dams (rumored near Winslow) threaten LCR base flow (~200 cfs) and aquifer-fed spring inputs (e.g., [[Blue Springs]]).
  - Uranium extraction contamination risk in the LCR drainage, drawing parallel to issues in Rio Puerco and Moenkopi Wash.
- **Side Tributary Populations (Grand Canyon):**
  - **Shinumo Creek:** Both adult and juvenile chub captured historically (including 7 juveniles in 1991); suspected small resident spawning population.
  - **Havasu Creek:** Adults observed/photographed and captured (via angling) near creek mouth.
  - **Kanab Creek:** Reports of juvenile/YOY captures by Arizona Game & Fish Department (AZG&F).
  - **Bright Angel Creek:** Historic type locality reference (Miller 1946); population likely extirpated due to brown trout predation; potential razorback sucker sighting reported.
  - **Spencer Creek (RM 246):** Juveniles historically captured (Wallis 1954); anecdotal recent sighting.
  - **Paria Creek:** Suspected presence of razorback sucker/flannelmouth hybrids.

## Key Findings

- **Critical habitat as proposed (8 miles of LCR) is argued to be biologically inadequate**, failing to secure the species against upstream water development impacts or to support a future "jeopardy" or "destruction/adverse modification" finding under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act.
- The **Draft Biological Opinion** was criticized for failing to meet **Section 4(b)(8)** requirements, lacking description/evaluation of foreseeable water diversion, impoundment, or recreational development proposals affecting the LCR aquifer and base flow.
- Advocacy for critical habitat designation extending to the **bases of existing headwater impoundments** within the LCR drainage, and inclusion of [[Blue Springs]] (USFWS RM 21) at minimum.
- Recommendation to designate multiple Grand Canyon side tributaries — [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Havasu Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Spencer Creek]], and [[Paria Creek]] — as critical habitat for both **Humpback Chub** and **Razorback Sucker**, based on documented captures/sightings of adults and juveniles.
- Mainstem Colorado River critical habitat boundaries (RM 34 Nautiloid Canyon to RM 208 Granite Park) were noted as excluding documented humpback chub captures down to **RM 227** and excluding both historically proposed dam sites (Marble Canyon, Bridge Canyon).
- No critical habitat was proposed for the **Colorado Pikeminnow (Squawfish)** below Lake Powell, despite historical widespread distribution — flagged as a significant gap.
- Concerns raised regarding inadequate critical habitat for **Bonytail Chub**, given historically wider distribution.
- The 1993 spawning season (with major scouring floods in September 1992 and January 1993) reportedly produced an "extremely successful" young-of-year cohort, supporting the hypothesis that flood-scoured clean substrates are important for reproductive success.
- Behavioral note: chub appear to enter the LCR on a **descending hydrograph**, and stabilized/regulated flows in both the LCR and mainstem could create static hydrologic conditions that effectively "close the doors" to chub movement between systems.
