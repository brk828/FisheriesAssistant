## Executive Summary

This document reports on a U.S. Department of the Interior (DOI) Record of Decision (ROD) recommending **25 river segments across 13 river areas in Arizona** for designation under the **National Wild and Scenic Rivers System**, following a ten-year review by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM). While the report is primarily a land-management and policy communication, it is directly relevant to aquatic ecology because the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]] (1968) explicitly requires that designated rivers possess measurable "fish and wildlife" values alongside scenic, recreational, geologic, and cultural values. The rivers recommended for protection include several systems of major importance to native desert fish conservation in the American Southwest, including the [[Gila River]], [[San Pedro River]], [[Aravaipa Creek]], and [[Bonita Creek]] — waterways historically significant as refugia for endemic and imperiled desert fish fauna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Free-flowing desert rivers and associated riparian corridors on BLM-managed public lands in Arizona (spanning nearly 14 million acres under study).
- **Qualifying ecological values** under the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]]: scenic, recreational, geologic, **fish and wildlife**, and cultural values — fish habitat quality is a formal legal criterion for designation suitability.
- **Key river systems recommended as suitable** (total 233.5 miles, 65,500 acres of public land), many of which are recognized [[Riparian Ecosystem|riparian habitats]] supporting native Southwestern aquatic fauna:
  - [[Paria River]] – 28 mi
  - [[Big Sandy River]] – 9.2 mi
  - [[Burro Creek]] – 24.2 mi
  - [[Bill Williams River]] – 19 mi
  - [[Santa Maria River]] – 17.6 mi
  - [[Agua Fria River]] – 22.4 mi
  - [[Gila River]] (Middle) – 17.6 mi
  - [[Aravaipa Creek]] – 10 mi
  - [[Bonita Creek]] – 8.1 mi
  - [[Lower San Francisco River]] – 6.4 mi
  - [[Gila River]] (Gila Box) – 26.6 mi
  - [[Cienega Creek]] – 10.5 mi
  - [[San Pedro River]] – 44 mi
- **Virgin River watershed** (Utah, Arizona, Nevada) recommended for a separate Congressional study, notable as habitat for several endemic desert fish species.
- **Rivers deemed "nonsuitable"** and released from further consideration (172.3 miles total across 14 river areas), continuing under standard BLM land-use management rather than Wild and Scenic protections:
  - [[Wright Creek]], [[Francis Creek]], [[Big Sandy River]] (additional segment), [[Burro Creek]] (additional segment), [[Bill Williams River]] (additional segment), [[Santa Maria River]] (additional segment), [[Hassayampa River]], [[Gila River]] (Middle, additional segment), [[Turkey Creek]], [[Bonita Creek]] (additional segment), [[Lower San Francisco River]] tributaries, [[Swamp Springs Canyon]], [[Hot Springs Canyon]], [[San Pedro River]] (additional segment).
- Ecological framing: designation is intended to preserve river systems in a **free-flowing condition**, which is a critical habitat requirement for many native desert fish species that depend on natural hydrologic connectivity and unimpeded flow regimes (as opposed to impounded or heavily diverted systems).

## Key Findings

- The DOI/BLM Record of Decision recommends **25 segments (233.5 miles)** across 13 Arizona river areas for Wild and Scenic River designation, protecting 65,500 acres of public land.
- **19 segments (172.3 miles)** in 14 river areas were determined "nonsuitable" and released from further consideration, though they remain under existing BLM land-use management.
- The [[Virgin River]] watershed (spanning Utah, Arizona, and Nevada) was recommended for a dedicated Congressional study rather than immediate designation.
- The review process spanned **1986–1994**, with the Final Environmental Impact Statement completed in December 1994, followed by the ROD.
- Fish and wildlife values are explicitly codified as one of the qualifying criteria under the [[Wild and Scenic Rivers Act]] of 1968, linking river hydrological protection directly to aquatic habitat conservation outcomes.
- Congress holds final authority to formally designate these recommended segments as wild and scenic rivers; the ROD represents an administrative recommendation, not final legal designation.
- BLM currently administers 33 of the 159 rivers (2,022 of 10,700 total miles) in the National Wild and Scenic Rivers System, underscoring the agency's substantial role in freshwater and riparian habitat stewardship nationally.
- No specific fish species, population data, or morphological/ecological fish studies are presented in this source; its relevance to ichthyology is contextual — establishing regulatory protection for river habitats that sustain native Southwestern freshwater fish assemblages, such as those historically found in [[Aravaipa Creek]] and the [[Gila River]] system, both noted centers of endemic fish biodiversity in Arizona.
