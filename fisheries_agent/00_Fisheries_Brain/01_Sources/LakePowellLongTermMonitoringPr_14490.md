# Lake Powell Long-Term Monitoring Program

## Executive Summary
This document is a **Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)** proposal (Susan Hueftle, 1995) to continue long-term limnological monitoring of **[[Lake Powell]]**, a large, warm desert reservoir on the Colorado River formed by **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]**. Rather than a fish-specific study, the paper focuses on the **physical, chemical, and biological (plankton) dynamics** of the reservoir and its tailwater, which govern downstream water quality entering the **[[Grand Canyon]]** reach of the Colorado River. The program, active since 1965, tracks reservoir stratification, nutrient cycling, and plankton populations, providing foundational data that other agencies use to study native and non-native **[[Lake Powell Fishery|fishery dynamics]]** and toxicology. The proposal requests continuation of quarterly lake-wide sampling, monthly forebay/tailwater monitoring, and continuous automated data logging, with a total FY96–97 budget of $534,680.

*Not applicable — this source is a program/methods proposal focused on reservoir limnology and monitoring infrastructure rather than organismal morphology.* No fish species, anatomical descriptions, or taxonomic keys are presented. Biological sampling described is limited to:

- **[[Chlorophyll-a]]**: measured via 0.45 µm glass filtration and tri-chromatic lab analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study addresses the **reservoir ecosystem** as a transitional and evolving habitat:
- **[[Lake Powell]]** is characterized as a young, evolving reservoir (post-1965 fill) with incompletely understood **stratification**, thermoclines, chemoclines, and biogeochemical cycling.
- Water quality changes over a ~2-year residence time before release, directly shaping conditions in the **[[Colorado River]]** through **[[Glen Canyon]]** and **[[Grand Canyon]]**.
- Key habitat zones monitored include the **epilimnion** (productive surface zone), **penstock depths** (site of organic entrainment and selective withdrawal effects), and tributary **plungelines** (Colorado, San Juan, and Escalante River inflows).
- Tailwater habitat below the dam (**Lees Ferry** and immediately below Glen Canyon Dam) is tracked for downstream recovery of water quality, relevant to riverine fish habitat in the **[[Grand Canyon]]** ecosystem.
- The paper explicitly notes that its limnological data supports separate agency-led studies on **native and non-native fisheries** and toxicology, positioning this work as foundational rather than fishery-specific.

## Key Findings
- **Historical Program Phases**: Monitoring since 1965 is divided into four phases (1965–71, 1972–81, 1982–90, 1990–present), with increasing sophistication from basic temperature/DO meters to multiprobe datalogging (Hydrolab H20/SV3) and biological sampling (plankton, chlorophyll).
- **Sampling Design**: Quarterly lake-wide surveys cover **13 permanent stations** (30-year history) plus ~12 auxiliary stations, using a 31-ft Uniflite cabin cruiser and a jet boat for tributary access.
- **Continuous Monitoring**: Hydrolab datasondes are permanently deployed below the dam and at Lees Ferry, logging temperature, conductivity, DO, and pH hourly.
- **First Full Reservoir Cycle**: Summer 1995 marked the **first completed fill–drawdown–fill cycle** for Lake Powell, though peak-pool periods in the mid-1980s were under-monitored — a noted data gap.
- **Interagency Integration**: Data is stored in a relational database (**INGRES**), integrated with GIS mapping, and shared with agencies including the **U.S. Geological Survey**, **Arizona Game and Fish**, and **Glen Canyon National Recreation Area**.
- **Downstream Relevance**: Correlating forebay/penstock data with below-dam conditions is identified as key to understanding river recovery rates and to informing **Selective Withdrawal** structure planning.
- **Budget**: Total proposed budget for FY1996–1997 is **$534,680**, covering personnel, vessel maintenance, chemical/biological analyses, and travel.
- **Fishery Linkage (Indirect)**: The proposal explicitly states its limnological and plankton data "will in turn benefit other agencies... focusing on Lake Powell's fishery dynamics," but does not itself report fish population, species, or morphological data.
