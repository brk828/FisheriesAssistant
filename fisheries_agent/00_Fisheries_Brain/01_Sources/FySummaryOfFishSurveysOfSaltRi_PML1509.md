# FY2003 Fish Surveys of Central Arizona Project Related Canals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report, prepared by Paul C. Marsh under a cooperative agreement with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), summarizes fish sampling in three canal systems of the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona during fiscal year 2003 (October 2002 to September 2003). The surveyed waters were the [[Salt River Project]] Arizona Canal (SRPN), the [[Salt River Project]] South Canal (SRPS), and the [[Florence Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG) of the [[San Carlos Irrigation Project]]. Sampling followed protocols from [[Clarkson 1996 Monitoring Protocol]].

Sixteen taxa were reliably recorded, excluding species reported separately by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. No species new to the Gila River basin was found. Three native species were collected, all in the two SRP canals. The remaining taxa were [[Non-Native Species]]. Catch totals varied widely because sampling effort, gear, and habitat differed among sites, so the report warns against direct comparison of catches among sites.

## Background & Description

The monitoring program is a long-term effort to track fish populations in selected waters of the Gila River basin. Sampling was conducted by Marsh and BR staff between 30 October 2002 and 13 January 2003. Sites were sampled with [[Electrofishing]], [[Seine Netting]], [[Gill Netting]], and trammel netting, and voucher specimens were retained where needed.

Three native taxa were recorded:
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarkii*)

Non-native taxa included cyprinids, catfishes, livebearers, sunfishes, temperate basses, and cichlids. Among the most important were [[Grass Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Striped Bass]], and [[Blue Tilapia]]. Appendix 1 of the report lists the full set of common and scientific names.

Crayfish and [[Asiatic Clam]] were also noted at several sites, and a softshell turtle was observed in FCG.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Canal sites were not uniform in habitat. Conditions ranged from full flow to dry, and water was clear, turbid, or milky greenish-blue. Substrates were mostly concrete, with patches of sand, silt, muck, and debris. Canal closures and maintenance drawdowns strongly shaped what could be sampled.

Several observations stand out:
- In the SRPS, a concrete spillway and plunge pool below the Lehi Road (Mesa) Demossing Station held a large group of active adult roundtail chub and many adult largemouth bass. The report describes this pool as a valuable refuge for native roundtail chub if it keeps adequate water during canal dry-up.
- Dead and dying Sonora sucker were seen throughout the Tempe Canal reach near the Triple Junction. The report does not give a cause.
- In FCG, sediment and debris prevented a gate from closing on schedule. Sampling was delayed to 30 October 2002, when the canal had not fully drained. The report suggests this delay likely reduced the total fish count.
- Gill netting favors larger fish, so catches at some sites may not reflect smaller individuals.

## Key Findings

**Overall monitoring results**
- A total of 16 taxa was recorded during FY2003 canal monitoring.
- No species new to the Gila River basin was found.
- Striped bass was taken in SRPS. Bluegill and largemouth bass were taken in FCG. None of these three species appeared in the site-specific records of Clarkson (1996).
- Twelve species were taken from SRPN, 13 from SRPS, and seven from FCG.
- Native taxa made up about 25 percent of total species richness in SRPN and SRPS. None was found in FCG.
- Sonora sucker was numerically predominant in all SRPN samples and in several SRPS samples. Roundtail chub and desert sucker were only a small fraction of total numbers.

**Protocol deviations and recommendations**
- No deviations from protocol occurred.
- Late communication from water management entities has undermined the reliability of the monitoring program. The first recommendation is to improve communication between canal operators and the fish-monitoring crews so that sampling can coincide with scheduled and unscheduled outages.

**South Canal (SRPS), 29 to 30 November 2002**
- The survey sampled four sites from the electric fish barrier at Granite Reef Diversion Dam (CM 0.0) to the Triple Junction (CM 9.0). Thirteen species were recorded, 23 percent of them native.
- Site 1 (below the electric barrier) yielded seven species in 60 minutes of seining and dip netting, including roundtail chub, desert sucker, and Sonora sucker. Table 2A reports a total of 63 fish, with Sonora sucker and red shiner common.
- A large flathead catfish (about 15 pounds) was captured by hand in a drying reach about 0.5 mile downstream and returned to the canal.
- Site 2 (River Road Siphon, CM 2.5) yielded five species in a 25-minute gill net set. Table 2B reports a total of 11 fish. Many large grass carp were observed but none was collected.
- Site 3 (RWCD Turnout, CM 4.0) yielded eight species, including roundtail chub, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, [[Channel Catfish]], mosquitofish, bluegill, largemouth bass, and striped bass. Table 2C reports a total of 47 fish. Sonora sucker made up 18 fish (38 percent), bluegill 10 (21 percent), channel catfish 6 (13 percent), and largemouth bass 7 (15 percent).
- One striped bass that resembled white bass (*Morone chrysops*) was retained as a voucher. No white bass was collected.
- Site 4 (Tempe Canal, Triple Junction, CM 9.0 to 9.2) yielded five species: grass carp, Sonora sucker, channel catfish, flathead catfish, and largemouth bass. Only the sucker is native.

**Florence Casa Grande Canal (FCG), 28 to 30 October 2002**
- A total of seven fish species, all non-native, was taken. The total number of fish was small compared with most earlier years.
- Common carp, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, bluegill, and largemouth bass were rare. Red shiner and mosquitofish were common.
- This is the first recorded occurrence of bluegill in the FCG system. The species is common elsewhere in the Gila River basin and interconnected canals.
- Site 1 (below Ashurst-Hayden Diversion Dam, CM 0.0 to 0.2) yielded two species (red shiner and mosquitofish, both common). The sample was not quantitative.
- Site 2 (below China Wash fish barrier, CM 2.6) yielded six species (red shiner, young-of-year common carp, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, bluegill, and largemouth bass). Table 3B lists single specimens of each species except common carp, of which there were two.
- Site 3 (below the first lateral turnout past the Highway 79 bridge, CM 14.5) yielded three species. Mosquitofish was abundant. Table 3C reports 29 fish total, including 12 red shiner and 17 mosquitofish. Four voucher specimens of the mosquitofish were identified by Dr. C. Hubbs of the University of Texas at Austin as *Gambusia affinis*.
- Site 4 (below the radial gate at the Pima Lateral Canal turnout, CM 15.2) yielded red shiner and mosquitofish, both rare. Table 3D reports a total of 2 fish.
- Sites 5 and 6 (Pima Lateral Canal and Pima Lateral Feeder Canal) had no fish. The first held a thin sheet of flow, and the second was dry.
