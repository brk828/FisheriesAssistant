# Fountain Darter (*Etheostoma fonticola*) Genetic Studies: Interim Progress Report

## Executive Summary
This interim report presents preliminary allozyme electrophoresis data investigating genetic differentiation among wild and hatchery populations of the [[Fountain Darter]] (*Etheostoma fonticola*), a small percid fish endemic to spring-fed rivers in central Texas. The study addresses concerns that anthropogenic habitat alteration—including [[Dam Construction]], aquifer over-extraction, and a historical extirpation/reintroduction event in the [[Comal River]]—may have caused genetic bottlenecks, drift, and reduced gene flow among subpopulations in the [[San Marcos River]] and Comal River systems. Using starch gel electrophoresis across ~50 protein-coding loci, the study found strong fixed genetic differences between fountain darters and congeneric [[Orangethroat Darter|orangethroat]] and [[Greenthroat Darter|greenthroat]] darters, no evidence of hybridization, measurable loss of allelic diversity in a hatchery-reared strain relative to wild source populations, and unexpected patterns of allele distribution suggesting genetic drift or previously unsampled variability rather than simple upstream-to-downstream gene flow.

## Background & Description
This report is genetic/biochemical rather than morphological in focus; no external morphometric data are provided. Data instead center on **allozyme (protein) phenotypes** used as genetic markers:
- Tissue types sampled: brain/eye, liver, and skeletal muscle.
- ~50 protein-coding loci scored via vertical starch gel electrophoresis and histochemical staining (modified [[Morizot and Schmidt 1990]] methods), including loci such as ACO, ADA, ADH, CA, CK, GALT, GPI, GOT, GR, GAPD, GLO, GUK, ITP, IDH, LDH, MDH, ME, MPI, MAN, PEPD, PEPLGG, PGM, 6PGD, PGK, PP, PK, and TPI.
- Genotypes assigned assuming **codominant allele inheritance**, based on known subunit structures of fish enzymes.
- 11 polymorphic loci were the focus of detailed frequency comparisons: ADA, CKB, GLO, GPI1, GPI2, IDH1, PGD, PGM, PGK, TPI1, TPI2.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Fountain darters inhabit spring-fed segments of the [[San Marcos River]] (including the large spring source known as "Spring River" at Aquarena Springs) and the [[Comal River]] in central Texas, both fed by the Edwards Aquifer.
- **Habitat threats**: Heavy municipal water use from the recharge aquifer has lowered flow rates to dangerously low levels during droughts, threatening habitat stability.
- **Population structure**: Population sizes are described as very large at the Spring River source but much smaller at downstream San Marcos River localities. Multiple dams potentially restrict both upstream and downstream gene flow, fragmenting the metapopulation.
- **Extirpation and reintroduction**: Native fountain darters in the Comal River system were extirpated but reestablished via introduction of >200 individuals from the San Marcos River (~30 years prior to this study).
- **Syntopic species**: [[Orangethroat Darter]] (*E. spectabile*) and [[Greenthroat Darter]] (*E. lepidum*) co-occur at some sampling localities and were examined as outgroups and for potential hybridization.
- **Conservation context**: Findings are directly relevant to the design of captive **refugium populations** intended to preserve genetic diversity of this federally-relevant, endemic Texas fish.

## Key Findings

### 1. Interspecific Genetic Divergence
- Comparison of 38 loci between fountain darters and orangethroat darters revealed **probable fixed allelic differences at 19 loci (50%)**.
- Similar strong divergence was found between fountain darters and greenthroat darters.
- **Orangethroat and greenthroat darters showed much higher genetic similarity to each other** than either does to the fountain darter.
- **No evidence of hybridization** was detected (no heterozygosity observed at loci with fixed species-level differences).

### 2. Hatchery vs. Wild Genetic Variability
- At 11 polymorphic loci, **no novel alleles** were found in the hatchery strain that were absent from wild-caught samples.
- **19 of 41 total alleles (46%)** detected across wild populations were **absent from the hatchery strain**, indicating significant loss of genetic variability during hatchery propagation.
- Compared specifically to its source population (Spring River), the hatchery strain lacked **7 alleles** present in the wild source, while gaining apparent overrepresentation of one allele (PGK) not detected in the Spring River sample.
- Notable losses included **TPI1\*4** and **GPI2\*1**, both present at >20% frequency in the Spring River wild population — a substantial concern for the genetic health of hatchery-derived refugium stocks.

### 3. Geographic Variability Among Wild Populations
- The introduced **Comal River population** exhibited **5 alleles** (GPI1\*1, \*4, \*5; PGD\*0; TPI2\*1) that were **not detected in any San Marcos River sample**, despite being the presumed source population.
  - Proposed explanations: (a) the native Comal River population was not fully extirpated and retained historic variability, or (b) these alleles exist in San Marcos River but were missed due to limited sampling (~30 individuals per site).
- Within the San Marcos River, genetic patterns suggested **restricted gene flow or drift** rather than expected unidirectional upstream-to-downstream dispersal from the large Spring River source population:
  - Of 36 alleles scored at 11 polymorphic loci, only **1 allele (CKB\*1)** was unique to the Spring River site.
  - **9 alleles** were found only in one or both downstream San Marcos River localities but **not in the Spring River sample**, contrary to the assumption that Spring River would be the primary reservoir of genetic diversity feeding downstream sites.
- Proposed explanations include historical genetic drift/bottlenecks at Spring River, or possible **selective advantage of downstream alleles** in more environmentally variable habitats.

### 4. Conservation & Sampling Recommendations
- Additional sampling (~30 more individuals) from Spring River recommended to test whether "downstream-only" alleles are simply undersampled at the source.
- Refugium founder populations should draw significantly from **downstream San Marcos localities**, not just Spring River, to capture the full known allelic diversity.
- Given the number of relatively rare alleles, effective refugium founder populations should be **large (>100 individuals, preferably 100 pairs)** to retain known genetic variation.
- At least **14 additional polymorphic loci** have been identified but remain incompletely resolved due to tissue degradation issues (particularly liver tissue) and incomplete side-by-side comparisons; fresh recollection of ~30 individuals per locality is recommended for a more complete dataset.
