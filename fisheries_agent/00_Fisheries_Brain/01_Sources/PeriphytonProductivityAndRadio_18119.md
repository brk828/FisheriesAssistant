# Periphyton Productivity and Radionuclide Accumulation in the Columbia River, Washington

#ichthyology #marine-biology #periphyton

## Executive Summary

This study by C. E. Cushing of the [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] Pacific Northwest Laboratory examined periphyton (attached algal community) productivity and the uptake of radionuclides, specifically [[Phosphorus-32]] (³²P) and [[Zinc-65]] (⁶⁵Zn), from the [[Columbia River]] below the [[Hanford Site]] reactors. Glass slides were exposed for two-week periods from August 1963 to May 1964 in a side channel riffle. The aims were to identify the biological and environmental factors governing radionuclide accumulation, to relate common production indices to environmental variables, and to infer the mode of sorption. The author concludes that radionuclide accumulation tracked biomass (dry and ash weight, chlorophyll *a*) rather than organic fraction, pointing to adsorption as the dominant uptake mechanism.

## Background & Description

- **Ecological role:** Periphyton is generally the main primary producer in small, fast-flowing streams. Large rivers such as the Columbia carry phytoplankton largely derived from periphyton and lentic habitats along their course.
- **Radionuclide concern:** The autotrophic nature and large surface-to-volume ratio of algae concentrate certain radionuclides by several orders of magnitude relative to ambient water, making them of radiobiological and ecological interest.
- **Source of radioactivity:** Columbia River water used as reactor coolant at Hanford is exposed to neutron bombardment, producing more than 60 identified radioisotopes before the water is returned to the river.
- **Study scope:** A preliminary investigation to guide later studies of mineral cycling in the Columbia River. Data cover August 1963 to May 1964.

### Methods

- **Sampling:** 21 microscope slides were exposed for two-week periods in a sampler located in a side channel below the reactors. Of these, 19 slides were allocated per sampling event (5 gravimetric, 5 chlorophyll, 3 pooled for ³²P and ⁶⁵Zn, 5 for ¹⁴C productivity, 1 visual). The ¹⁴C slides were later destroyed in a laboratory fire.
- **Effective area:** 27.5 cm² per slide after discarding the frosted end.
- **Biomass measures:** Dry weight (60 °C), ash-free dry weight (ignition at 525 °C), and chlorophyll *a* (spectrophotometric, Parsons & Strickland equations).
- **Productivity:** Net Production Rate (NPR) in mg dry weight/cm²/day, after Sladecek & Sladeckova (1964).
- **Radioactivity:** ⁶⁵Zn counted by gamma spectrometry; ³²P by differential absorber (beta) techniques, corrected for decay and self-absorption.
- **River transport:** Estimated from integrated water samples collected about 35 km downstream as part of routine monitoring, available only for 1964.
- **Environmental variables:** Solar radiation (Eppley pyrheliometer), water temperature (averaged at slide placement and removal, since continuous recorders could not be installed), and water level and discharge.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology:** Water velocity fluctuates seasonally with runoff and daily because of regulation by a power dam about 48 km upstream. Daily water-level fluctuations of about 2 m are common.
- **Seasonal radionuclide patterns:** Earlier work (Davis 1960) reported high winter and low spring radionuclide concentrations in sessile algae and plankton, attributed to dilution by runoff. In this study ⁶⁵Zn followed a similar pattern (high in fall and early winter, low in March), whereas ³²P fluctuated without a clear trend.
- **Nutrients:** Spring increases in phytoplankton and periphyton coincided with declining inorganic phosphorus. Neither phosphorus nor zinc appeared to limit autotrophic production (Cushing 1964). Silker (1964) attributed the phosphorus decline to dilution alone, whereas the author argues biological uptake also contributes.
- **Community succession:** Fall-winter communities were dominated by diatoms (mainly *Synedra* and *Melosira*, with *Cymbella*, *Gomphonema*, *Fragilaria*, and *Achnanthes*). The filamentous green alga *Ulothrix zonata* dominated from late February through April, and diatoms became dominant again in May.
- **Comparative biomass:** Reported values from other systems (Table II) range widely. The present study's 14-day glass-slide exposure gave a chlorophyll *a* value of 22.0 mg/m² and a biomass of 4.2 g dry wt/m². Comparisons are cautious because substrates and community stability differ.
- **Literature gaps:** Little prior work exists on radionuclide accumulation by periphyton. Most relevant studies involve marine algae (see [[Radionuclide Uptake in Marine Algae]]).

## Key Findings

- **Net Production Rate:** Ranged from 0.005 to 0.070 mg dry weight/cm²/day. NPR was closely related to chlorophyll *a* and solar energy. Values likely reflect maximal rather than normal rates, since colonization started from bare surfaces.
- **Solar radiation:** Showed high correlation with NPR and chlorophyll *a*. Light effects on chlorophyll were probably modulated by community age, species composition, and other factors.
- **Biomass indices:** Dry weight, ash weight, and chlorophyll *a* were the best-correlated measures. Chlorophyll *a* correlated equally well with ash and dry weight but less with ash-free dry weight. Low correlation between ash and ash-free dry weight reflected shifting species composition.
- **Radionuclide accumulation:** Significant correlations appeared only when expressed per substrate (not per gram dry weight). ³²P and ⁶⁵Zn were highly correlated with dry and ash weight. ⁶⁵Zn correlated slightly better with chlorophyll *a* than ³²P did.
- **River burden:** No significant correlation existed between periphyton radionuclide content and river activity, whether expressed as total curies transported or average pCi/L. The author suggests particulate matter in the river burden data may explain this.
- **Mode of uptake:** Higher correlation with dry and ash weight than with ash-free dry weight supports **adsorption** rather than absorption as the dominant mechanism.
- **Methodological caveat:** The ³²P extraction technique was not perfected during the first three months of the study.

**Note on the source:** This is a research article, and it contains no species-level taxonomic data. The organisms studied were algal communities (diatoms and filamentous greens), not fish.
