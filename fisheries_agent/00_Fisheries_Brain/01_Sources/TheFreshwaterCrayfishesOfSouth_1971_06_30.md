# The Freshwater Crayfishes of South America

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Parastacidae #Decapoda #Crustacea #freshwater-invertebrates

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic revision by Edgar F. Riek addresses the poorly known freshwater crayfish fauna (family [[Parastacidae]]) of South America, a group previously lumped almost entirely into the genus *Parastacus* following Faxon's 1898 revision. Riek demonstrates that South American parastacids actually comprise two morphologically and ecologically distinct lineages, warranting separation into two genera: the burrowing genus [[Parastacus]] (chelae move vertically) and a newly erected genus, [[Samastacus]] (chelae move horizontally), restricted to stream- and lake-dwelling species. The paper recognizes eight valid species (six in *Parastacus*, two in *Samastacus*) plus two additional undescribed species known from inadequate material. Distribution is restricted to two disjunct regions: southern Chile (with an incursion into the Argentinian Cordillera at Lake Nahuel-Huapi) and Uruguay/Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (~30–35°S). The work situates South American Parastacidae within a broader Southern Hemisphere biogeographic context, drawing close morphological comparisons to Australian genera such as [[Engaeus]], [[Geocharax]], [[Cherax]], and [[Parastacoides]], consistent with Gondwanan distribution patterns shared with Australia, New Zealand, Tasmania, New Guinea, and Madagascar.

## Background & Description

### Family-level (Parastacidae vs. Astacidae)
- Distinguished from Northern Hemisphere freshwater crayfish family [[Astacidae]] primarily by secondary sexual characteristics, especially in males.

### Genus *Parastacus* (burrowing group)
- Cervical groove deeply impressed, tending toward V-shaped at the meson (cf. Australian *Engaeus*).
- Postcervical groove nearly indistinguishable from cervical groove due to close approximation.
- Branchiocardiac groove merges laterally with cervical groove.
- Postorbital ridges variably developed (well-developed to subobsolete), comparable to variation in Australian genus *Cherax*.
- Chela (first pereiopod): dactylus moves in a **vertical plane**; propodus expands vertically; carpus bears tubercles on inner margin.
- Abdomen large; first segment pleura well developed.
- Merus of third maxilliped densely setose on entire lower surface.
- Male genital papilla: a calcified process (as in *Engaeus*).
- **Unusual trait**: all individuals possess both male and female genital apertures (gynandromorphism), a condition rare elsewhere in Parastacidae.

### Genus *Samastacus* (stream/lake group) — new genus
- Cervical groove deeply impressed, **U-shaped** at meson (cf. Australian *Geocharax*), nearly interrupted midlaterally with marked directional change.
- Postcervical groove separate from but close to cervical groove, meeting abruptly at midlateral cephalothorax.
- Branchiocardiac groove merges angularly with postcervical groove.
- Postorbital ridges defined, ending anteriorly in a spine or tubercle.
- Rostrum long and pointed.
- Chela: dactylus moves **obliquely/almost horizontally**; propodus expands horizontally; carpus bears an enlarged mesal tooth plus additional small spines (cf. *Parastacoides*, *Geocharax*).
- Merus of third maxilliped very densely haired over entire lower surface.
- Male genital papilla: a long, narrow, calcified ring — more produced than in the Madagascan genus [[Astacoides]].
- Sexes discrete (unlike *Parastacus*); no partial gynandromorphs known.

### Diagnostic Key Character (Genus-level)
| Feature | Parastacus | Samastacus |
|---|---|---|
| Cervical groove shape | V-shaped at meson | U-shaped at meson |
| Dactylus movement | Vertical | Oblique/horizontal |
| Male genital papilla | Mesal calcified projection from coxopodite | Separately movable, long, narrow calcified ring |
| Genital apertures | Both sexes present in all individuals | Sexes discrete |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat partitioning by genus**: *Parastacus* species are described as "strong burrowers," occupying fossorial habitats, while *Samastacus* species inhabit permanent streams and lakes (e.g., *[[Samastacus spinifrons]]* recorded "in streams and lakes").
- **Biogeography**: Crayfish distribution is patchy and apparently constrained to two disjunct regions of the continent:
  1. Southern Chile (Valparaiso ~33°S south to Taitao Peninsula ~47°S), with an incursion into the [[Lake Nahuel-Huapi]] area of the Argentinian Cordillera.
  2. Uruguay and adjoining Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (~30–35°S).
- Distribution of Parastacidae appears influenced by that of the family [[Aegleidae]] (another freshwater decapod group), which is widely distributed and common in South American streams.
- **Gondwanan affinities**: The family Parastacidae is shared across South America, New Zealand, Australia (including Tasmania), New Guinea, and Madagascar — notably absent from Africa and India — supporting a Southern Hemisphere continental-drift biogeographic pattern.
- Close morphological convergence/relationship is drawn between:
  - *Parastacus* and Australian *[[Engaeus]]* (especially *E. lyelli*), both burrowing forms.
  - *Samastacus* and Australian *[[Geocharax]]*, both stream/lake forms with horizontally-articulating chelae.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic revision**: The previously monogeneric South American parastacid fauna (all placed in *Parastacus* by Faxon 1898) is split into two genera — *Parastacus* (retained, burrowing, vertical chelae) and *Samastacus* (new genus, stream/lake-dwelling, horizontal chelae) — based on chelae articulation and reproductive morphology.
- **Species recognized**: Eight species total, plus two additional undescribed species known only from inadequate material.
  - *Parastacus*: *[[Parastacus pugnax]]*, *[[Parastacus nicoleti]]*, *[[Parastacus pilimanus]]*, *[[Parastacus saffordi]]*, *[[Parastacus varicosus]]*, *[[Parastacus defossus]]*.
  - *Samastacus*: *[[Samastacus spinifrons]]* (type species, comb. nov.), *[[Samastacus araucanius]]* (comb. nov.).
- **Synonymies resolved**: 
  - *Astacus brasiliensis* von Martens 1869 synonymized with *Parastacus pilimanus* (syn. nov.).
  - *Astacus chilensis* Milne-Edwards 1837 confirmed distinct from *Astacus chilensis* sensu Nicolet 1849 (= *P. nicoleti*).
  - *Parastacus hassleri* Faxon 1898 = *P. pugnax*.
  - *Parastacus agassizii* Faxon 1898 = *Samastacus spinifrons*.
- **Type locality correction**: *Parastacus varicosus* Faxon's stated type locality of "Colima, Mexico" is identified as an error; actual specimens derive from Uruguay and Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil.
- **Unresolved taxonomic issue**: Specimens from Lake Nahuel-Huapi (Argentina), previously identified as *S. spinifrons* by Faxon (1914), likely represent an undescribed species; Faxon's use of the name "bimaculatus" for the Chilean form added further confusion.
- **Reproductive biology anomaly**: *Parastacus* is notable among Parastacidae for having all individuals bear both male and female genital apertures (a form of structural gynandromorphism), a highly unusual condition rarely seen elsewhere in the family.
- **Distributional constraint**: Freshwater crayfish in Chile do not range north of Valparaiso (~33°S), and the southernmost Chilean records reach the Taitao Peninsula (~47°S).
