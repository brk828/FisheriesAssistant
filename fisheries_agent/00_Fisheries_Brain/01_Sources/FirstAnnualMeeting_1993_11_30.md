## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but an **organizational meeting notice and agenda** circulated by Wayne Gustaveson to Colorado River biologists, announcing the "First Annual Meeting" of what would become an independent Colorado River Biologists group (formerly the Sport Fish Committee of the [[Colorado River Water Council (CRWC)]]). The meeting, held January 12–13, 1994, was designed as a biologist-driven forum (not administrator-driven) to exchange management-relevant findings on sport and native fish populations across the Colorado River system, from the Lower Colorado River through the Upper Basin. While no raw scientific data are presented in this text, the agenda reveals the key fisheries issues, species of concern, and geographic segments under active study/management at the time.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although quantitative ecological data are absent, the agenda implies several ecological and habitat contexts under discussion:

- **Reservoir and tailwater habitats**: Distinct fish communities are recognized across a chain of connected but ecologically distinct habitats:
  - [[Lake Havasu]] and its tailwater
  - [[Lake Mohave]]
  - [[Lake Mead]]
  - [[Lake Powell]] and the [[Lee's Ferry Tailwater]]
  - [[CAP Canal]] and other inland/diverted waters
  - Upper Basin riverine systems: [[San Juan River]], [[Elephant Butte Reservoir]], and tributary waters in New Mexico, Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming
- **Sport vs. native fish dynamics**: The agenda explicitly flags "Sport/Native fish conflicts and management opportunities," indicating competitive or predatory interactions between introduced sport species (e.g., striped bass) and native Colorado River fishes.
- **Species introduction pressure**: Topics on "Utah's new species introduction efforts," "any new species being considered for introduction," and "ramifications of USFWS increased interest in stocking of all fish in Colorado River" point to an ecosystem under active manipulation via stocking programs, with implications for niche overlap and native fish recovery.
- **Population irruption**: The "[[Lake Mohave]] striped bass explosion" suggests a boom in an introduced predator/competitor population, a notable ecological event tied to the work of a "Native Fish work group."
- **Critical habitat concerns**: "Big River Fishes critical habitat status and impact" reflects ongoing concern for endangered/native large-river fish species dependent on main-channel riverine habitat, likely referencing federally listed Colorado River fishes.

## Key Findings
- The meeting represents institutional restructuring: the group is separating from the CRWC's Sport Fish Committee to operate independently, prioritizing biological/scientific discussion over politics and administration.
- Reporting structure was standardized geographically, covering nine discrete Colorado River segments from the Lower Colorado River to Upper Basin tributary states, reflecting a system-wide monitoring approach.
- Key management-relevant topics flagged by members for 1994 discussion included:
  - [[Lake Powell]] fishery response to removal of striped bass catch limits (no striper limit policy)
  - Status and impact of critical habitat designations for native "[[Big River Fishes]]"
  - Progress on native fish reintroduction programs
  - New species introduction planning (Utah and system-wide)
  - Native vs. sport fish management conflicts
  - Tailwater-specific fishery dynamics
  - The striped bass population explosion in [[Lake Mohave]] and associated Native Fish work group accomplishments
  - Anticipated impacts of expanded USFWS stocking authority/interest across the Colorado River system
- The organizational agenda for Day 2 (January 13) indicates the group intended to formalize governance: naming, scope, bylaws, officer elections, meeting cadence, and oversight — signaling the founding of a durable, recurring scientific/management body for Colorado River fisheries science, effectively an early instance of a coordinated interstate/interagency biologist network for this drainage.
