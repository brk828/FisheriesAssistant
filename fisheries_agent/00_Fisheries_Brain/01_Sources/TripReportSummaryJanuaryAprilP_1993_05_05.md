## Executive Summary

This trip report summarizes results from seven sampling expeditions conducted by the Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit (AZCFWRU) on the lower 10 km of the [[Paria River]] and its confluence with the [[Colorado River]] between January and April 1993. The primary objectives were to collect macrohabitat/microhabitat transect data and to conduct repeat seine sampling for fish. The dominant species encountered was the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], with smaller numbers of [[Speckled Dace]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Rainbow Trout]] also captured. The study documented a complete spring spawning migration of flannelmouth sucker into the Paria River, tracked via PIT tagging, seine-haul catch-per-effort, and larval fish sampling (which yielded no larvae). The report was submitted by Steve Weiss to Chuck Minckley, referencing coordination with Mike Douglas (ASU) and Rich Valdez (Bio/West) on the PIT tag dataset.

## Background & Description

- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** — primary study species; total sample n=663
  - Mean total length (all individuals): 494 mm
  - Mean length, males: 469 mm (n=329)
  - Mean length, females: 524 mm (n=285) — females notably larger than males
  - Size range: 380 mm (smallest) to 615 mm (largest)
  - Sexual dimorphism noted: males developed **raised nuptial keels** during the spawning period; several individuals with "very distinctive raised keels" were captured, one photographed
  - Reproductive condition tracked via expression of milt (males) and eggs (females) — a standard field method for assessing spawning readiness in [[Catostomidae]]
- **[[Bluehead Sucker]]** — minor bycatch species (4 individuals captured/tagged)
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** — minor bycatch species, no morphometric detail provided
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** — minor bycatch species (5 individuals), non-native salmonid in this system

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Lower 10 km of the [[Paria River]], a tributary entering the [[Colorado River]] (relevant to [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem studies); sampling also occurred at the river confluence itself, where catch rates were influenced by proximity to and flow levels of the mainstem Colorado River.
- **Spawning migration & phenology**:
  - Flannelmouth sucker first observed entering the Paria in **February**, at stream temperatures of **4–6°C**
  - Males were ripe (expressing milt) continuously from **mid-February through April 10**
  - Females first observed expressing eggs in **mid-March**; ripe females captured through **April 10**, at stream temperatures of **8–20°C**
  - Spawning activity documented up to **10 km upstream** from the Colorado River confluence
  - By the final trips (April 16–27), stream temperatures reached **9–22°C** and spawning activity had concluded
- **Larval fish sampling**: Conducted April 16–18 and 24–27 using plankton drift nets and dip nets; **no larval fish were captured** in either effort, despite confirmed spawning activity — suggesting either larval drift outside sampled windows/locations, low detection efficiency, or rapid emigration to the Colorado River.
- **Confluence dynamics**: Fish (particularly flannelmouth) remained catchable at the Paria/Colorado confluence after spawning ended in the Paria proper, especially **at night** and during **higher Colorado River flow levels**, indicating a behavioral/habitat shift toward the confluence zone post-spawn.
- **Connectivity with Little Colorado River (LCR)**: Of PIT-tagged fish encountered that had been tagged elsewhere, most originated in or near the [[Little Colorado River]], indicating meaningful inter-tributary movement of flannelmouth sucker within the Colorado River mainstem system — relevant to [[Colorado River Fish Movement]] and metapopulation connectivity in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach.

## Key Findings

- **Total flannelmouth sucker sampled**: 663 individuals measured across the season; catch summary tables document effort at both the confluence and upstream Paria sites.
- **Confluence catch summary** (Feb–Apr 1993): 480 flannelmouth sucker (FMS) captured over 39 seine hauls, total sampled area 20,320 m², overall catch rate of **2.3 fish/100 m²**; catch rate peaked at **4.6/100 m²** (Apr 8–10) and was lowest at **0.5/100 m²** (Feb 12–14).
- **Upstream Paria catch summary**: 282 FMS captured over 79 seine hauls, total sampled area 24,636 m², overall catch rate of **1.1 fish/100 m²**; catch rate peaked at **4.2/100 m²** (Apr 8–10) and was lowest at **0.06/100 m²** (Feb 12–14).
- **Habitat transect effort**: 228 habitat transects and 3,110 habitat points collected across six trips (Feb–Apr), capturing depth, flow, and substrate data to characterize seasonal habitat use.
- **PIT tagging program**:
  - 692 flannelmouth sucker and 4 bluehead sucker tagged (cumulative, since fall 1992)
  - Recapture rate of **5%** (n=35) among tagged fish
  - 19 fish previously PIT-tagged at other locations (mostly near the [[Little Colorado River]]) were recaptured in the Paria/confluence area — direct evidence of interbasin/intertributary fish movement
  - 7 fish bearing readable **AZGFD or USFWS Floy tags** were also recovered
  - Data disks forwarded to Mike Douglas (Arizona State University) and Rich Valdez (Bio/West) for broader Colorado River Basin database integration
- **No larval flannelmouth sucker detected** despite active spawning through mid-April — a notable null result warranting further larval-sampling methodology consideration in future [[Catostomidae]] recruitment studies.
- Distinctive **nuptial keel morphology** observed in some male flannelmouth sucker, documented photographically, suggesting a possible subject for follow-up morphological or genetic investigation.
