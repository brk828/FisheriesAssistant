# Federal Response to FOIA Request and 12-Month Finding Correspondence on Roundtail and Headwater Chubs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) letter, dated July 20, 2006, responding to a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request from Sally E. and Jerome A. Stefferud. The letter concerns the Service's [[12-Month Finding]] on a petition to list a distinct population segment (DPS) of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and to list the [[Headwater Chub]] (*Gila nigra*) as endangered or threatened, with critical habitat (71 FR 26007). The Service enclosed a CD holding the administrative record for the finding, withholding only attorney-client privileged communications. The enclosed record includes an index of roughly 200 internal emails (January to May 2006) that document how the finding was drafted, reviewed, and prepared for publication. The index is the bulk of the document and is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Background & Description

- **Letter:** From Steven L. Spangle, Field Supervisor, [[Arizona Ecological Services Field Office]], USFWS, Phoenix. Reference: FOIA 2006-0606 (OCR-corrected from "200G0606").
- **Addressees:** Sally E. and Jerome A. Stefferud of Phoenix, who had written on July 7, 2006.
- **Copies:** Thomas E. Dowling and Paul C. Marsh, School of Life Sciences, [[Arizona State University]]; the Regional Director in Albuquerque, NM; and the USFWS FOIA coordinator.
- **Species concerned:**
  - *Gila robusta* (Roundtail Chub), for which a DPS listing was petitioned.
  - *Gila nigra* (Headwater Chub), for which listing with critical habitat was petitioned.
- **Legal context:** On May 17, 2006, the [[Center for Biological Diversity]] notified the Service of its intent to sue over the 12-month finding.
- **Contacts named in the letter:** Glen Knowles (ext. 233) and Debra Bills (ext. 239).

The email index covers the period from early January to May 8, 2006. Subject lines show the work moved through several stages:

- Briefing papers and "chub briefing" drafts for senior staff.
- Drafts of the 12-month finding, with comments from Regional and Washington Office reviewers.
- A "90-day" finding and related outreach packages.
- Literature-citation checks ("lit cites").
- A [[Range-Wide Conservation Strategy]] for three species, and a final draft of the [[State Conservation Agreement]] and Strategy.
- Federal Register publication planning, with the 12-month finding for the headwater chub and roundtail chub DPS scheduled for publication on May 3, 2006.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document gives little direct ecological data. It identifies the two taxa as freshwater cyprinid fishes of the southwestern United States, linked to the Lower Colorado River Basin. The email subjects name several specific waters and programs:

- **East Clear Creek**, a stream in Arizona that appears in several threads.
- **Glen Canyon AMWG** (Adaptive Management Work Group) meetings, which were tied to the chub correspondence.
- **Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) Region 2005 trip reports** on spinedace, roundtail chub, and Gila chub.
- A **genetic data statement** on chubs, which was reviewed separately.

Habitat characteristics, population counts, and threat analyses are not reported in the letter. Those details appear only in the enclosed administrative record and the 12-month finding itself, which are not part of this document.

## Key Findings

1. **Status of the petition.** The Service issued a 12-month finding on petitioned listings for the roundtail chub DPS and the headwater chub. The letter responds to a request for records of that process.

2. **Record disclosure.** The administrative record was provided on CD, with attorney-client privileged material withheld. The record contains the documents the Service "utilized and relied upon" in its decision-making.

3. **Litigation risk.** The Center for Biological Diversity's notice of intent to sue (May 17, 2006) was known to the Service while the finding was in preparation.

4. **Drafting timeline.** Email subjects show that drafts of the finding were circulated repeatedly from roughly March to May 2006. Key steps included:
   - a "Draft 12-Month Finding" circulated in early and mid-March;
   - "threats and finding sections" revised in early April;
   - the "modified outreach package" for the 2-chub 12-month finding in April;
   - "Chubs to Federal Register" messages in early May, when publication was set for May 3, 2006.

5. **Coordinated outreach.** Several threads concern outreach materials, news releases, and a request to post finding references on the Service website. These show the finding was planned as a public communication as well as a regulatory action.

6. **Key participants.** The most frequent names in the index are Susan Jacobsen, Andrea Cherepy, Steve Spangle, Jeff Whitney, Jeff Humphrey, and Elizabeth Slown. Other names include Debra Bills, Glen Knowles, Chris Cantrell, and Mike Lopez.

7. **Extension.** One subject line refers to a chub-related extension that was "granted," but the date is partly illegible in the source and is not reported here.
