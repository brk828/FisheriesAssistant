# Status and Future of the American Fisheries Society

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is the opening presidential address to the Eighty-third Annual Meeting of the [[American Fisheries Society]], reprinted from Volume 83 (1953) of the *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* and published in 1954. The address appraises the Society's organizational health and proposes reforms to improve its governance, its influence on fishery policy, and the scientific basis of its programs. It contains no species, habitat or quantitative research data; its concerns are institutional.

## Background & Description

The President frames the address around the "status" of the Society, noting that recent criticism of the organization, much of it constructive, reflects an engaged membership. Membership is described as being at a record level, with finances sound and interest in Society activities heightened. The address identifies the membership as the Society's principal resource, including a high share of professionals in the fishery field: [[Fishery Administrators]], [[Fishery Research Biologists]], [[Fish Culturists]] and technologists, along with supporting sport and commercial interests.

Governance questions raised for consideration include:
- Whether officers should be elected by general ballot of all members.
- Whether procedures for amending the Constitution should be simplified (one of two existing procedures is described as impractical, the other cumbersome).
- Whether certain [[Standing Committees]] should be discontinued or new ones created.
- Whether the [[Executive Committee]], then 17 members and growing with new Regional Divisions and Standing Committees, should be streamlined.
- Whether traditions should be given the legal status of the Constitution and By-Laws.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document contains no ecological or environmental data. Its only environmental framing is the general aim of "sound fishery programs" and the Society's stated affiliation with the [[Natural Resources Committee]], which the President cites as a first step toward policy influence.

## Key Findings

- **Organizational health:** Membership is at a record level, finances are sound, and member engagement is increasing.
- **Governance reform:** Proposed changes include general-ballot officer elections, simpler amendment procedures, review of Standing Committees, a leaner Executive Committee, and clarification of the status of traditions.
- **Policy influence is weak:** Resolutions approved at meetings are often not disseminated until months later in the *Transactions*, which has limited distribution. The President describes the Society's record in influencing policy as "not good."
- **Proposed mechanisms:** Redefine the [[Publicity Committee]] to disseminate records of action to officials, agencies, news outlets and publishers; consider a small committee empowered to speak for the Society between meetings.
- **Need for the [[North American Fish Policy]] revision:** The policy, "long since overdue for revision," had nonetheless received wide distribution and use.
- **Research basis for management:** The President criticizes a "mania for action" and a misconception of basic research as impractical, arguing that management programs lacking a knowledge foundation have produced disappointing results. He calls for research to be made the basis of management practice and for evaluating its results.
- **Standards and programs:** Calls for sound, carefully planned fishery programs executed by trained, adequately compensated personnel; avoidance of fanaticism, fads and biased views; and stronger meetings and publications.
- **Meeting program:** The national meeting program has been slow to adapt to [[Regionalization]]; this year's program is described as an attempt at that adjustment, and members are invited to comment.
