# Degeneration of Melanin Pigment in Cavernicolous Populations of Astyanax mexicanus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This German-language study by [[Horst Wilkens]] (1970), published in the *Zeitschrift für zoologische Systematik und Evolutionsforschung*, examines how melanophore number and melanin content are reduced in two cave populations of [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] (Characidae) relative to their surface-dwelling ancestor, the [[River Fish]]. The two cave forms are the [[Pachon Cavefish]] and the [[Sabinos Cavefish]]. By crossing these forms and counting melanophores in F1, F2 and backcross generations, the author concludes that pigment reduction depends on three gene systems and that the cave populations diverged from each other by different numbers of mutations. The work also shows that cavefish retain the ability to change colour physiologically, but many hybrids cannot change colour morphologically.

## Background & Description

- **Study organism:** [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] (Filippi). The surface river form is known as the River Fish (Ast). Two cave derivatives were originally described as separate species, *Anoptichthys hubbsi* and *A. antrobius*. Crossing tests showed they are ecological races of one species.
- **Cave populations:**
  - Pachon Cave, about 90 km from the Rio Tampaon, with the Pachon fish.
  - Sabinos Cave, about 24 km from the river, with the Sabinos fish.
  - Chica Cave, near the river, with the Chica fish (*A. jordani*). This population showed secondary hybridisation with the river fish and was excluded from the analysis.
- **Pigment cell types:** Melanophores (main pigment elements), guanophores, iridophores and xanthophores.
- **Methods:** Melanophores were counted under a microscope at 35x magnification with an ocular grid micrometer. Melanin was contracted with noradrenaline to make cells countable. Counts were made on the dorsal side near the dorsal fin, across several scales. Fish were 2.5 cm standard length.
- **Genetic framework:** Eye reduction and pigment reduction are considered convergent regressive traits. The author builds on earlier work by [[Breder And Rasquin]] (1943, 1947), [[Sadoglu]] (1958) and [[Sapocu]] (1954, 1955, 1958).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Background influence on pigment:** Melanophore number changes with background albedo. [[Sumner]] (1940) showed in *Lebistes reticulatus* that fish on black backgrounds had about twice as many melanophores as fish on white ones. Eyes are required for this response. Light from above has a much smaller effect. Temperature and water chemistry may also contribute.
- **Non-genetic variation:** Melanophore density varied within a single tank, with the first-killed fish having more melanophores than later ones. Dark-reared fish, blinded fish and separately reared siblings also differed, so the author treats these as modifying influences.
- **Cave conditions:** The loss of selection in darkness is compared with domestication. Wilkens notes that cave variants resemble domesticated animals, as observed earlier by [[Kosswig]] (1940).

## Key Findings

1. **Surface river fish:** Mean melanophore density was 5.7 cells per unit (s = 0.38). Fish on white substrate averaged about 3.9 and laboratory-reared fish in an artificial cave about 3.3.
2. **Sabinos fish:** Mean density was 1.75 cells per unit (s = 0.31), about 30% of the river fish value. Melanin content per cell was low. The basic scale-determined pattern was retained.
3. **Pachon fish:** Mean density was 0.9 cells per unit (s = 0.28), about 15% of the river fish value. Some true albinos occur, caused by a single recessive gene.
4. **F1 hybrids:** River × cave F1 hybrids resembled the river fish in both number and melanin content, rather than being intermediate as [[Sapocu]] (1958) had proposed. The F1 Sabinos × Pachon cross was phenotypically like its parents (mean 1.10 cells per unit).
5. **F2 hybrids:** These showed a normal distribution of melanophore density across the full range between the parents. Fish on black backgrounds showed the full phenotypic variety. Fish on white backgrounds looked uniformly pale.
6. **Backcrosses:** Backcrosses to the cave forms showed strong positive skew, a larger range (similar to F2) and some individuals with river-fish melanophore numbers.
7. **Physiological colour change:** This ability was retained in all forms and hybrids. Blind hybrids remain expanded and look black.
8. **Morphological colour change:** This was not retained in all hybrids. Wilkens proposes that it depends on several non-allelic modifier genes, and that the cave populations have lost some of them through degenerative mutation.
9. **Gene systems:** Pigment depends on three systems: (a) genes controlling melanophore number, (b) genes controlling melanin content, and (c) modifier genes for morphological colour change. Melanophore-number genes act additively and polymerically. The Pachon fish differs from the river fish by a dihybrid inheritance and the Sabinos fish by a monohybrid one.
10. **Melanin content:** One recessive gene reduces melanin content in both cave populations, based on tri- and dihybrid ratios in the crosses.
11. **Population comparison:** The Pachon population carries two or three degenerative mutations in the pigment system relative to the river fish, versus one in the Sabinos population. The author questions the proposed phylogenetic gradient, arguing that the time between the two cave colonisations may have been too short for a systematic divergence in gene content.
12. **Eye-gene comparison:** Eye-related degenerative gene numbers are about equal in the two populations (Wilkens 1970). The author suggests the difference in pigmentation rests on the Pachon fish having one extra mutation step, and that the next research question is how similar the two genomes are.
