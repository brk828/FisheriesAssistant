# Plan Piscícola para el Estado de México (1963)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 bulletin article, by Biol. Rodolfo Ramírez Granados and published by the Dirección General de Pesca in the *Boletín de Piscicultura Rural* (Vol. XII), proposes a rural fish culture plan for the [[Estado De México]]. The plan is meant to be extended to other Mexican states. It argues that fish culture can raise rural incomes, improve campesino diets, expand natural resources, provide recreation and prevent water pollution. The text combines a regional synthesis of climate, geology, hydrography and water chemistry with thermal zoning and species recommendations. It closes with a concrete action plan for surveys, species introductions and community outreach.

## Background & Description

The author frames the problem socially. Campesinos make up about 60% of the population but receive about 20% of national income. The state population grew from 886,320 in 1923 to 1,389,907 in 1950, a 56% increase in 27 years. The state lies between 18°27' and 20°18' N and 90°37' and 100°27' W. Its terrain is mostly volcanic, with mountain ranges around former lake basins such as [[Lake Texcoco]], Chalco, Zumpango and Lerma. Elevations include 2,260 m in the lake depression and 2,680 m in the Toluca Valley.

The state had a long fishing tradition and a historical fish-culture base. The trout nursery at Chimaliapa was founded in the late 19th century. Other stations included Almoloya del Río and the [[El Zarco Fish Station]].

The article covers:
- **Climate:** Climate is classified by Contreras Arias (1942) using a thermal efficiency index (I′), with classes of Frío (32–64), Semifrío (64–80), Templado (80–101), Semicálido (101–128) and Cálido (128+). Most stations fall in the semifrío and templado classes. Frost is most frequent from November to March.
- **Hydrography:** The state spans parts of the Pánuco basin, the Mexico basin (originally endorheic) and the [[Balsas Basin]], which covers the south. The Lerma River rises in the centre. The Dirección General de Pesca estimated about 4,111 water bodies using Army photogrammetric maps.
- **Water chemistry:** Tables list temperature, pH, hardness, alkalinity, turbidity, dissolved oxygen and colour for dozens of localities. Recorded pH ranges roughly from 6.0 to 9.8. Many state waters are turbid, which limits fish production to a few tolerant species.
- **Land use and pollution:** The text describes deforestation, overgrazing, erosion and sedimentation. It also describes silver, gold and copper mining in El Oro, Sultepec, Zacualpan and Temascaltepec, sewage from the [[Gran Canal Del Desagüe]], and hydroelectric and industrial development.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author ties fish productivity to water chemistry and temperature. Alkaline, hard waters (calcareous regions and lake depressions) are more productive than acidic, soft volcanic waters. Dissolved oxygen, turbidity and colour control light penetration and primary production. Still waters warm more, hold less oxygen and are more productive than running waters.

The state is divided into four fish-culture zones that broadly match climatic zones:

1. **Warm waters (cálidas):** [[Cichlid Mojarras]] (Cichlasoma), the bagre del Balsas (Istlarius balsanus) and [[Highland Mullet]] (Agonostomus monticola).
2. **Semi-warm waters (semicálidas):** Pescado blanco de Chapala (Chirostoma sphyraena), carp, [[Largemouth Bass]], catfish from Chapala and the Lerma, and mojarras.
3. **Temperate waters (templadas):** Pescado blanco de Pátzcuaro and Mexico basin pescado blanco, largemouth bass, carp and bagres.
4. **Cool to cold waters (semifrías y frías):** [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], carp and juiles.

The author notes that the boundaries are transitional, and that local conditions such as thermal springs can override the regional pattern. Forage species such as Cyprinidae, Goodeidae, Atherinidae, Poeciliidae and Characidae, along with crustaceans (acociles) and aquatic insects, support predatory fish.

The author proposes that water pollution from mining and sewage can be treated. He cites an oxidation-pond plan that would treat Gran Canal wastewater and refill Laguna de Zumpango for fish culture. Dams are noted to block migratory fish, but the author argues this is not a major issue in the state because most economically important species are sedentary.

## Key Findings

- **Rural fish culture is presented as a development tool.** It is linked to rural economic uplift, improved diet, resource conservation, recreation and the restoration of the water cycle.
- **Zoning by thermal class** guides species selection. Each zone has recommended species (see Ecological & Environmental Context).
- **Reproductive and growth data** are summarized for each recommended species:
  - [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*): natural range from the Bering Sea to Durango. Optimal pH is 6.7–7.0 and dissolved oxygen tolerance is about 5 ppm. Females produce 1,500–2,000 eggs per kg. At El Zarco, a 2,500 g female produced up to 5,000 eggs. Incubation takes 30–35 days at 8–9 °C (270–280 degree-days).
  - [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*): Spawns from September to November in Mexico (October to mid-December for the local variety). Incubation at El Zarco takes 56 days at 8 °C and 49 days at 9 °C (448 and 441 degree-days). Average size is about 25 cm.
  - [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): Omnivorous and fast-growing, with females of 7–10 kg producing up to 2,000,000 eggs. Optimal growth is at 20–25 °C. Maximum size is 80 cm and 10–15 kg.
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*): Spawns when water reaches about 15.5–22 °C. Females produce 4,000–14,000 eggs per kg. It is strongly carnivorous and cannibalistic. Maximum sizes are about 12 kg in Florida and 6–7 kg normally.
  - [[Pescado Blanco]] and [[Charales]] (*Chirostoma* spp.): Carnivorous, spawning from January to August. A 365 mm female yielded about 20,000 mature eggs. Average adult size is 20–25 cm. High protein and mineral content.
  - [[Bagres]] (*Ictalurus*, *Ameiurus*): Omnivorous, spawning in spring and nesting in sand and mud. Some species lay up to 10,000 eggs.
- **Water quality data** (Lake Pátzcuaro): Temperature 14–23 °C, dissolved oxygen about 5 cc/L and pH 8.7 or higher.
- **Recommended action plan:**
  - Survey state water bodies and test physico-chemical and biological conditions.
  - Stock species by zone, with priorities for trout, carp, bass, pescado blanco, catfish and forage species.
  - Supply equipment, boats, nets, traps, manuals and educational materials.
  - Run an education campaign through rural teachers, ejido commissioners and municipal presidents.
  - Promote soil, forest, pasture and water conservation, including terracing, contour planting and controlled grazing based on carrying capacity.
- **Social conditions are treated as essential.** The author argues that technical success depends on land reform, social justice and popular participation. He describes water as the primary technical concern and fish culture as one effective tool for water retention, drought mitigation and nutrition.
