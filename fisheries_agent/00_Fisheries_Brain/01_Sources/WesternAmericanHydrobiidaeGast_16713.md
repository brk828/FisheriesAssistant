# Western American Hydrobiidae: Anatomical Redescription of Tryonia and Related Genera

#hydrobiidae #malacology #gastropoda

## Executive Summary

This systematic paper by [[Jeff Hershler]] and [[Fred G. Thompson]] (Smithsonian Institution and Florida State Museum) examines the anatomy of small freshwater snails of the family [[Hydrobiidae]] from western North America. The authors focus on two predominant southwestern genera, [[Tryonia]] and [[Fontelicella]], and on the Florida genus [[Hyalopyrgus]]. Their central conclusions are that *Hyalopyrgus* from Florida should be assigned to *Tryonia*, that a northern Mexican taxon should also be placed in *Tryonia*, and that *Tryonia* should be redescribed on the basis of shell and soft-anatomy characters. The paper also addresses the subfamily placements of these genera.

## Background & Description

North American Hydrobiidae are a major element of the continent's freshwater fauna, comprising about 28 genera and 148 species (Burch citation in the text). Their systematics remain poorly understood because the anatomy of few species is known. Earlier anatomical work covered southeastern taxa (Thompson, 1968, 1969, 1984; Thompson and McCaleb, 1975), but the arid Southwest was largely unstudied. There, *Tryonia* and *Fontelicella* dominate, with each genus containing more than a dozen species, most of them undescribed.

Previous placements were inconsistent. [[Taylor]] (1966) placed *Tryonia* in the [[Littoridininae]] on the basis of its turreted shell and glandular penial lobes, but later penial illustrations show it belongs to the [[Nymphophilinae]] (see Thompson, 1979). The authors note that the scant published morphological data prevent meaningful comparison with other members of either subfamily.

**Materials and methods**
- Specimens were preserved in dilute Bouin's solution and examined by dissection under a dissecting microscope.
- Ciliation on the cephalic tentacles and penis was studied by [[Scanning Electron Microscopy]] (Hitachi S-570), using specimens dehydrated and dried by critical point drying.
- Shells and radulae were cleaned with Clorox and photographed by SEM.
- Examined lots include material from Moapa Springs (Clark County, Nevada), Lake Dora and Alexander Springs (Florida), Campo Creek (San Diego County, California), and Oasis (Nye County, Nevada). Paratypes of *Fontelicella* were included.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The genera occupy contrasting regions:
- ***Tryonia***: found in much of the Southwest, including parts of California, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico and Texas, and in parts of northern Mexico.
- ***Fontelicella***: distributed across northeastern and southeastern North America, much of the Southwest, and parts of northern Mexico.
- ***Hyalopyrgus***: previously considered restricted to eastern North America, with Florida records.

The text notes that some species of *Fontelicella* may have been affected by habitat loss. The authors report that a 1981 survey at Fish Spring-related localities failed to find living specimens of *Pyrgulopsis* (the type locality of a synonymized genus), and that the species is apparently extinct. The text does not provide further habitat or water-chemistry data.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- The anatomy of *Hyalopyrgus* from Florida supports its allocation to *Tryonia*.
- A northern Mexican species previously assigned elsewhere is also placed in *Tryonia* on anatomical grounds.
- The authors redescribe *Tryonia* and discuss its affinities with the Nymphophilinae and Littoridininae.

**Diagnostic characters of Tryonia** (from the paper's diagnosis)
- Shell colorless, transparent, elongate-conic to turreted, with about 4.0–8.0 whorls, typically high-spired with rounded whorls and indented sutures.
- Aperture simple and unthickened; umbilicus narrow or absent.
- Strong sexual dimorphism, with males often about half the shell height of females.
- Penis flattened and slender, with a single enlarged glandular lobe at its base and one to four smaller glandular lobes on the inner curvature. The distal portion is ciliated to varying degrees.
- Females brood embryos in an enlarged capsule gland. The bursa copulatrix is small and stunted, a feature the authors attribute to the extremely small size of the snail.

**Diagnostic characters of Fontelicella** (from the paper's diagnosis)
- Shell 1.2–5.0 mm high, globose to elongate-conic, with about 2.0–6.0 whorls.
- Penis with a narrow, elongate filament and glandular ridges, sometimes on stalked tubercles. Some species have simple penes with few ridges, while others have more complex penes with accessory crests.
- Capsule gland with two tissue sections and a near-terminal opening. Distinctive pigment markings are often present on the mantle or penial filament.

**Comparative notes**
- The eastern *Marstonia*-group species are united by a penis with few glandular ridges and a broad penial lobe. The authors state that they intergrade with western species.
- Thompson (1977) suggested that *Marstonia* may be a synonym of *Pyrgulopsis*. The authors could not test this because the type species of *Pyrgulopsis* is apparently extinct.
