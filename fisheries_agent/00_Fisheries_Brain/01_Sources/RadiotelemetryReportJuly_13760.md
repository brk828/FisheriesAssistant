# Gila River Razorback Sucker Radiotelemetry Study (1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This 14 July 1988 progress report from [[Arizona State University]] documents a radiotelemetry study of [[Razorback Sucker]] released into the [[Gila River]] in Arizona. On 24 May 1988, 14 adult fish were implanted with radio transmitters and released at [[Old Safford Bridge]]. Four were wild adults from [[Lake Mohave]] and ten were hatchery-propagated fish from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], New Mexico. Tracking over roughly one month showed that transmitter loss, mortality and movement were the main outcomes. Three Lake Mohave fish died within nine days of release, and at least one tagged fish was lost from contact. Most fish that could be tracked moved downstream.

## Background & Description

The study used surgically implanted [[Radiotelemetry]] transmitters to follow individual fish in the river. Implantation was performed by H.M. Tyus of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Colorado River Fishery Project]], Vernal, Utah. Participants listed were W.L. Minckley, C.M. Williams, H.M. Tyus, K.L. Young, F.C. Marsh and R. Timmons.

Fish sources:
- **Wild stock:** Four adults collected 20–23 May 1984 at Carp and Cottonwood coves and Eldorado Canyon, [[Lake Mohave]]. These are listed as "Mohave" in the data table.
- **Hatchery stock:** Ten fish from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], New Mexico. Their age is not legible in the scan.

Fish were transported overnight to the release site. The scanned transport dates are garbled and conflict with the 24 May release date, so they are not reported here.

Transmitter specifications and fish sex were recorded for each tag. Transmitter frequencies and sizes are listed in the source table but are not reproduced in this note.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish were released into a reach of the Gila River that includes several landmarks and tributary confluences. Recorded locations include:
- [[Subia's Fences]] (upstream and downstream fence lines)
- [[San Francisco River]] confluence
- [[Black Canyon]]
- Mouth of Dry Canyon
- York Valley, near the Greenlee Country Club, where one fish was found in a school with carp and channel catfish

Several recovery sites were on sand-gravel bars, in pools below cottonwoods and on a *Cladophora* algal mat. These details describe where transmitters and carcasses were found. Water chemistry and habitat measurements are not reported in the document.

## Key Findings

**Initial release and tagging**
- 14 fish were implanted and released on 24 May 1988 at Old Safford Bridge.
- Tag 701 was found dead within 24 hours. Its transmitter was moved into a hatchery fish (Dexter tag 718) on 25 May.

**Fate and mortality**
- Three Lake Mohave fish were dead within 9 days of implantation. All had moved downstream (1.6 to 7.2 km) before they died.
- Tag 704 (a Lake Mohave male) was last located at Old Safford Bridge on 24 May and was presumed lost.
- Several transmitters were recovered without carcasses, and some were presumed to have come loose from fish. Fish 714 and 716 were last located alive on 23–24 June, and fish 709 was reported active and apparently healthy on 24 June.
- The fate summary category counts are not legible in the scan. The movement summary annotation refers to 9 mortalities.

**Movement**
- The reported average downstream movement was modest, and the longest recorded movement was about 32 km (range figures are partly illegible in the scan).
- Movement summary ranges printed in the scan include days at large of 1–32 days and distances of 1.6–32 km. These are not fully legible.
- Fish that moved upstream are reported separately in the summary table, but those values are not fully legible.

**Data quality note**
- The source is a scanned report. Several cells, counts and dates are garbled or inconsistent, including transport dates, fate summary totals and the age of hatchery fish. These are noted above rather than estimated.
