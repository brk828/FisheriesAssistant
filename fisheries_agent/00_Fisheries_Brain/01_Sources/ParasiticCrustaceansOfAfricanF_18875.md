# Parasitic Crustaceans of African Freshwater Fishes from the Nile and Niger Systems

#ichthyology #marine-biology #copepoda #branchiura #lernaeidae #argulidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 taxonomic paper by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] of the [[Freshwater Biological Association]] describes new crustacean parasites of African freshwater fishes from the [[Nile River]] and former Nile drainage, the [[Niger River]], and [[Lake Albert]]. It describes four new taxa, clarifies a group of closely related [[Argulus]] species, corrects older published records, and presents new distribution data. The work is primarily a systematic revision supported by morphological figures and host and locality records.

## Background & Description

The paper covers parasites of African freshwater fishes, most collected in waters connected or formerly connected with the Nile, with some from the Niger. Specimens were supplied by several collectors, including [[Jan Rzoska]], [[Robert Dollfus]], [[John Green]], and [[John Talling]] with [[Eric Hamblyn]]. Material is housed in the [[British Museum (Natural History)]], the [[Humboldt Museum Berlin]], and Paris collections.

Four new taxa are described:
- [[Lamproglena cornuta]] (copepod, Nile): distinguished by a dorsal thoracic "horn" and a maxilliped ending in a single sclerotised spine plus a lobe, unlike the three or five hook-like spines of other species. Length up to about 11 mm. Host: [[Heterobranchus bidorsalis]], Lake No.
- [[Opistholernaea laterobrachialis nilotica]] (subspecies, Nile): attachment to gill arches rather than the palate, with the lateral outgrowth immediately behind the head. Host: [[Tilapia nilotica]], lower Nile.
- [[Opistholernaea contorta]] (copepod, Niger): length up to about 13 mm, with a strongly contorted posterior trunk and a four-armed anchor. Hosts: [[Distichodus rostratus]] and [[Distichodus brevipinnis]], from Konna and Mopti.
- [[Argulus cunningtoni]] (branchiuran, [[Lake Albert]]): named after [[W. A. Cunnington]]. Adult female to about 9.3 mm, adult male to about 8.1 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Parasite–host relationships are central. Several lernaeid copepods show site-specific attachment (gill arches, palate, buccal cavity, mouth), and the paper treats consistent site selection as taxonomically meaningful. Argulids share the habit of storing eggs in carapace lobes.

Distribution patterns are emphasised. [[Argulus rhipidiophorus]] appears confined to the Nile or former Nile drainage, while [[Argulus africanus]] occurs only in waters above, but not including, Lake Albert within that system. Records from the [[Ethiopian Rift Valley Lakes]] (Zwai, Oitu/Langano, Awassa, Abaya/Margherita) and the [[Awash River]] system (Koka Dam) are included, with Lake Tana also represented. The paper notes that the Kivu region formerly drained into the Nile, and that Lake Kivu now belongs to the Congo system.

Hosts recorded include [[Clarias lazera]], [[Lates niloticus]], [[Bagrus bayad]], [[Synodontis schall]], [[Auchenoglanis occidentalis]], [[Hydrocyon lineatus]], [[Distichodus niloticus]], and various [[Tilapia]] and [[Barbus]] species.

## Key Findings

- **Four new taxa** were described: *Lamproglena cornuta*, *Opistholernaea laterobrachialis nilotica*, *Opistholernaea contorta*, and *Argulus cunningtoni*.
- ***Lamproglena clariae* variation:** a specimen from the White Nile near Khartoum, from *Clarias lazera*, showed serrated leg-1 edges and furcal armature differences. Re-examination of Lake Nyasa material showed these features vary within that population too, so they were judged unreliable for sub-specific separation.
- ***Opistholernaea laterobrachialis nilotica*** was recognised as a geographical sub-species of the Congo-system form, based on consistent differences in form, attachment site and geographical separation. The author acknowledges the decision is "somewhat subjective" because biological data were lacking.
- ***Opistholernaea contorta*** showed variable thoracic torsion. Sinistral torsion of about 100° between legs 1 and 2 was usual, while one specimen showed no torsion.
- **Additional Niger records:** *Opistholernaea longa* from *Lates niloticus* at Kolenze, which the author describes as the first record from the Niger system (previously known from the Nile and Congo). *Lernaea hardingi* from *Tilapia zillii* at Mopti showed variation in the pregenital prominence that cuts across known lake forms.
- **Argulus taxonomy corrected:** the author shows that [[Argulus africanus]] records from Lake No and Lake Albert (Cunnington, 1913) and from Thiele's Nile material refer to *A. rhipidiophorus*. Thiele's Lake Edward record also refers to *A. rhipidiophorus*. The earlier suggestion that *A. rhipidiophorus* was a sub-species of *A. africanus* is rejected; they are distinct species.
- **Diagnostic male characters:** the modified legs 2–4 of males were identified as the most reliable key, with the leg-2 coxal projection and plate-like lamella separating the species.
- **Argulus dageti** (Niger, Dollfus 1960) is only doubtfully distinct from *A. africanus* until adult males are available. *A. reticulatus* (Congo) is considered distinct, most similar to *A. cunningtoni* in male accessory organs.
- **Distribution:** *A. rhipidiophorus* was recorded from Lake Kivu, Lake Edward, the Kazinga Channel, Lake Albert, White Nile at Khartoum, and four Ethiopian rift lakes. *Dolops ranarum* was recorded from Lake Tana and the Koka Dam. *Lernaea barnimiana* was recorded from Lakes Awassa and Ararobi and the Koka Dam. A specimen from Lake Tana (*Varicorhinus* sp.) was close to *L. cyprinacea* but not clearly assignable.
- ***Lamproglena elongata*** was recorded from Lake No (Upper White Nile) on *Hydrocyon lineatus*, extending its known range beyond the Niger.
