# Station Profile: Walhalla National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #fish-hatchery #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

The [[Walhalla National Fish Hatchery]] is a coldwater fish production station operated by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], located near Walhalla, South Carolina. Established in 1935 under the authority of the [[White Act of 1930]], construction was completed in 1937 by the Work Progress Administration, with the first fish stocked that same year. Between 1955 and 1965 the facility underwent significant modernization, replacing circular ponds with raceways and adding permanent brick infrastructure.

Historically, the hatchery produced [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], and [[Brown Trout]] broodstock, distributing eggs regionally (North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee) and internationally. Today its mission has narrowed to supplying fish for federal waters in South Carolina and Georgia, primarily the [[Sumter National Forest]] and [[Chattahoochee National Forest]], as well as U.S. Army Corps of Engineers tailwaters. The station also produces mitigation fish tied to a Savannah River federal water development project, under a tripartite agreement between the USFWS, the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]], and the [[South Carolina Wildlife and Marine Resources Department]]. Notably, Walhalla maintains the only [[Brown Trout]] broodstock in the southeastern United States, with all eggs produced consumed on-station.

## Background & Description

This station profile is administrative/operational in nature and does not provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical data on the species reared. The document focuses on hatchery infrastructure, production statistics, and distribution logistics rather than biological descriptions of [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], or [[Brown Trout]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target habitat**: Coldwater streams and tailwaters on National Forest Service lands, specifically within the [[Sumter National Forest]] (South Carolina) and [[Chattahoochee National Forest]] (Georgia).
- **Stocking role**: Supplementation of federal, state, and mitigation waters affected by federal water development (notably the Savannah River project), supporting recreational and ecological trout populations in the southeastern Appalachian coldwater fisheries system.
- **Broodstock significance**: The hatchery's [[Brown Trout]] broodstock is ecologically unique as the sole source population maintained in the Southeast, underscoring its role in regional genetic and stocking sustainability for this non-native but established salmonid.
- **Distribution range**: Historically extended across the southern Appalachian region (NC, SC, GA, TN); current operational range is restricted to South Carolina and Georgia federal waters and associated Army Corps of Engineers reservoir tailwaters.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment & Construction**: Founded 1935 under the White Act of 1930; construction completed 1937 by the WPA; first stocking occurred in 1937.
- **Infrastructure Modernization (1955–1965)**: Circular ponds converted to raceways; two brick buildings constructed (food prep/cold storage; office/lab/garage/restrooms).
- **Current Physical Facilities**: Includes 24 raceways, 12 rearing tanks, 2 water intake structures, 5 stacks of egg incubators, 6 aeration columns, liquid oxygen storage, 3 staff residences, and supporting service buildings.
- **Funding Trend (1991–1994, $ thousands)**:
