# Mollusca from Greenwood County, Kansas

#malacology #freshwater-ecology #terrestrial-gastropods #unionidae #biogeography

## Executive Summary

This 1946 survey documents the molluscan fauna of [[Greenwood County]], southeastern [[Kansas]], reporting 16 species of [[Unionidae|unionid mussels]], 6 species of aquatic gastropods, and 19 species or subspecies of terrestrial gastropods. The study represents the first systematic malacological investigation of this region, situated on the eastern slopes of the [[Flint Hills]]. Collections were made at eight stations encompassing streams ([[Fall River]], [[West Creek]], [[Burnt Creek]]), artificial ponds, and terrestrial habitats. The fauna shows strong similarity to that of [[Douglas County]] (100 miles northeast), with every species found also known from Douglas County, suggesting relatively uniform molluscan distribution across eastern Kansas despite latitudinal differences.

## Background & Description

[[Greenwood County]] lies on the eastern slopes of the [[Flint Hills]], drained by [[Fall River]], the [[Verdigris River]], and their tributaries. The landscape consists of grass-covered hills with limited timber, though riparian zones support stands of elm, oak, sycamore, maple, and hickory with dense native shrubs. Prevailing rock outcrops are limestones and shales providing calcium-rich soils favorable for mollusks, though occasional sandstone outcrops create local environmental variations. Arenaceous (sandy) soils are generally devoid of mollusks with certain exceptions.

Eight collecting stations were established:
- **Station 1**: Headwaters of [[Burnt Creek]], near Reece; debris from high waters
- **Station 2**: [[Fall River]] floodplain near Fall River town; heavily timbered with deep leaf mold
- **Station 3**: [[Shawnee Creek]] hardwood timber; [[Ireland Sandstone]] outcrops with contact springs
- **Station 4**: [[Carlysle Creek]] limestone slopes; well-drained with mixed hardwoods
- **Station 5**: Treeless grassland hill with seeping springs on Hibbard farm
- **Station 6**: [[West Creek]] shoals and gravel bars
- **Station 7**: Artificial pond west of Madison in shallow depression
- **Station 8**: Headwaters of [[Verdigris River]] tributary in treeless Flint Hills; notably devoid of mollusks despite apparently ideal conditions

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The molluscan fauna reflects the transitional nature of Kansas biogeography. Terrestrial gastropods show preference for specific microhabitats: woodland species like [[Triodopsis albolabris]] and [[Mesodon thyroidus]] require heavy timber and moisture, while others like [[Hawaiia minuscula]] and [[Pupoides marginatus]] occur in both woodlands and open grasslands. [[Anguispira alternata]] reached extraordinary abundance at Station 2, where "it was literally impossible to walk without crushing their shells at every step."

Aquatic species show habitat partitioning: [[Lymnaea bulimoides techella]] prefers ephemeral pools and burrows during dry seasons, while [[Helisoma antrosa]] prefers clean running water compared to the more ubiquitous [[Helisoma trivolvis]]. [[Physa hawnii]] proved common throughout streams and ponds.

The complete absence of mollusks from Station 8 streams presents an "unsolved problem" despite ideal ecological conditions (clear water, gravel substrate, limestone-derived calcium). Authors speculate that contamination from nearby oil wells may have exterminated populations, though no contamination was evident at time of survey.

## Key Findings

**Pelecypoda (Unionidae)** - All from Station 6 ([[West Creek]]):
- [[Lampsilis siliquoidea]]: Most common Lampsilis; shells unusually heavy, sometimes malformed
- [[Quadrula quadrula]]: Common; shells "gigantic for the species"
- [[Lasmigona complanata]]: Found abundantly; typical
- [[Fusconaia flava]]: Common; shells unusually large for species
- [[Amblema costata]]: Numerous; ridge development atypical, tending toward A. peruviana
- [[Quadrula pustulosa]]: Common; pustules very poorly developed or absent
- [[Proptera purpurata]]: Common; identification uncertain due to shell character variability
- [[Leptodea fragilis]]: Fairly common; shells unusually heavy for species
- [[Obliquaria reflexa]]: Rare; "never found in numbers in Kansas streams"
- Additional species: [[Truncilla truncata]], [[Lampsilis anodontoides]], [[Lampsilis ventricosa]], [[Ligumia subrostrata]], [[Tritogonia verrucosa]], [[Anodonta grandis]], [[Uniomerus tetralasmus]]

**Aquatic Gastropoda**:
- Family [[Lymnaeidae]]: [[Lymnaea bulimoides techella]] (5 stations), [[Lymnaea humilis rustica]], [[Lymnaea parva]]
- Family [[Planorbidae]]: [[Helisoma antrosa]] and [[Helisoma trivolvis]]
- Family [[Physidae]]: [[Physa hawnii]] (5 stations, "common everywhere")

**Terrestrial Gastropoda** (19 species/subspecies across 8 families):
- [[Bulimulus dealbatus]]: Locally abundant, particularly at Station 7; typically southern species ranging North Carolina to Texas
- [[Anguispira alternata]]: Extremely abundant at Station 2; high-spired form (previously named "eriensis") co-occurs with typical form at Station 3 on sandstone substrate, suggesting phenotypic variation rather than valid subspecies
- [[Stenotrema monodon aliciae]]: Present but taxonomically problematic; "further elucidation of the fraterna-monodon complex in Kansas is needed"
- [[Gastrocopta]] species: Four taxa recorded, including western forms [[Gastrocopta procera sterkiana]] and [[Gastrocopta procera mcclungi]]
- [[Strobilops labyrinthica parietalis]]: Minute snails with "exquisitely sculptured shells"; woodland-restricted

**Biogeographic Patterns**:
- Every species found in Greenwood County also known from [[Douglas County]] (100 miles northeast)
- Relative abundance patterns similar between counties except [[Bulimulus dealbatus]] more abundant in Greenwood County
- Southern genera ([[Goniobasis]], [[Haplotrema]], [[Mesomphix]], [[Polygyra]]) apparently absent despite suitable conditions and drainage patterns
- Molluscan fauna shows stronger east-west stratification than north-south, reflecting soil, rainfall, and flora zonation patterns
- [[Pupilla muscorum]] likely represented by Pleistocene fossils in drift rather than living populations
- [[Hendersonia occulta]] not found; "notorious for its erratic, local distribution"

**Methodological Notes**:
- Pelecypod list incomplete, compiled from single creek collection; river faunas generally more diverse
- [[Fall River]] collections prevented by near-flood conditions during visits
- Collections necessarily restricted to few selected stations due to time constraints
- Some expected genera ([[Campeloma]], [[Vertigo]], [[Amnicola]]) not found but likely present

Authors note that Kansas serves as "meeting-ground for both eastern and western, and southern and northern, species of mollusks," though east-west zonation predominates over north-south patterns.
