#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1997 final project report by W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) describing assistance provided to Robert Rush Miller (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology) in completing his landmark treatise, *Freshwater Fishes of Mexico*. The report itself contains limited primary fish data but appends excerpted manuscript material, including introductory biogeographic context for the Mexican ichthyofauna and detailed species accounts from the "Family Cyprinidae, Carps and Minnows" chapter, focused on the taxonomically problematic genus [[Gila]]. Two species accounts are provided in detail: [[Gila ditaenia]] (Sonora chub) and [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail), both relevant to transboundary Mexico–U.S. conservation concerns tied to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program.

## Background & Description

**Genus [[Gila]] (Baird & Girard)** — Noted by Miller as "badly in need of generic revision," being clearly polyphyletic as currently constituted (Barbour & Miller 1978). Species identification relies heavily on meristic characters (e.g., vertebral counts), first compiled by Uyeno (1960).

**[[Gila ditaenia]]** (Sonora chub / "Carpita sonorense")
- Maximum known standard length (SL): 145 mm
- Vertebral counts (including 4 Weberian vertebrae + urostyle), n=50 specimens: 37 (9 individuals), 38 (34 individuals), 39 (7 individuals)
- Juveniles as small as 17–22 mm SL observed
- Displays geographic variation in size classes, likely reflecting habitat variability

**[[Gila elegans]]** (Bonytail / "Carpita elegante")
- Maximum known total length (TL): ~64 cm
- Longevity: estimated up to 37+ years or more, based on otolith analysis of a 52-cm individual
- Large-bodied river cyprinid adapted to swift, turbid mainstem river channels

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**[[Gila ditaenia]]**
- **Range:** Pacific slope, Río de la Concepción basin (tributary flowing to the Gulf of California), spanning Arizona (U.S.) and Sonora (México)
- **Habitat:** Small, shallow, clear streams ~1 m deep, often intermittent, with moderate-to-swift current over sand/gravel substrates when flowing; associated with watercress and flowering aquatic plants. Preferred cover includes fallen logs, dense vegetation, and undercut roots (Hendrickson & Juárez-R. 1990).
- **Diet:** Aquatic and terrestrial insects and algae (documented in early summer, Arizona; Minckley 1973)
- **Reproduction:** Spawning season possibly spans December–October in México, or year-round under favorable conditions; nuptial coloration most frequently observed in adults during March.

**[[Gila elegans]]**
- **Range:** Historically throughout the Río Colorado basin; in the Mexican fauna restricted to the mainstem Río Colorado below Yuma, Arizona, extending into Baja California and Sonora (Follett 1961) — now **extirpated** from Mexican waters.
- **Habitat:** Moderate-to-swift current in large river channels, over unstable sand-silt substrates; utilizes pools and eddies.
- **Diet:** Terrestrial insects, plant debris, and algae.
- **Reproduction:** Spawning observed in a lower Colorado River reservoir in May, over gravel shelves at depths up to 9.0 m.

## Key Findings

- The manuscript *Freshwater Fishes of Mexico* documents **at least 503 species** across **48 families**, representing roughly 65% of the combined ichthyofauna of the United States and Canada, despite México occupying only ~20% of the surface area of the continental U.S.
- Mexican freshwater fish fauna derives roughly equally (~30% each) from Nearctic/Holarctic sources, Neotropical/Middle American elements, and marine-derived lineages; the remainder (<10%) is the highly endemic, autochthonous family [[Goodeidae]] of the Mesa Central.
- Approximately 25% (127 species) are classified as primary freshwater fishes and 43% (218 species) as secondary freshwater fishes (per Myers 1938, 1949 classification).
- The three most speciose freshwater fish families in México are [[Poeciliidae]] (86 species), [[Cyprinidae]] (76 species), and [[Cichlidae]] (45 species).
- A working salinity threshold of **1.0 ppt** was used to define "freshwater" fish inclusion in the treatise, though exceptions extend to ~1.5 ppt or higher (e.g., Cuatro Ciénegas basin, Coahuila; San Diego, Chihuahua thermal springs).
- Numerous nominally "primary freshwater" cyprinids show marked salinity tolerance exceptions worldwide (e.g., *Rutilus rutilus* in 10–18 ppt; *Ictalurus punctatus* in 15.1 ppt; *Lepomis macrochira* in 17.4 ppt), and México's own *Astyanax mexicanus* invades brackish mangrove esteros.
- The genus [[Gila]] is taxonomically unresolved and polyphyletic, containing multiple undescribed species indicated only by provisional distribution mapping.
- [[Gila ditaenia]] (Sonora chub) is considered threatened in the United States but remains widely distributed and relatively secure in México.
- [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail) is a critically endangered, long-lived large-river cyprinid essentially extirpated throughout the Río Colorado basin, including its historic Mexican range; its taxonomic status has been actively debated (Smith et al. 1979; Douglas et al. 1989).
- As of the report date (1997), the *Freshwater Fishes of Mexico* manuscript was ~80% complete for species distribution ("dot") maps and ~75% complete for general range maps, with publication negotiations underway with the University of Chicago Press (anticipated 1998).
