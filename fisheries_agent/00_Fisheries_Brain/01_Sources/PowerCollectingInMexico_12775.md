# Field Collection of Endemic and Undescribed Cichlids in Northern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This first-person account describes a four-day collecting trip to northern Mexico in which the author and colleague Irv (leader of the breeding program) gathered live and preserved [[Cichlid]] specimens for experiments in Miami. The two targets were the endemic [[Cichlasoma Minckleyi]] in the marshes of [[Cuatro Cienegas]] (Coahuila) and cichlids in the canals and lake of [[La Media Luna]] (San Luis Potosi). The team collected several cichlid species, including the yellow cichlid later identified as the previously described [[Cichlasoma Labridens]], and returned with live fish despite flight delays, a chaotic drive through [[Mexico City]], and tight schedules.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose of the trip:** The author was in Miami checking breeding experiments on [[Cichlasoma Minckleyi]]. Irv proposed a trip to Mexico to collect fish for further experiments, with the added lure of a possible new cichlid that [[Robert R. Miller]] (University of Michigan) had mentioned.
- **Preparation:** Equipment included snorkeling gear, a seine net, hand nets, four knockdown shipping boxes for live fish, plastic bags, antibiotics, two small air pumps and a dry ice container. The trip was arranged in about a week, leaving only four days in Mexico.
- **Route:** Miami to Mexico City, then Monterrey (rented Subaru), a two-hour drive to Cuatro Cienegas, then San Luis Potosi (rented Volkswagen microbus) for La Media Luna, and finally a four-hour drive back to Mexico City for the flight to Miami.
- **Species described in the text:**
  - *C. minckleyi*: endemic to the Cuatro Cienegas marshes. Individuals have either molariform or papilliform pharyngeal teeth, and each marsh has a different ratio of the two types. Some evolutionary biologists think these tooth-type populations may reveal mechanisms of [[Speciation]].
  - *C. bartoni*: caught in a canal near La Media Luna. In breeding coloration both sexes are white on the dorsal half and jet black on the ventral half, which inspired the nickname "Mexican mloto."
  - *C. labridens*: a bright yellow cichlid with black markings. The author first believed it was an undescribed species, but it was later identified as a species that had been described previously and was not in commercial production.
  - *C. cyanoguttatum*: a small number of specimens were caught at La Media Luna.
  - Other taxa: *Cyprinodon* pupfish were the only other fish seen in the Cuatro Cienegas marshes. *Oreochromis* was reported in the La Media Luna lake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cuatro Cienegas:** The surrounding area is arid, devoid of trees, and ringed by mountains. The four main marshes ("Four Marshes") differ in size and depth and each holds its own *C. minckleyi* population. The largest, [[El Mojarral]], measures about 200 yards by 50 to 70 yards and is several yards deep. Its water was very clear and quite cool on the day of the visit, and a caption gives a year-round temperature of about 75°F (24°C).
- **La Media Luna:** The area is crossed by many miles of man-made concrete canals and has a more tropical vegetation and a higher elevation than Cuatro Cienegas. The lake is over 200 yards long, roughly circular, and about 50 feet deep at the center. Its edges were murky and densely vegetated, so collecting was not possible there. The adjacent canal had clear water but a strong current, which made seining difficult.
- **Hazards and disturbance:** Local people were bathing and washing laundry upstream in one canal, which quickly clouded the water and ended the seining there.

## Key Findings

- **Collection effort at Cuatro Cienegas:** Minnow traps baited with tortillas were used to catch small *C. minckleyi* for breeding experiments. A large barrier net was stretched across part of El Mojarral to capture larger individuals, with snorkelers herding fish into the net. The team sampled all four marshes and collected 10 to 20 individuals per marsh for liver tissue used in DNA typing. Irv and Marcie Friedman recorded dentition and removed the livers.
- **Collection effort at La Media Luna:** The team seined the canal and collected several *C. bartoni* and *C. cyanoguttatum*, plus a pair of yellow cichlids (*C. labridens*) that were first preserved in formalin for scientific description. Two specimens were preserved this way, with the author objecting to the decision.
- **Live transport:** After the preserved specimens were set aside, the team found a second pair of yellow cichlids guarding a nest and captured it alive. The four shipping boxes carried live fish back to Miami.
- **Identity revision:** The yellow cichlid, initially thought to be undescribed, was later confirmed as *C. labridens*, which had been described earlier.
- **Outcome:** The team completed its objectives within four days, despite a late start, an airport strike, and a difficult drive through Mexico City. They also recruited local assistance (Coco, a Mexican field assistant, and students Marcie Friedman and Loni) and were guided by Irv, who knew the route.
