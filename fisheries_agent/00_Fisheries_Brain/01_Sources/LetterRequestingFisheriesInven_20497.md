# Letter Requesting Fisheries Inventory Assistance on the San Pedro River

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Safford District Office, addressed to Dr. W. L. Minckley of the [[Arizona State University]] Department of Zoology. It requests scientific assistance in conducting a fisheries inventory of recently acquired BLM lands along the [[San Pedro River]] in Arizona. The letter itself does not present primary morphological, taxonomic, or ecological data; rather, it outlines the *scope and purpose* of a proposed fisheries survey intended to support environmental compliance documentation (an Environmental Impact Statement [EIS] or Environmental Assessment [EA]). The requested inventory is explicitly linked to the identification of potential habitat for [[Threatened and Endangered Fish Species]] within the river system.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The letter is a procedural/administrative request and does not describe specific fish species, body plans, or diagnostic features. Any such data would presumably be generated by the requested inventory itself, not contained within this correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no direct ecological data (e.g., water chemistry, substrate, flow regime) are provided, the letter implies several ecologically relevant points:

- **Habitat context**: The [[San Pedro River]] is identified as a riverine system with recently BLM-acquired property segments warranting biological assessment.
- **Conservation relevance**: The survey is explicitly framed around locating "potential habitat areas for threatened or endangered fish," indicating the river is recognized as ecologically significant for rare or imperiled aquatic species — consistent with the San Pedro's known status as critical habitat for desert fish fauna in the [[Sonoran Desert]] region.
- **Spatial framework**: Data collection is to be organized "by section of river (mapped)," implying a longitudinal, reach-based approach to characterizing fish distribution — a common method in [[Riverine Fish Community Surveys]].
- **Historical dimension**: A "historical review" is requested, suggesting recognition that the river's fish fauna may have shifted over time, possibly due to anthropogenic pressures, water diversion, or invasive species introduction — issues well-documented in southwestern U.S. desert streams.

## Key Findings
- The BLM Safford District Office formally requested Dr. W. L. Minckley's assistance in an inventory of fish species on newly acquired San Pedro River properties.
- The requested inventory scope includes: (1) a species list, (2) occurrence and relative abundance data, (3) spatial distribution mapped by river section, and (4) a historical review of the fish fauna.
- The data are intended to inform the Biological Resources Section of a formal environmental compliance document (EIS or EA).
- A stated objective of the survey is to identify potential habitat for [[Threatened and Endangered Fish Species]], underscoring the conservation significance of the [[San Pedro River]] watershed.
- Logistical support (housing, field per diem, and local transportation) was offered by BLM to facilitate volunteer research participation, indicating a collaborative government–academia model for regional ichthyological fieldwork.
- No specific species, families, or quantitative ecological/morphological findings are included in this letter; it functions solely as a request initiating future field research, likely connected to broader efforts such as those documented in [[Minckley Desert Fishes Research]].
