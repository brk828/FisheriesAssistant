# Psarolepis romeri and the Origin of Bony Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sarcopterygii

## Executive Summary

The source is a bundle of several items. The main research content is a 1999 *Nature* letter on [[Psarolepis Romeri]], a roughly 400-million-year-old fossil fish from the Lower Devonian of [[Qujing]], Yunnan, China. The letter describes shoulder girdle and cheek bone material that shows a mix of [[Sarcopterygii]] (lobe-finned) and [[Actinopterygii]] (ray-finned) features, plus large paired and median fin spines previously known only in [[Placoderms]] and [[Acanthodians]]. The authors argue that *Psarolepis* may lie near the base of [[Osteichthyes]] (bony fishes), though its exact position remains unresolved. The bundle also contains a reference fragment from a Hawaiian hotspot and plate-motion study and a non-aquatic vision paper on the motion aftereffect. These are summarized briefly below.

## Background & Description

*Psarolepis romeri* was described from skull and lower jaw material from the Lower Devonian of Qujing (about 400 million years ago). Material from the Upper Silurian (about 410 million years ago) of China and Vietnam was later assigned to the same genus, making it one of the earliest known osteichthyans.

Early work placed *Psarolepis* within Sarcopterygii, either as a basal member of Dipnomorpha or among basal crossopterygians. The skull and lower jaws combine sarcopterygian overall morphology with primitive actinopterygian traits, including a toothed median rostral and five coronoids in the lower jaw (most sarcopterygians have three).

The new material comes from the second and third of four fossil beds at Qujing. The first bed belongs to the Yulongsi Formation (Pridoli), the second and third to the Xishancun Formation (early Lochkovian), and the fourth to the Xitun Formation (late Lochkovian). The shoulder girdle and cheek plates are preserved as internal and external moulds. They share a distinctive ornamentation of large, closely spaced pores on the cosmine surface, which underpins their assignment to *Psarolepis*.

## Key Findings

- **Mixed character set:** The shoulder girdle bears a huge pectoral spine that resembles those of some placoderms and acanthodians. A symmetrical median fin spine suggests the presence of median spines in front of the unpaired fins, as in sharks and acanthodians.
- **Shoulder girdle:** The endoskeletal girdle is a massive, plate-shaped bone pierced by vessel and nerve openings, similar to placoderms. The anterior cleithrum has a denticulate postbranchial lamina, shared with placoderms and actinopterygians. The dorsal cleithrum is high and pointed, as in actinopterygians, onychodonts and primitive coelacanths.
- **Cheek bones:** The preopercular has a complete preopercular canal and lacks a jugal canal. It is posteriorly expanded in contact with the maxillary. These features, together with the absence of a separate squamosal, distinguish *Psarolepis* from all previously known sarcopterygians.
- **Phylogenetic analysis:** The authors added *Psarolepis* and three non-osteichthyan outgroups (*Acanthodes*, *Ctenacanthus*, *Dicksonosteus*) to a 33-taxon matrix from ref. 14. They also added 9 characters to the original 140, giving 37 taxa and 149 characters. Two analyses used different character codings.
- **Competing placements:**
  - With the modified codings from ref. 4, *Psarolepis* is the sister group of all osteichthyans. The strict consensus tree has 64 most parsimonious trees at 311 steps (consistency index 0.543, retention index 0.804).
  - With the original codings from ref. 14, *Psarolepis* is the sister group of all previously known sarcopterygians. The strict consensus tree has 64 most parsimonious trees at 314 steps (consistency index 0.538, retention index 0.798).
- **Effects on character definitions:** In the first tree, Sarcopterygii is defined by ten synapomorphies instead of fourteen. Osteichthyes has no synapomorphy and is supported only by homoplasies. In the second tree, Sarcopterygii has four synapomorphies, and "Actinopterygii" is paraphyletic.
- **Implications:** Porolepiform-like features in *Psarolepis* can no longer be used to define Porolepiformes or Dipnomorpha. The intracranial joint and cosmine cannot serve as sarcopterygian synapomorphies if *Psarolepis* is a basal osteichthyan.
- **Conclusion:** The authors state that the position of *Psarolepis* is uncertain. Its unique character combination will nonetheless affect future studies of osteichthyan evolution.
