## Executive Summary

This 1965–1966 completion report from the Arizona Game and Fish Department documents early-stage environmental review of the proposed **[[Orme Dam]]** (also called Maxwell Dam) at the confluence of the **[[Salt River]]** and **[[Verde River]]** in Maricopa County, Arizona. The project, part of the larger Central Arizona Project, would create a large reservoir with major implications for both fish and wildlife resources. The report is primarily a procedural and land-use status update rather than a quantitative fisheries study, but it establishes the anticipated tradeoff central to the project: significant **gains in warmwater sport fishing habitat** versus **net losses in terrestrial wildlife habitat**, particularly upland and big game species. No specific fish species, population counts, or water quality data are presented in this segment; the substantive fish and wildlife impact analysis was being conducted separately by the **[[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]]**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Proposed habitat alteration**: Construction of Orme Dam would inundate the confluence zone of the Salt and Verde Rivers, converting a lotic (flowing river) ecosystem into a lentic (reservoir) system.
- **Reservoir specifications**:
  - Maximum capacity: 1,647,000 acre-feet
  - Minimum pool: 43,000 acre-feet, covering ~2,300 surface acres
  - Minimum pool level maintained ~40% of project lifespan
  - Dam surcharge elevation considered: 1,513.5 ft
- **Land inundation footprint** (under the site evaluated by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife):
  - 1,300 acres of the **[[Salt River Indian Reservation]]**
  - 7,900 acres of the **[[Tonto National Forest]]**
  - 14,800 acres of the **[[Fort McDowell Indian Reservation]]**
- **Habitat conversion effects**:
  - Expected creation of substantial new **warmwater fishery habitat**, benefiting recreational fishing near the Phoenix metropolitan area.
  - Expected net **loss of terrestrial wildlife habitat**, primarily affecting upland game species, with secondary impacts to big game.
  - Wildlife losses attributed to direct habitat destruction and indirectly to increased human recreational activity in the reservoir area.
- **Comparative fieldwork**: Investigators visited existing impoundments on the Salt River (analogous reservoir systems) to assess comparable recreational use and habitat conditions.
- **Proposed mitigation**: Development of a game management area at one end of the minimum pool was under consideration to offset wildlife habitat losses.

## Key Findings

- The **[[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]]** completed a preliminary draft feasibility-grade report evaluating fish and wildlife impacts of the Orme Dam Project; Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) personnel reviewed and commented on this draft prior to submission to the Regional Office.
- Multiple potential dam sites were under consideration near the Salt-Verde confluence due to land acquisition difficulties, with the **[[Bureau of Reclamation]]** investigating two alternate sites.
- Land administration conflicts among the Salt River Indian Reservation, Fort McDowell Indian Reservation, and the U.S. Forest Service (Tonto National Forest) — particularly over perimeter land acquisition and administration — represented a major obstacle to project planning, generating public and media controversy.
- The project is framed as a net positive for **[[Warmwater Fisheries]]** (increased sport fishing opportunity in the Phoenix area) but a net negative for terrestrial wildlife absent mitigation.
- AGFD committed to continued cooperation with the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and other agencies to ensure fish and wildlife provisions were incorporated into final construction plans.
- The report recommends continuation of the study, with a further review of the finalized Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife report anticipated during the sixth segment ("Job 6") of the project.
- This document should be read alongside the prior year's report, **[[Manes 1965 Orme Dam Report]]**, which provided the initial project description referenced here.
