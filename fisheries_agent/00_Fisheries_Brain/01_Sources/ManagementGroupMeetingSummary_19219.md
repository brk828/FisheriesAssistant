# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summary (February 5, 1990)

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-program #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #water-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a meeting summary from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group meeting held February 5, 1990, in Lakewood, Colorado. The Recovery Implementation Program is a cooperative multi-agency effort established by agreement in January 1988 to recover four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, candidate species). The meeting addressed program updates, technical group recommendations, water rights acquisition, Section 7 consultations (particularly [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations), five-year planning documents ("Road Maps"), and organizational structure reviews. Significant discussion focused on coordinating hatchery feasibility studies, sediment impacts on fish spawning, nonnative fish management, and selenium contamination surveys throughout the basin.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] operates under a Cooperative Agreement signed in January 1988 involving federal agencies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]]), state agencies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming), water development interests, environmental groups, and power agencies ([[Western Area Power Administration]]). The program structure includes a Recovery Implementation Committee (policy level), Management Group (coordination and execution), and Technical Group (scientific review and prioritization).

The four target species are endemic cyprinids and catostomids adapted to the large, turbid rivers of the Colorado Basin. [[Colorado Squawfish]] is a large piscivorous minnow requiring specific flow regimes and spawning substrates. [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] are specialized cyprinids with distinctive morphology. [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-bodied catostomid. All have experienced severe population declines due to habitat modification (dams, flow alterations), water depletions, nonnative fish introductions, and contaminants.

The program addresses five recovery elements: habitat management (flow regimes, temperature, sediment), habitat development and maintenance, native fish stocking and genetic management, nonnative fish management, and research/monitoring. Water management is central, as the [[Colorado River Storage Project Act]] mandates Upper Basin water utilization while endangered species protection requires specific flow and temperature regimes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Requirements**: The endangered fishes evolved in a dynamic riverine environment characterized by high spring flows (snowmelt-driven), warm summer temperatures, turbid conditions, and specific substrate conditions for spawning. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] on the [[Green River]] and other mainstem dams have fundamentally altered these conditions through flow regulation, temperature depression, and sediment trapping.

**Tributary Importance**: The [[Yampa River]] and [[San Juan River]] are identified as essential to recovery, particularly for [[Colorado Squawfish]]. The Yampa provides relatively unregulated flows that maintain natural hydrologic and thermal regimes. Phase II flow recommendations for the Yampa were under review, with comments due February 5, 1990.

**Sediment Dynamics**: Sediment discussion focused on substrate conditions for egg incubation. Concerns exist that altered flow regimes may not provide adequate "flushing flows" to maintain suitable spawning substrates. A proposed flume modeling study would examine hydraulics and egg survival under various sediment conditions, potentially conducted at [[Colorado State University]] or Bureau of Reclamation Research Center.

**Nonnative Fish Competition**: Management of nonnative fishes (sport fish and other introduced species) represents a major challenge. The program sought agreements with state fish and game agencies (Utah, Wyoming) to review stocking proposals that might affect endangered species. Wyoming expressed "little interest in a review process."

**Contaminant Threats**: Extensive selenium contamination was documented throughout the basin from irrigation return flows. Reconnaissance surveys (1986-1989) at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]], [[Stewart Lake Waterfowl Management Area]], [[Pariette Draw]], and along the [[Gunnison River]] and [[Uncompahgre River]] revealed elevated selenium in fish, birds, and plants. At Ouray NWR, waterfowl reproductive problems and deformed embryos with elevated selenium were documented. Heavy metals (zinc, copper) from mining operations also contaminate the Uncompahgre River upstream of [[Dallas Creek Reservoir]].

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Organization**: Lee Mills reviewed the program's organizational structure compared to 25-30 similar fishery restoration efforts nationwide (Columbia River, Sacramento River, Klamath River, Atlantic Striped Bass, etc.). He concluded the program operates "fairly well, but has room for improvement." Key recommendations included:

- Restructuring the Technical Group, with Management Group forming a steering committee
- Creating standing committees for Biology, Hydrology, Sport Fishery Management, Information & Education, Propagation, Habitat Improvement, and Water Rights
- Establishing a Senior Scientific Advisory Group of recognized experts for peer review
- Developing a complete detailed charter with formal meeting procedures and documentation
- Addressing underfunding relative to tasks required
- Ensuring adequate staff time commitment from participating agencies

**Section 7 Consultations**: Since program initiation (January 1988), 30 Section 7 consultations were completed involving water depletions totaling approximately 5,000-6,000 acre-feet annually, generating approximately $50,000-60,000 in depletion charges at $10/acre-foot. Four consultations were pending, including major projects:

- [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operation (consultation initiated 1978, draft Biological Opinion due June 1, 1990)
- [[Sandstone Reservoir]], Wyoming (30,600 acre-feet depletion, $318,546 in charges)
- [[Price-San Rafael Project]], Utah (25,623 acre-feet, $238,910 in charges)

The Consolidated Biology and Hydrology Reports for Flaming Gorge clearly indicated that dam operations "have adversely affected and are likely to continue to adversely affect the endangered fishes." The Biological Opinion would address which operational aspects are likely to jeopardize species and provide reasonable and prudent alternatives.

**San Juan River Reinitiation**: New information in the [[San Juan River]] and Upper Basin warranted reinitiation of Section 7 consultation on [[Animas-La Plata Project]]. FWS Region 6 would take lead and consult with the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] to reaffirm that the San Juan River is essential to recovery and revise the [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan]].

**Water Rights Acquisition**: 

- **Yampa River**: Wyoming objected to "general provisions" in the [[Little Snake River]] cooperative agreement; Solicitor's Office working to resolve. Appraisal of [[Cross Mountain Ranch]] nearly complete. [[The Nature Conservancy]] planned to approach another willing seller on the Little Snake for a small water right that would consolidate with Cross Mountain.
- **Colorado River**: Final [[Ruedi Reservoir]] lease agreement being signed. A scope of work for the 15-mile reach water availability study would cost approximately $12,000, with the study itself costing $50,000-$100,000. [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] and River District evaluating options and approaches.

**Propagation and Hatchery Development**: The [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] hatchery feasibility study schedule must mesh with the overall FWS propagation plan. CWCB selected three firms for short list and was developing RFP with Technical Advisory Committee (Colorado Division of Wildlife and USFWS personnel); selection scheduled for mid-March 1990. Important issues included identifying facilities or refugia for genetic stock maintenance. The policy was not to remove fish from the wild unless facilities are ready to receive them. PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tagging protocols were under development.

**Five-Year Road Maps**: Draft "Recovery Management Plan" (name changed back to "Five-Year Road Map") presented for review. The document would distinguish projects already begun and include FY-91 priorities. Review by Management, Technical, and Biology groups with written comments due March 2, 1990. Revised Road Map would be included with annual request for proposals, sent out March 19, 1990.

**Annual Project Reports**: The 1989 Annual Project Report required revisions. FWS would return individual reports needing revision to authors by February 16, 1990; revised reports due March 16, 1990. Reports should undergo quality check by Management Group members before submission. Revisions included: identifying report authors/principal investigators in table of contents, making individual project reports an appendix, adding column showing funds actually expended, and including only revised budget table in final report.

**Budget and Funding**: FY-91 funding status discussed. Need identified to get FWS add-on permanently in budget to avoid annual congressional requests. Multiyear funding policy statement revised and recommended for Implementation Committee approval. Discussion of using Section 7 funds to finance studies.

**Field Activities**: Proposed 2-night/3-day river trip through Blackrocks/Westwater for 1990, with Yampa River trip more timely in 1991. Purpose included public relations with key Congressional representatives.

**Contaminant Survey Results** (Attachment 8): Detailed reconnaissance and intensive surveys documented widespread selenium contamination:

- **Ouray NWR**: American coots showed high selenium levels from wetlands receiving irrigation drainwater; waterfowl reproductive problems and deformed embryos documented. Selenium uptake studies (1989) showed captive birds rapidly assimilated high selenium levels. Engineering designs completed for pipeline from [[Pelican Lake]] to deliver high-quality water to refuge.
- **Gunnison River**: Elevated selenium in fish, birds, and plants throughout drainage from irrigation drainwater.
- **Uncompahgre River**: Elevated selenium, zinc, and copper in fish from mining operations upstream of Dallas Creek Reservoir.
- **Stewart Lake WMA**: Moderate selenium in coots, highly elevated levels in aquatic plants and fish.
- **Desert Lake WMA**: Selenium levels in American coot eggs approach levels causing waterfowl reproduction problems.

**Meeting Schedule and Process**: Budget process timeline established: Researchers' Meeting (February 7-8, 1990), Recovery Implementation Committee Meeting (February 21, 1990), RFPs with road maps sent out (March 19, 1990), proposals due (May 1, 1990), Technical Group ranking meeting (June 5-7, 1990), Management Group review (July 11, 1990), Recovery Implementation Committee approval (August 22, 1990). Next Management Group meeting tentatively scheduled for April 25, 1990.
