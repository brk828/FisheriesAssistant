# Contaminants in Fish and Wildlife of Havasu National Wildlife Refuge (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 2 Contaminants Program report (May 1997; Brenda J. Andrews, Kirke A. King, and Denise L. Baker) presents the first comprehensive assessment of organochlorine pesticides and trace elements in the aquatic ecosystem of [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]] (Havasu NWR), on the Colorado River between Parker Dam and Lake Havasu City, Arizona. Samples of sediment, vegetation, clams, fish, coots, and grebe eggs were collected April–August 1993.

Organochlorines were essentially absent. [[Selenium]] was the primary element of concern: it was elevated in every biota group sampled, showed clear [[Bioaccumulation]] through the food chain, and reached levels associated with reproductive impairment in fish, fish-eating birds, and the grebes' eggs. Arsenic, cadmium, chromium, copper, and lead were also elevated in some samples.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** Havasu NWR was established in 1941 as a migratory bird refuge (about 37,515 acres) along 26 miles of the [[Colorado River]]. [[Topock Marsh]] (about 4,000 acres), fed by a 6.4-km inlet canal, and the river-dominated [[Topock Gorge]] unit were sampled, including Catfish Paradise, Glory Hole, North Dike, Pintail Slough, Devils Elbow, and Blankenship Bend. River flows are managed by the Bureau of Reclamation.

**Rationale.** Earlier work documented selenium-related risk in downriver refuges, notably [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], where selenium threatened fish and the endangered [[Yuma Clapper Rail]]. Selenium was presumed to originate upriver from natural weathering of seleniferous soils, coal combustion, uranium extraction, and irrigation agriculture. Contaminant levels upstream at Havasu NWR were previously unknown.

**Sampling.**
- Sediment: six sites (five-subsample composites, top ~10 cm).
- [[Spiny Naiad]]: two composites from backwater areas.
- [[Asiatic Clam]]: composites from Catfish Paradise, Glory Hole, and North Dike.
- Fish: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Common Carp]], composited by species (n = 2–5 per site); nine whole-body fish samples in total.
- [[American Coot]]: five birds from Topock Gorge (livers and kidneys analyzed; kidneys of three birds for selenium only).
- [[Western Grebe]] and [[Clark's Grebe]]: five eggs each, one per nest.

**Methods.** Elements were analyzed at Research Triangle Institute (cold-vapor and hydride-generation atomic absorption, and ICP spectroscopy). Organochlorines were analyzed at Mississippi State University by soxhlet extraction, lipid removal, and gas chromatography. Fish element levels were compared with the [[National Contaminant Biomonitoring Program]] (NCBP) 85th percentile of the nationwide geometric mean, and sediment with Arizona background data (mean ± two standard deviations, USGS). Statistics used t-tests and ANOVA with P < 0.05.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Organochlorines.** No organochlorines were detected in sediment or clams. Only DDE, a breakdown product of DDT, was found in coot carcasses (0.01–0.31 µg/g wet weight), at levels generally below those linked to reproductive problems. The authors concluded organochlorines likely do not pose a threat to fish and wildlife at the refuge, while noting that fish and birds should be tested directly.

**Selenium pathway.** Selenium is mobilized by microorganisms and moves into food chains. The study documented increasing concentrations along this chain:
- Sediment: 0.50–1.26 µg/g dry weight in five of six samples, comparable to earlier Topock Marsh data but below the 4 µg/g level suggested for food-chain contamination.
- Spiny naiad: 0.35 and 0.49 µg/g wet weight, well above background (~0.4 µg/g dry weight) and above the level linked to waterfowl reproductive failure.
- Clams: all sites exceeded 3 µg/g dry weight, a level potentially lethal to birds that eat them.
- Fish: all samples exceeded the NCBP 85th percentile (0.73 µg/g wet weight), at 0.79–2.60 µg/g wet weight.
- Coots: four of five livers exceeded the normal range (about 10 µg/g dry weight in birds).

Selenium levels were higher in backwater lakes than in seep lakes, consistent with Martinez (1994), and Topock Marsh sediment and fish contained more selenium than Topock Gorge samples.

**Trace elements.**
- Arsenic in clams averaged about 2.5 times background; all samples were above the background range in bioaccumulating mollusks.
- Cadmium in clams was highest at North Dike (up to 1.94 µg/g dry weight); fish levels were not toxic.
- Copper was elevated in carp (above the NCBP 85th percentile of 1.0 µg/g) and is considered a contaminant of potential concern.
- Lead exceeded the NCBP 85th percentile in 83% of fish samples, and two Topock Gorge samples exceeded the 0.5 µg/g harm threshold.
- Chromium exceeded 4.0 µg/g dry weight in two Topock Gorge carp.
- Mercury in fish was 0.02–0.08 µg/g wet weight, below concern thresholds; mercury in grebe eggs (0.19–0.86 µg/g dry) was within normal background.

**Birds and eggs.**
- Coot livers contained no mercury; zinc was high relative to domestic-bird toxic thresholds.
- Coot kidney:liver ratios were 1.56–1.86 (all greater than 1), indicating dietary selenium levels that warrant concern.
- Grebe eggs had selenium two to four times the 3 µg/g teratogenic threshold, and 80% of eggs exceeded 8.0 µg/g. Clark's grebe eggs from Topock Marsh contained significantly higher selenium than western grebe eggs (P = 0.02).

## Key Findings

- **Organochlorines:** Not detected in sediment or clams; only low DDE in coots. Unlikely to affect biota.
- **Selenium** is the element of greatest concern and was elevated in all sampled biota, from spiny naiad through fish, coots, and grebe eggs.
- **Fish:** Mean selenium of 1.73 µg/g wet weight exceeded the mean in fish from other NCBP stations; Lake Havasu was among the ten highest of 117 NCBP stations. Concentrations are below the 6.9–7.2 µg/g threshold of [[Gillespie and Baumann]] but above the 12 µg/g dry-weight reproductive-failure level suggested by Lemly and Smith (1987) for some fish; the authors describe them as suggesting potential selenium toxicity.
- **Arsenic, cadmium, chromium, copper, and lead** were elevated in some samples, warranting further monitoring, though most concentrations were below toxic thresholds.
- **Mercury and boron** were not of biological concern in birds.
- **Upstream contamination:** Selenium contamination extends upriver of Imperial and Cibola refuges, indicating a broader lower-Colorado River source.
- **Recommendations:** Continued study of selenium levels and effects in fish and piscivorous birds, including reproductive success and teratogenesis, should be given high priority. Collection of fish and birds for organochlorine analysis is recommended, and further research on the diet and habitat of western and Clark's grebes is needed to explain egg mercury and selenium differences.
