## Executive Summary

This 1973 paper by Holsinger & Bowman describes **[[Lirceus usdagalun]]**, a new species of freshwater isopod (family [[Asellidae]]) and the **first known troglobitic (obligate cave-dwelling) species** in the genus *Lirceus*. Unlike its epigean (surface-dwelling) congeners, this species is blind (eyeless) and unpigmented, discovered in three interconnected caves in the Powell Valley of Lee County, Virginia. The paper also reassesses the taxonomic value of the male second pleopod endopod tip as a diagnostic character for the genus, provides ecological/demographic data (sex ratios, brood size, size distributions) for the new species, and catalogs additional undetermined *Lirceus* populations (sp. A and sp. B) from caves across the Appalachian region (southwestern Virginia and eastern Tennessee).

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic context**: Within the family [[Asellidae]], the genus *Asellus* (s. lat.) is widespread in both epigean and cave habitats with many troglobitic species, but the genus *Lirceus* had previously only been recorded from epigean habitats (springs, small streams). Prior cave records of *Lirceus* (Missouri, Tennessee, Georgia) showed no extreme troglomorphic modifications and were not restricted to cave waters.
- **New species**: *[[Lirceus usdagalun]]* n. sp. is described from type material collected in Gallohan Cave No. 1, Surgener Cave, and Thompson Cedar Cave (holotype male USNM 143680, plus numerous paratypes).
- **Etymology**: "Usdagalun" is a Cherokee word meaning "cave" or "hole under rock," honoring the Cherokee inhabitants of the Powell Valley region.
- **Diagnosis**: A small, eyeless, unpigmented species reaching 7.0–7.5 mm in length. Distinguished by:
  - Deep, narrow lateral incision of the head
  - Relatively wide spaces between anterior pereonites
  - Absence of median/distal processes on the gnathopodal propod palm (except a rudimentary median process in larger males)
  - Slender spur on the endopod of male pleopod 2
  - Proportionately short uropods (~1/3 length of pleotelson)
- **Morphological detail**: Body ~64% longer than wide with subparallel sides; head ~1/3 as long as wide with deep lateral incisions; pleotelson subtriangular; antenna 2 relatively long (70–75% of body length); mandible lacks palp; maxilliped bears 4–5 retinacula. Male pleopod 2 endopod bears three distinct processes (caudal, cephalic, mesial) and a slender, finger-like spur — a character the authors argue has been undervalued taxonomically in this genus.
- **Type locality**: Gallohan Cave No. 1, developed in Hurricane Bridge limestone (Middle Ordovician), part of the hydrologically integrated Surgener-Gallohan Cave System.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: *L. usdagalun* inhabits cave streams, typically found on upper/lower surfaces of submerged rocks, occasionally among stream gravels.
- **Co-occurring troglobitic fauna** in the type locality stream include:
  - The isopod *[[Asellus recurvatus]]*
  - The amphipod *[[Crangonyx antennatus]]*
  - A planarian, *Sphalloplana* sp. (probably *S. virginiana*)
  - A snail, *Fontigens* sp.
  - Snails and planarians were far less abundant than isopods and amphipods.
- **Distribution**: Known only from three caves in the Powell Valley (south-central Lee County, Virginia), all developed in a broad band of Middle Ordovician limestone (Hurricane Bridge and Martin Creek members) along the Cedar Syncline. Despite the limestone corridor favoring subterranean dispersal, extensive searching between known sites failed to reveal additional populations.
- **Possible competitive exclusion**: The authors hypothesize that interspecific competition for food and space among cavernicolous crustaceans (per [[Culver 1973]]) may restrict *L. usdagalun*'s distribution. Notably:
  - Most Powell Valley caves with aquatic habitats contain *Crangonyx antennatus*, and many also contain *Asellus recurvatus*.
  - Gallohan Cave No. 1 is the only studied cave where *L. usdagalun* and *A. recurvatus* co-occur.
  - In Thompson Cedar Cave, *A. recurvatus* is absent, but *L. usdagalun* is abundant alongside a small *C. antennatus* population — suggesting possible competitive interactions shape co-occurrence patterns.
- **Population/reproductive data** (from 89 specimens across 5 samples, 3 caves):
  - Sex ratio: ~4.5:1 female-to-male (73 females : 16 males)
  - Of 73 females, 5 were ovigerous/larviparous, ranging 4.5–6.5 mm (mean 5.6 mm)
  - Reproductive females found only in July and August samples
  - Average brood size: 27.5 eggs/female (based on only 3 females)
  - Newly hatched young: 1.0–1.3 mm in length
  - Overall size ranges: females 2.5–7.5 mm; males 3.5–7.0 mm

## Key Findings

- **First troglobitic *Lirceus***: *L. usdagalun* represents the first documented obligate cave species (eyeless, unpigmented) in the genus *Lirceus*, contrasting with the genus's typical epigean ecology.
- **Taxonomic re-evaluation of pleopod morphology**: Contrary to Hubricht and Mackin's (1949) dismissal of the male pleopod 2 endopod tip as a weak taxonomic character, Holsinger & Bowman demonstrate clear, diagnostic differences in this structure among *L. usdagalun*, *[[Lirceus brachyurus]]*, and *[[Lirceus fontinalis]]* — arguing it should be considered a valuable species-level character.
- **Comparative relationships**:
  - *L. brachyurus* shares short uropods and reduced gnathopod processes with *L. usdagalun* but differs in larger adult size, lack of head incisions, and endopod tip structure (smaller, sharper caudal process; flattened mesial process; sharply tapering spur).
  - *L. fontinalis* shares lateral head incisions but differs in larger size, longer uropods, additional palmar processes, and a shorter, thicker (non finger-like) spur.
  - *[[Lirceus hargeri]]* (springs of the Powell and Holston valleys) shares head incisions but differs in size, additional palmar processes, and longer uropods.
- **Regional cave surveys**: Additional, taxonomically unresolved *Lirceus* populations were documented across multiple caves in the upper Tennessee River drainage of eastern Tennessee and southwestern Virginia:
  - **Lirceus sp. A**: small-eyed, lightly pigmented, with posteriorly lobed head margins; recorded from caves in Claiborne, Hancock, and Roane counties (TN) and Lee, Scott, and Washington counties (VA).
  - **Lirceus sp. B**: found in Fallen Rock Cave and Hugh Young Cave (Tazewell County, VA), part of the Ward Cove–Maiden Spring karst system near the Clinch River headwaters. This form has very tiny eyes, very light or absent pigmentation, lacks posterior head lobes (resembling *L. brachyurus* in head shape), and co-occurs with the troglobitic isopod *[[Asellus richardsonae]]* in Hugh Young Cave.
- **Broader implication**: The taxonomic confusion surrounding *Lirceus* (noted by Styron 1969 and Williams 1972) is partly attributed to intergradation between species and lack of a single reliable diagnostic character — underscoring the importance of the newly emphasized endopod tip morphology for future systematic work.
