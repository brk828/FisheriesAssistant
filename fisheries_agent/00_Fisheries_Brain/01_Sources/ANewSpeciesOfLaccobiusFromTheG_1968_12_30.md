# Laccobius antillensis (new species)

#entomology #Hydrophilidae #Coleoptera #AquaticBeetles #Hydrobiini

> **Note:** This source concerns an aquatic beetle (Coleoptera: Hydrophilidae), not a fish species. The note is structured to faithfully reflect the entomological/ecological content of the paper rather than forcing a fish-centric framing.

## Executive Summary
This paper describes **[[Laccobius antillensis]]**, a newly identified species of water scavenger beetle (family [[Hydrophilidae]], tribe Hydrobiini) within the genus **[[Laccobius]]**. The species fills a notable zoogeographic gap: prior to this discovery, *Laccobius* was known to be absent from Florida and the Gulf Coast states, and was considered absent from the Antilles altogether (per [[D'Orchymont 1942]]'s revision of American *Laccobius*). Examination of historical and newly collected aquatic beetle material revealed this single new species distributed across **Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Haiti, the Dominican Republic, and Cuba**. The author hypothesizes a colonization route from Mexico or Central America, where congeneric species are known to occur, bypassing the depauperate Gulf Coast/Florida populations.

## Background & Description
- **Size:** Holotype male body length 2.75 mm; greatest width 1.4 mm (small-bodied hydrophilid).
- **Coloration:**
  - Head piceous (blackish) with testaceous (yellowish-brown) spot anterior to each eye.
  - Pronotum piceous on disc, broadly testaceous laterally.
  - Scutellum piceous.
  - Elytron testaceous with dark humeral macula, two suture-adjacent maculae at midlength, one lateral macula, and dark punctures along serial rows.
  - Venter dark brown except testaceous hypopleura/epipleura.
  - Appendages (palpi, antennae, legs) largely light testaceous with darker apical segments.
- **Surface sculpture:**
  - Head microreticulate, moderately coarse/dense punctation, denser near eyes.
  - Labrum feebly microreticulate, densely punctate, feebly emarginate medially.
  - Pronotum >2× broader than long, microreticulate, finely margined laterally, with a submarginal basal row of punctures.
  - Elytron with 10 serial rows of coarse punctures plus scattered coarser interstitial punctures; finely margined laterally.
  - Scutellum sparsely punctate with smaller punctures than pronotum/elytra.
- **Sexual dimorphism (diagnostic):**
  - **Male:** Fore tarsus 4-segmented; first segment apically expanded; second segment darker, expanded, with a pubescent adhesive pad beneath (used in mating clasping).
  - **Female:** Fore tarsus normal, 5-segmented, not broadened, lacking pubescent pad.
- **Functional/structural adaptations:**
  - Hydrofuge (water-repellent) pubescence on front leg coxa, trochanter, and basal femur — an adaptation for aquatic life (trapping air/reducing wetting).
  - Venter bears hydrofuge pubescence except smooth posterior half of first and entire second abdominal sternum.
  - Shallow concavity on abdominal sterna lined with fine hairs and containing a "hyaline substance," structurally analogous to that found in the genus *[[Chaetarthria]]* — potentially related to plastron respiration or secretory function.
  - Prosternum longitudinally carinate; mesosternum strongly protuberant medially with an anterior tooth, carinate posteriorly (likely locomotor/structural support features).
- **Male genitalia (key diagnostic trait):** Aedeagus with a median lobe that is **very broad from base to apex** (illustrated in Figs. 1–2), distinguishing it from congeners with slender median lobes.
- **Intraspecific variation:** Elytral maculae (suture-adjacent pair and lateral spot) vary in size/distinctness; in some specimens anterior and lateral maculae are nearly absent while the posterior macula is reduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Freshwater lotic systems — rivers and streams (e.g., Rio Gurabo, Rio de Bayamon, Rio de la Plata, Rio Guanajibo, roadside streams) at elevations ranging from near sea level to ~4500 ft (Blue Mountains, Jamaica) and 3000–4000 ft (La Hotte, Haiti).
- **Biogeography:** Endemic to the **[[Greater Antilles]]** (Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Haiti, Dominican Republic, Cuba); part of the broader Western Hemisphere distribution of genus *Laccobius*, which ranges from southeastern Alaska/Canada through the United States into Mexico.
- **Zoogeographic significance:** Fills a previously documented distributional hiatus — the genus was thought absent from the southeastern U.S. (Gulf Coast, Florida) and the Antilles despite adequate dispersal capability, as noted by [[D'Orchymont 1942]] and supported by thorough regional surveys ([[Leng and Mutchler 1918]], [[Blatchley 1919]], [[Young 1954]]).
- **Proposed colonization pathway:** Likely arrived in the Greater Antilles via Mexico or Central America, where multiple congeneric *Laccobius* species occur, rather than via a Gulf Coast/Florida route.
- **Associated fauna/comparative taxa:** Comparable aquatic beetle genus *[[Chaetarthria]]* shares a similar abdominal hyaline structure, suggesting convergent aquatic adaptations within [[Hydrophilidae]].

## Key Findings
- Description of **[[Laccobius antillensis]]** as a new species, the first confirmed record of genus *Laccobius* in the Antilles.
- Resolves a long-standing biogeographic puzzle noted by D'Orchymont (1942): the apparent absence of *Laccobius* from Florida/Gulf Coast and the Antilles despite the genus's otherwise wide Western Hemisphere distribution and dispersal ability.
- Species-level diagnosis relies primarily on **aedeagal morphology** (broad median lobe from base to apex) to distinguish it from morphologically similar congeners: *[[Laccobius agilis]]*, *[[Laccobius ellipticus]]*, *[[Laccobius mexicanus]]*, and *[[Laccobius californicus]]* (all keying to couplet 5/5' in D'Orchymont's key).
- Extensive type series collected across multiple Puerto Rican localities (Juncos, Buenos Aguas, Comerio, San Lorenzo, Toro Negro, Bayamon, San German, Paso Seco, Magueyes) plus additional Antillean localities in Jamaica, Haiti, Dominican Republic, and Cuba — indicating a well-established, geographically widespread island population rather than a recent or marginal introduction.
- Type material deposited in multiple major institutions (USNM, MCZ, California Academy of Sciences, British Museum, Canadian National Collection, and others), supporting broad taxonomic accessibility.
- Reinforces the value of re-examining older, previously unstudied museum collections (e.g., Darlington's MCZ material) alongside new field surveys for resolving distributional gaps in aquatic insect taxa.
