## Executive Summary

This paper, authored by [[Leo Shapovalov]], presents a critical review of the systematics and classification status of the **fresh-water and anadromous fish fauna of California**, updating and correcting the 1931 distributional checklist by [[Barton Warren Evermann]] and [[Howard Walton Clark]]. Shapovalov recognizes **74 species across 11 families**, three fewer than Evermann and Clark, and argues that many nominal species — particularly within [[Salmonidae]], [[Cyprinidae]], and [[Cottidae]] — likely represent geographic races, subspecies, or hybrids rather than valid full species. The paper is largely a taxonomic and nomenclatural critique, emphasizing the effects of hatchery stocking, hybridization, and habitat alteration (logging, agriculture, damming) on the integrity and classifiability of native California fish populations.

A brief preceding section (likely from a companion paper on Western [[Pacific Ocean]] marine resources) discusses potential fisheries for mantis shrimp and oysters in Japanese, Chinese, and Malayan waters, and describes large schools of porpoises, tuna, and scombrids in Oriental tropical waters as bioindicators of abundant prey fish (e.g., sardines).

## Background & Description

**Marine section (secondary context):**
- Porpoise schools observed were exceptionally large — one school tracked over two hours was estimated at >6 miles long and 3+ miles wide, with hundreds of individuals visible leaping simultaneously at any given time.
- Whales (in the broad, colloquial sense) and smaller cetaceans (blackfish down to small porpoises) occur throughout the western Pacific.

**Freshwater section (primary focus):**
- No single morphological description is given for a species; instead, the paper catalogs **diagnostic difficulty** across genera:
  - [[Cottus]] (sculpins): 5 recognized species, but many populations show ambiguous, non-intergrading morphology depending on whether they inhabit isolated coastal streams (e.g., [[Waddell Creek]]) vs. large interconnected systems like the [[Sacramento River]] drainage.
  - [[Apocope]] spp. (*robusta*, *carringtoni*, *klamahensis*) and [[Siphateles]] spp. (*formosus*, *obesus*, *bicolor*) across the Sacramento, Lahontan, and Klamath systems are described as "very much alike," complicating species-level distinctions.
  - The genus [[Cheonda]] is flagged as based on a **generic hybrid type specimen** (*Cheonda cooperi*), undermining its taxonomic validity relative to [[Richardsonius]].
  - Evidence of natural hybridization is noted between *Richardsonius egregius* and *Siphateles obesus*, and *Richardsonius microdon* (Lake Tahoe) is suggested to be a synonym of *R. egregius*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** California's native fresh-water fish fauna is characterized as a **relict fauna of an arid region**, less diversified than eastern US, European, or Asian faunas. Many species are anadromous or salt-tolerant, reflecting adaptation to variable coastal drainage conditions.
- **Drainage basin structure:** Three major basins — [[Sacramento River]], [[Klamath River]], and [[Lahontan Basin]] — each independently support parallel coarse-scaled and fine-scaled forms of *Catostomus* (suckers), and parallel sibling forms within *Apocope* and *Siphateles*, suggesting repeated, basin-specific evolutionary divergence.
- **Anthropogenic impacts:** Logging, agriculture, and hydropower development are identified as primary drivers of habitat alteration affecting native fish distribution and abundance.
- **Introduced species impact:** Non-native fishes (e.g., *Micropterus salmoides* [Largemouth Bass], *Roccus lineatus* [Striped Bass], *Salmo trutta* [Brown Trout]) have altered environmental conditions and shifted native species' distribution and abundance, even though most do not hybridize with native fauna.
- **Marine trophic context:** In the western Pacific, large schools of porpoises, tuna, and scombrid fishes are presented as indirect evidence of abundant forage fish populations (e.g., sardines), illustrating a predator-abundance-as-bioindicator concept.

## Key Findings

- **Species count revision:** Shapovalov recognizes **74 native fresh-water/anadromous species in 11 families** in California, three fewer than the 77 listed by [[Evermann and Clark 1931]], reflecting ongoing taxonomic consolidation.
- **Salmonid complexity:** The *Salmo gairdnerii* (rainbow/steelhead) and *Salmo clarkii* (cutthroat) groups each comprise numerous nominal species (e.g., *S. stonei*, *S. gilberti*, *S. agua-bonita*, *S. whitei*, *S. rosei*, *S. evermanni*, *S. aquilarum*, *S. regalis*; and *S. henshawi*, *S. seleniris*, *S. pleuriticus*) that the author suspects are better treated as **subspecies or geographic races** rather than full species — a nomenclature vs. zoology distinction he stresses repeatedly.
- **Gairdnerii vs. irideus debate:** The paper resolves that *gairdnerii* has nomenclatural priority over *irideus* for the rainbow/steelhead complex, provided the original type specimen was indeed a trout (genus *Salmo*) and not a Pacific salmon (genus *Oncorhynchus*) — a matter of biological identification, not International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature jurisdiction.
- **Golden trout taxonomy:** Contra Evermann and Clark's three species, Shapovalov recognizes only two golden trout forms (*S. agua-bonita*, *S. whitei*), citing Brian Curtis's 1934 suggestion that *S. rooseveltii* is merely a color variant.
- **Cottidae mosaic problem:** Sculpin (*Cottus*) taxonomy in large systems like the Sacramento shows a non-intergrading "mosaic" pattern — geographically distant populations can look nearly identical while geographically close populations appear distinct, defying simple species/subspecies classification.
- **Cyprinodontidae uncertainty:** Only three *Cyprinodon* species (*macularius*, *nevadensis*, *diabolis*) are provisionally recognized, with additional described forms (*C. browni*, *C. californiensis*) of uncertain validity.
- **Family reclassification:** The former family [[Empetrichthyidae]] (containing *Empetrichthys merriami*) is no longer recognized as distinct and was folded into [[Cyprinodontidae]] by [[G.S. Myers]] (1931); associated type localities were corrected to Kings Spring and Pahrump Spring, Nevada.
- **Hybridization as a confounding factor:** Three hybridization pathways are identified as complicating classification: (1) hatchery propagation hybridization, (2) native × introduced hybridization in the wild, and (3) hybridization via anthropogenic waterway connections (e.g., mining diversions) — especially significant within *Salmo*.
- **Methodological recommendations:** The author calls for (1) critical literature review, (2) statistical analysis of large specimen samples, and (3) experimental genetic studies to resolve the subspecies/race/species status of California's fish fauna.
- **Marine bycatch/fisheries note:** Untapped commercial potential is noted for mantis shrimp (as a canned crab substitute) and modernized oyster/mollusk culture in Chinese, Filipino, and Malay coastal waters, contrasted with well-developed Japanese oyster aquaculture.
