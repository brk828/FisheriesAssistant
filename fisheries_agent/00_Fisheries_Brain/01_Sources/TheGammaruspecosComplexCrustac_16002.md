# Title

#crustacea #amphipoda #Gammaridae #taxonomy #morphology

## Executive Summary

This comparative taxonomic study examines seven populations of freshwater-to-miohaline amphipods from the [[Gammarus-Pecos Complex]] inhabiting springs and streams in areas of Texas and New Mexico once overlain by [[Permian Seas]]. The study includes three described species—[[Gammarus Pecos]], [[Gammarus Hyalelloides]], and [[Gammarus Desperatus]]—plus four undescribed populations. Using 20 [[Mann-Whitney U Tests]] on morphological characters, the author establishes relationships among populations and identifies at least two additional undescribed species. All members share diagnostic features including absence of calceoli, presence of C-setae on mandibular palps, and narrow oostegites. The populations inhabit calcium-sodium sulfatochloride waters with salinities ranging from fresh (0.123 ‰) to miohaline (5.85 ‰), making them euryhaline organisms. Geographic distribution is confined to within 90 km of the [[Pecos River]], representing the most southerly inland Gammarus species in North America.

## Background & Description

The [[Gammarus-Pecos Complex]] comprises amphipod populations discovered over approximately 30 years in areas once covered by Permian seas, including the [[Roswell Artesian Basin]] of New Mexico, the western [[West Texas Permian Basin]] (including [[Toyah Basin]]), and the [[Delaware Basin]]. The Permian marine sediments have been partially eroded by the [[Pecos River]], and all known populations occur within 90 km of this river in [[Chaves County]] and [[Eddy County]], New Mexico, and [[Jeff Davis County]], [[Reeves County]], and [[Pecos County]], Texas.

**Described Species:**
- **Gammarus pecos** (symbolized P): From [[Diamond Y Spring]] and [[Leon Creek]], Pecos Co., TX. Mean male length 13.52 mm.
- **Gammarus hyalelloides** (H): From [[Phantom Lake Spring]], Jeff Davis Co., TX. Mean male length 7.07 mm.
- **Gammarus desperatus** (D): From [[North Spring]], Roswell, Chaves Co., NM. Mean male length 12.98 mm.

**Undescribed Populations:**
- **Population S**: [[San Solomon Spring]], Balmorhea State Park, Toyahvale, Reeves Co., TX. Mean male length 12.76 mm.
- **Population C**: Large species in canal system fed by Phantom Lake Spring, co-occurring rarely with G. hyalelloides. Mean male length 10.71 mm.
- **Population M**: Small form from spring "3.5 miles west of Toyahvale" (possibly [[Phantom Cave Spring]]). Mean male length 8.79 mm.
- **Population E**: From pool near Carlsbad, Eddy Co., NM. Mean male length 9.85 mm.

**Diagnostic Morphological Features:**
All populations share: absence of calceoli on second antennae; C-setae on mandibular palps; narrow oostegites (brood lamellae); at least one spine at posterodistal corner of first peduncular article of antennule. Larger individuals have coxae 1-4 abundantly armed with long setae. The group is characterized as banded forms with elongate reniform eyes.

**Mandibular Palps:**
C-setae present, distinguishing them from most North American Gammarus except [[Gammarus Fasciatus]] and [[Gammarus Tigrinus]]. A-setae much longer than B-setae, with alpha-setae arranged in curved row. Proximal E-seta typically short, straight, and lanceolate. Variations among populations: P and S have C-setae not extending beyond D-spines; other populations have conspicuously longer C-setae. Populations E and D show clustering of C-setae in couplets or triplets.

**Brood Lamellae:**
Exceptionally narrow oostegites, more strap-like than in [[Gammarus Zaddachi]], the type species of proposed genus [[Lagunogammarus]]. First brood lamella widens very gradually from base rather than flaring abruptly. This feature led to discussion of possible assignment to Lagunogammarus, though author notes the G.-pecos complex is more homogeneous and differs from G. zaddachi in several respects.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Characteristics:**
The populations inhabit sulfatochloride waters derived from Permian marine sediments. Water chemistry varies from calcium sulfatochloride when dilute to sodium sulfatochloride in more concentrated states—classified as athalassohaline (not derived from direct seawater mixing).

**Salinity Ranges:**
Converted filtrable residue data (using factor 0.88) yield salinities:
- [[Lander Spring Brook]]: 3.87 ‰
- North Spring (G. desperatus): 5.02 ‰
- Diamond Y Spring (G. pecos): 5.85 ‰
- San Solomon Spring: 1.85 ‰
- Phantom Lake Spring system: 0.123-2.03 ‰

These values span fresh water (<0.5 ‰), oligohaline (0.5-3 ‰), and miohaline (3-8 ‰) categories, demonstrating euryhaline tolerance. The author characterizes these habitats as exhibiting poikilohalinity (variable salinity).

**Biogeographic Context:**
The G.-pecos group represents the most southerly inland Gammarus species in North America. The Permian Basin was connected narrowly to open sea through Delaware and Marfa basins via geosyncline crossing northern Mexico. However, linking these amphipods to Permian Sea ancestors appears improbable based on known gammarid evolutionary history.

**Potential Additional Populations:**
[[Lander Spring Brook]] in Chaves Co., NM, once supported what Noel (1954) termed "G. fasciatus" as the most abundant metazoan. This population is now extinct but may have been conspecific with G. desperatus or represented another distinct species. The source spring and brook were dry by summer 1967. Many other springs in the Toyah Basin remain unsearched and may harbor additional populations.

## Key Findings

**Statistical Analysis Results:**
Twenty Mann-Whitney U tests were applied to morphological attributes, with null hypothesis rejected at p<0.05. When reduced to 15 non-redundant tests, results suggest:

1. **Populations P and S are conspecific**: [[Gammarus Pecos]] from Diamond Y Spring and the San Solomon Spring population show 97% similarity across tests. Population S shows some affinities to the larger C animals in nearby Phantom Lake Spring system.

2. **Population C represents probable new species**: The large canal-dwelling form at Phantom Lake Spring is more closely related to M and H populations (87.9% and 72.1% similarity respectively) than to other populations, but sufficiently distinct to warrant species status.

3. **Gammarus desperatus (D) is unique**: Shows 58.8% similarity to other populations but demonstrates some relationship to population E. Characterized by shortest antenna-to-body-length ratio and unusual mandibular palp features.

4. **Population E probably represents new species**: From Carlsbad area, shows closest relationship to G. desperatus (58.8% similarity) but sufficiently distinct. Both D and E populations share tendency for C-setae clustering in couplets/triplets and unusually short B-setae.

5. **Populations M and H appear conspecific**: [[Gammarus Hyalelloides]] from Phantom Lake Spring and the small form from "3.5 miles west of Toyahvale" show high similarity. Both characterized by long first antennae relative to body length and reduced setation.

**Morphometric Patterns:**

*Antenna 1 characteristics:*
- Populations H, M, C have longest antennae relative to body length (ratios 0.633-0.721)
- Population D has shortest relative antenna length (0.474)
- Flagellum/peduncle ratios highest in M (2.78) and P (2.75)
- Accessory flagellum articles: P highest (5.08), H lowest (3.60)

*Antenna 2 characteristics:*
- Flagellum/peduncle ratios highest in D (0.896) and M (0.869)
- Peduncular setation highest in S (6.3 tufts) and P (5.2 tufts)
- Flagellar articles: P highest (15.4), E lowest (11.0)

*Coxal plate setation (plates 1-4):*
- Population D most heavily armed (104.4 total setae)
- Population E second (47.0 setae)
- Populations H and M least armed (19.25 and 22.83 setae)
- Anteroventral setae on coxa 1: most populations have >4 setae longer than 0.2 mm; H, M, C exceptions with 1-3 shorter setae

*Pereopod base armament:*
- Population D most heavily armed on bases 1-4 (192.2 setae) and bases 5-7 (83.0 structures)
- Populations H and M least armed
- Posterior margin setae on basis 7: most populations 0.25 mm; H, M, C only 0.11-0.17 mm

*Urosome characteristics:*
- Uronite spinulation highest in D (19.8) and E (17.6)
- Uropod 3 endopod/exopod ratio: S highest (0.846), H lowest (0.667)
- All populations have ratio >0.7, with basolateral telson spines present

**Taxonomic Implications:**
Study identifies at least two undescribed species (populations C and E), potentially raising North American freshwater/euryhaline Gammarus species to approximately 14. A sixth species may have existed in extinct Lander Spring Brook population. The complex represents a distinct monophyletic group within Gammarus, sharing unique combination of characters not found together in other North American species.

**Comparison with Other Species:**
Only [[Gammarus Fasciatus]] and [[Gammarus Tigrinus]] share the combination of C-setae on mandibular palps, elongate reniform eyes, banded coloration, and abundant coxal setation. However, these species have broader oostegites. The G.-pecos complex differs from proposed [[Lagunogammarus]] grouping in mandibular palp setation patterns and other features, warranting possible future assignment to separate subgenus or genus.
