# Distribution of Speckled Dace along Middle Sonoita Creek and Assessment of Effects of Proposed Railroad Abutment Removal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #rhinichthys

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by Marie Sullivan (Arizona Ecological Services Office, Phoenix) in March 1995, reviews the historical and recent distribution of [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) along [[Sonoita Creek]] in Santa Cruz County, Arizona. It assesses the potential effects of removing the most southern (downstream) [[Railroad Abutment Removal|railroad abutment]] within the [[Patagonia-Sonoita Creek Preserve]], a project proposed under the [[Partners For Wildlife Program]]. The removal is intended to benefit the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and riparian habitat by restoring natural channel meander. The Service concluded that the project will not have adverse long-term effects on the Sonoita Creek population of speckled dace, though short-term losses are possible. The final report is dated April 13, 1995, and the abutment was scheduled for removal at the end of April 1995.

## Background & Description

**Species status.** Speckled dace (*R. osculus*) in the [[Gila River Basin]] and [[Bill Williams River Basin]] were added to Category 2 of the list of [[Candidate Species]] for Federal listing on April 15, 1994. Candidate species receive no legal protection under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and Federal agencies are not required to evaluate impacts on them. The Service nonetheless initiated this evaluation to protect the whole Sonoita Creek ecosystem, including its native fish community.

**Taxonomy.** The taxonomy of the species is in flux. Discussions about dividing the species into several subspecies or species have continued for many years (Minckley 1985). The Gila and Bill Williams forms of the *R. osculus* complex were listed as a Category 2 candidate pending further taxonomic clarification.

**Project context.** The proposed action is removal of the southernmost railroad abutment on Sonoita Creek, within The Nature Conservancy's preserve. The two upstream abutments, where the historic railroad first crossed the creek, and the northern abutment of the downstream pair will not be removed. The stated objective is to increase aquatic and riparian habitat diversity by eliminating an artificial channel constriction and allowing the creek to meander naturally.

**Other native fish.** Sonoita Creek also supports [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarkii*). The Sonoita Creek speckled dace population is the only remaining one in the Santa Cruz River watershed. In the southern half of the Gila River basin, the species persists only in Sonoita Creek, [[Aravaipa Creek]], and Cave Creek in the Chiricahua Mountains (Minckley 1981).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Historical distribution and decline.** The species' range extends from the Columbia River basin to the Gila River basin (Lee et al. 1980; Minckley 1985). Its Gila and Bill Williams populations have declined through dewatering, diversion, dam construction, channel and watershed erosion, and the introduction of non-native species. Further water development and watershed damage threaten large parts of the remaining range.

**Sonoita Creek population history.**
- Records from Sonoita Creek date to 1928 (Burt 1928).
- The population was considered extirpated in 1959 (Miller 1961).
- A few individuals were found in 1963 (Minckley 1969).
- Following favorable rainfall, numbers increased in 1967 (Minckley 1969).
- Overall status is considered variable, with populations fluctuating with high runoff and streamflow (Minckley, pers. comm.).

**Hydrogeomorphic change.** In the late 1800s, Sonoita Creek flowed through a broad, marshy floodplain in multiple channels, with low velocity, meandering flow, and dense streamside herbaceous vegetation. Land use and possibly climate change have since channelized and entrenched the creek, particularly through the Preserve. Current channel conditions are a single entrenched channel with low habitat diversity.

**Habitat requirements.** Speckled dace is frequently associated with [[Riffle Habitat]], where it inhabits swift, turbulent water over gravel-cobble substrate (Minckley 1969). It persists under adverse conditions, including flooding and ponded, intermittent flow. Larger adults use low-velocity water with cover from undercut banks or debris (Minckley 1985). The species has a bimodal reproductive cycle, spawning in spring and late summer (John 1963). By contrast, the Gila topminnow prefers low to moderate velocity habitat in backwaters, cienegas, and pools.

**Flow regime.** Sonoita Creek near Cottonwood Spring is characterized by cottonwood-willow and cienega habitat with low-velocity perennial flow. The creek is intermittent from about one-half mile downstream of Cottonwood Spring to Patagonia, and perennial between Patagonia and [[Patagonia Lake]]. Below Patagonia Dam, it is perennial for about seven miles. Through the Preserve, it consists mainly of riffles and runs with few pools.

**Reach-by-reach habitat (Preserve).** Habitat surveys by Arizona Game and Fish Department (1994) and Gori (1993, 1994) recorded the species mainly in run habitat with gravel substrate, and in lower numbers in pools. Pools nonetheless provide important refugia at low flow. Surveys on November 11, 1994, by Service biologists and Nature Conservancy staff walked the creek from the road crossing upstream of the Preserve to Patagonia Lake. Key reaches included:
- **Northern boundary to first abutments:** low habitat diversity, mostly sandy runs, with a few short cobble riffle stretches.
- **First to second abutments:** about 30% cobble/gravel riffle habitat and 50% sand/gravel runs; large woody debris has formed several pools.
- **Below second abutments:** wide shallow runs and riffles with sand and gravel.
- **Below the Preserve:** the floodplain broadens, the channel braids, and bank vegetation is reduced, with increased sediment from Temporal Gulch and unstable banks. About 25–30% of this reach is suitable riffle habitat.
- **Alum Canyon to Squaw Gulch:** about 25% riffle habitat.
- **Below Squaw Gulch:** 30–50% riffle habitat of small cobbles or large rocks.
- **Highway 82 rest area to old USGS gaging station:** 25–30% cobble/gravel runs and riffles.
- **Above the gaging station to Patagonia Lake:** mostly shallow sandy runs, riffles under 25%, and heavily embedded substrate.

Overall, about 30% of Sonoita Creek between the northern Preserve boundary and Patagonia Lake provides suitable habitat, with most located between the northern railroad abutments and the old USGS gaging station, about five miles.

## Key Findings

1. **Distribution is concentrated upstream of the target abutment.** Most historical and recent records come from the vicinity of the upper abutments. The species has been recorded near the lower abutment, but numbers there were significantly lower than at other sites (Arizona Game and Fish Department 1994; Gori 1993, 1994).

2. **Limited spatial influence of the project.** Geomorphic effects are estimated to extend about 0.25–0.3 mile downstream and 0.1 mile upstream of the southern abutment (Paul Tashjian, pers. comm.). Little suitable habitat lies immediately downstream, and suitable habitat between the abutment and the Preserve boundary is limited to about 500 feet.

3. **No adverse long-term effect predicted.** The Service concludes that project implementation will not have adverse long-term effects on the Sonoita Creek population, because most records are upstream of the abutment and about 30% of the creek between Patagonia and the USGS gaging station remains suitable habitat.

4. **Short-term risks identified.**
   - Fish may be lost when abutment debris falls into the creek during detonation.
   - Fish may be lost from disturbance while material is removed from the active channel.
   - Recontouring and stabilization of the cut bank behind the abutment may temporarily increase turbidity and deposit material into the active floodplain, potentially causing losses.
   - No direct effects are expected from recontouring the floodplain outside the wetted channel.

5. **Incidental take limit.** The first amendment to the [[Intra-Service Biological Opinion]] (March 11, 1994) sets incidental take at 20 fish. The Service will monitor take of both the Gila topminnow and speckled dace during abutment removal.

6. **Benefits outweigh short-term losses.** Any short-term adverse effects to speckled dace are judged to be outweighed by long-term benefits to the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), [[Northern Gray Hawk]] (*Buteogallus anthracinus maximus*, a Category 2 candidate), and the downstream riparian ecosystem.

7. **Monitoring commitments.** The [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] will continue annual fish data collection under Section 6 of the Endangered Species Act. Effects on the hydrogeomorphology of Sonoita Creek will be monitored for 10 years after removal, and the abutment is scheduled for removal at the end of April 1995.

8. **Data limitations.** Early records (e.g., Chamberlain 1904; Miller 1928–59) lack site-specific locations, and some map notations may be off by several hundred feet. Habitat data from the early 1900s and the last 30 years are inconsistent, so temporal changes in distribution cannot be determined. The review aimed to assess whether abutment removal would adversely affect the species, not to pinpoint each historical population.
