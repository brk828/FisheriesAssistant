## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper but a set of administrative meeting notes from the **Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program (LCRMSCP) Biology Subcommittee**, convened May 20, 1997. The meeting's purpose was to brainstorm and rank **Interim Conservation Measures (ICMs)** for endangered and sensitive species of the Lower Colorado River (LCR) basin, split between an avian breakout group (focused on [[Willow Flycatcher]] and [[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]]) and a **fish and aquatic resources breakout group**. The fish-focused proposals center overwhelmingly on captive rearing, genetic management, and habitat restoration for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], with secondary attention to [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Desert Pupfish]], and broader "big river fishes" recovery planning. The top-ranked ICM by vote was a Lake Mohave razorback sucker juvenile rearing project (26 votes), underscoring the program's priority on captive propagation and reintroduction as the dominant conservation strategy for LCR native fishes in the mid-1990s.

## Background & Description

The LCRMSCP Biology Subcommittee met to identify near-term conservation actions ("ICMs") that could be funded ahead of a full multi-species habitat conservation plan. Twenty-two ICMs were proposed across two breakout groups; participants balloted to rank-order them, and the Steering Committee (meeting June 26, 1997 in Las Vegas) was tasked with selecting projects for FY1997-98 funding.

Key native fish taxa discussed:
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*, family Catostomidae) — an endangered, large-bodied sucker endemic to the Colorado River basin. Focus of the top-ranked ICM (Lake Mohave juvenile rearing) and the third-ranked ICM (Achii Hanyo rearing facility near Poston, AZ).
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*, family Cyprinidae) — a critically endangered cyprinid also targeted for rearing at the Achii Hanyo facility and for basic ecological/genetic research (movement tracking, broodstock genetic characterization) led by Dr. Paul Marsh (ASU).
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*) — proposed for a status survey of an isolated population below Davis Dam to assess population trends and risk.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) and **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, now Colorado Pikeminnow) — mentioned as beneficiaries of a proposed "Big River Fishes Management Plan" led by USFWS, though not the subject of specific ICMs in this meeting.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*, family Cyprinodontidae) — proposed for refugium development on National Wildlife Refuge lands.

Rearing and genetics infrastructure discussed included the **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery**, **Boulder City Fish Lab**, **Dexter National Fish Hatchery**, the **Achii Hanyo/CRIT ponds** near Poston, AZ, and **Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery** (AGFD), the latter proposed for renovation specifically to rear razorback sucker.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Lower Colorado River basin has experienced substantial historic habitat degradation, including channelization, reservoir impoundment (Lake Mohave, Lake Mead), and loss of backwater/wetland habitat critical to native fish recruitment. The **Imperial Division Restoration and Enhancement** proposal explicitly notes that ~50% of habitat in the reach between Imperial NWR and Imperial Dam degraded following flooding in 1983, prompting a dredging-based restoration plan (target cost up to $4 million) to restore historic channels, backwaters, and wetlands — habitat framed as benefiting **razorback sucker**, **[[Yuma Clapper Rail]]**, and other species jointly.

Native fish populations in the LCR are described as small, fragmented, and dependent on ongoing artificial propagation:
- Lake Mohave supports a wild razorback sucker population sustained through larval capture and captive rearing (the 1996-97 cohort was the subject of ICM #1).
- Genetic diversity preservation is an explicit management goal for razorback sucker, tracked via systematic sampling of reared cohorts and archived genetic samples at ASU, overseen by the **Native Fish Work Group (NFWG)**.
- Bonytail in the wild are rare enough that "tracking movements of wild bonytail" is treated as a distinct, valuable research activity.
- Flannelmouth sucker below Davis Dam represents an isolated population whose status was unknown at the time, warranting baseline surveys.

The document also situates fish conservation alongside co-occurring listed/sensitive taxa in the same riparian-aquatic corridor: **[[Willow Flycatcher]]**, **[[Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]]**, **[[Relict Leopard Frog]]**, and **[[Yuma Clapper Rail]]**, reflecting an ecosystem-level, multi-species approach to LCR conservation planning rather than a single-species fisheries focus.

**Fish and Aquatic Resources Breakout Session — Full Project List:**

1. **Complete rearing of 1996-97 razorback sucker cohort (Lake Mohave)** — Contact: Tom Burke (USBR); $100k; fish to be stocked by March 1998.
2. **Achii Hanyo Fish Rearing Facility (CRIT ponds)** — Rearing [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], potentially other big-river fishes and amphibians; Contact: C.O. Minckley (USFWS); $100k; facility ~1 month from operational, 10–12 months to rear a cohort to stockable size.
3. **Relict Leopard Frog Refugium** — non-fish, but proposed for co-location with fish rearing facilities (Boulder City Wetlands Park); $5–10k.
4. **Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) Refugium Development** — Contact: Kirk Young (AGFD), C.O. Minckley (USFWS); $80k; functional within 2 years.
5. **Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery Renovation (AGFD)** — Dedicated to razorback sucker rearing (bonytail not yet approved); Contact: Kirk Young (AGFD); $20k; functional within 1 year.
6. **Razorback sucker genetic sample analysis (ASU archive)** — Verifies genetic diversity preservation goals of the [[Native Fish Work Group]]; Contact: Tom Burke (USBR), Paul Marsh & W.L. Minckley (ASU); $75k.
7. **Relict Leopard Frog genetics/field survey** — non-fish; $15k genetics + $20k field survey.
8. **Imperial Division Restoration and Enhancement** — Dredging-based habitat restoration benefiting razorback sucker and clapper rail; Contact: Jim Romero (USBR); up to $4M total, with $580k AZ Water Protection Fund + Reclamation match already underway.
9. **Relict Leopard Frog Research and Monitoring (Lake Mead NRA)** — non-fish; $60k/yr for 3 years.
10. **Flannelmouth Sucker Status Surveys (below Davis Dam)** — Assessing an isolated population's status/trends; Contact: C.O. Minckley (USFWS), Mike Burrell (NDOW); $15–20k; 2-year study.
11. **Bonytail Basic Research (Lake Mohave & LCR)** — Database maintenance (Paul Marsh, ASU), genetic characterization of broodfish, wild movement tracking; Contact: C.O. Minckley (USFWS), Paul Marsh (ASU); $100k; ongoing/long-term.
12. **Complete Big River Fishes Management Plan (USFWS)** — Recovery implementation plan covering [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]]; Contact: C.O. Minckley (USFWS); funding described only as "moral support."

**Programmatic takeaways:**
- Captive propagation and genetic management (via ASU-held broodstock and sample archives) were treated as the primary near-term conservation tools for razorback sucker and bonytail, rather than in-situ habitat recovery alone.
- Habitat restoration projects (Imperial Division, Deer Island) were framed as multi-species benefits, serving both avian riparian species and native fish backwater habitat.
- Species status for flannelmouth sucker below Davis Dam was identified as a knowledge gap requiring baseline surveys before any protective strategy could be designed.
- A long-deferred **USFWS Big River Fishes Management Plan** (covering four federally listed/sensitive fish species) was flagged as an important but unfunded planning gap for the broader [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]].
