# Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study: Consultant Agreement and Request for Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a consultant services agreement and the accompanying Request for Proposal (RFP) from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], acting for the Arizona Game and Fish Commission. It commissions a study of the decline of two endemic, threatened Gila River basin fishes, [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and of their interactions with the introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*). The agreement is cost-reimbursable, not to exceed $58,350. The RFP describes a one-year study, with a possible four-year continuation dependent on funding.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** Spikedace and loach minnow are monotypic genera endemic to streams of the [[Gila River Basin]]. Both are listed in Group 3 of the Arizona Game and Fish Commission's list of Threatened Native Wildlife and have been proposed for federal listing as Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Morphology and habitat:** The loach minnow is darter-like and occupies gravel/cobble bottoms of shallow riffles. The spikedace is a midwater fish of turbulent and eddying currents where riffles flow into pools.
- **Decline:** Both species have lost substantial geographic range and population size over the past century. Stream modification from human activity is implicated, as is interaction with introduced fishes, especially the red shiner.
- **Current distribution:**
  - Spikedace (Arizona): upper Verde River, [[Aravaipa Creek]] and Eagle Creek. (New Mexico): a few scattered localities in the Gila basin above Redrock.
  - Loach minnow (Arizona): Aravaipa Creek, the upper White River, and the Blue and San Francisco rivers above their confluence. It persists in the San Francisco River drainage of New Mexico, unlike the spikedace.
- **Existing knowledge:** Spikedace biology is relatively well studied compared with other southwestern desert fishes. Autecological work comes from Aravaipa Creek, where the species lives with five other native fishes and no exotics. Diet data are available for both species from Aravaipa Creek, and New Mexico studies cover sites where the species lives with exotics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native–exotic relationship:** Native fish abundance and diversity in the Gila basin are inversely correlated with those of exotic species. Whether exotics directly cause native declines remains unknown; both groups may be responding independently to altered habitats.
- **Red shiner spread:** The red shiner was introduced to the Colorado River as a bait fish (Hubbs 1954). It spread through the Gila basin and is now almost mutually exclusive in range with the two endemic species, which persist mainly in upstream areas.
- **Replacement mechanisms:** Replacement of lotic-adapted natives by similarly adapted exotics is believed to involve less obvious, more complex mechanisms. It occurs in relatively little-altered physical habitats, at varying rates. Very rapid replacement of spikedace by red shiner has been documented at some sites, yet the two species have coexisted at one site for at least 15 years.
- **Point and diffuse threats:** Land management practices and established exotics are diffuse threats. Planned impoundments lie within much of the species' range and would likely cause local extirpation of large populations if built.

## Key Findings

**Study objectives (six):**
1. Determine whether loach minnow and/or spikedace interact directly with red shiner and other exotics, and characterize the interaction (predation, competition, interference, etc.).
2. Describe and quantify interspecific interactions (dietary overlap, predation mortality, reproductive success, habitat displacement).
3. Quantify physical and chemical habitat requirements and preferences of all life stages of native species and exotics.
4. Describe spatial and temporal patterns of any interactions found.
5. Relate findings to the historic decline and build evidence-supported hypotheses on its mechanisms.
6. Suggest management methods to curtail or reverse the decline of native fish faunas.

**Scope and methods:**
- Methods are left to investigators' discretion. Descriptive and experimental approaches are expected, covering the entire Gila basin range of spikedace, loach minnow and red shiner.
- Field and/or laboratory work is acceptable. Designs should quantify community/habitat interactions and use sound statistical design.

**Contract terms:**
- **Scope:** The consultant provides original copies of all reports, maps, drawings and data on request, without restriction.
- **Start:** Work begins within 15 days of a Notice to Proceed.
- **Payment:** Cost-reimbursable, not to exceed $58,350, paid monthly against itemized invoices approved by the Department.
- **Standards:** Services follow generally accepted principles and practices. The Department decides factual and quality disputes. Arbitration applies to monetary claims of $15,000 or less, after administrative review.
- **Termination:** The Department may terminate or abandon work on not less than seven days' written notice.
- **Liquidated damages:** $50.00 per calendar day for untimely completion of plan development phases.
- **Equipment:** Items with a depreciable life of one year or more and a value of $500 or more become Department property.
- **Assignment:** Subletting or transferring the agreement requires written Department consent, because the award rests on the personal qualifications of the consultant's staff.
- **Records:** Records are open to State inspection and audit for five years after completion.
- **Signatories:** Ronald E. Barr (Assistant Vice President for Research, consultant) and Duane L. Shroufe (Secretary to the Commission).

**Schedule and deliverables:**
- One-year study, with a possible four-year continuation contingent on funding.
- Quarterly progress reports due 1 December, 1 March and 1 June, with a final report due 30 September 1987.
- The start date is printed as "1 September 1966," which appears to be a typographical error, since the report schedule and cited literature (1985–1986) point to 1986.
- The contractor supplies all equipment, supplies and manpower. The Department provides technical liaison Dean A. Hendrickson.

**Literature base:** The RFP cites roughly 25 references, including Minckley (1973, *Fishes of Arizona*), Propst et al. (1985a, b), Barber et al. (1970), Schreiber and Minckley (1982), Hubbs (1954) and Meffe (1985), which together form the basis for the study's justification.
