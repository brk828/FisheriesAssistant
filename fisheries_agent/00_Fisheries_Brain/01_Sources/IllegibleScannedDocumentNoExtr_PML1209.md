# Hart's Rivulus Records in California and Differential Distribution of Striped Mullet

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #mugilidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a two-part Notes section from a fisheries journal. The first note reports the first confirmed record of [[Hart's Rivulus]] (*Rivulus harti*) in California, a South American species collected in 1967 and 1968 from a ditch near a closed [[Tropical Fish Farm]] on the eastern side of the [[Salton Sea]]. The author attributes the introduction to the farm, where the species had been reared. The second note examines the distribution and life history of the [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) across the [[Colorado River]] system and the adjacent Gulf of California. Size-class and collection data suggest slow upstream migration from marine waters, but the capture of postlarvae far upstream indicates that freshwater spawning may occur, which calls into question a salinity requirement for successful spawning.

## Background & Description

### Note 1: Addition of Hart's Rivulus to the California Fauna

- **Species:** [[Rivulus Harti]], a South American cyprinodont.
- **Collection site:** A small ditch below the [[Hot Mineral Spa]] adjacent to Del Rancho El Sargent, a tropical fish farm in Imperial County.
- **Associated species in the ditch:** [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), mollies (*Poecilia latipinna* and *P. mexicana mexicana*), [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), and [[Tilapia Mossambica]].
- **Prior record:** *Tilapia mossambica* was first discovered at the same ditch in 1964 (St. Amant, 1966).
- **Identification:** Specimens were identified by [[Carl L. Hubbs]].

### Note 2: Differential Distribution of the Striped Mullet

- **Taxon:** [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*), family Mugilidae.
- **Range:** Coastal waters and estuaries of tropical and subtropical zones, roughly 42°N to 42°S (Thomson, 1963).
- **Life history:** Commonly described as [[Catadromy]] (catadromous), though reports conflict (Breder and Rosen, 1966).
- **Study context:** Part of a study by [[Donald W. Johnson]] on the effects of seasonal factors, reproduction, migration, and salinity on the histology of endocrine organs (interrenal organs and corpuscles of Stannius).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hart's Rivulus record.** The ditch lies on the eastern margin of the Salton Sea and receives outflow from a hot mineral spring. The assemblage contains both native-range desert species (desert pupfish) and introduced or aquarium-trade species (mosquitofish, mollies, tilapia). The author notes that the fish farm was the probable introduction pathway, since R. harti had been reared there. The farm is now closed, so the source of the population is no longer active. The record shows how the [[Aquarium Trade]] can introduce exotic fishes into California inland waters.

**Striped mullet distribution.** Collections were made in 1967 and 1968 at about two-month intervals from:
- Hunters Hole, a slough near Gadsden, Arizona
- Below Imperial Dam, about 110 and 150 miles upstream from the Gulf of California (as stated in source)
- El Golfo de Santa Clara, Sonora, Mexico, near the Colorado River delta (marine collections)

Gradients in fish size with distance from the Gulf provide the basis for inferring migration patterns. Freshwater and estuarine conditions along this gradient (salinity, seasonal flow) shape the spawning and migration ecology of the species.

## Key Findings

### Hart's Rivulus (Rivulus harti)

1. **First California record.** Specimens collected 13 January 1967 represent the first confirmed record of *R. harti* in California.
2. **Specimen data:**
   - The first specimen (13 January 1967) measured 35 mm.
   - Additional specimens collected 13 November 1968 included one adult male and two adult females measuring 42 to 45 mm (standard length as reported).
   - The author also collected adults and juveniles during inspection trips in 1969.
3. **Likely introduction source.** Because *R. harti* had been reared at the tropical fish farm, the author considers the farm the probable source. The farm is now closed.
4. **Co-occurring species.** The ditch also held mosquitofish, two mollies, desert pupfish, and tilapia, indicating a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fishes.

### Striped Mullet (Mugil cephalus)

1. **Size-class structure.** Five distinct size groups (standard length) were identified:
   - (i) 7–13 cm, Gulf collections, July to September
   - (ii) 17–20 cm, Gulf collections
   - (iii) 21–28 cm, Gulf and Hunters Hole collections
   - (iv) 29–37 cm, Gulf, Hunters Hole, and Imperial Dam collections (source OCR reads "29-87 em")
   - (v) 39–46 cm, Gulf and Imperial Dam collections
2. **Mean sizes.** Colorado River fish (n = 86) had a mean standard length of 33.1 ± 1.4 cm (95% confidence limits). El Golfo fish (n = 756) had a mean of 22.4 ± 1.7 cm.
3. **Age inference.** Using growth rates reviewed by Thomson (1963), the size groups were estimated at approximately age 1 (group i), ages 1–2 (group ii), age 2 (group iii), ages 3–4 (group iv), and ages 4–5 (group v).
4. **Upstream migration.** The progressive increase in size class with distance from the Gulf indicates an extremely slow upstream migration. The author concludes these findings support a marine origin for the striped mullet of the Colorado River.
5. **Postlarval record.** On 19 March 1966, 31 postlarval striped mullet (A.S.U. No. 2293) were collected below [[Morales Dam]], about 120 miles upstream. Mean standard length was 32.4 ± 1.1 mm (range 28–40 mm). Their size implies spawning in December and January.
6. **Salinity implication.** Because postlarvae were found far from salt water, the author argues they probably originated from freshwater spawning. This is the first report of postlarvae at this distance upstream at this life stage. The authors conclude these findings call into question any salinity requirement for successful striped mullet spawning.
7. **Gear selectivity.** The authors state that gear selectivity was not a factor in the absence of smaller size classes in river samples. Larger size classes were difficult to collect in Gulf waters.
