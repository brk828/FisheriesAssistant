# Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area: Draft Interdisciplinary Activity Plan / Environmental Assessment (BLM, 1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #riparian-management

## Executive Summary

This August 1993 draft plan from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Safford District sets out a 15-year management plan for the Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area (NCA). Congress designated the NCA in the Arizona Desert Wilderness Act of 1990 (Public Law 101-628) to conserve, protect and enhance its riparian areas and associated aquatic, wildlife, cultural, scenic and other resources. The plan weighs five alternatives: a Preferred Alternative, Alternative One (environmentally preferred, most restrictive), Alternative Two, Alternative Three (No Action, current management) and Alternative Four. Three issues drove the planning: road access, livestock grazing and recreation facility development. Fish-related actions include razorback sucker monitoring, off-channel pools, native fish reintroduction analysis, macroinvertebrate monitoring and, in Alternative One only, a fish barrier and predator catfish control. The document is mostly a management and policy plan; its fish content is a management component rather than new field data.

## Background & Description

- The NCA lies about 180 miles east of Phoenix and 12 miles east of Safford, in Graham and Greenlee counties. It holds about 21,767 acres of public land (the letter cites 20,767) and 1,720 acres of private land within its boundary; the plan's own tables use 1,411 acres of private land, 309 of them belonging to the City of Safford.
- It contains 15 miles of [[Bonita Creek]] and 23 miles of the [[Gila River]], including the "Gila Box" canyon. [[Eagle Creek]] and the [[San Francisco River]] also flow in, giving four perennial waterways in a desert setting.
- The plan replaces the 1985 Gila Box Coordinated Resource Management Plan and conforms to the Safford District Resource Management Plan.
- A seven-member Advisory Committee and the planning team met 19 times between October 1991 and November 1992. Scoping meetings were held in Safford, Clifton and Tucson, and the public comment period is 90 days.
- The NCA is divided into three management zones: Zone A (hands-off, hand tools only), Zone B (localized actions) and Zone C (more active management and mechanical tools).
- Zone acreages for the Preferred Alternative and Alternative Two are A 989, B 12,222 and C 8,556. Alternative One has A 1,272, B 19,447 and C 1,048. Alternative Four has A 447, B 10,599 and C 10,721. Alternative Three does not use zones.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Basin and Range terrain between the Gila and Peloncillo Mountains, with local rocks mostly volcanic (16–25 million years old) and Gila Conglomerate basin fill. Precipitation averages about 12 inches (range 10–16), with high-intensity summer storms.
- **Hydrology:** Winter discharge comes from snowmelt and frontal storms; summer discharge comes from local rainfall. Bonita Creek is mostly perennial, with four small intermittent reaches. The Safford infiltration gallery, about four miles above the creek mouth, yields an average 3.1 million gallons per day. The creek is designated "Unique Waters" under Arizona water quality rules.
- **Floods:** Major floods in 1983 and 1993 stripped riparian vegetation and damaged the City of Safford's collection system.
- **Riparian vegetation:** Key species are cottonwood ([[Populus fremontii]]), willow, [[Arizona Sycamore]], Arizona walnut, net-leaf hackberry and velvet ash, plus mesquite bosque. Many grasses and shrubs are listed as monitoring key species.
- **Fish and aquatic life:** Bonita Creek supports five native fish species, and the Gila River supports game fish such as catfish. The plan cites 15 native fishes of the Gila River for public outreach. It names 10 native fishes for reintroduction analysis: razorback sucker, bonytail chub, Colorado squawfish, roundtail chub, flannelmouth sucker, woundfin minnow, spikedace, loach minnow, desert pupfish and Gila topminnow. Large pools are identified as a limiting factor for [[Razorback Sucker]] in Bonita Creek. Non-native fishes frequently invade lower Bonita Creek.
- **Listed and candidate species:** The listed species are peregrine falcon, bald eagle and razorback sucker. Candidates include [[Gila Chub]], southwest toad, lowland leopard frog, ferruginous pygmy owl, willow flycatcher, goshawk and bats. Eagle Creek also holds the candidate round-tail chub.
- **Constraints:** Public Law 101-628, the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act (the Gila River, Bonita Creek and the San Francisco River are "eligible"), the Endangered Species Act, Visual Resource Management Class II, the Clean Water Act and state water standards. The NCA is withdrawn from mineral entry except valid existing rights, which here means only the Dorothy B claims.

## Key Findings

**Objectives and goals**
- BLM will manage the riparian area to reach proper functioning condition between large floods.
- Bonita Creek riparian density targets within seven years after a major flood, in trees/acre (sapling targets in parentheses): segment 1, 30 (37); segment 2, 12 (17); segment 3, 19 (29); segment 4, 17 (18); segment 5, 6 (10). Gila River targets are pending Ecological Site Inventories (Gila River by 1 January 1995, Bonita Creek by 1 January 1996).
- Water quality: meet state standards for fecal coliform (single-sample maximum 800 colonies) and dissolved oxygen (6.0 mg/L), with quarterly sampling.
- Recreation: 15–35 access points or sites. Cultural resources: at least five scientific studies and one interpretive site per historic theme.
- Species population objectives include 183 breeding pairs of peregrine falcon (Southwest regional goal) and 200 bighorn sheep. For many species the objectives are still to be determined.

**Actions common to all alternatives (fish and aquatic)**
- Apply for instream flow water rights on the Gila River, San Francisco River and Bonita Creek.
- Inventory aquatic habitat and fisheries, repeating the 1979 Minckley et al. methods with the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), and monitor the fishery twice during the plan.
- Monitor [[Razorback Sucker]] annually, build off-channel pools, evaluate native fish reintroduction under an interagency agreement (BLM, AGFD, US Fish and Wildlife Service), and run a public outreach program. Reintroductions must not impair Safford's water supply.

- Alternative One alone builds a permanent fish barrier near the Bonita Creek mouth, evaluates Bonita Creek low flow for native fish, and controls predator catfish in the Gila River. It also removes livestock from riparian areas.
- The Preferred Alternative builds 3 campgrounds and 8 picnic areas. It designates 3.2 miles of public riparian road and 4.0 miles of administrative road, and closes 7 road sites. Off-channel pools number about 5–10 (Alternatives Two, Three, Four and Preferred) or 2 (Alternative One). Livestock use is capped at 20–40% utilization or excluded, mostly in winter.
- Alternative Three keeps present practices (livestock at an average 40% utilization, driving the full length of Bonita Creek). Alternatives One, Two, Four and the Preferred meet the riparian objective; the No Action alternative does not do so in its entirety.
- The impact comparison rates fish effects as positive and moderate to high for the action alternatives (the table is partly illegible). It shows negative effects for the No Action alternative.
- Other findings: the Dorothy B claims will get a validity exam, Eagle Creek lower-12-mile lands are recommended for acquisition from willing sellers, and an ethnoecology study of the Gila River arm is planned. A moratorium on new river-running permits is lifted, with use levels of 12, 20 and 30 boat parties per day for Zones A, B and C. The Wild and Scenic suitability report is due by December 1994 and the final plan in 1994.
