#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 study by Donald C. Tarter examines the reproductive biology of the [[Western Blacknose Dace]] (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*) in Doe Run, a spring-fed stream in Meade County, Kentucky. Based on examination of 948 gonads and sex determination of 1,426 individuals collected between October 1965 and September 1966, the study establishes the timing and physiological markers of the spawning season, quantifies fecundity in relation to body length, documents sexual dimorphism, and tests for departures from a 1:1 sex ratio. The spawning period was determined to extend from late May to late July, coinciding with peak gonadosomatic ratios and maximum ova diameters. This work is part of a broader doctoral dissertation on the species' biology and situates Doe Run findings against comparable data from other blacknose dace subspecies (eastern and southern) studied by Raney (1940), Schwartz (1958), and Noble (1964).

## Background & Description

- **Size at sexual maturity**: Smallest tuberculate male = 83 mm total length (Age Group I); smallest female with distinct yolky/white egg classes = 82 mm total length (Age Group I). Most individuals mature by the end of the second year or during the third year of life.
- **Sexual dimorphism (tuberculate males)**:
  - Nuptial tubercles present on: head, operculum, anal fin, pelvic fins, occasionally pectoral fins, and occasionally scales (dorsal and ventral).
  - Nuptial pads on pectoral fins were light orange.
  - Tubercles first observed late March/early April; persisted until late July or mid-August.
  - Tubercles absent from caudal fin (unlike eastern subspecies per Raney 1940).
- **Coloration**: Older males (Age Groups II and III) displayed a bright lateral rust-red stripe from the back of the operculum to the base of the caudal fin, present year-round but brighter during breeding season. This contrasts with the seasonal/suffused red-pink coloration reported in other populations (e.g., [[Rhinichthys Atratulus]] per Trautman 1957).
- **Fin morphology (sex-distinguishing traits, verified via t-test at 0.05 level)**:
  - Anal fin height: greater in females than males.
  - Pectoral and pelvic fin length: longer in males than females.
- **Female breeding morphology**: Older/larger females became slightly humpbacked during the breeding season; no dichromatism observed (unlike southern subspecies, which shows a yellow-green belly tint per Schwartz 1958).
- **Ova characteristics**:
  - Average diameter progression: October (0.57 mm) → gradual increase through winter to March (0.66 mm) → April (0.84 mm) → May (0.99 mm) → mid-May peak (1.00 mm) → rapid decline from July to a minimum of 0.52 mm by mid-September.
  - Largest single ovum recorded: 1.80 mm (mid-June) — larger than maxima reported for other dace populations (0.8–1.6 mm).
  - Two distinct egg types observed in females during breeding season: large/yolky and small/white.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Doe Run, a torrent spring stream near Ekron, Meade County, Kentucky, characterized by segmented gradients (27–40+ ft/mile), sand/gravel/bedrock substrates, and three historic man-made dams restricting upstream fish movement for ~150 years.
- **Vegetation associations**: Dace fry were found in quiet water near beds of *Vaucheria geminata* and *Nasturtium officinale* (watercress), sometimes in mixed schools with fry of [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*).
- **Trophic role**: Functions as a secondary consumer in the stream's trophic structure (per Krumholz 1967), exerting influence on other biota due to its distribution and abundance.
- **Spawning habitat**: Believed to occur under overhanging banks and ledges, based on observed aggregations of active large males and females in these microhabitats during the breeding season; no egg masses or nests were located.
- **Thermal regime**: Water temperatures during the spawning period (late May–late July) ranged from 11.6°C to 19.5°C, with an average of 14.7°C, reflecting the thermal constancy typical of spring-fed headwater streams.
- **Co-occurring species**: Native fish community in the headwater region limited to three species (Minckley 1963; Krumholz 1967); dace fry co-mingled with [[White Sucker]] fry.

## Key Findings

- **Gonadosomatic index (GSI) peak**: For individuals ≥80 mm total length, average GSI reached maxima in May for both males (0.44%) and females (9.76%), indicating synchronized ripening between sexes. Maximum individual ratios recorded: 1.19% (male), 18.69% (female).
- **Spawning season**: Extended from late May until late July, based on convergent evidence from GSI trends, ova diameter trends, first appearance of young-of-year fry (mid-June), and field stripping of milt/ripe eggs. Breeding condition persisted longer in males (into August) than females.
- **Fecundity**:
  - Strong positive correlation (r = 0.94) between total length and egg number.
  - Regression equation: **Y = -6983.05 + 103.99X** (Y = egg count, X = total length in mm).
  - Total egg counts across 161 females ranged from 725 to 9,142; overall average = 2,674 eggs/female (430,466 total eggs counted).
  - This fecundity range substantially exceeds prior reports for eastern blacknose dace (428–1,116 eggs; Traver 1929) and other western blacknose dace populations (400–2,500 eggs; Noble 1964), attributed to larger body size of Doe Run females.
- **Sex ratio**: Chi-square testing (0.05 significance level) on 1,426 dace showed a significant deviation from 1:1 only in Age Group I (χ² = 6.42, p = 0.01–0.05); no significant deviation in Age Groups 0, II, or when all groups were pooled (χ² = 2.88, p = 0.05–0.10).
- **Age at maturity**: Both sexes typically reach sexual maturity by the end of the second year or during the third year — earlier than the third-year maturity reported for Iowa populations by [[Noble 1964]], but consistent with expectations from [[Hubbs And Cooper 1936]].
- **Photoperiod/temperature hypothesis**: Author suggests photoperiod (rather than temperature alone) likely plays the dominant role in triggering gonadal maturation and spawning in Doe Run, given the relative thermal constancy of the spring-fed system, aligning with broader literature on piscine sexual cycles (e.g., [[Harrington]], [[Hazard And Eddy 1951]]).
- **Comparative taxonomy note**: Morphological and chromatic differences in nuptial tubercle distribution and lateral stripe coloration distinguish the Doe Run population from eastern ([[Rhinichthys Atratulus Atratulus]]) and southern ([[Rhinichthys Atratulus Obtusus]]) blacknose dace subspecies described in prior literature.
