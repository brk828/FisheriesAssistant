# The Hydrographic and Faunal Independence of Certain Isolated Deepwater Seas in Eastern Asia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Macrouridae #deep-sea-ecology #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This 1929 paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] examines the relationship between hydrographic conditions and deep-sea fish fauna in two contrasting isolated marine basins of the Western Pacific: the [[Sea of Japan]] and the [[Sulu Sea]] complex. The study demonstrates how physical barriers (shallow straits) create distinct temperature regimes at depth, which in turn produce isolated and endemic deep-sea fish faunas. The [[Sea of Japan]] excludes macrouroid fishes entirely due to shallow rim depths (<180 meters), while the [[Sulu Sea]] maintains unusually warm bottom temperatures (approximately 10°C at depths >550 meters) and harbors a rich, distinctive deep-sea fauna with numerous endemic species. This work establishes the [[Philippine Subregion]] as a distinct zoogeographic unit coordinate with the [[East Indian]] region.

## Background & Description

The paper addresses the zoogeography of isolated deepwater basins, comparing them to other isolated faunal regions such as islands and mountain tops. Two contrasting systems are examined:

**[[Sea of Japan]]**: Nearly enclosed by land or straits nowhere deeper than 180 meters. Receives warm [[Kuro Shiwo]] current through [[Chosen Strait]] at the south and cold subarctic waters through northern straits. The cold water sinks beneath warm surface layers. [[Charles Henry Gilbert]] reported tropical pelagic animals occurring over subarctic bottom fauna during the 1906 [[Albatross]] cruise.

**[[Sulu Sea]] Complex**: Bounded by [[Luzon]], [[Samar]], [[Mindanao]], [[Sulu Archipelago]], [[Borneo]], and [[Palawan]]. Connected to surrounding waters ([[South China Sea]], [[Pacific Ocean]], [[Celebes Sea]]) by numerous straits with depths between 350-550 meters. Includes [[Mindoro Sea]], [[Mindanao Sea]], [[Surigao Sea]], [[Visayan Sea]], [[Tanon Strait]], and [[Ragay Gulf]].

The [[Macrouridae]] (macrouroid fishes) serve as the primary indicator group, being ordinarily the most abundant deep-sea fishes in both species and individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Temperature Regimes:**

- **[[Sulu Sea]] proper**: Bottom temperature drops to 10°C at approximately 550 meters depth, then remains constantly slightly below 10°C to depths greater than 2000 meters
- **Associated inland waters** ([[Mindanao Sea]], [[Surigao Sea]], [[Visayan Sea]]): Bottom temperature remains nearly constant at depths >550 meters but averages approximately 11.5°C, almost 2° warmer than [[Sulu Sea]] proper
- **[[Tanon Strait]]** (between [[Negros]] and [[Cebu]]) and **[[Ragay Gulf]]** (southern [[Luzon]]): Much higher bottom temperatures, approximately 17°C at depths between 400-700 meters
- **Surrounding open waters** ([[South China Sea]], [[Pacific Ocean]], [[Celebes Sea]], [[Gulf of Tomini]], [[Strait of Makassar]], [[Flores Sea]]): Show continued decline in bottom temperature with increasing depth, with temperatures dropping below 7°C at depths >950 meters

**Hydrographic Isolation Mechanism:**

The [[Sulu Sea]] is isolated from cold deep water because:
1. All surrounding surface waters remain tropical (>25°C all winter)
2. No cold surface water is available to sink into depths
3. Surrounding waters drop below 10°C at depths between 350-550 meters
4. Connecting channels warm this water to above 10°C at their maximum depths, preventing cold water intrusion

**Bathymetric Barriers:**

The [[Sea of Japan]]'s rim everywhere lies above the bathymetric range of [[Macrouridae]], preventing colonization despite successful trawling at appropriate depths. Macrouroids were abundant in the [[Okhotsk Sea]] and along the eastern shore of the Japanese chain but completely absent from the [[Sea of Japan]].

## Key Findings

**[[Sea of Japan]] Fauna:**

- Complete absence of [[Macrouridae]] despite numerous successful trawl hauls at appropriate depths
- Macrouroids abundant in adjacent [[Okhotsk Sea]] and along eastern Japanese coast
- Exclusion attributed to shallow rim (<180 meters) lying above the family's bathymetric range
- Tropical pelagic animals occur over subarctic bottom fauna

**[[Philippine Subregion]] Definition:**

[[Gilbert]] and [[Hubbs]] established the [[Philippine Subregion]] as distinct from the [[East Indian]] region, including waters around central [[Philippine Islands]], north of [[Tawi Tawi]] group, south of west-central [[Luzon]], excluding the [[Pacific Ocean]] along east coast of [[Luzon]].

**Endemic [[Macrouridae]] Species:**

The [[Philippine Subregion]] contains numerous endemic or distinctive forms:
- [[Bathygadus sulcatus]]
- [[Gadomus magnifilis]]
- [[Coryphaenoides semiscaber]] (representing East Indian C. tydemani)
- Three [[Coryphaenoides]] species known only from type specimens in [[Sulu Sea]]: C. paradoxus, C. dubius, C. camurus
- [[Coelorhynchus]] species: C. quincunciatus, C. thompsoni, C. velifer, C. macrolepis, C. carinifer, C. acutirostris
- [[Hymenocephalus gracilis]] (closely related to Hawaiian H. tenuis)
- [[Hymenocephalus longiceps]]
- [[Hymenocephalus striatissimus torvus]] (representing H. s. striatissimus of [[Japan]], [[Formosa]], eastern [[Luzon]], and H. s. aeger of [[East Indies]])
- [[Malacocephalus luzonensis]]
- [[Ventrifossa macronemus]] (most nearly related to V. misakia of [[Japan]] and eastern [[Luzon]])
- [[Ventrifossa lucifer]]
- [[Lionurus decimalis]]

**Absent East Indian Species:**

Despite intensive dredging over months, the [[Albatross]] failed to obtain certain [[East Indies]] species in the [[Philippine Subregion]]:
- [[Bathygadus filamentosus]], B. entomelas
- [[Gadomus multifilis]]
- Multiple [[Coryphaenoides]] species: C. asprellus, C. tydemani, C. heyningeni, C. aequatoris, C. orthogrammus
- [[Coelorhynchus platoirhynchus]], C. acantholepis
- [[Hymenocephalus striatissimus aeger]], H. grimaldii
- [[Malacocephalus laevis]]
- Multiple [[Lionurus]] species: L. infranudis, L. evides, L. vittatus, L. richardii, L. parvipes
- [[Mataeocephalus adustus]]
- [[Cetonurus robustus]]

**Temperature-Depth Relationships:**

- [[Sulu Sea]] depths >400 fathoms (732 meters) vary little from 50°F (10°C)
- Temperature nowhere falls below 49°F (9.4°C) even at depths >1000 fathoms (1829 meters)
- Northern [[China Sea]] off southern [[Luzon]] shows normal temperature decline with depth
- This northern district shares few endemic species with southern [[Philippine Subregion]], containing mainly [[Coelorhynchus velifer]], C. macrolepis, C. carinifer

**Evolutionary Implications:**

The paper concludes that the [[Philippine Subregion]]'s peculiar species have probably evolved within this region of unusual environmental conditions (warm temperatures at great depths). The evolutionary process was likely aided or hastened by isolation in this partially enclosed sea. The shallow-water fauna does not show such distinctness, indicating the isolation effect is depth-dependent.
