# Arizona Fish Commission Report for Southern Territory 1883-1884

#ichthyology #aquaculture #Cyprinidae #historical-fisheries #territorial-administration

## Executive Summary

This historical report from Commissioner Richard Rule to Governor F.A. Tritle documents the activities and challenges of the [[Arizona Fish Commission]] for the southern territory during 1883-1884. The commission focused primarily on [[Carp]] distribution and propagation, receiving approximately 400 carp from the [[U.S. Fish Commission]] over the reporting period. Despite operational challenges including delayed shipments, inadequate pond preparation by recipients, and lack of proper fish care, several successful propagation sites were established. The report also documents an unusual discovery of unknown fish species emerging from artesian wells in [[Sulphur Spring Valley]] and advocates for expanded commission authority to include game protection and stronger penalties for illegal fishing methods.

## Background & Description

The [[Arizona Fish Commission]] was established approximately four years prior to this 1885 report. Commissioner Rule served as fish commissioner for southern Arizona, responsible for receiving, distributing, and monitoring [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio) obtained from the [[U.S. Fish Commission]]. The commission operated across three territorial sections, each with its own commissioner.

The primary species under propagation was [[Carp]], distributed to private pond owners throughout southern Arizona. Recipients were provided with Prof. Hessel's pamphlet on carp care along with verbal instructions regarding feeding, pond requirements, and fish husbandry practices.

An unknown fish species was discovered emerging from artesian wells at the Sanderson & Lyle property in [[Sulphur Spring Valley]]. These fish measured 1 to 2 inches in length, displayed color variations from dark brown to light yellow or golden tinge, possessed perfect eyes (unlike typical cave-dwelling fish), and demonstrated remarkable reproductive capacity. Females contained large numbers of eggs relative to body size, and spawning was confirmed through pond examination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report describes several aquatic habitats across southern Arizona:

**Successful Carp Sites:**
- [[Ash Canyon]] in the southeast [[Huachucas Mountains]]
- Igo's location on the [[Barbacomare River]]
- Sanderson & Lyle ponds in [[Sulphur Spring Valley]]

**Native Fish Populations:**
The [[Gila River]] and [[San Pedro River]] supported populations of "Gila or San Pedro salmon" (likely referring to native large-bodied fish), described as fine table fish weighing 5 to 25 pounds. The [[Barbacomare River]] also contained smaller fish species.

**Threats to Fish Populations:**
- Natural predators including snakes, frogs, and turtles in poorly maintained ponds
- Illegal use of explosives (dynamite, giant powder) for fish harvest
- Flooding events washing away fish from inadequate enclosures
- Inadequate pond construction and maintenance by recipients

**Water Sources:**
Artesian wells in [[Sulphur Spring Valley]] reached depths of 48 to 58 feet, with sufficient water pressure to eject fish to the surface. These wells continuously produced the unknown fish species over a two-year period.

## Key Findings

**Carp Distribution and Success:**
- Approximately 400 carp distributed since Commissioner Rule's appointment
- One dozen applications remained on file at time of reporting
- Successful sites showed remarkable growth: fish initially 6 inches at stocking reached 10-25 inches and 0.25-6 pounds after 16 months
- Females expected to spawn in the coming summer (1885)
- Most recipients failed to provide adequate pond conditions despite representations to the contrary

**Operational Challenges:**
- Delayed and unreliable shipments from [[U.S. Fish Commission]]
- Expected 1884 carp shipment had not arrived by December 15, with no prospect until late spring due to frozen eastern carp ponds
- Recipients consistently misrepresented pond readiness and suitability
- Fish placed in inadequate springs and streams without feeding or predator protection
- High mortality from neglect and environmental factors

**Artesian Well Fish Discovery:**
- Unknown species emerged from wells 48-58 feet deep
- Initial emergence: 3 fish from 48-foot well, larger numbers from 58-foot well within half-hour periods
- Size range: 1 to 2 inches maximum length
- Color variations: dark brown, light brown, light yellow/golden
- Perfect eye development (unlike typical subterranean fish)
- Pronounced sexual dimorphism: females more "paunched" than males
- High fecundity relative to body size
- Continued reproduction over two-year observation period
- Population remained "countless" despite repeated pond cleaning and fish removal
- Size did not materially increase after surface emergence

**Recommendations:**
- Appropriation of $175-200 per territorial section for commission-controlled propagation ponds
- Amendment of Fish Commission Act to include wild game protection authority
- Enhanced penalties including imprisonment for possession of out-of-season game
- Territorial prison sentence for use of explosives in fishing
- Stricter verification of pond suitability before fish distribution

**Game and Native Fish Conservation Concerns:**
- Rapid depletion of antelope and deer populations from out-of-season hunting
- Week-old fawns observed for sale in markets
- Quail and other game birds similarly affected
- Large native fish (5-25 pounds) in [[Gila River]] and [[San Pedro River]] killed by illegal explosive use
- Existing game laws deemed "practically inoperative"

**Cost Efficiency:**
Commissioner Rule reported zero territorial expenditure for southern Arizona fish commission operations over four years despite handling large numbers of fish, with no funds from the general Fish Commission appropriation spent in the southern section.
