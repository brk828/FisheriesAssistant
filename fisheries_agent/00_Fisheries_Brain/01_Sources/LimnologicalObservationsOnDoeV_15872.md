# Limnological Observations on Doe Valley Lake during Impoundment

#aquatic-ecology #limnology #oscillatoria

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[W. L. Minckley]] and Donald R. Tindall reports water-chemistry and thermal observations from [[Doe Valley Lake]], a reservoir of about 500 surface acres in the steep-walled valley of [[Doe Run]], Meade County, Kentucky, sampled during its filling in August and September 1961. The study covers temperature, dissolved oxygen, alkalinity, pH and turbidity, plus a brief note on plankton. The main result is a strong, transitory [[Metalimnetic Oxygen Maximum]] (up to about 210% of saturation) driven by the alga [[Oscillatoria Aghardi]], which had disappeared by late September. The authors describe this as the first known report of a phenomenon of this magnitude in impounded waters. No fish data are presented.

## Background & Description

- **Site:** Doe Valley Lake was impounded on July 9, 1961, to form a lake of about 500 surface acres, within the narrow valley of Doe Run. The area was described earlier by Minckley (1963, *Wildlife Monographs*).
- **Basin preparation:** Woody vegetation was removed and burned, and forbs and grasses from fallow fields were chopped into the soil. Nutrients were therefore highly available to the filling lake, and the inflowing stream was also relatively rich.
- **Filling history:** The cofferdam washed out on July 16–17 after flooding from heavy rains. The lake was re-impounded on July 20 and filled slowly through summer and autumn.
- **Sampling:** Work was done on August 4 and September 26, 1961. Stations were run upstream to downstream from about 11:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., with samples fixed in the field where possible. Stations used the "Creek Miles" from Minckley's earlier Doe Run study and were sampled in the channel to capture a maximum vertical profile.
- **Methods:** Chemical analyses followed those of the earlier Doe Run study.
- **Funding:** Support came from a U.S. Atomic Energy Commission contract (University of Louisville, principal investigator Louis A. Krumholz) and an [[National Science Foundation]] grant (principal investigator Arland T. Hotchkiss).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal structure and inflow.** On August 4 the lake extended to Mile 6.0, and the inflow from Doe Run was detectable by temperature, alkalinity and dissolved oxygen as far as Mile 6.7. A strong [[Thermal Stratification|metalimnion]] was present at upper stations and thickened toward the dam. At Mile 7.7, just upstream of the dam, the temperature profile was relatively smooth from surface to bottom. In September the water had reached Mile 5.1, and the metalimnion was weaker and deeper toward the dam. Inflow velocity was about 45 ft/sec in both periods.

**Oxygen dynamics.** Surface oxygen was about 100% of saturation in July. Heterograde profiles developed in August and were then largely erased by hypolimnetic deoxygenation in September. The [[Hypolimnion]] held less than 0.5 mg/l of oxygen in August.

**Water chemistry.** Alkalinity (as mg/l CaCO₃) was higher at depth than at the surface in both months. Turbidity was greater in deeper water and at the upper end of the lake, but it never exceeded 90 mg/l and was generally below 20 mg/l.

**Biota.** In August the lake was green, slightly brown-stained in shallow upstream areas, and dominated by *Oscillatoria aghardi*. Crustaceans were abundant near the surface but rare at depth. Other plankton were sparse, mainly diatoms (*Melosira* sp.) and rotifers.

**Interpretation.** The authors link the August oxygen pulse to cool floodwaters from July, later overridden by warmer creek water. Doe Run also carried slightly supersaturated, nutrient-rich water into the metalimnion. *Oscillatoria aghardi* is known from other lakes as a cause of metalimnetic oxygen maxima (citing [[Eberly]]). In September, the declining plankton population and organic matter carried downward by density currents created high oxygen demand that erased the maxima.

## Key Findings

1. **Filling and inflow:** The lake was impounded July 9, 1961, breached July 16–17, and refilled from July 20. By August 4 the water had reached Mile 6.0, and by September 26 it had reached Mile 5.1.
2. **Metalimnetic oxygen maximum:** Oxygen at Mile 6.7 peaked at 14.2 mg/l at 23.5 °C (about 170% of saturation), and at Mile 7.6 reached about 210% of saturation in August. The authors report this as the first known occurrence of such a magnitude in impounded waters.
3. **Hypolimnetic depletion:** Oxygen in the hypolimnion fell below 0.5 mg/l in August.
4. **Transience:** By September the positive heterograde profiles had been largely obliterated. Any remaining supersaturation added no more than 1.5 mg/l above surface values.
5. **Alkalinity:**
   - August surface: 142–160 mg/l, mean 153 (5 samples)
   - August bottom: 184–223 mg/l, mean 207 (6 samples)
   - September surface: 152–169 mg/l, mean 162 (6 samples)
   - September deep water: 170–240 mg/l, mean 217 (20 samples, within 1 m of bottom)
6. **pH:** Upstream stations were fairly uniform in August at 7.8–7.9. At Mile 7.6 the pH was 8.7 at the surface and 7.2 at the bottom. In September the pH was 7.8–8.4 in the upper 3 m and dropped abruptly to about 7.0–7.4 below that.
7. **Turbidity:** Never above 90 mg/l, generally below 20 mg/l, and higher in deep water.
8. **Plankton:** *Oscillatoria aghardi* dominated in August, with abundant surface crustaceans and sparse diatoms (*Melosira*) and rotifers.
9. **Comparison:** [[Tom Wallace Lake]], a small impoundment in Jefferson County, Kentucky, showed smaller metalimnetic oxygen maxima (about 125% of saturation) in an earlier study by Cole (1954). Those were also transitory. The authors suggest such events may be more common than the literature shows.
