# Grand Lake, Shadow Mountain Reservoir and Granby Reservoir Fish Management Investigations 1953

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #salmonidae #reservoir-ecology #colorado

## Executive Summary

This 1953 fisheries management report documents comprehensive investigations of three connected water bodies in Colorado: [[Granby Reservoir]], [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]], and [[Grand Lake]]. The study employed creel census, rough fish control operations, limnological sampling, food habit analysis, and tagging studies to assess fish populations and angling success. All three water bodies experienced declining catch-per-man-hour (CPMH) compared to 1952. [[Rainbow Trout]] dominated catches in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] (87%) and [[Grand Lake]] (90%), while [[Native Trout]] were most prevalent in [[Granby Reservoir]] (40%). Rough fish control operations removed 10,253 pounds of suckers through trapping, seining, and netting. Tagged fish studies in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] showed first-year returns of 12.0% for rainbow trout and 8.7% for brown trout.

## Background & Description

The three water bodies form an interconnected system in Colorado managed by the Colorado Fish Management Division. [[Granby Reservoir]] is the largest and deepest (sampled to 100 feet), followed by [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] (sampled to 30 feet), which connects to [[Grand Lake]] via a channel. The reservoirs are part of the [[Colorado River]] system, with the [[North Fork Colorado River]] feeding into [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]].

Fish species present include game fish ([[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Native Trout]], [[Kokanee Salmon]], [[Montana Grayling]]) and rough fish (three sucker species: [[Western Longnose Sucker]] *Catostomus catostomus*, [[Western White Sucker]] *Catostomus commersoni*, [[Northern Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] *Pantosteus delphinus*). Historical stocking included 486,000 native trout in 1950 and 9,848 kokanee salmon in 1951-1952 in [[Granby Reservoir]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Limnology**: Plankton sampling occurred weekly from May 28 to October 23, 1953, with Secchi disc readings and temperature profiles. [[Granby Reservoir]] showed entomostracan counts ranging from 2.9 to 38.3 organisms per liter (increased from 1952's 3.2-12.0 range). Surface temperatures ranged 43-66°F with thermoclines at 18-27 feet depth during July-August. [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] exhibited high turbidity (Secchi disc 3-6 feet) with zooplankton counts of 2.9-154.6 organisms per liter and heavy phytoplankton blooms reaching 1.7 cubic centimeters per liter in August. [[Grand Lake]] maintained clearer water (Secchi disc up to 18 feet) with zooplankton counts of 1.9-37.5 organisms per liter.

**Habitat**: River flow in the [[Colorado River]] below [[Granby Dam]] was regulated from 20 to 75 cubic feet per second. The [[North Fork Colorado River]] experienced spring runoff reaching 600 cubic feet per second by June 14, with normal summer flow below 100 cfs.

**Food Webs**: Plankton constituted the primary diet across all trout species in all three water bodies. In [[Granby Reservoir]], 15 rainbow, 11 native, 12 brown, and 2 brook trout stomachs showed plankton dominance with aquatic insects, terrestrial insects, and fish as secondary items. [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] rainbow trout showed 54% plankton by volume (38% by occurrence), with boat-caught fish consuming more plankton and shore-caught fish more aquatic insects. One [[Montana Grayling]] contained 100% aquatic insects.

## Key Findings

**Creel Census Results**:

- **[[Granby Reservoir]]**: 598 trout caught in 4,169 hours (CPMH = 0.14, down 0.04 from 1952). Species composition: Native trout 40%, Rainbow trout 35%, Brown trout 17%, Brook trout 8%, Kokanee salmon <1%. Average length 13.3 inches; native trout averaged 14.2 inches (up from 11.9 inches in 1952). One tagged fish recovered. 772 fishermen made 866 trips; 94% Colorado residents, 96% caught ≤3 fish per season, 90% made only one trip.

- **[[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]]**: 942 fish caught (including 9 tagged) in 3,317 hours (CPMH = 0.28, down 0.10 from 1952). Species composition: Rainbow trout 87%, Brook and Brown trout 12%, Native trout and Grayling 1%. Average size 11.0 inches. 767 fishermen checked; 73% resident men. Boat fishing (85% of effort) with artificial lures (96% of boat anglers) yielded CPMH of 0.28; shore fishing with combination lures achieved 0.38 CPMH.

- **[[Grand Lake]]**: 185 trout in 590 hours (CPMH = 0.31, down from 0.58 in 1952). Species composition: Rainbow trout 90%, Brown trout 7%, Brook trout 2%, Native trout and Grayling 1%. Average size 11.1 inches. 162 fishermen checked; 83% fished from boats, 94% used artificial lures. No throwbacks recorded.

**Angler Demographics**: Denver County provided 56-63% of resident anglers across all waters, with Jefferson and Arapahoe counties contributing 12-19%. Non-residents primarily from Kansas, Oklahoma, and Texas (65-69% of out-of-state anglers). Average 2.81 fishermen per car. Peak fishing activity occurred 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM, capturing 75-85% of daily effort.

**Rough Fish Control**: Total removal of 10,253 pounds of suckers through multiple methods:
- **Trapping**: [[North Fork Colorado River]] trap (April 20 - October) captured 2,492 pounds of suckers; major run May 7 through June. Trap washed out May 28 during peak runoff. Game fish passage: 105 rainbow trout, 51 brown trout, 2 grayling.
- **[[Colorado River]] trap** (below [[Granby Dam]]): 491 suckers (586 pounds) removed. Upstream migrants tagged: 74 rainbow trout (mainly April-June), 56 brown trout (mainly August-October). Eight fish were previously tagged in 1951.
- **Seining**: Five hauls in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] (August) using 800-900 foot seines captured 7,175 pounds of suckers; fish transported to [[Bellvue Hatchery]] for trout food.
- **Gill-netting**: [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]]: 272 suckers, 8 trout; [[Grand Lake]]: 110 suckers, 3 trout; [[Willow Creek Reservoir]]: 5 suckers, 3 trout.
- **Rotenone treatment**: Shoal areas on west shore of [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] poisoned with derris (5% rotenone); successfully killed sucker fry without harming trout.

**Tagging Studies**: 
- 500 rainbow and 500 brown trout tagged annually 1951-1953 in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]].
- **1951 plant cumulative returns**: Rainbow 20.2% (11.4% year 1, 8.8% year 2, 0.0% year 3); Brown 18.0% (10.0%, 7.8%, 0.2%).
- **1952 plant two-year returns**: Rainbow 18.0% (15.4% year 1, 1.6% year 2); Brown 14.6% (13.6%, 1.0%).
- **1953 plant first-year returns**: Rainbow 12.0%, Brown 8.7%.
- **Migration patterns**: Rainbow trout showed greater movement (75% caught in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]], 11.7% each in [[Granby Reservoir]] and spillway, 1.6% in pump canal). Brown trout less migratory (82.7% in [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]]).
- **Growth**: 1952-planted rainbow trout grew 4.8 inches in one year; brown trout 2.7 inches. 1953 plant showed 0.1 inch (rainbow) and 1.3 inch (brown) growth by season end.
- **[[Colorado River]] trap**: 10 tagged fish reported caught; only one recorded moving downstream, suggesting high upstream retention or low survival.

**Stocking vs. Catch Analysis**: 
- [[Granby Reservoir]]: High native trout returns (40% of catch, 12.0-15.9 inch size class) attributed to 1950 stocking of 486,000 fry. Rainbow trout catch believed from natural reproduction (no plants until 1952). [[Kokanee Salmon]] from 1951-1952 plants (9,848 fish) showing excellent growth; one spawning male measured 15.0 inches, 1.25 pounds in November.
- [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]]: Shift from fry to creel-size stocking (1948) reduced total numbers planted, not meeting fishing pressure demand. July 1953 catch showed 7.0-8.9 inch size class from May-June plant of 13,000 creel-size rainbow.
- [[Grand Lake]]: Declining success attributed to poor fry survival and growth. 1947 plant of 28,000 native trout fry yielded only one 11.4-inch fish in 1953 census.

**Management Recommendations**: 
1. Change [[Grand Lake]] stocking to creel-size fish; supplement [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] creel-size plants with fry and fingerlings.
2. Continue rough fish control: add traps on [[Stillwater Creek]] and [[Colorado River]]; repeat rotenone treatments; continue seining and gill-netting.
3. Construct hatchery troughs at [[Grand Lake]] state property for native trout egg handling.
4. Bag limit restrictions would require reduction to ≤3 fish for effectiveness (96-97% of anglers already catch ≤3 fish).
5. Concentrate 1954 creel census on weekends and holidays; reduce sampling frequency for plankton (4-5 times per season) while maintaining regular Secchi disc readings.

**Spillway Census**: [[Shadow Mountain Reservoir]] spillway (May-June): 50 fishermen caught 99 fish (CPMH = 0.37, down from 0.61 in 1952). Species: Rainbow 56%, Brook 26%, Brown 15%, Native 3%.
