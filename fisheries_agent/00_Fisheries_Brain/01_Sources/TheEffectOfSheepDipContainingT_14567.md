# The Effect of Sheep-Dip Containing BHC on the Fauna of a Small Stream

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #ecotoxicology

## Executive Summary
This 1961 field study by H.B.N. Hynes documents the ecological aftermath of an accidental BHC (benzene hexachloride) sheep-dip discharge into a small trout stream on the Isle of Man. Approximately four weeks after contamination, the stream showed a sharply zonated faunal disruption: below the discharge point, [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) populations collapsed from abundant to nearly absent, and a broad suite of aquatic invertebrates—including [[Gammarus]] shrimp, stoneflies, mayflies, caddisflies, beetles, and most Diptera—were eliminated or severely reduced. Conversely, filamentous algae (*Cladophora*), oligochaete worms, and two chironomid species proliferated markedly below the dip, likely due to release from predation and grazing pressure. The study draws close parallels between BHC effects and previously documented DDT impacts on North American streams, with the key finding that BHC—unlike DDT—also decimated Hydracarina (water mites).

## Background & Description
The paper does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of fish; observational data on *Salmo trutta* were limited to relative abundance and size class:

- **Species observed:** [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta* L.)
- **Population structure above dip:** "Large numbers of small trout" — indicating a dense population of small-bodied (likely juvenile or stream-resident) individuals typical of a small headwater trout stream.
- **Population structure below dip:** Only a single very small specimen observed ~300 yards downstream of the sheep-dip installation; otherwise, trout were effectively absent from the affected reach.
- No morphological abnormalities, lesions, or pathological signs were reported in the surviving specimen — the paper focuses on presence/absence rather than physiological or morphological damage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Habitat context:**
- Small stream (≤2 yards wide, ≤1 foot deep), flowing from 350 ft elevation over ~2.25 miles to the sea, near the village of Arbory, Isle of Man.
- Substrate: silty-stony-gravelly for most of its length except the final 600 yards (silted).
- Normally supports a "typical trout-stream invertebrate fauna" and a large population of small [[Brown Trout]].
- Part of an ongoing long-term study site for competition between [[Gammarus]] species (freshwater amphipod shrimp).

**Trophic/ecological role of trout:**
- Positioned as a top invertivorous predator in this trout-stream food web, dependent on the aquatic insect community (mayflies, caddisflies, dipterans, etc.) as a food base.
- The author hypothesizes that trout disappearance downstream was **not necessarily direct toxic mortality**, but potentially **behavioral avoidance/emigration** from reaches where invertebrate prey had been eliminated by the insecticide ("The fish may not have been killed, but may have moved out of reaches where their normal insect food was no longer available").

**Contamination event:**
- BHC-based sheep-dip normally discharged into a closed ditch beside the stream without incident in previous years.
- In early June 1960, a different farmer's use of the installation apparently resulted in spent dip entering the stream directly, introducing an unknown quantity of BHC roughly four weeks before the survey.

**Zonation of effects (sampled at 8 stations spanning 510 yds above to 1,910 yds below the dip):**
- Upstream (control) zone: normal trout-stream fauna, thin algal film only, abundant Gammarus, mayflies (*Baetis rhodani*), stoneflies (*Leuctra* spp.), caddisflies.
- Immediately below dip (~0–300 yds): dense *Cladophora* algal blanket, near-total elimination of trout, Gammarus, stoneflies, mayflies, caddis larvae, beetles, most Diptera, and Hydracarina (mites).
- Mid-downstream (~300–1,600 yds): gradual recovery of some taxa (e.g., *Helophorus*, *Latelmis volkmari*, *Limnius tuberculatus*, *Simulium latipes*, *S. ornatum*).
- Far downstream (~1,900+ yds): stream bed and algae return to pre-contamination "normal" appearance.

## Key Findings
1. **Trout population crash:** Brown trout (*Salmo trutta*) were abundant above the BHC discharge point but virtually absent below it, with only one small specimen recorded downstream — the most direct fish-relevant finding in the paper.
2. **Likely mechanism — trophic collapse rather than direct toxicity to fish:** The author suggests fish disappearance may reflect emigration due to loss of invertebrate prey (mayflies, caddisflies, Gammarus, Diptera) rather than direct lethal toxicity of BHC to fish themselves.
3. **Selective elimination of invertebrate predators and grazers:** [[Gammarus Duebeni]], *G. pulex*, stoneflies (*Leuctra hippopus*, *L. inermis*), mayflies, caddis larvae, beetles, most Diptera, and Hydracarina were eliminated or drastically reduced below the discharge — removing key predators of Chironomidae and grazers of algae.
4. **Compensatory blooms:** Release from predation/grazing pressure likely drove large increases in oligochaete worms (e.g., *Tubifex tubifex*, *Nais pseudobtusa*), two chironomid taxa (*Cricotopus bicinctus*, *Cardiocladius capucinus*), and filamentous algae (*Cladophora*, *Spirogyra*) in the affected reach.
5. **Unaffected taxa:** Flatworms (*Crenobia alpina*, *Polycelis felina*), Oligochaeta, and Mollusca (*Potamopyrgus jenkinsi*, *Limnaea pereger*, *Ancylastrum fluviatile*) appeared unaffected by BHC exposure — though some mollusc increases were attributed to independent longer-term range expansion, not the spill.
6. **BHC vs. DDT comparison:** BHC effects on stream invertebrate communities closely paralleled documented DDT effects in North American streams (elimination of caddisflies, mayflies, stoneflies, most Diptera; increases in Chironomidae; no effect on Annelida/Mollusca). The key divergence: **BHC severely affected Hydracarina (mites), whereas DDT does not affect mites** at comparable concentrations.
7. **Broader management implication:** Because insecticide treatments selectively remove key invertebrate predators (e.g., [[Gammarus]], *Rhyacophila*, *Dicranota*) while favoring resilient/fast-recolonizing prey taxa like Chironomidae and *Simulium*, attempts to control *Simulium* (blackfly) via insecticide dosing in the absence of continuous treatment could paradoxically **increase** *Simulium* populations post-treatment — reinforcing concerns raised in [[Hynes 1960]] regarding DDT use for *Simulium* control in Africa.
8. **Expected recovery trajectory:** Given the short reach affected and absence of further discharge, rapid recolonization was anticipated, potentially aided by the survival of unhatched insect eggs present in the stream substrate at the time of the spill (many stream insects overwinter/oversummer in egg form in June).
