# Review of Potential Forage Fish Introductions for Lake Mead

#ichthyology #marine-biology #forage-fish

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department report (Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration, Dingell-Johnson Project F-14-R-14, May 1986, by [[Richard Dreyer]] and William R. Persons) reviews the literature on 20 candidate [[Forage Fish]] species for [[Lake Mead]]. It was prepared because [[Striped Bass]] growth and condition had declined after the collapse of [[Threadfin Shad]] populations. The Colorado River Wildlife Council's Striped Bass Committee nevertheless recommended **no forage fish introductions**, citing Lake Mead's low productivity and potential impacts on existing species, including [[Endangered Fishes]].

## Background & Description

**Context.** [[Striped Bass]] were introduced into Lake Mead in 1969, when [[Threadfin Shad]] were abundant. Striped bass preyed heavily on shad. Shad numbers declined during the 1970s, and striped bass subsequently showed poor growth. Adult striped bass were emaciated and apparently declined in numbers in the early 1980s. The Striped Bass Committee discussed the need for a new forage fish in 1983, and agency biologists conducted a literature review. Arizona's contribution was presented at the committee meeting in Bullhead City, Arizona, in 1983.

**Scope.** The report profiles each candidate species under the same headings: size, distribution, habitat, biology (spawning, fecundity, maturity, diet, predators), and importance as forage. The species are:

| Family | Species (common name) |
|---|---|
| Atherinidae | [[Mississippi Silverside]], [[Brook Silverside]] |
| Catostomidae | [[Lake Chubsucker]] |
| Clupeidae | [[Blueback Herring]], [[Alewife]], [[American Shad]], [[Gizzard Shad]] |
| Coregonidae | [[Longjaw Cisco]], [[Lake Herring]], [[Deepwater Cisco]], [[Kiyi]], [[Bonneville Cisco]] |
| Osmeridae | [[Pond Smelt]], [[Rainbow Smelt]] |
| Cyprinidae | [[Lake Chub]], [[Utah Chub]], [[Silver Chub]], [[Emerald Shiner]], [[Spottail Shiner]], [[Red Shiner]] |

The bibliography contains roughly 80 references, mostly North American fisheries literature from 1956 to 1983.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Mead productivity:** The committee judged Lake Mead to be low in productivity, which limits the capacity to support an added forage species.
- **Endangered fishes:** Several candidates could affect native or endangered fishes in the [[Colorado River]] system. [[Rainbow Smelt]] is carnivorous and was flagged as a possible detriment to endangered fishes there.
- **Temperature sensitivity:** [[Alewife]] are unable to acclimate to rapidly fluctuating temperatures, and die-offs are documented in the literature. Lake Mead's water temperature fluctuations were noted as a concern.
- **Competition:** Most species are reported to compete with existing forage and game fishes, including [[Largemouth Bass]], sunfishes, and centrarchids.
- **Predation:** Many candidates are preyed on by existing piscivores such as [[Largemouth Bass]], walleye, and northern pike, which affects their value as forage in Lake Mead.
- **Habitat and depth:** Several candidates are pelagic or live at depths unlikely to be available to pelagic striped bass, or they occupy littoral zones where centrarchids forage.

## Key Findings

**Overall recommendation:** No forage fish introductions for Lake Mead. The committee recommended against introducing any of the candidates.

**Species-level assessments:**

- **[[Mississippi Silverside]]:** A surface-dwelling, littoral fish that spawns over vegetation in shallow water. It is a good forage fish for centrarchids, but it is probably not available to pelagic striped bass and would be rarely found below 2 feet in depth.
- **[[Brook Silverside]]:** Good forage for sunfish and bass, but possibly unavailable to pelagic striped bass. It may compete with existing forage and game fish.
- **[[Lake Chubsucker]]:** Could serve as forage for large striped bass but may compete with other species.
- **[[Blueback Herring]]:** Could be forage for large striped bass, but may grow too large to serve as forage for many game fishes.
- **[[Alewife]]:** Could be an excellent forage fish for striped bass, but massive die-offs occur, and the species cannot acclimate to rapidly fluctuating temperatures. Lake Mead's water temperature regime is a particular risk.
- **[[American Shad]]:** Young-of-year could serve as striped bass forage, but they grow too large quickly. Massive die-offs occur from spawning stress and abrupt temperature changes.
- **[[Gizzard Shad]]:** Young shad provide good forage, but growth is rapid and they soon become too large. Adults could provide forage for large striped bass.
- **Ciscoes ([[Longjaw Cisco]], [[Lake Herring]], [[Deepwater Cisco]], [[Kiyi]], [[Bonneville Cisco]]):** Generally good forage as larvae and young-of-year. Adults could feed large striped bass, but are generally of limited use in Lake Mead.
- **[[Pond Smelt]] and [[Rainbow Smelt]]:** Smelt could forage for striped bass, but rainbow smelt preys on fish eggs and young-of-year game fish and may harm endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **[[Lake Chub]], [[Utah Chub]], [[Silver Chub]]:** Lake chub are good forage but may compete with other fishes. Utah chub tend to overpopulate. Silver chub could be suitable forage for large striped bass but may compete with other forage fish.
- **[[Emerald Shiner]]:** Good forage, but likely only seasonally available to striped bass. It could supplement forage for largemouth bass and other centrarchids in littoral areas.
- **[[Spottail Shiner]]:** Good forage, but may not be available to striped bass because of vertical distribution. It could provide additional forage for centrarchids.
- **[[Red Shiner]]:** Good forage, but unsuitable for striped bass because of limited seasonal availability. The report notes that Lake Mead's bays and coves could produce more red shiners if spawning and escape habitat were enhanced. Doing so could also boost existing [[Golden Shiner]] and fathead minnow populations, providing more forage for largemouth bass and other centrarchids.

**Conclusion:** Each candidate has some forage value, but the combination of Lake Mead's low productivity, temperature constraints, seasonal and vertical availability problems, and risks to endangered fishes led the Striped Bass Committee to recommend no introductions.
