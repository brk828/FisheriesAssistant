# First Canadian Record of the Black Buffalo (*Ictiobus niger*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Ictiobus

## Executive Summary
This note documents the **first confirmed Canadian record** of the Black Buffalo (*[[Ictiobus niger]]*), a large, deep-bodied catostomid sucker, captured on 20 June 1978 in the Canadian waters of [[Lake Erie]] (Pigeon Bay, west of Point Pelee, Ontario). The specimen was taken incidentally in a commercial trap net operated by F.W. Krause and Sons Fisheries, Leamington, Ontario. Initially misidentified as an unidentified sucker (possibly *[[Ictiobus cyprinellus]]*), detailed morphological and cranial examination by G. R. Smith and R. M. Bailey (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology) confirmed the specimen's identity as *I. niger*, extending the known range of the species into Canadian waters and adding a new species to the Canadian fish fauna.

## Background & Description
The specimen was examined post-fixation (formalin) and preservation (ethyl alcohol):
- **Total Length:** 630 mm
- **Weight:** 3.56 kg
- Initial field identification was uncertain due to morphological overlap with *[[Ictiobus bubalus]]* (Smallmouth Buffalo) and *I. niger*, and superficial resemblance to a possible interspecific hybrid.
- Diagnostic confirmation required **cranial fontanelle** and **cranial bone** analysis, as external characters among *Ictiobus* species are noted as difficult to interpret, particularly in larger, less commonly encountered specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- *Ictiobus niger* is primarily a species of the **Mississippi River system**, ranging from the Ohio River and its tributaries south to Mexico.
- It has been historically cited as **native to southern Lake Michigan** (Hubbs and Lagler 1964), suggesting prior, sparse presence within the **Great Lakes system**.
- **Lake Erie status:** Moore (1968) previously listed *I. niger* (but not *I. bubalus*) from Lake Erie. Trautman (1957) recorded no confirmed specimens of either *I. niger* or *I. bubalus* in Ohio waters of Lake Erie.
- Historical **Sandusky Bay** specimens (Lake Erie) previously identified as hybrids between *[[Ictiobus cyprinellus]]* and *I. bubalus*/*I. niger* may represent earlier undetected occurrences of *I. bubalus* or *I. niger* in the lake, rather than true hybrids.
- Both *I. bubalus* and *I. niger* are considered **uncommon** in Lake Erie relative to the dominant congener, *[[Ictiobus cyprinellus]]* (Bigmouth Buffalo).

## Key Findings
- **First verified Canadian record** of *Ictiobus niger* (Black Buffalo), captured 20 June 1978 in Pigeon Bay, Lake Erie, Ontario.
- Prior to this record, *I. cyprinellus* was the only *Ictiobus* species confirmed in Canadian waters.
- The specimen was initially misclassified in the field, underscoring the **taxonomic difficulty** in distinguishing *Ictiobus* species using standard external characters alone; cranial osteology was required for definitive identification.
- The finding corroborates earlier, less certain reports of *I. niger* in Lake Erie (Moore 1968) and suggests that historical "hybrid" catostomid records from Sandusky Bay may instead reflect unrecognized populations of *I. niger* or *I. bubalus*.
- The specimen is permanently vouchered at the **Royal Ontario Museum** (Catalog No. **ROM 34562**).
- Highlights the ongoing value of **commercial fisheries bycatch monitoring** (via cooperators such as [[Rudy Krause]]) in documenting range extensions and rare ichthyological records.
