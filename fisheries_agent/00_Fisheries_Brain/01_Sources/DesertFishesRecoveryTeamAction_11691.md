## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) briefing package for the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** (September 1994, Phoenix, Arizona), rather than a primary research paper. It compiles administrative and conservation-status updates on a suite of imperiled southwestern desert fishes — including [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Virgin River Chub]], and several native cyprinids, catostomids, and dace. The material covers candidate species listings, critical habitat designations, ongoing lawsuits, and multi-site recovery actions (livestock exclosures, barrier construction, reintroductions, and long-term monitoring) across Arizona river systems such as the [[San Pedro River]], [[Verde River]], [[Aravaipa Creek]], and [[Gila River]]. While not a traditional ecological study, it provides a valuable snapshot of applied conservation status and regulatory history for these species circa 1993–1994.

## Background & Description

The **Desert Fishes Recovery Team**, guided by ichthyologist W.L. Minckley among others, coordinated recovery planning for federally listed and candidate fish species native to arid-land drainages of Arizona and New Mexico. Species discussed span several families:

- **Poeciliidae**: [[Gila Topminnow]] (endangered) — small live-bearing fish restricted to isolated spring and stream refugia.
- **Cyprinodontidae**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (endangered, Arizona populations) — extremely restricted to spring systems like [[Quitobaquito Springs]].
- **Cyprinidae**: [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*, threatened), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*, threatened), [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*, Category 2 candidate), [[Yaqui Chub]] (Category 2), [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), the latter two newly added as Category 2 candidates in Bill Williams and Gila basins.
- **Catostomidae**: [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), and an undescribed [[Little Colorado Sucker]] unit — all added as Category 2 candidates.
- **Salmonidae**: [[Apache Trout]] — noted as having no active recovery action in Arizona at the time of this report.

The team also flagged a distinct **Rio Yaqui fishes** subgroup for separate management attention, reflecting the biogeographically isolated Rio Yaqui basin fauna in southeastern Arizona/Sonora.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

These species inhabit fragmented, often isolated desert aquatic habitats — perennial springs, headwater streams, and larger rivers subject to intense anthropogenic pressures:

- **Habitat threats**: livestock grazing along riparian corridors, road and flood-control channelization, nonnative fish introductions (a recurring, dominant threat across nearly all species/sites), groundwater pumping, and water diversions (e.g., Indian Health Service diversion for the town of Whiteriver).
- **Key drainages**: [[Aravaipa Creek]], [[San Pedro River]], [[Verde River]], [[Gila River]], [[Eagle Creek]], [[Blue River]], [[Fossil Creek]] (implied via "Cottonwood" sites), [[Sonoita Creek]], and Redrock Canyon — many of which host multiple listed species simultaneously (sympatric spikedace/loach minnow populations in Aravaipa Creek, for example).
- **Regional-scale concerns**: broader hydrological connectivity issues were raised regarding the Central Arizona Project (CAP), including impacts on **lower Colorado River, delta, and Gulf of California** flows, indicating downstream/estuarine linkage concerns beyond the immediate desert stream habitats.
- **Conservation interventions** frequently combine physical habitat modification (barrier construction to exclude nonnatives, livestock exclosures, headcut/erosion control) with population-level actions (augmentation stocking, reintroduction) and legal/regulatory tools (Section 7 consultations under the Endangered Species Act).

## Key Findings

- **Candidate species additions**: Following team recommendations, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Rhinichthys osculus* (Bill Williams/Gila basins), *Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarki*, *Catostomus insignis*, and an unnamed *Catostomus* sp. were added to the Category 2 candidate list.
- **Status surveys**: An Arizona Game and Fish Department survey of [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) was underway under FWS funding, with completion expected (delayed) past November 1994.
- **Conservation agreements**: The Arizona Ecological Services Office concluded that conservation agreements in lieu of listing were **not** a viable approach for Arizona fishes, given scattered distributions, many involved parties, and diverse threats — no such agreements were in progress.
- **Listing actions**: Critical habitat for [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was designated in March 1994.
- **Petitions**: A "warranted but precluded" finding was issued on a petition (filed by Greater Gila Biodiversity Project, Southwest Center for Biological Diversity, and Biodiversity Legal Foundation) to uplist spikedace and loach minnow; a further petition to expand their critical habitat was in preparation. A petition to list [[Gila Chub]] was being held pending progress on the ongoing status survey.
- **Litigation**:
  - Catron County sued FWS over NEPA compliance for critical habitat and recovery plans for spikedace/loach minnow.
  - Greater Gila Biodiversity Project won a suit forcing FWS to finalize critical habitat designations.
  - Southwest Center for Biological Diversity filed notice of intent to sue over the CAP biological opinion (nonnative fish protections, water depletion effects on the lower Colorado, delta, and Gulf of California) and over San Pedro River water depletion tied to Endangered Species Act compliance in Sierra Vista-area development.
  - A settlement required proposing critical habitat for [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Virgin Spinedace]] by mid-1995.
- **Recovery actions by species** (multi-site, ongoing):
  - **Gila Topminnow**: livestock exclosures, road closures/revegetation, biannual monitoring across Redrock Canyon, Sonoita Creek, Sharp Spring, Cienega Creek, and multiple reintroduction sites.
  - **Desert Pupfish**: annual population census at Quitobaquito Springs, planned nonnative removal at "Bog Hole," and San Pedro River habitat restoration/monitoring.
  - **Spikedace & Loach Minnow**: quarterly monitoring in Aravaipa Creek, nonnative fish barrier construction, Section 7-driven reductions in road/flood-control and livestock impacts across the San Pedro, Gila, Eagle Creek, and Verde River systems.
  - **Gila Chub**: barrier construction and planned nonnative removal/reintroduction at Arnett Creek; ongoing status survey.
  - **Longfin Dace, Desert Sucker, Sonora Sucker, Speckled Dace**: newly designated Category 2 candidates with fish-community structure, distribution, and habitat-use studies underway on the Verde River and Arnett Creek.
  - **Apache Trout**: no active recovery measures reported in Arizona at time of writing.
- **Section 7 consultations**: A list of ongoing Section 7 consultations was referenced but not completed in time for inclusion in this document, indicating extensive regulatory review activity beyond what is detailed here.
