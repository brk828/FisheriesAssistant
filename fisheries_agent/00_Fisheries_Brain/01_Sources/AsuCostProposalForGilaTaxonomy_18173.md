# Gila Taxonomy Research Proposal - Arizona State University

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #gila #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a contract proposal submitted by [[Arizona State University]] to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Upper Colorado Region) on August 28, 1990, in response to Solicitation No. O-SP-40-09700. The proposal outlines a comprehensive taxonomic and genetic study of native fish species across the [[Colorado River Basin]] and related drainages. Principal Investigator [[W. L. Minckley]] and co-investigators [[T. E. Dowling]], [[M. E. Douglas]], and [[P. C. Marsh]] proposed four research schedules covering morphological investigations, protein electrophoresis, DNA analysis, and chromosomal investigations. The university bid on Schedules I-III (declining Schedule IV) with a combined cost of $993,693.00, representing approximately $500,000 in savings compared to separate awards. The proposal includes extensive field sampling across multiple river systems in Arizona, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico, Wyoming, Nevada, and proposed additional work in northwestern Mexico.

## Background & Description

The proposal addresses taxonomic research needs for native fish populations across the [[Colorado River Basin]] and adjacent drainages. The work encompasses multiple investigative approaches:

**Schedule I - Morphological Investigations** ($464,321.00): Traditional taxonomic analysis based on physical characteristics and measurements of fish specimens.

**Schedule II - Protein Electrophoresis Investigations** ($469,276.00): Biochemical genetic analysis using protein markers to assess population structure and relationships.

**Schedule III - DNA Investigations** ($559,758.00): Molecular genetic analysis using DNA techniques to resolve taxonomic questions and evolutionary relationships.

**Schedule IV - Chromosomal Investigations**: No bid submitted by ASU.

The research team emphasized that scientific and economic benefits would result from consistency of researchers and methodology across all three schedules. An unsolicited proposal addendum suggested additional investigation in Mexico to enhance the research, though this was presented separately and not intended to modify the primary solicitation response.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposed research spans diverse aquatic habitats across the southwestern United States:

**Major River Systems**: [[Gila River]] basin (Arizona), [[Bill Williams River]] system (Arizona), [[Virgin River]] and [[Moapa River]] systems (Arizona, Nevada, Utah), [[White River]] system (Arizona, Nevada, Utah), [[San Juan River]] system (Colorado, New Mexico), [[Upper Colorado River]] system including [[Dolores River]] and [[Gunnison River]] (Colorado), western [[Green River]] tributaries (Utah), upper [[Yampa River]] and [[Little Snake River]] systems (Colorado, Wyoming), upper [[Green River]] system (Wyoming).

**Reservoir Habitats**: [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona, Nevada) with specific operations planned for [[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail).

**International Component**: Proposed sampling in tributaries of the [[Gulf of California]] in northwestern Mexico (2 trips, 40 days total).

**Out-group Sampling**: Four sampling trips (12 days) to adjoining basins to obtain specimens for comparative taxonomic analysis.

The geographic scope reflects the complex biogeography of southwestern native fish fauna and the need for basin-wide taxonomic resolution to support conservation and management decisions by federal agencies.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Research Structure**: ASU offered a comprehensive, integrated approach across three major analytical methodologies (morphology, protein electrophoresis, DNA analysis) with consistent personnel and methods, providing substantial cost savings ($500,000) compared to separate contracts.

**Field Sampling Plan**: Detailed travel and per diem estimates totaling $31,550 (excluding Mexico) or $41,550 (including Mexico) covered:
- 8 major sampling trips across Colorado River Basin drainages (4-7 days each)
- 4 trips to adjoining basins for out-group specimens (12 days total)
- Optional 2 trips to Mexico (40 days, $10,000 additional)
- Participation in agency personnel sampling on larger rivers (Yampa-Green, Middle Green, Colorado River, Grand Canyon, Lake Mohave)
- 2 trips to Denver for meetings ($3,400)

**Cost Calculations**: Travel computed at $0.225/mile/vehicle, $55.00/day lodging, $25.00/day meals (university rates). Camp rate of $25.00/day/person applied in Mexico and Lake Mohave. Mexican costs included foreign vehicle insurance ($10/day/vehicle) and estimated scientific collecting permit filing costs ($800/trip).

**Personnel**: Key personnel designated as essential: W.L. Minckley (Professor), [[Paul Marsh]] (Associate Professor), Thomas E. Dowling (Professor), Michael Douglas (Research Specialist).

**Administrative Structure**: Contract administration through ASU Office of Research Development (Tempe, Arizona), with technical direction from Bureau of Reclamation Contracting Officer's Technical Representative (Bob Williams, Salt Lake City).

**Compliance**: Proposal included all required federal certifications regarding independent price determination, contingent fees, lobbying disclosure, taxpayer identification, debarment/suspension, drug-free workplace, equal opportunity, and environmental compliance.

**Strategic Value**: The proposal emphasized that taxonomic resolution across the Colorado River Basin would benefit from unified methodology and consistent research team, supporting endangered species management and recovery programs administered by the Bureau of Reclamation.
