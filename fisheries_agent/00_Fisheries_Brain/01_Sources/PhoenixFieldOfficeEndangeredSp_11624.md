## Ecological & Environmental Context

While explicit habitat descriptions are absent, taxonomic and geographic groupings in the source imply the following ecological context:

- **Colorado River Basin mainstem specialists**: [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-river, historically pelagic/benthic species adapted to the pre-dam hydrology of the Colorado River mainstem and major tributaries.
- **Gila River Basin small-stream/spring endemics**: [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Gila Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta grahami*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) — occupy perennial desert streams and spring-fed refugia vulnerable to dewatering and nonnative species introgression/predation.
- **Virgin River drainage endemics**: [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*) and [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*).
- **Yaqui River Basin (Mexico-border) endemics**: [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*), [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*) — associated with San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge (SBNWR) support office, indicating isolated spring/stream refugia near the international border.
- **Isolated spring endemics**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon sp.*) — restrict to single or few desert spring/marsh systems, the classic pattern of extreme habitat specialization and vulnerability to spring desiccation.

- **Sonora/Mexican drainage stoneroller**: [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*), associated with FW (Fish and Wildlife lead) and SBNWR support.
- **Sonora Chub** (*Gila ditaenia*): a binational Sonoran Desert stream endemic (Sonora, Mexico/Arizona border streams).
