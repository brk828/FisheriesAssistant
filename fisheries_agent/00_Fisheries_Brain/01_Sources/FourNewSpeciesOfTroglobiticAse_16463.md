# Four New Species of Troglobitic Asellids (Crustacea: Isopoda) from the United States

#isopoda #asellidae #troglobitic-asellids #crustacea-taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This paper, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (Vol. 84, No. 57, pp. 489-500, 29 February 1972), by [[Laurence E. Fleming]] of Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, describes four new species of troglobitic asellid isopods from the United States: [[Asellus Ancylus]], [[Asellus Steevesi]], [[Asellus Metcalfi]], and [[Asellus Paurotrigonus]]. The descriptions are offered as partial preparation for a later paper on the evolution of epigean and troglobitic nearctic asellid faunas. Species are separated mainly by male gnathopod armament, first and second pleopod morphology, and the processes at the endopodial tip of the male second pleopod. The paper also discusses an unusual record of a troglobitic species from an epigean (surface) habitat in Mississippi, which the author describes as an enigma pending further collections.

## Background & Description

- **Author and affiliation:** [[Laurence E. Fleming]], Department of Biology, Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, Blacksburg, Va. 24061.
- **Purpose:** Increased discovery of previously unknown North American troglobitic asellids gives systematists enough material to consider the evolution of both epigean and troglobitic nearctic asellid faunas. This paper covers only four new species.
- **Acknowledgments:** Material was collected and supplied by J. R. [[J. R. Holsinger]], R. Norton, A. L. Metcalf, and T. D. Thornhill. Dr. Perry C. Holt reviewed the manuscript.
- **Taxonomic framework:** All four species are placed in the genus *Asellus*, family Asellidae. The author proposes that [[Asellus Paurotrigonus]] belongs to the [[Stygius Group]] of troglobitic asellids.
- **Diagnostic characters used:**
  - Male gnathopod (peraeopod 1) propodus palmar margin processes and spines
  - Number of coupling hooks on the peduncle of the first pleopod
  - Morphology of the endopodial tip of the male second pleopod (lateral process, mesial process, cannula, caudal process, accessory process)
  - Male uropod proportions (where present)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitats:**
  - [[Brewer Cave]] (Boone County, Arkansas): mud bottom of a stream
  - [[Three Forks Cave]] (Adair County, Oklahoma): cave record for two species
  - [[Carrico Cave]] (Dade County, Missouri): cave type-locality for [[Asellus Steevesi]]
  - Seeps off 7th Street in Baxter Springs (Cherokee County, Kansas): epigean seep record
  - A farm well about 8 miles southeast of Dexter (Cowley County, Kansas): type-locality for [[Asellus Metcalfi]]
  - Ditch under the Homochitto River bridge on U.S. 61 (Adams County, Mississippi): type-locality for [[Asellus Paurotrigonus]]
- **Distribution notes:** [[Asellus Ancylus]] and [[Asellus Steevesi]] are each known from caves in two states and may be restricted to the central United States. [[Asellus Metcalfi]] is known only from its type-locality.
- **Epigean troglobites:** Earlier reports of troglobitic asellids in surface habitats (Leonard and Ponder, 1949; Dexter, 1954; Minckley, 1961) came from cave-stream resurgents, springs, or streams over cavernous limestone. Barr (1960) attributed such occurrences to accidental displacement. This explanation seems less likely for the Mississippi record because no nearby cavernous areas are known.
- **Interstitial habitat hypothesis:** Personal communication from Holsinger (April 1971) indicated that an "interstitial" species in the coastal plains area is very likely. For amphipods, this is a common habitat, and specimens are often "washed" out by water table rises during spring rains.

## Key Findings

### Asellus ancylus (new species; Figs. 1-4)
- **Type specimens:** Holotype USNM 135254; allotype USNM 135255; 1 paratype LEF 18-Y. Collected from Brewer Cave, mud bottom of stream, Boone County, Arkansas, by J. R. Holsinger, 26 June 1964.
- **Size:** Holotype 4.6 mm long, 1.0 mm wide. Allotype (ovigerous) 4.1 mm long, 1.2 mm wide at brood pouch.
- **Body proportions:** Length about 4.6 times width in the holotype and 3.4 times width in the allotype.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Male gnathopod propodus has no processes but 5 or 6 short, stout spines on the palmar margin. Dactyl has 3 or 4 long, stout spines on the opposable margin. First pleopod peduncle has 4 or 5 coupling hooks.
- **Second pleopod:** The endopodial tip has three processes: a long, slender lateral process with a cephalically recurvate apex; a cannula extending beyond the caudal process; and a large, broadly rounded caudal process.
- **Uropods:** Absent in specimens collected.
- **Etymology:** From Greek *ancyl*, "crooked" or "bent," for the recurvate lateral process.
- **Variation:** Mesial apophysis may be well developed or reduced. The apex of the lateral process may be acute or rounded.
- **Affinities:** Closest to [[Asellus Metcalfi]] and [[Asellus Spatulatus]] (Mackin and Hubricht, 1940). It is distinguished from both by its longer, recurvate lateral process.
- **Material examined:** Three Forks Cave, Adair County, Oklahoma, collected by Jeffery H. Black, 1 August 1970, recorded as "266" in the OCR text (count notation unclear).

### Asellus steevesi (new species; Figs. 5-9)
- **Type specimens:** Holotype USNM 135727; allotype USNM 135728; 10 paratypes LEF 28-G. Collected from Carrico Cave, Dade County, Missouri, by J. Holsinger and R. Norton, 20 August 1968.
- **Size:** Holotype 8.3 mm long, 1.5 mm wide. Largest male 10.5 mm. Allotype 8.3 mm long, 1.3 mm wide. Largest female 10.1 mm.
- **Body proportions:** Length (excluding uropods) about 5.5 times width in the holotype and 6.3 times width in the allotype.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Male gnathopod propodus palmar margin has 4 processes (proximal, two median, and distal) plus a robust proximal spine. Dactyl has 3 or 4 small spines. First pleopod peduncle has 4 to 6 coupling hooks.
- **Second pleopod:** The endopodial tip has five processes: mesial, lateral (with two projections), cannula (with two projections), caudal, and accessory.
- **Uropods:** Male peduncle about 2.7 times the exopod length; endopod about 1.8 times the exopod length.
- **Etymology:** Named in honor of Dr. Harrison R. Steeves III ([[Harrison R. Steeves III]]).
- **Variation:** Two setae on the exopod of the first pleopod may be absent. The two conjoined mesial processes on the gnathopod may be absent.
- **Affinities:** No known close affinities with previously described troglobitic asellids. Regarded as a unique species.
- **Distribution:** A seep in Kansas, a cave in Missouri, and a cave in Oklahoma. Probably restricted to the central United States.
- **Material examined (other than type-locality):**
  - Kansas: seeps off 7th Street, Baxter Springs, Cherokee County, J. R. Holsinger, 12 June 1964, recorded as "94 6; 1229" in the OCR text (sex counts unclear).
  - Oklahoma: Three Forks Cave, Adair County, Jeffery H. Black, 1 August 1970, recorded as "76 6; 529" in the OCR text (sex counts unclear).

### Asellus metcalfi (new species; Figs. 10-15)
- **Type specimens:** Holotype USNM 135263; allotype USNM 135264; 36 paratypes LEF 33-W. Collected from a well about 30 feet west of the A. C. Metcalf farmhouse, about 8 miles southeast of Dexter, Cowley County, Kansas, by Artie L. Metcalf. The collection date is not given in the text.
- **Size:** Holotype (largest male) 5.8 mm long, 1.0 mm wide. Allotype (largest female) 7.7 mm long, 1.5 mm wide.
- **Body proportions:** Holotype length (excluding uropods) about 5.8 times width. The text gives the allotype ratio as "approximately 5.1 times the length in allotype," which appears to be a reporting error.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Male gnathopod palmar margin has no processes but many slender spines. Dactyl has 2 or 3 short setae. First pleopod peduncle has 3 coupling hooks.
- **Second pleopod:** The endopodial tip has four processes: a short fingerlike lateral process, a stiff cannula, a broad mesial process with a heavily sclerotized apex, and a wide caudal process.
- **Uropods:** Endopod about 1.3 times the exopod length.
- **Etymology:** Named in honor of Mr. A. C. Metcalf, on whose property the well is located.
- **Variation:** A common form has a laterally separated lateral process with a slightly rugose apical lobe. In some specimens the mesial process partly conceals the cannula.
- **Affinities:** Very close to [[Asellus Spatulatus]]. Distinguished from it by gnathopod processes (none in *A. metcalfi*, at least two in *A. spatulatus*), uropod proportions, and the shape of the lateral and mesial processes. Also resembles [[Asellus Ancylus]] in endopod shape.
- **Distribution:** Known only from the type-locality.

### Asellus paurotrigonus (new species; Figs. 16-20)
- **Type specimen:** Holotype USNM 135726. Collected from a ditch under the Homochitto River bridge on U.S. 61 at the Wilkinson-Adams County line, Adams County, Mississippi, by T. D. Thornhill, 1 March 1969.
- **Size:** Holotype 16.7 mm long, 2.8 mm wide. Body length (excluding uropods) 5.9 times width.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Male gnathopod palmar margin has 3 processes (small proximal, large mesial, small distal). First pleopod peduncle has 7 or 8 coupling hooks. Second pleopod peduncle has 4 long, stiff setae on the mesiodistal margin.
- **Second pleopod:** The endopodial tip has three processes: a broad caudal process, a lateral process extending slightly beyond the caudal process, and a broad tubular cannula directed mesiad.
- **Uropods:** Male peduncle about 3.4 times the exopod length; endopod about 3.2 times the exopod length.
- **Etymology:** From Greek *pauro* ("small") and *trigon* ("triangle"), for the caudal process, which resembles a small triangle.
- **Affinities:** Closest to [[Asellus Stygius]] (Packard, 1871) and [[Asellus Alabamensis]] (Stafford, 1911) in the [[Stygius Group]]. Shares uropod shape, gnathopod armament, and cannula and caudal process form. Differs mainly in the enlarged, elongated lateral process, which in the other two species is small and rounded.
- **Remarks:** The species shows typical troglobitic traits (absence of pigmentation and eyes, elongated appendages) but was collected in a ditch with no nearby caves or karst substrate.
- **Distribution and material:** Known only from the holotype and type-locality.

### Broader Conclusions
- The author describes four new asellid species, three from cave or seep habitats in the central United States and one from a Mississippi ditch.
- Male second pleopod endopodial tip morphology is the main diagnostic character across all four species.
- The Mississippi record of a troglobitic asellid in an epigean ditch is unexplained. The author states it "will remain an enigma until further collections of it are taken and their locations noted."
