# Fishes Collected in Oklahoma and Arkansas in 1927

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, the third report of the [[University of Oklahoma Fish Survey]], presents the fishes collected during summer 1927 by a field party from the Museum of Zoology of the University of Oklahoma, led by A. I. Ortenburger, with [[Carl L. Hubbs]] as co-author. The party seined streams in southeastern Oklahoma, western Arkansas, and northeastern Oklahoma, all within the Red River, Ouachita, and Arkansas drainage systems. The survey sampled 60 stations and documents 82 numbered species accounts, with one new species described, [[Notropis greenei]]. The authors report 21 species new to the Oklahoma state list and record a fauna that is a mixture of northern, southern upland, and Mississippi lowland forms. The paper also includes taxonomic revisions, hybridization observations, and detailed larval pigmentation and nuptial tubercle descriptions.

## Background & Description

The survey built on two earlier reports by the same authors. Arkansas additions were few, because that state's fishes had been studied by [[Girard]], [[Jordan and Gilbert]], [[Meek]], and [[Fowler]]. The authors emphasize that the eastern Oklahoma fauna was rich and that further exploration was urgent. Approximately 25 species reported from Oklahoma by earlier collectors were not obtained in 1927 but were found in the same region in other samples.

The paper treats taxonomy at length. It addresses the status of [[Notropis volucellus]] subspecies, the [[Notropis lutrensis]] complex, the [[Hybognathus]] species pair, and the synonymy of [[Campostoma anomalum]]. Identification keys are not provided, but species descriptions cover meristic counts (scales, fin rays, teeth, gill-rakers), body proportions, sexual dimorphism, and nuptial tubercles, which the authors describe as an underused diagnostic character.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey covered 60 stations grouped into five areas: (A) Red River system in Oklahoma, (B) Red River system in Arkansas, (C) Ouachita River system in Arkansas, (D) Arkansas River system in Arkansas, and (E) Arkansas River system in Oklahoma. Stations include spring-fed upland creeks, riffle-pool streams such as the [[Kiamichi River]], backwater and flood pools, and large turbid rivers such as the [[Arkansas River]] and [[Illinois River]].

Recorded conditions include:
- Water temperatures ranging from about 66°F to 90°F, depending on time of day and station.
- pH values from less than 6.8 in acid upland streams to 8.4 in Caddo Creek and 8.2 at Hominy Creek.
- Bottom types from granite and boulders to clean gravel, sand, and soft mud.
- Emergent and submergent plants such as [[Nymphaea]] (water lily), sedges, and [[Dianthera americana]].

Station 59 (Hominy Creek, Osage County) was notable for oil films, killed vegetation, salty taste, and natural gas bubbles, indicating industrial or petroleum influence. Several stations were in flood or out-of-bank conditions, which the authors flagged as atypical.

Biogeographically, the authors conclude that northern taxa (e.g., [[Cottus]], [[Chrosomus]], [[Semotilus]]) inhabit cool northeastern upland creeks, southern upland species (e.g., [[Notropis greenei]], [[Hadropterus]]) occur in similar waters, and Mississippi lowland forms (e.g., [[Gambusia]], [[Pomoxis]], [[Aplodinotus]]) extend westward along main river channels.

## Key Findings

**Faunal and distributional results**
- 21 species were recorded for Oklahoma for the first time; the authors note that about 100 species were already known from the state.
- Two Arkansas additions were made: *Notropis ortenburgeri* and *Notropis volucellus buchanani*, plus a doubtful darter identification.
- *Poecilichthys variatus* was confirmed for Arkansas from Spring River, extending its known range beyond the Ohio River basin.
- Two lowland forms, *Lepisosteus platostomus* and *Amia calva*, were first recorded for Oklahoma.

**New species and taxonomic decisions**
- *Notropis greenei* was described as new, from the Arkansas and Oklahoma Elk and Fourche la Fave systems; the holotype is a 42 mm breeding male.
- *Notropis percobromus* was provisionally recognized as distinct from *N. rubellus*.
- *Poecilichthys coeruleus* was treated with subspecies *pulchellus* and *spectabilis*, and northeastern Oklahoma populations were shown to be intermediate.
- *Campostoma anomalum* was made the valid name for *Dionda plumbea*, *D. spadicea*, and *D. grisea*.
- Three *Dionda* species were recognized: *D. episcopa*, *D. nubila*, and *D. rasconis*.
- *Hybognathus placitus* was confirmed as distinct from *H. nuchalis*.

**Hybridization and intergradation**
- Hybrid sunfish (*Helioperca incisor* × *Xenotis megalotis breviceps*) and many bass hybrids (*Micropterus dolomieu* × *M. pseudaplites*) were found at West Cache Creek and headwaters of the Kiamichi River. The bass population at one locality was described as almost entirely hybrid.
- *Notropis volucellus* and *N. v. buchanani* intergrade in mosaic fashion, with upland streams held by volucellus and large rivers by buchanani.

**Teratology and developmental observations**
- *Dorosoma cepedianum* (gizzard shad) larvae show two forms: normal and macrocephalic. The authors argue the macrocephalic young are teratological but develop into normal adults.
- A *Minytrema melanops* specimen had a three-chambered air bladder, similar to the normal condition in *Moxostoma*.
- A *Hypargyrus velox* male had nuptial tubercles in three rows, as in *Hyborhynchus*.

**Methodological contributions**
- Larval pigmentation patterns are described for several taxa (e.g., *Minytrema*, *Moxostoma*, *Notropis lutrensis*, *N. percobromus*) as aids to identifying very young fish.
- Nuptial tubercle arrangement is presented as a diagnostic character for Cyprinidae.

**Notable population and ecological notes**
- [[Notropis lutrensis]] was absent from all Red and Ouachita collections in Arkansas, and its distribution with [[Notropis whipplii]] was almost wholly complementary.
- *Notemigonus crysoleucas* was absent from Arkansas, which the authors attributed to its preference for sluggish water. Oklahoma specimens were taken across pH 6.8 to 8.2.
- *Moxostoma erythrurum* was described as one of the commonest fishes of eastern Oklahoma and Arkansas.
- *Notropis boops* was among the commonest upland stream species and bred during July and late June.
- *Fundulus catenatus* was common in the Ouachita system; the authors describe the breeding male's coloration in detail.

**Specimen summary**
- Species accounts give station-by-station specimen counts. Examples include 67 young *Lepisosteus* and *Notropis lutrensis* from the South Canadian River at Norman, and 104 specimen counts for Kiamichi River stations. Full counts are not reproduced here.
