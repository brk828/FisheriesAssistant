# Wildlife and Fish Habitat Relationship System Model Testing and Validation Study Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #trout

## Executive Summary

This study plan, prepared by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Research Branch) under [[Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration]] (Region 3, 1987 to October 31, 1991; revised 07-18-89), sets out procedures to test and validate the [[Wildlife and Fish Habitat Relationship System]] (WFHRS) developed by the [[USDA Forest Service]]. The work focuses on coldwater trout streams in the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] and [[Coconino National Forest]]. The plan is organized into five sub-objectives: sampling design adequacy, a land-aquatic classification test, local validation of WFHRS models, model refinement, and dissemination of results. The document also serves as a scope of work for part of a cooperative contract and a supplement to a Memorandum of Understanding between the agencies.

## Background & Description

**Rationale.** Land managers have long needed a quantified link between land use and stream and riparian habitat quality and the fisheries they support. The Forest Service, which manages thousands of miles of riverine habitat, began funding research in the 1960s on livestock grazing and silviculture effects on riparian habitat, stream morphology, and fish populations. This work produced standard stream survey routines and quantitative models relating fish standing crop to habitat quality.

**WFHRS structure.** The system has three elements:
1. A systematic inventory technique (the [[General Aquatic Wildlife Survey]], GAWS) for existing stream, riparian, and fish population conditions.
2. A land-aquatic classification scheme that integrates fisheries into land management, based on [[Rosgen Channel Classification]].
3. Predictive models that assess present and optimal habitat capability and species carrying capacity.

The WFHRS was developed outside Arizona and is now at the testing and refinement stage. The plan states that models must be tested locally before they can guide management decisions.

**Institutional framework.** The work supports the [[Cold Water Fisheries Strategic Plan]] (1985-1990) and the Arizona-USFS cooperative effort. Under the 1973 Memorandum of Understanding and its supplement, the Commission and Forest Service agreed to:
- Designate two-person core teams.
- Conduct field surveys to estimate fish populations and map riparian vegetation.
- Develop a land systems inventory and a computer model predicting trout biomass and land use effects.
- Share report preparation, habitat surveys, water quality analysis, and macroinvertebrate analysis.
- Develop a standardized survey form and data storage system.

**Scope.** The contract covers an inventory of cold-water streams within the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, with the goal of determining fish standing crops, fish habitat, and riparian conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Information gaps.** The plan identifies several needs:
- *Standardized sampling.* WFHRS model testing requires a standardized stream sampling method, which the Strategic Plan also calls for. A standard survey is essential for long-term monitoring.
- *Carrying capacity.* The Apache-Sitgreaves and Coconino National Forests provide most of Arizona's stream trout habitat and harvest, yet their optimal productivity and carrying capacity have not been determined. This information is needed to identify degraded streams and prioritize improvement projects.
- *Land use relationships.* Precise relationships between land use practices and stream habitat and fish populations are unknown in Arizona. Such findings are needed to identify [[Best Management Practices]].

**Habitat-fish relationships.** The plan says that understanding stream habitat and fish population relationships will help managers estimate populations where direct sampling is inappropriate, document habitat change from land or water projects, identify manageable habitat variables, and identify the land use practices with the greatest impact on fishes.

**Sediment modeling constraint.** The [[FISHSED Model]] predicts sediment yields and their effects on fish, but it requires local sediment yield data that are not yet available for Arizona. The plan therefore states that FISHSED cannot be tested in this study.

**Study design approach.** Hypotheses are written in null form. Field work follows the GAWS methodology, with stream reaches divided into homogeneous units by channel type and stations sampled along transects.

## Key Findings

The plan does not report measured results. Its main content consists of the study design, hypotheses, and planned analyses.

**Sub-Objective 1: Sampling design and reliability**
- *Hypothesis 1.1:* Habitat data from a single station do not differ significantly from other stations in the same reach. Two years of GAWS survey data already exist. The original design required a minimum of two stations for reaches under 1.6 km, three for 1.6-16.0 km, and five for reaches over 16 km. The plan notes that some short Arizona reaches were surveyed with only one station because of misinformation.
- A station consists of five transects spaced 20 m apart and covers 100 m of stream. The 1989 modified design uses three stations for reaches under 500 m, five for 500-1500 m, and ten for reaches over 1500 m. Low-discharge first-order streams use 10 m transect spacing.
- *Hypothesis 1.2:* A single three-pass electrofishing survey does not differ significantly from others in the reach. Population estimates use the [[Zippin Removal Method]] (1958).
- *Hypothesis 1.3:* GAWS transect habitat data do not differ from [[Habitat Typing]] data (Bisson et al. 1982). The steering committee decided on this comparison on March 30, 1989. Data will be compared at station and reach levels on at least three streams, using variables such as percent pools, percent riffles, pool-riffle ratios, width-depth ratios, and stream depth.

**Sub-Objective 2: Land-aquatic classification**
- *Hypothesis 2.1:* The WFHRS channel type classification is not appropriate for Arizona. At least four field survey personnel will be interviewed about how easy and appropriate the rating technique is.
- *Hypothesis 2.2:* Fish standing crops and habitat variables do not differ among similar reach types from different streams.

**Sub-Objective 3: Local testing of WFHRS models**
- *Hypothesis 3.1:* No significant relationship exists between the [[Habitat Condition Index]] (HCI), which uses six composite variables, and observed trout standing crops. Regression slope is tested with the t statistic.
- *Hypothesis 3.2:* The [[Habitat Vulnerability Index]] (HVI) does not differ significantly from independent vulnerability ratings by stream habitat experts. About ten streams will be selected. Roughly ten biologists or managers will tour the streams and rate reaches on a 0-10 scale.
- *Hypothesis 3.3:* No significant relationship exists between standing crops predicted by the [[COWFISH Model]] and observed standing crops. COWFISH uses five habitat variables (undercut bank, vegetative overhang, streambank alteration, embeddedness, and width-depth ratio) to estimate existing and optimal trout standing crops. Only meadow or C-type channels will be used for testing, since cattle have the greatest impact there.
- *Hypothesis 3.4:* No significant relationship exists between COWFISH predicted optimal standing crops and observed standing crops in pristine streams. Surveys are planned on Reservation Creek and other relatively unimpacted streams on the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] reservation. Negotiations are in progress.
- *Hypothesis 3.5:* The shapes of the COWFISH PSI curves do not differ from those derived empirically from Arizona streams. Analysis uses [[Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test]] comparisons of PSI histograms.
- *Hypothesis 3.6:* No significant relationship exists between the [[Biotic Condition Index]] (BCI) and observed fish standing crops. Macroinvertebrates are sampled with a modified [[Surber Net Sampler]], using three sets of three riffle samples per stream. Water quality is sampled quarterly for one year near the stream mouth. Parameters include temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, specific conductance, sulfate, total alkalinity, nitrate nitrogen, and orthophosphate. Samples are analyzed at the USFS Aquatic Ecosystem Analysis Lab at Brigham Young University.
- *Hypothesis 3.7:* No significant relationship exists between the [[Dominance and Taxa Diversity Index]] (DAT) and observed fish standing crops.
- *Hypothesis 3.8:* WFHRS estimates of [[Potential Spawning Area]], [[Potential Rearing Area]], and [[Potential Overwintering Habitat]] do not adequately reflect these habitat quantities in Arizona streams. Spawning area is defined as the percent of substrate covered by 3.2-76.2 mm gravel. Rearing area is the percent of stream area with velocity below 0.3 m/s. The overwintering definition could not be found in the handbook. Steering committee members will be surveyed about these definitions.

**Sub-Objective 4: Model refinement**
- *Hypothesis 4.1:* Habitat variables do not predict fish standing crops in Arizona streams. Non-predictive variables could be removed to increase survey efficiency.
- *Hypothesis 4.2:* A new or revised habitat model does not explain a greater proportion of variation in fish standing crop than existing WFHRS models. Stepwise regression will use about half of the surveyed streams. Principal components analysis may address correlated predictors. Model validation requires that the 95% confidence limits around the regression line bracket the stated proportion of observed values (this value is garbled in the scanned text). Models will be compared by R-squared and residual error across stratification levels such as reach, fish species, and sub-basin.
- *Hypothesis 4.4:* A new model using all surveyed streams cannot explain more variation than the model developed under Procedure 4.2.3.

**Sub-Objective 5: Dissemination**
- A comprehensive final report with management recommendations will be published in-house after steering committee review.
- A statistically efficient sampling scheme and a standardized stream survey manual will be issued as a separate report.
- Key results will be submitted to journals such as *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* and *North American Journal of Fisheries Management*, and presented at workshops and professional meetings.
