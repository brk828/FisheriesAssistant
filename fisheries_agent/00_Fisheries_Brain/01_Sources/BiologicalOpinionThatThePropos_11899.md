# Gila Topminnow Biological Opinion: Bank Revetment on Sonoita Creek, Arizona State Route 82

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This is a [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services State Office) on June 21, 1994, under section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The action agency is the [[Federal Highway Administration]], which requested consultation for the repair, replacement, and construction of bank revetment along [[State Route 82]] at mile posts 15.4 and 16.9 in Santa Cruz County, Arizona. The listed species is the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*).

The Service concluded the project is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the Gila topminnow. It anticipates incidental take through direct mortality and habitat loss, and it attaches a [[Incidental Take Statement]], [[Reasonable and Prudent Measures]], and mandatory [[Terms and Conditions]], plus discretionary [[Conservation Recommendations]].

## Background & Description

**Species.** The Gila topminnow is a small livebearing fish, about one to two inches long, of the family [[Poeciliidae]]. It occurs in the Gila, Sonora, and de la Concepcion river drainages of Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico, but is listed only in the U.S. portion of its range. It was listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. No critical habitat has been designated. Once one of the most common fishes in the Gila River system, it has been extirpated from most of its range through water diversion, channelization, impoundment, and predation or competition from nonnative [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*).

**Status.** The document reports about 10 naturally occurring U.S. populations and about 20 reintroduced populations. Nine of the 10 natural populations are small and isolated, and only three are free of mosquitofish. Reintroductions have had low success, with about 9 percent survival across more than 300 stocked sites since the 1930s.

**Sonoita Creek.** The creek supports one of the natural populations, and four other natural populations lie in its tributaries. Topminnows occur sporadically from Cottonwood Spring to the mouth, excluding [[Patagonia Lake]]. Their distribution above the lake shifts with flow and channel configuration. The only ongoing monitoring above the lake is at the [[Patagonia Preserve]], where the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] has not collected Gila topminnow since 1990.

**Project.** The proposal covers about 1,812 linear feet of bank revetment using "rail bank protection" (steel rails driven into the streambed, lined with galvanized wire mesh and filled with crushed rock). Key components:

- **MP 15.4:** 1,209 linear feet of existing revetment, with 770 feet repaired and 439 feet replaced. Stream disturbance is about 0.5 acres. Rails are driven 10 feet below the streambed.
- **MP 16.9:** Revetment on an eroding 6–8 foot embankment, 394 feet upstream and 209 feet downstream of the [[Alum Canyon]] box culvert (603 feet total). Stream disturbance is about 0.25 acres. Rails are driven 15 feet below the streambed. Flow is diverted through an old high-flow channel using a temporary earthen dam.
- **Schedule:** 6 to 8 weeks of work, scheduled September through January to avoid the nesting season of the [[Rose-Throated Becard]] near MP 15.4.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Baseline condition.** The Service describes the species' status as poor and Sonoita Creek as a highly perturbed system. Since the mid-1800s, cienega and marsh areas have been drained, roads and railroads have altered the channel, grazing and agriculture have reduced [[Riparian Vegetation]], and Patagonia Lake eliminated several miles of stream and introduced predatory nonnative fishes. Suburban development is further changing water availability and runoff.

**Habitat at the sites.**
- *MP 15.4:* The floodplain is a narrow rocky canyon. The stream runs against the road embankment, and the channel is naturally narrow. Backwaters between a rock outcrop and the revetment are the main suitable habitat. Three juvenile topminnows were observed there on May 31, 1994.
- *MP 16.9:* The valley is wider (floodplain 200 to 300 feet). The south bank is largely barren, while the north bank and high-flow channel support extensive cottonwood and willow. Watercress-covered shallows may contain suitable habitat.

**Cumulative effects.** Future non-federal activities are expected to add development, land disturbance, reduced vegetative cover, increased groundwater pumping, and water pollution. Future federal actions are handled under section 7 and are not counted as cumulative effects.

## Key Findings

**Conclusion and jeopardy.** The proposal is not likely to jeopardize the Gila topminnow. The Service found that the revetment would not expand the armored bank area, so long-term conditions would not change from current conditions. The main impacts are temporary construction effects.

**Adverse effects identified:**
- Stream diversion can strand fish in dewatered backwaters, side channels, and marshes.
- Crushing by heavy equipment, and accidental release of petroleum or toxic chemicals, can kill fish.
- Loss of riparian vegetation adds shade, nutrient, and bank-stability losses, and increases sediment input. The Service judged the effect on the topminnow not major because of the limited project extent and the species' generalist tolerance of temperature and food.
- At MP 16.9, the diversion shortens the channel from 1,379 feet to 882 feet (497 feet lost). The diverted channel is straighter and faster, which is unlikely to support topminnows. Downcutting of about 2 feet over the 882 feet is estimated to move about 260 cubic yards of sediment downstream, filling pools and slow-water habitat in the short term. Sediment would eventually reach Patagonia Lake.
- The temporary earthen dam can damage riparian vegetation and bank stability, and may cause long-term erosion if breached.

**Incidental take statement:**
- *Direct mortality:* Population-based take cannot be quantified. Take is exceeded if more than **20 dead fish** of any species are found in the project area or within **500 yards downstream**.
- *Habitat:* Take of all topminnow habitat along the revetted and repaired length, plus buffers and the dam area, and substantial habitat modification for **2,600 linear feet** downstream of MP 16.9.
- *Discrepancy:* The body text totals the habitat-loss length at 1,812 feet plus 50 feet at each end plus 100 feet at the dam, which equals 2,012 feet. The Incidental Take Statement summary in the same document lists 2,112 feet. The source does not reconcile the two figures.
- If these limits are reached, consultation must be reinitiated immediately, and operations must stop if the additional take would be irreversible.

**Reasonable and prudent measures (RPMs):**
1. Minimize direct mortality of topminnows.
2. Minimize loss and alteration of habitat.
3. Monitor fish and habitat to document take.
4. Keep complete and accurate records.

**Key terms and conditions:**
- Minimize work in the wetted channel and prevent pollutants from entering surface waters.
- Limit heavy equipment to the authorized footprint and access tracks.
- Avoid riparian damage on the bank opposite the revetment and at the dam site.
- Monitor for dead or dying fish within 500 yards downstream whenever work is within 100 yards of the creek.
- Monitor the sites every other month for six months and once after the next late summer or spring flood. Submit written reports within 60 days of each monitoring event.
- Submit a written project report with before-and-after photographs within 90 days of completion.

**Conservation recommendation (discretionary):** No trees over 6 inches diameter at breast height should be removed or damaged in the project area, because an intact riparian community supports high-quality topminnow habitat and other species.

**Other permitting.** Non-listed species and other resource concerns will be reviewed during the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] permit process under [[Clean Water Act Section 404]].

**Reinitiation triggers.** Consultation must be reopened if take limits are reached, if new information reveals unconsidered effects, if the action is modified, or if a new species is listed or critical habitat is designated that may be affected.
