# Yaqui Chub, Yaqui Catfish, and Rio Yaqui Longfin Dace — Biological Opinion Summary

## Tags
#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #ConservationBiology #FreshwaterFishes #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary
This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Biological and Conference Opinion (April 14, 1998) evaluates a proposed Incidental Take Permit (PRT-837858) for the [[El Coronado Ranch And Cattle Company]] in Cochise County, Arizona. The permit addresses incidental take of the endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) and threatened [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) under Sections 10(a)(1)(B) and 10(a)(2) of the Endangered Species Act, alongside conservation measures for the unlisted [[Yaqui Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*, Rio Yaqui form). The action area encompasses 6,153 ha along [[West Turkey Creek]], including Coronado National Forest grazing allotments and private ranch lands. The Service concluded that the anticipated level of take is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of either listed species or adversely modify critical habitat, contingent on implementation of the accompanying Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP).

## Background & Description
- **Yaqui Chub (*Gila purpurea*)**: Described as a "medium sized fish" of the family [[Cyprinidae]] (Minckley 1973).
- **Taxonomic clarification**: Until 1991, *Gila purpurea* was considered conspecific with chub populations throughout the Rios Sonora, Matape, and Yaqui basins (Hendrickson et al. 1980). DeMarais (1991) established that chubs in the Rios Sonora/Matape and the Rio Yaqui system downstream of San Bernardino Creek constitute a distinct species, **[[Gila Eremica]]**, separating *G. purpurea* as endemic to the San Bernardino Creek drainage.
- **Yaqui Catfish (*Ictalurus pricei*)**: Family [[Ictaluridae]]; no detailed morphometric data provided in this excerpt beyond taxonomic listing and threatened status.
- **Yaqui Longfin Dace**: Referenced taxonomically as the Rio Yaqui form of *Agosia chrysogaster*; no morphological detail given beyond population/distribution status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution**: *Gila purpurea* is endemic to San Bernardino Creek (Arizona/Mexico) and possibly the Willcox Playa basin (Varela-Romero et al. 1990; DeMarais 1991). Extant populations occur in Bathhouse Spring, Black Draw, House Pond, Mesquite Pond, North Pond, Oasis Pond, Robertson Cienega, Twin Pond, and Two MD Ponds on the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (SBNWR).
- **Stocking history**: The [[Leslie Creek]] population was established in 1969 from stock taken at [[Astin Spring]] (Minckley and Brooks 1985). The [[West Turkey Creek]] population in the Chiricahua Mountains was stocked in 1986 and 1991 using Leslie Creek stock raised at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Habitat type**: Spring-fed cienega/pond systems and low-order desert streams; artificial ponds and ditches on ranch lands function as refugia and source populations for stream reintroduction.
- **Co-occurring unlisted taxon**: West Turkey Creek supports the only known extant stock of the Rio Yaqui form of longfin dace in the [[Sulphur Springs Valley]], making its protection a conservation priority to preclude future ESA listing.
- **Threats from non-native species**: Rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*), western mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*), and largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*) have established or expanded within the watershed, alongside bullfrogs (*Rana catesbeiana*), tiger salamanders (*Ambystoma tigrinum*), and crayfish — all posing predation/competition risks to native fishes. The HCP permits stocking of a single lowermost pond with non-reproductive game fish (likely trout) for recreational use, mitigated by inlet/outlet barriers to limit interaction with listed species.
- **Disturbance history**: The 1994 Rattlesnake Fire caused massive ash/sediment runoff into the watershed, nearly eliminating fish and aquatic organisms from streams and ponds; recolonization occurred from unidentified refugia, resulting in mixed native/non-native repopulation of West Turkey Creek and ranch ponds.
- **Habitat management**: HCP measures include rock gabions on upland washes to reduce erosion, increase soil retention, and restore intermittent flow in previously ephemeral washes — intended to progressively improve downstream aquatic habitat quality.

## Key Findings
- **Listing status**: Yaqui chub listed endangered August 31, 1984, with critical habitat limited to "all aquatic habitat on the San Bernardino NWR" (FWS 1984); Leslie Creek was acquired after this designation and is therefore **not** included in critical habitat.
- **Historic occurrence**: An 1896 record (Rutter) documents Yaqui chub in West Turkey Creek, but voucher specimens were lost in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, precluding confirmation (Miller and Lowe 1964).
- **Reintroduction success**: Yaqui chub released onto El Coronado Ranch (ECR) in 1986 (Astin Spring/Leslie Creek lineage) subsequently dispersed naturally into West Turkey Creek; ECR ponds now serve as a refugium/source population.
- **Take determination**: Total incidental take for Yaqui chub, Yaqui catfish, and Yaqui longfin dace is deemed **indeterminable** due to difficulty detecting mortality (carcass detection, cause-of-death ambiguity, unreliable population estimates, and confounding seasonal fluctuations) over the 25-year permit duration.
- **Take triggers/thresholds**: Incidental take is considered exceeded if pond/ditch maintenance surpasses **6.5 acres of ponds and 0.5 miles of ditches** in a calendar year; maintenance of stream diversions and water gates does not count against this threshold.
- **Water diversion effects**: Five existing diversion structures reduce West Turkey Creek flow, primarily during high-runoff periods (spring runoff, monsoon floods) rather than critical low-flow periods, minimizing — but not eliminating — take risk from stranding, injury, or habitat reduction.
- **Cumulative effects**: No additional authorized/foreseeable projects are expected to contribute to cumulative losses; the action is judged to have a **net positive cumulative impact** on the chub and other species of concern across the ECR and West Turkey Creek watershed.
- **Overall jeopardy determination**: The Service found that the anticipated take level is **not likely to result in jeopardy** to the Yaqui chub or Yaqui catfish, nor destruction/adverse modification of critical habitat.
- **Monitoring & reporting**: Annual reports required for first 5 years, triennial for next 15 years, then every 5 years for remainder of permit; reports must include incidental take accounting and may include broader fish/habitat monitoring data.
- **Related taxa flagged for conservation concern**: [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*) is cited in supporting literature (Probst and Stefferud 1994) as a comparably distributed Rio Grande Basin cyprinid of conservation interest, illustrating the broader regional pattern of imperiled Gila-basin cyprinids.
