## Executive Summary

This document is a March 1963 U.S. Department of the Interior review of the **[[Green River Fish Eradication Program]]**, a chemical treatment operation conducted in September 1962 by the Utah and Wyoming game and fish departments under supervision of the **[[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]]**. The stated goal was to eliminate existing (largely native, "undesirable" for sport purposes) fish populations in the **[[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]]** and tailwaters to establish a put-and-take regional trout fishery. While the intended trout fishery goal was achieved, toxic piscicide agents unexpectedly migrated downstream beyond containment measures, causing an unplanned, widespread kill of fish and aquatic insects within **[[Dinosaur National Monument]]**. The report is notable not for morphological or taxonomic data but for its documentation of an ecological management failure, prompting new federal directives on toxicant research, genetic/species conservation risk, and mandatory follow-up population studies.

## Background & Description

The source text is an administrative/policy review and does **not provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical data** on any fish species. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System affected:** The **[[Green River]]** downstream of the newly closed **[[Flaming Gorge Dam]]**, extending into the tailwaters and the aquatic habitats of **[[Dinosaur National Monument]]**.
- **Intended niche shift:** Native fish communities in the reservoir and tailwater were targeted for eradication using piscicides (**[[Rotenone]]** and **[[Potassium Permanganate]]**) to convert the habitat into a coldwater trout fishery, a common mid-20th-century reservoir fisheries management practice.
- **Unintended niche disruption:** Toxic materials were not fully neutralized ("detoxified") before reaching downstream reaches, resulting in a kill affecting both fish and aquatic insect (benthic macroinvertebrate) populations within the protected boundaries of Dinosaur National Monument — an area explicitly outside the operation's intended treatment scope.
- **Post-dam ecological context:** The report acknowledges that closure of **[[Flaming Gorge Dam]]** itself had already begun altering downstream habitat and fish population dynamics in the Monument, independent of the eradication treatment, underscoring cumulative anthropogenic pressures on the river system.
- **Conservation concern raised:** Explicit federal concern for "unique species" and the potential irreversible loss to the regional gene pool, foreshadowing later Endangered Species Act–era concerns for Green River endemics such as **[[Humpback Chub]]**, **[[Bonytail]]**, **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**, and **[[Razorback Sucker]]**.

## Key Findings

1. **Program Outcome (Intended):** The eradication treatment successfully cleared the reservoir and tailwaters of prior fish populations, enabling establishment of a "regional trout fishery" expected to persist "several years," with trout stocking scheduled to begin spring 1963.
2. **Unintended Consequence:** Despite prior planning and chemical detoxification precautions, toxicants spread downstream beyond the intended treatment zone, causing a "widespread kill of fish and aquatic insects" in **[[Dinosaur National Monument]]** — a federally protected area.
3. **Administrative Response:** Due to the disparity between anticipated and actual outcomes, the Secretary's Office conducted a formal review of the operation.
4. **New Directive 1 — Research Requirement:** Future programs must undertake adequate research into the ecological effects of **[[Rotenone]]**, **[[Potassium Permanganate]]**, or other piscicides under varying environmental conditions *before* implementation, with results applied appropriately during execution.
5. **New Directive 2 — Genetic/Species Risk Priority:** Where a "unique species" may be endangered, the potential loss to the genetic pool of living material must be treated as a **dominant consideration** in evaluating whether to proceed with a management project.
6. **New Directive 3 — Independent Oversight:** Future projects will be reviewed to ensure experimental data are properly considered and that potential deleterious effects are assessed by "competent and disinterested parties" (i.e., independent scientific review).
7. **Follow-up Research Mandate:** The National Park Service and Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife were directed to:
   - Conduct **fish population studies** in summer 1963 in Dinosaur National Monument to quantify species and population impairment from the 1962 incident.
   - Design a **longer-range research project** assessing habitat and population changes in the Monument resulting from the closure of **[[Flaming Gorge Dam]]**.
8. **Policy Significance:** This incident is an early federal acknowledgment of unintended ecological damage from piscicide-based fisheries management and helped establish precedent for environmental review procedures in subsequent U.S. fishery management operations.
