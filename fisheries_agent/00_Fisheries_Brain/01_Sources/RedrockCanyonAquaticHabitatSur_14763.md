# Redrock Canyon Aquatic Habitat Survey (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-topminnow

## Executive Summary

This 1996 report by fisheries biologist Jerome A. Stefferud repeats a 1989 photo-point survey of [[Redrock Canyon]], Santa Cruz County, Arizona, to document changes in aquatic habitat after cattle exclusion. The canyon supports the only natural population of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] on [[Coronado National Forest]] lands. Drought in 1995–1996 left very little live water. Surface water shrank in fenced areas, which the author attributes to channel aggradation and vegetation encroachment after grazing stopped. Riparian vegetation recovered inside the exclosures, but unauthorized cattle use in 1996 reversed some of that progress. The report closes with recommendations on road closure, cattle exclusion, nonnative fish removal, and topminnow transplants.

## Background & Description

Redrock Canyon is the only National Forest System site with a natural Gila topminnow population. [[Rinne et al. 1980]] recommended protecting occupied habitat with fencing, removing exotic fishes from tanks, restricting vehicles, and transplanting topminnow to suitable nearby springs. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Recovery Plan repeated these recommendations.

Two management plans followed:

- **[[Redrock Canyon Action Plan]] (1990):** aimed to improve riparian woodland by changing livestock management, building fences, closing roads, modifying channels, and planting riparian trees.
- **[[Arizona Trail]] segment (1992):** Canelo Pass to Patagonia; the trail opened to the public in 1995 and was routed to avoid the exclosures.

Both plans received [[Biological Opinion]]s from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Under the Action Plan, four [[Riparian Exclosure]]s were built:

| Exclosure | Completion | Protected length |
|---|---|---|
| [[Cott Tank]] | 1992 (body text) | about 1.8 miles of stream |
| [[Falls Exclosure]] | 1993 (body text) | about 0.6 miles |
| [[Gate Spring]] | 1993 (body text) | about 0.2 miles |
| [[Pig Camp Spring]] | 1994 | one springhead |

The 1989 report contains the site maps and location data. The 1996 study used the same photo points and the same survey dates.

**Objectives:** Monitor "Determine extent and type of aquatic habitat units" and "Determine distribution of aquatic habitat," as set out in the 1993 monitoring plan.

**Methods:** Photo sites were relocated and rephotographed. Notes covered channel width, substrate, riparian vegetation, live water, and fish presence. In Cott Tank drainage, pool locations and lengths were measured with a hip chain.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Drought:** Southern Arizona was in drought during 1995–1996, which made live water scarce throughout the drainage.

**Channel change:** Inside the exclosures, the stream channel is changing. Without grazing, vegetation stabilizes the bed. [[Aggradation]] (gravel and sand deposition) has filled shallow runs and pools. Recent floods were not strong enough to scour new pools. [[Seepwillow]] has spread along channels outside the exclosures. Because it is shallow-rooted and easily washed out, the report calls the observed channel narrowing and increased definition possibly ephemeral. These patterns depend on rainfall and runoff.

**Fish communities:** Annual autumn sampling since 1988 shows that [[Gila Topminnow]] dominate at Falls, Gate Spring, and lower Cott Tank. [[Western Mosquitofish]] dominate between Pig Camp Spring and Cott Tank. Both species co-occur in Redrock Canyon. Floods and drought are the likely drivers of this pattern. Nonnative [[Bluegill]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] in Cott Tank prey on topminnow. [[Longfin Dace]] were present in the Falls exclosure pools, but no topminnow were observed there in 1996.

**Grazing pressure:** Trespass cattle damaged the exclosures. In Gate Spring, grazing in 1996 reduced herbaceous plants to the root crowns and broke woody stems. In Cott Tank, three animals were present in November 1995 and were still there in 1996. Around 10–15 "wild cows" were reported in one area, and their trail led to Gate Spring.

## Key Findings

- **Live water was scarce:** Perennial water with fish was found at only three locations: Falls, Gate Spring, and Cott Tank drainage. Live water totaled less than 1,000 feet, mostly in isolated pools in Cott Tank drainage. [[Pig Camp Spring]] was dry.
- **Pool loss in Cott Tank:** Of 57 pools counted in the [[Cott Tank]] exclosure, only 21 held water. Surface area and volume of individual pools were 35–50% smaller than in 1989.
- **Falls and Gate Spring declining:** Surface water at [[Falls Exclosure]] decreased after fencing. The lower 500 feet of [[Gate Spring]] held the only surface flow in the exclosure in 1996. A shallow channel that had reformed in 1995 was erased by grazing in 1996.
- **Vegetation recovery:** Herbaceous cover at Falls expanded, and willow and cottonwood saplings established. In Cott Tank, eroded banks were covered by overhanging vegetation, and shading over pools increased. Wet road crossings have nearly healed. Woody recruitment was not occurring in Cott Tank drainage.
- **Channel morphology:** Channels in the exclosures are aggrading, and outside them channels are narrower and more defined. Many channels have downcut 1–3 feet. Some reaches have lost their surface sand and gained coarser substrate.
- **Fish pattern:** Topminnow and mosquitofish coexist, with a distribution pattern shaped by flood and drought events. Cott and Downunder tanks release mosquitofish into the drainage during overflow floods.
- **Management status:** Most Action Plan activities were completed. Others were postponed or dropped. Grazing protection should improve aquatic conditions for topminnow, but further actions are still needed.

**Recommendations:**

1. Close the Redrock Canyon Road between Red Bank Well and Redrock Well to vehicles. Vehicle tracks were seen reaching the Gate Spring exclosure, and rocks had been placed to make a stream crossing.
2. Make sure permittees and hired cowboys know that cattle must stay out of all exclosures. The lower gap fence at Cott Tank was opened on purpose during summer 1996.
3. Work with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to remove nonnative fish. Priority targets are mosquitofish in [[Downunder Tank]] and other exotics in Cott Tank.
4. Evaluate riparian condition in [[Lampshire Canyon]] and [[Oak Grove Canyon]] against Forest Plan standards.
5. Transplant topminnow to Lampshire and Oak Grove canyons and to other waters in the Redrock drainage. Both canyons could support topminnow in years of normal or above-average surface water.

**Data inconsistencies:** Completion dates for the Cott Tank and Falls exclosures differ between the body text (1992 and 1993) and the figure captions (Falls and Gate Spring fenced in 1994, and Cott Tank fenced in 1994). The report also says that the photos should be used together with the 1989 report for location data.
