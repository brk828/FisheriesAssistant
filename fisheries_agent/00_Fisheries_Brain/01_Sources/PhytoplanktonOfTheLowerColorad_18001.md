# Phytoplankton of the Lower Colorado River, Grand Canyon Region

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bacillariophyceae

## Executive Summary

This study by Crayton and Sommerfeld (Arizona State University) documents the species composition, population size and distribution of phytoplankton in the [[Colorado River]] between Lees Ferry and Diamond Creek (225 river miles) in and around [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. Six float-trip sampling periods in 1975–1976 recorded 127 species in 63 genera. Diatoms dominated in both diversity and abundance, and most were tychoplankters (benthic forms occurring in the water column by chance). Phytoplankton populations were very small and variable, with a maximum of about 2,834 organisms/liter. The authors conclude that, although water chemistry appears adequate, low water temperature from the hypolimnion of [[Lake Powell]], high turbidity, short water residence time and fluctuating flows prevent development of a true euplanktonic community.

## Background & Description

- **Motivation:** Phytoplankton data were scarce for the Colorado River and its tributaries. Earlier diatom surveys (Williams and Scott, 1962; U.S. Department of the Interior, 1966) predated the completion of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], which sits about 16 miles (27 km) above Lees Ferry.
- **Regional context:** Related work covered [[Lake Powell]] (Czarnecki and Blinn, 1977; Stewart, 1974), [[Lake Mead]] (Staker, Hoshaw and Everett, 1974), and periphyton in the Grand Canyon (Czarnecki, Blinn and Tompkins, 1976; 345 periphytic algae recorded).
- **Monitoring:** The U.S. Geological Survey began monitoring total phytoplankton and periphyton at a national network site at Lees Ferry in 1975.
- **Study design:** Water was sampled at numerous sites (Table 1 lists 34 sites, from river mile 0.0 to 225.0) during six float trips: April 22–May 5, June 3–12, August 11–21, November 18–25 (1975), and March 1–9 and May 31–June 10 (1976).
- **Methods:** Forty gallons of river water were filtered through a No. 20 plankton net, concentrated to 60 ml, preserved with iodine solution, and counted in Sedgwick-Rafter chambers at 300x. The minimum detectable density was 1.4 organisms/liter. Samples were centrifuged to about 20 ml (3000 rpm, 20 min) because numbers were low. Turbidity data came from [[Cole and Kubly (1976)]], and discharge data from USGS station 0902500 at Grand Canyon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow and temperature:** Water released from the hypolimnion of Lake Powell is usually well below 10 °C. In August 1975 the river warmed only about 0.02 °C per mile from Lees Ferry to Diamond Creek. Knopp (1960) suggested at least 12 °C is needed for a healthy phytoplankton community.
- **Residence time:** Water takes about three days to reach [[Lake Mead]] (roughly 456 km). Kofoid (1903, 1908) and McGaha and Steen (1974) indicate that 9–20 days of flow are typically needed to build an appreciable lotic plankton crop.
- **Turbidity:** Floods from the [[Paria River]] and [[Little Colorado River]] raised turbidity. August values ranged from 1 JTU at Lees Ferry to 334 JTU below the Little Colorado River. Turbidity can reduce photosynthesis, mechanically damage cells, and speed settling of plankton.
- **Nutrients and trace chemistry:** Nutrient levels appeared sufficient for larger populations (Cole and Kubly, 1976). Several heavy metals were present at non-toxic concentrations (Sommerfeld, Crayton and Crane, 1976).
- **Hydrologic variability:** Discharge ranged from 3,400 to 22,200 ft³/s across sampling periods, and turbulence and high velocity further favor tychoplanktonic forms.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 127 species in 63 genera across six divisions. Diatoms (Bacillariophyceae) had 73 species in 27 genera, more than half the total. Chlorophyta had 27 species and Cyanophyta 22. Pyrrophyta had 2 species (*Ceratium carolinianum*, *C. hirundinella*), and Cryptophyta, Euglenophyta and other Chrysophyta groups were represented by a few species each.
- **Seasonal richness:**
  - May: 37 genera, 58 species (highest; turbid, flood-influenced).
  - June: 26 genera, 44 species.
  - August: 32 genera, 46 species.
  - November and March: 42 species each (lowest).
- **Abundance:** Densities were classed as Rare (<100 organisms/liter), Frequent (100–999), or Dominant (≥1,000). Maximum density was about 3,000 organisms/liter in August; March counts were too low to quantify.
- **Dominant taxa:** [[Diatoma Vulgare]], [[Rhoicosphenia Curvata]] and [[Cocconeis Pediculus]] were the most common. *D. vulgare* dominated in August and June 1976; *R. curvata* dominated in November.
- **Euplanktonic taxa:** Rare species such as *Asterionella formosa*, *Ceratium hirundinella* and *Fragilaria crotonensis* appeared in low numbers, probably from releases out of [[Lake Powell]]. *Biddulphia laevis* was found only in single samples at river miles 133, 143 and 225.
- **Comparison with reservoirs:** 12 genera and 8 species from Warm Creek Bay (Lake Powell) were common to the river; 33 genera and 23 species were shared with [[Lake Mead]].
- **Impoundment effects:** Pre-dam densities of six diatom species were 400–1,600 per ml (Williams, 1964). After [[Glen Canyon Dam]], they fell to under 1,000 per liter (Crayton, 1977).
- **No downstream gradient:** No consistent downstream trend in species richness or abundance was found.
- **Conclusion:** Phytoplankton in the river is small in number and mostly tychoplanktonic. Low temperature, high turbidity, short water age and fluctuating flows limit euplanktonic development, even though chemistry appears suitable.
