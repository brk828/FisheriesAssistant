# Arizona Game and Fish Commission Live Wildlife Rules (Article 4, 1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-wildlife-regulation

## Executive Summary

This document is the Article 4 "Live Wildlife Rules" of the [[Arizona Game And Fish Commission]], promulgated under Arizona Revised Statutes 17-306 and effective January 1, 1995. It governs the importation, transport, release, possession, and sale of live wildlife in Arizona. Its central mechanism is the list of [[Restricted Live Wildlife]], which is controlled by a set of [[Special License]] and permit types. The rules cover aquatic species that are regulated because they threaten native fish and ecosystems, including several invasive fish, mussels, and crustaceans. The text is regulatory rather than research-based and reports no ecological field data.

## Background & Description

The rules are organized as follows:

- **R12-4-401 Definitions:** Defines terms such as [[Aquarium Trade]], [[Fish Farm]], [[Live Bait]], [[Live Baitfish]], [[Stock And Stocking]], and [[Restricted Live Wildlife]].
- **R12-4-402 Prohibited acts:** Bans giving away, renting, selling as live bait, propagating, stocking, exporting, or killing captive wildlife, and operating a shooting preserve, except as authorized.
- **R12-4-403 Escaped or released wildlife:** Lets the Department remove wildlife that threatens native wildlife or public safety.
- **R12-4-404 Wildlife taken on a hunting or fishing license:** Permits live possession only where a Commission order sets a live bag and possession limit. Progeny of reptiles and amphibians may be held for 12 months.
- **R12-4-405 Importing without a license:** Allows lawful import of most mammals, birds, and reptiles not listed as restricted. Aquatic wildlife for the aquarium trade, or for licensed restaurants and markets where it is killed before transport, is also exempt.
- **R12-4-406 Restricted species list:** Names restricted mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, crustaceans, and mollusks (detailed below).
- **R12-4-407 Exemptions:** Covers veterinarians, transit through the state (72 hours), fairs and circuses, advertising (10 days), restaurant game fish, and research facilities.
- **R12-4-408 to R12-4-409:** Department holding authority and general special-license provisions, including expiration on December 31, inspections, and penalties.
- **R12-4-410 to R12-4-428:** Individual special licenses and permits, plus captivity standards (R12-4-428).

Director Duane L. Shroufe administered the Commission at the time of issue.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The rules are motivated by the risk that live wildlife poses to native fauna. Under R12-4-401(14), restricted wildlife is wildlife the Commission has determined to be an actual or potential significant threat to indigenous wildlife through **competition, disease or parasites, habitat degradation, predation, or impact on population management**, or to public safety. Several provisions address aquatic ecology directly:

- **Stocking review (R12-4-410):** Applicants proposing to introduce species not previously present must address purpose, expected benefits, adverse economic impacts, dangers to indigenous or game fish, ecological problems, inherent diseases, hybridization, and post-introduction evaluation. Stocked fish must be "compatible with, and pose no threat to," wildlife in the drainage.
- **Disease control (R12-4-410(D)):** Imported fish and eggs must be certified free of listed pathogens, including [[Viral Hemorrhagic Septicemia]], [[Infectious Hematopoietic Necrosis]], [[Infectious Pancreatic Necrosis]], [[Ceratomyxosis]], [[Spring Viremia Of Carp]], [[Bacterial Kidney Disease]], [[Furunculosis]], and [[Whirling Disease]].
- **White amur (R12-4-424):** Triploid [[White Amur]] may be stocked only in [[Closed Aquatic System]]s, to control aquatic weeds or impaired water quality, and watersheds with threatened native wildlife trigger a formal biological impact proposal.
- **Baitfish (R12-4-411):** Live bait species such as [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Goldfish]], [[Mosquito Fish]], [[Red Shiner]], and [[Threadfin Shad]] are regulated, and [[Waterdogs]] are restricted in parts of Santa Cruz and Cochise counties.

## Key Findings

**Restricted aquatic wildlife (R12-4-406, Section E):** The rules list 37 restricted fish, plus crustaceans and mollusks. The most important entries are:

- **Invasive or ecologically damaging fish:** [[Bighead Carp]] (*Aristichthys nobilis*), [[Silver Carp]] (*Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*), [[Crucian Carp]] (*Carassius carassius*), [[Indian Carp]] (*Catla catla*, *Cirrhina mrigala*, *Labeo rahita*), [[Rudd]] (*Scardinius*), and [[Tiger Fish]] (*Hoplias malabaricus*).
- **Predatory and aggressive taxa:** [[Nile Perch]] (*Lates*), [[Piranha]] (several genera), [[Pike]] (Esocidae), [[Bony Tongue]] (*Arapaima gigas*), and [[Sharks]] (multiple orders, marine and freshwater).
- **Broad families:** [[Bass]], [[Catfish]], [[Trout]] (Salmonidae), [[Sunfish]] (Centrarchidae), [[Gars]], [[Shad]] (Clupeidae, except Threadfin Shad), [[Herring]], [[Lampreys]], [[Mooneye]], and [[Goldeye]].
- **Native and game species on the list:** [[American Grayling]] (*Thymallus arcticus*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[White Perch]] (*Morone americanus*), and [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*).
- **Crustaceans and mollusks:** [[Asiatic Mitten Crab]] (*Eriocheir sinensis*), [[Australian Crayfish]] (Parastacidae), and [[Zebra Mussel]] (*Dreissena polymorpha*).

Restricted amphibians include Xenopus clawed frogs and giant or marine toads (*Bufo marinus*, *Bufo horribilis*, *Bufo paracnemis*).

**Special licenses and fees:**

- [[Minnow Dealer License]]: $30
- [[Private Game Farm License]]: $40
- [[Shooting Preserve License]]: $100 (sites up to 1,000 acres)
- [[Field Trial License]]: $5
- [[Wildlife Hobby License]]: $5
- [[Zoo License]]: $100
- [[Falconer License]]: $75 for 3 years
- [[White Amur Stocking License]]: $100
- Stocking, holding, collecting, rehabilitation, and service permits are free.

**Key administrative rules:**

- Stocking permits: Applications for species not previously introduced take up to **60 days**; other applications, **30 days**. Authorization is limited to **20 days**.
- Stocked fish and eggs must be certified by a fish health inspector or pathologist certified by the American Fisheries Society, based on an inspection within the **12 months** before shipment.
- Licenses expire **December 31** of the year issued.
- Rehabilitated wildlife may be held for **90 days**, and raptors for **180 days**, without extension.
- Captive progeny of reptiles and amphibians may be held for **12 months**.
- Desert tortoises possessed before **April 28, 1989** are exempt. Their progeny may be held for **24 months**.
- Three written notices of the same violation within **two years** count as a failure to cure.
- Violators may face criminal charges, license suspension, seizure, or a denial of new licenses for up to **five years**.

**Captivity standards (R12-4-428):** Facilities must prevent escape, provide potable water and wholesome food, keep waterborne species with adequate access to water, isolate diseased animals, and be inspected by a veterinarian at least once a year for animals held one year or more.
