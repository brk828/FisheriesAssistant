# Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification — Biological Assessment, Section 7 Consultation

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the Bureau of Reclamation (Regional Director Robert W. Johnson) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, initiating formal Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act regarding the [[Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification Project]]. The correspondence confirms that the proposed powerplant modification "may affect" two federally endangered freshwater fish species: the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The letter does not contain primary morphological, ecological, or population data; rather, it references an existing Biological Assessment and Final Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) and formally commits Reclamation to consultation and species recovery efforts, particularly for the Razorback Sucker in [[Lake Mohave]].

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) are provided in this source document. The text is purely administrative/regulatory in nature and defers to an unincluded Biological Assessment for species-specific biological detail.

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: Federally listed as endangered (status referenced, no physical description given).
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: Described as "recently designated endangered" at the time of writing (1991), consistent with its listing history; no physical description given.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are associated with the [[Colorado River]] system, specifically the reach influenced by [[Hoover Dam]] and [[Lake Mohave]].
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] is specifically noted as a focal species for active recovery initiatives in [[Lake Mohave]], implying this reservoir serves as critical habitat or a recovery/refuge population site.
- The [[Bonytail Chub]] is mentioned as similarly at risk from the proposed hydropower project modifications, indicating shared habitat overlap with the Razorback Sucker in the project-affected area.
- The ecological context implies that alterations to dam operations (flow, temperature, or power generation regimes) at [[Hoover Dam]] are the mechanism of potential effect on these species' riverine/reservoir habitat.

## Key Findings
- The Bureau of Reclamation's Biological Assessment concluded that the [[Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification Project]] **may affect** the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*).
- The Razorback Sucker had been **recently designated as endangered** at the time of this 1991 correspondence.
- Reclamation cited its "active initiative and commitment" to species recovery, specifically referencing ongoing [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery work in [[Lake Mohave]].
- Environmental commitments had already been made in the **Final Environmental Impact Statement (EIS)** for the powerplant modification project.
- As a result of the FWS's "may affect" determination and stakeholder input (including from Arizona State University researchers Dr. Paul C. Marsh and Dr. W. L. Minckley, Arizona Game and Fish Department, National Park Service, and Nevada Department of Wildlife), Reclamation formally requested initiation of **Section 7 formal consultation** under the Endangered Species Act.
- Distribution list indicates multi-agency and academic involvement in the consultation process, underscoring the regional significance of [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]] and the Lower Colorado River Basin for endangered fish conservation.
