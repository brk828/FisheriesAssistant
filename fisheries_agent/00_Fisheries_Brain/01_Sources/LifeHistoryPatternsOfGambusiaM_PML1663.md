# Life History Patterns of Gambusia marshi (Poeciliidae) from Cuatro Ciénegas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1985 study in *Copeia* (no. 4, pp. 898–905, by Gene K. Meffe) describes the life history and ecology of [[Gambusia Marshi]], a poeciliid fish endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] and the [[Río Salado Drainage]] in [[Coahuila]], Mexico. Before this work the species' life history was virtually unknown. Fish were collected across seasons from natural lakes, rivers and an artificial canal and analyzed for reproduction, diet and parasites. The species reproduces seasonally, its fecundity rises with female size, and its diet is broad, dominated by detritus and insects. Its body condition was consistent across habitats and seasons. The author concludes that its flexible habitat and food use likely explain its local abundance within a small range.

## Background & Description

- **Regional context:** The Cuatro Ciénegas region is an intermontane basin west of Monclova, Coahuila. It is known for a high degree of biological endemism, including aquatic crustaceans, molluscs, fishes and turtles (Taylor and Minckley, 1966; Marsh, 1984; Minckley, 1969).
- **Research gap:** Despite intense interest in the regional fauna, particularly its fishes (reviewed by Minckley, 1984), few comprehensive studies had examined the basic ecology and life histories of its component species.
- **Study species:** *G. marshi* belongs to the family [[Poeciliidae]] (order Cyprinodontiformes, live-bearing "gambusiine" fishes). The study examined it as a habitat and food generalist that is locally abundant within its limited range.
- **Methods (summarized):**
  - Specimens were collected from [[Lagunas]], [[Rivers]] and an [[Artificial Canal]] in different seasons.
  - Fish were sexed, measured for standard length (SL) in size classes, and examined for reproductive state (ova, embryos, [[Superfetation]]).
  - Gut contents were analyzed for diet, and coelomic and gut parasites were recorded.
  - Statistical analyses used [[BMDP Statistical Software]] and length-frequency analysis.
  - Sample sizes per habitat and season are tabulated (Table 2), though the OCR text is only partly legible, so exact counts are not reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat use.** *G. marshi* occurs in all major habitats of the Cuatro Ciénegas system, from natural streams, marshes and lakes to artificial canals. Reproduction was similar across three habitat types (lagunas, rivers and canal), with differences mainly in the timing and length of the breeding season.

**Reproductive seasonality by habitat:**
- **Lagunas:** Reproduction began in January, when about one-third of females carried yolked ova or early embryos. It peaked through autumn (September) and presumably declined afterward. The reproductive season spanned about 9 months.
- **Rivers:** Fish were mostly inactive in December. Most were reproducing in spring and summer, and only about half remained active in September. Rivers also showed reproductive activity from January to September, with smaller broods at the start and end of the season.
- **Canal:** Fish were inactive in late January, with full reproductive activity in spring and summer. By mid-August reproduction was declining, with about two-thirds of fish no longer carrying offspring. The season was shorter than elsewhere, probably running from February or March to August.

**Diet.** *G. marshi* is primarily a detritivore–insectivore. Detritus and insects were the main food items, with other invertebrates, plant material and juvenile fish also present. Feeding varied with season and habitat, and the study reports that a cannibalistic component occurred in some populations. Dietary breadth supports the classification of the species as a food generalist.

**Parasites.** Parasites recorded included two nematodes (one small, one large), a trematode, and an acanthocephalan. Infection levels were generally low, and Table 2 gives infection counts by habitat and season.

**Somatic condition.** Condition, measured as the somatic condition factor (SCF), was consistent among seasons and habitats. Canal fish had the highest average SCF slope (0.0283), but the difference was not statistically significant, and the fish were not in poor condition. Most fish appeared robust. Visceral fat was generally absent or low in reproductively active females (spring and summer) and prevalent in autumn and winter, suggesting fat storage begins when reproduction declines.

## Key Findings

1. **Seasonal reproduction:** *G. marshi* reproduces seasonally, with reproductive activity varying by habitat. Lagunas had the longest season (about 9 months), rivers were intermediate, and the canal had the shortest (about February/March to August).
2. **Fecundity and body size:** Fecundity increases with female size. Larger females carried the largest broods, particularly in lagunas.
3. **Reproductive effort (RE):** RE generally increased with SL but was less dependent on body size than total reproduction. The author suggests that energetic investment in reproduction is flexible.
4. **Limited habitat influence:** Reproduction was not strongly habitat dependent. The main exception was reduced reproductive activity in the artificial canal, which is curious because canal fish were in good condition.
5. **Superfetation was rare:** Superfetation was observed in only 6 females (2.4%). Occurrences were recorded in the canal (once), lagunas and rivers, across several seasons.
6. **Diet breadth:** Detritus and insects were the major food items, with other invertebrates, plant matter and juvenile fish also consumed. This breadth reflects a generalist feeding strategy.
7. **Somatic condition:** Body condition was little affected by habitat or season, and most individuals were robust.
8. **Parasite fauna:** Four parasite types were found: two nematodes, one trematode and one acanthocephalan. Parasite burdens were generally light.
9. **Ecological generalism:** *G. marshi* is a habitat and food generalist. The author argues this flexibility likely explains its local abundance and success within a restricted geographic range.
10. **Comparison with congeners:** *G. marshi* is more of a habitat generalist than several restricted-range species, including [[Gambusia Amistadensis]], *G. gaigei*, *G. geiseri*, *G. georgei*, *G. heterochir*, *G. krumholzi* and *G. longispinis*. It is probably less generalized than the cosmopolitan [[Gambusia Affinis]].
11. **Typical gambusiine pattern:** The life history of *G. marshi* matches that of gambusiine fishes generally: seasonal breeding, generalist feeding and fecundity increasing with female size.

**Overall conclusion:** The study provides the first comprehensive account of *G. marshi* life history. The species' reproductive flexibility, broad diet and general habitat tolerance together appear to account for its abundance within the limited Cuatro Ciénegas and Río Salado range.
