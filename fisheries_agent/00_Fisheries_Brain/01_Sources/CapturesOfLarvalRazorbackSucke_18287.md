# Captures of Larval Razorback Sucker in Upper Colorado River Basin 1992-1995

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #larval-ecology

## Executive Summary

This map documents the spatial distribution and abundance of larval [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) captures throughout the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] during the period 1992-1995. The survey covered major river systems including the [[Green River]], [[Colorado River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Gunnison River]], and [[Duchesne River]] across Wyoming, Utah, Colorado, and Arizona. Capture locations are categorized by abundance classes ranging from 1-15 larvae to over 500 larvae per site, providing critical baseline data on recruitment patterns for this endangered catostomid species.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large-bodied catostomid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. This species is characterized by a distinctive sharp-edged keel behind the head. Larval sampling provides essential information on spawning success and recruitment, which are critical parameters for assessing population viability of this endangered species. The survey period (1992-1995) represents an important baseline for understanding historical distribution patterns of early life stages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses a vast drainage system spanning multiple states. Key sampling locations included:

- [[Green River]] system (Wyoming and Utah)
- [[Colorado River]] mainstem with notable captures at River Mile (RM) 170.3 and RM 6.2
- [[Duchesne River]] at RM 248.0
- [[Gunnison River]] system
- [[San Juan River]] extending to [[Lake Powell]]

Major reference points included towns such as [[Glenwood Springs]], [[Grand Junction]], [[Delta]], [[Moab]], and [[Bluff]]. The riverine habitats sampled represent diverse flow regimes and geomorphological conditions across the basin.

## Key Findings

**Larval Capture Distribution:**
- Larvae were captured at multiple locations throughout the basin with highly variable abundances
- Abundance categories documented: 1-15 larvae, 16-50 larvae, 51-100 larvae, 101-270 larvae, and >500 larvae per location
- Highest concentrations (>500 larvae) were documented at specific sites, indicating potential spawning hotspots
- The [[Colorado River]] showed captures at RM 170.3 and RM 6.2, suggesting spawning activity along the mainstem
- The [[Duchesne River]] confluence area (RM 248.0) yielded larval captures
- Distribution extended from upstream reaches in Wyoming through the lower basin near [[Lake Powell]]

**Geographic Scope:**
- Survey encompassed the entire [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] drainage (approximately 62,000 square units)
- Multi-state coverage provided comprehensive assessment of larval distribution across the species' historical range
- Captures documented in both mainstem rivers and major tributaries, indicating multiple potential spawning areas during this period
