## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory permit report/renewal application submitted by W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Permit PRT-676811), covering activities in 1993 and requests for 1994. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the report catalogs authorized "take" (capture, handling, propagation, and research use) of twelve federally listed or sensitive fish taxa from Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, and California. It documents ongoing graduate and faculty research programs at ASU addressing the genetics, taxonomy, phylogeny, ecology, and captive propagation of endangered southwestern fishes, framed explicitly within federal recovery-plan objectives. The note synthesizes the taxa, personnel, research foci, and management context described in the source.

## Background & Description

The source contains minimal direct morphological description, as it is an administrative permit document rather than a primary research paper. Morphology-related research programs referenced include:

- **[[Xyrauchen Texanus]]** (razorback sucker): Ph.D./M.S. research on **ontogenetic changes with age** and **gerontological effects on morphology**, based on wild-caught and hatchery-reared specimens housed in museum collections (C. R. Reimus).
- **[[Rhinichthys Osculus]]** (speckled dace): Range-wide phylogenetic analysis of diverse populations using mtDNA, with implications for morphological/taxonomic differentiation (D. Oakey).
- **[[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]]** (Gila topminnow): Studies on the **effects of inbreeding on morphology and ecology** (R. Scheffer), tied to a pending NSF-funded inbreeding study (P. Hedrick, W. L. Minckley).
- **Gila spp. taxonomy**: Ongoing biometric, morphological, and phylogenetic analyses of the genus *Gila* (M. E. Douglas), relevant to distinguishing taxa such as [[Gila Cypha]], [[Gila Elegans]], and [[Gila Seminuda]].
- No quantitative morphometric data (length, weight, meristics, coloration) are reported in this document; specimen "take" is only specified numerically per locality (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The taxa covered occupy a range of lotic, lentic, and spring habitats across the arid American Southwest, reflecting the highly endemic and fragmented nature of desert fish distributions:

- **[[Little Colorado River]]**: Habitat of *Gila cypha* (humpback chub); subject of U.S. Bureau of Reclamation-funded taxonomic and ecological study.
- **[[Lake Mohave]]** (Colorado River): Key habitat for *Xyrauchen texanus* population structure/ecology and *Gila elegans* movement studies (P. Marsh; incoming G. Knowles research).
- **[[Virgin River]]**: Site of a 1988 rotenone spill affecting native fish populations; subject of genetic-impact research (R. Timmons) relevant to *Gila seminuda* (Virgin chub) and other endemics.
- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]**, Arizona: Native fish community and population structure studied by A. Velasco, representing a relatively intact desert stream ecosystem.
- **[[Quitobaquito Springs]]**: Isolated spring habitat for *Cyprinodon macularius eremus* (Quitobaquito pupfish), an extremely range-restricted desert pupfish.
- Broader **Colorado River and Gila River basins**: Encompass habitats for *Ptychocheilus lucius* (Colorado squawfish/pikeminnow), *Plagopterus argentissimus* (woundfin), *Gila purpurea* (Yaqui chub), and *Oncorhynchus gilae* (Gila trout, a coldwater headwater species contrasting with the warmwater desert taxa).
- Captive/artificial habitats: **Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center** and **Arizona State University facilities** serve as refugial and experimental holding environments for propagation and genetic studies.

- **Conservation policy priority**: Explicit institutional preference for using hatchery- or captive-produced individuals over wild-caught specimens; wild fish taken only when genetic or biological questions cannot otherwise be resolved.

- **Active research programs** linked to recovery objectives:
  - Genetics and taxonomy of *Xyrauchen texanus* (USBR-funded; Dowling, Minckley).
  - Taxonomy/phylogeny of *Gila* spp. (USBR-funded; Douglas, Dowling).
  - Population ecology and management of *Xyrauchen texanus* in [[Lake Mohave]] (Marsh).
  - Inbreeding effects in *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* (pending NSF grant; Hedrick, Minckley, Scheffer).
  - Range-wide mtDNA phylogeny of *Rhinichthys osculus* (Oakey).
  - Genetic impacts of the 1988 Virgin River rotenone spill (Timmons).
  - Native fish community structure in Aravaipa Creek (Velasco).
  - Genetics/phylogeny of *Agosia chrysogaster* and prior M.S. work on *[[Lepidomeda Vittata]]* (Tibbets).

- **Institutional/administrative role**: W. L. Minckley chairs the **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]**; P. Marsh and Minckley participate in the **Big-River Fishes Work Group**; all listed faculty serve as advisors/consultants to federal and state recovery teams.

- **Animal husbandry note**: A bacterial fin-rot outbreak (*Aeromonas* sp.) in newborn *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* (Gila topminnow) laboratory stock was successfully controlled using increased salinity (NaCl) as a prophylactic treatment; no other unexplained laboratory mortalities were recorded over the preceding five years.

- **Specimen disposition policy**: Preserved specimens are curated in the Arizona State University Collections (M. Douglas, curator) under prior agreement with USFWS Albuquerque; live specimens are returned to USFWS facilities or otherwise disposed of per investigator agreements.

- **Related Ph.D. research** (not under this specific permit): Artificial spawning and larval rearing of *Xyrauchen texanus* by Michael Horn (now USBR, Boulder City, NV), conducted for dissertation research purposes.
