# Chromis Cuvier in Desmarest, 1814: Nomenclatural Gender Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Pomacentridae #Cichlidae #Pseudochromidae #zoological-nomenclature #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1980 submission to the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN) addresses a long-standing taxonomic ambiguity surrounding the grammatical gender of the damselfish genus **[[Chromis]]** (family [[Pomacentridae]]) and, by extension, all genus-group names ending in the suffix **-chromis** across multiple fish families. The authors — Bailey, Robins, and Greenwood — build on [[Emery 1975]]'s historical review, which established that *Chromis* was validly proposed by **Cuvier in Desmarest, 1814**, with **Sparus chromis Linnaeus, 1758** (the [[Mediterranean Chromis]]/damselfish, *Chromis chromis*) as its type species by original designation. While Emery concluded the genus was technically feminine (based on Cuvier's 1815 inclusion of *castanea* and *nilotica*), the present authors argue for a **masculine** designation on grounds of nomenclatural stability, historical predominance of masculine usage, and to prevent taxonomic chaos across the more than 70 genus-group names ending in -chromis (spanning [[Pomacentridae]], [[Cichlidae]], and [[Pseudochromidae]]). The Commission is formally petitioned to use its plenary powers to fix the gender of *Chromis* and all -chromis-suffixed genera as masculine, and to place *Chromis* Cuvier and *Sparus chromis* Linnaeus on the Official Lists of Generic and Specific Names in Zoology, respectively.

## Background & Description

This paper is a nomenclatural/taxonomic case study rather than a morphological description; no anatomical or meristic data for *Chromis* species are presented. However, key taxonomic identity points are established:

- **Type species**: *Sparus chromis* Linnaeus, 1758 (p. 280), originally described in the now-obsolete genus *Sparus*, later transferred to *Chromis*.
- **Original vernacular reference**: Desmarest (1814) referred to the type species by the French common name "le Petit Castagneau," noting it was universally called *Sparus chromis* by prior authors and warranted a new genus.
- **Species historically included by Cuvier (1815)** under *Chromis*: *Chromis castanea*, *Labrus niloticus* (= *Chromis nilotica*), and *Labrus punctatus* — this mixed authorship is central to the gender dispute, since *castanea* and *nilotica* carry feminine adjectival endings while the genus's classical Greek root (*chromis*/*chromios*, a sea fish) is of ambiguous/variable gender in antiquity.
- **Homonymy note**: *Chromis* Gronow, 1854 (family [[Pomatomidae]]) is a junior homonym and preoccupied; *Chromis* Cuvier, 1817 and *Chromis* (Cuvier) Günther, 1872 represent separate, unrelated usages within [[Cichlidae]] (partly synonymous with [[Tilapia]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not the primary focus, the paper situates *Chromis* and allied -chromis genera within broader ecological and applied contexts:

- **Pomacentridae** (damselfishes, including *Chromis*) are described as reef-associated marine fishes of major importance to **marine aquarists** and as frequent subjects of **ethological (behavioral) research**.
- **Cichlidae** genera ending in -chromis (57 genera enumerated) are predominantly **African rift-lake and riverine fishes** (e.g., Lake Malawi/Nyasa, Lake Victoria, Lake Edward, Lake George, Lake Nabugabo, Lake Kivu), many historically lumped under the genus **[[Haplochromis]]** sensu lato. These cichlids are noted as economically and scientifically important **aquarium fishes** and increasingly used as **behavioral/experimental model organisms**.
- **Pseudochromidae** genera (dottybacks) are also referenced, including *Anisochromis*, noted for including a protogynous hermaphroditic species (*Anisochromis straussi*, per Springer, Smith & Fraser, 1977).
- The taxonomic instability discussed reflects real biogeographic complexity: approximately 300 haplochromine cichlid species from the African Great Lakes were undergoing active reclassification at time of writing, with many species provisionally reassigned to genera such as **[[Cyrtocara]]**.

## Key Findings

1. **Historical priority established**: *Chromis* Cuvier in Desmarest, 1814 (p. 88) is the valid original citation, predating Cuvier's 1815 publication; Desmarest's account is confirmed to include multiple species (not merely the single title species), satisfying binomial nomenclature requirements — contra Emery's (1975) interpretation.

2. **Gender ambiguity in classical Greek**: Consultation with a classical studies authority (Dr. H.D. Cameron, University of Michigan) confirmed there is no definitive historical basis for assigning gender to the Greek root *chromis*/*chromios*; the matter is deemed a practical, modern nomenclatural decision rather than a resolvable linguistic one.

3. **Predominant usage favors masculine**: A survey of major ichthyological reference works (Beaufort 1940, Bini 1968, Blache/Cadenat/Stauch 1970, Fowler 1928, Herre 1953, Jordan & Evermann 1898, Marshall 1964, Meek & Hildebrand 1925, Metzelaar 1919, Monod 1973, Munro 1955/1967, Parr 1930, Shiino 1976, J.L.B. Smith 1965, Smith & Smith 1969, Woods & Schultz 1960) shows overwhelming masculine treatment of *Chromis*; only Allen (1975), Böhlke & Chaplin (1968), Mago-Leccia (1970), and Randall (1968) used feminine forms — largely traceable to the American Fisheries Society's 1960 checklist (later reversed in 1970).

4. **Broader taxonomic stakes**: More than 70 genus-group names ending in -chromis are cataloged across Pomacentridae, Pseudochromidae, Pomatomidae, and Cichlidae (with 57 in Cichlidae alone), of which roughly 50 are currently accepted genera. Only **[[Papiliochromis]]** (Kullander, 1977; now a junior synonym of **[[Microgeophagus]]**) had been explicitly treated as feminine, apparently influenced directly by Emery's 1975 paper.

5. **Risk of nomenclatural chaos**: The authors warn that without a uniform Commission ruling, gender assignment for newly described -chromis species (especially those with adjectival species epithets) would be applied haphazardly by individual authors, and taxonomic transfers of species between genera of differing assigned gender would create endless orthographic inconsistency.

6. **Precedent cited**: The ICZN's prior 1974 plenary ruling fixing all genus names ending in **-ops** as masculine (Bull. zool. Nom. 31(1): 81–83) is invoked as a direct precedent for the requested action.

7. **Formal requests to the Commission**:
   - (a) Rule that *Chromis* Cuvier in Desmarest, 1814, is **masculine**.
   - (b) Rule that **all** generic names ending in **-chromis** are masculine.
   - (c) Place *Chromis* Cuvier in Desmarest, 1814 (type species *Sparus chromis* Linnaeus, 1758, by original designation) on the **Official List of Generic Names in Zoology**.
   - (d) Place the specific name *chromis* Linnaeus, 1758 (as in *Sparus chromis*) on the **Official List of Specific Names in Zoology**.
