# Basic Survey of Lake Mohave (1955 Completion Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 completion report by [[Richard A. Wagner]] for the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Wildlife Restoration Division) documents a basic survey of [[Lake Mohave]], a young reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] between [[Davis Dam]] and [[Boulder Dam]]. The survey aimed to determine fishery stability, species composition, fish food availability, fishing pressure, catch per angler hour, growth and age of harvested fish, and to recommend management actions. The lake supports a fishery dominated by [[Rainbow Trout]] in [[Black Canyon]] and [[Largemouth Black Bass]] in the rest of the lake. Water-level fluctuation, warm releases from Boulder Dam, and a shortage of forage were identified as the main constraints. The report recommends annual trout plantings, stocking of [[Kokanee]] as planktivorous forage, introduction of [[Threadfin Shad]] for bass and other predators, and continued limnological study.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and context.** Anticipated road construction and growing urban populations were expected to raise fishing pressure sharply. The survey was designed to supply data for a comprehensive management plan.

**Objectives**
- Determine the stability, relative numbers and species of the game fishery.
- Determine fish food indexes and the effects of migration.
- Determine fishing pressure, catch per angler hour, growth rate and average age of harvested fish.
- Make recommendations for fishery management.

**Methods**
- **Fish sampling:** [[Gillnetting]], spot poisoning and seining, used periodically to assess species presence and relative abundance.
- **Food indexes:** plankton net, square-foot counter, fine-mesh dip net, and periodic [[Stomach Content Analysis]].
- **Creel census:** organized with the cooperation of boat landing concessionaires to estimate pressure, catch per angler hour and growth.
- **Age and growth:** [[Scale Analysis]] and standard-length measurements.

**Physical setting**
- Lake length: 67 miles. Roughly 22 miles lie in Black Canyon (north), 7 miles in Pyramid Canyon (south), and 38 miles are alternating narrows and basins. The largest basin is [[Cottonwood Basin]] (about 11 miles long, 5 miles wide), and the [[The Basin]] area is the center of bass production.
- Surface area: about 28,000 acres. Average width: 0.5 miles, ranging from 100 yards to 5 miles.
- Volume: the report gives a maximum theoretical volume of 1,892,000 acre-feet in one place and about 1,800,000 acre-feet in the summary. Dead storage at 1,533.39 ft elevation is 8,530 acre-feet.
- Shoreline: over 200 miles, largely steep-sided canyons with side washes.
- Climate: high winds, low rainfall, high evaporation, and intense sunlight. Vegetation in the watershed is almost absent.
- Access: four main ports of entry, two on the Arizona side (Willow Beach and Katherine Wash) and two on the Nevada side (El Dorado Landing and Cottonwood Cove), plus [[Lake Mohave Resort]].

**Fish species**
The report lists about 13 species in its table, with several noted as only reported or uncertain.
- **Game fish:** [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], a hybrid trout (*Salmo irideus lewisi*), [[Largemouth Black Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bullhead Catfish]], [[Bluegill]] and [[Green Sunfish]].
- **Native fish of concern:** [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Suckers]].
- **Non-native:** [[Carp]].
- Brown and brook trout were not captured or reported in the survey. Various cyprinids and catostomids were reported but not observed.
- Only five species were considered game fish, and only rainbow trout and largemouth bass supported a real fishery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Two aquatic zones**
1. **Black Canyon (Boulder Dam to Chalk Cliffs, about 22 miles):** a river-like reach with a perceptible current. Average water temperature is 56.9 °F, varying from 55 °F in winter to 79 °F in summer. Water quality and temperature are suitable for trout, but water-level fluctuation of up to 22 feet is not.
2. **Lower lake (Black Canyon south to Davis Dam):** warmer surface layers suited to bass, with a cold bottom current flowing toward Davis Dam.

**Water temperature and oxygen**
- Compressed air injected into penstock water at Boulder Dam raises dissolved oxygen (D.O.). Water directly below Boulder Dam measured 9 ppm D.O., and water behind Davis Dam measured 8 ppm. Wind mixing keeps most of the lake above 7 ppm.
- Lake Mohave has high turnover from turbine intake and exhaust. The report describes it as the coldest warm-water fish lake in the state.
- Surface temperatures in the main lake exceed 75 °F and often exceed 80 °F in protected coves in summer.
- The [[Thermal Stratification]] of the lake is shaped by wind. North winds push cold water south, and south winds stack warm surface water against the Chalk Cliffs.
- In 1951, refilling with warm surface water from [[Lake Mead]] (at times above 80 °F) forced most trout to move downstream or die.

**Water-level fluctuation**
Drawdowns in spring and summer, and refilling in late summer and fall, prevent the growth of submerged vegetation. Sago pondweed, spiny naiad, widgeon grass, horned pondweed and muskgrass are present as brood stock, but only [[Blue-Green Algae]] tolerate the fluctuation. Reducing fluctuation would allow these plants to recolonize.

**Food web**
- **Plankton:** Lake Mohave receives plankton from Lake Mead; Lake Mead's own water supply is essentially free of plankton. The lowest plankton level recorded in Black Canyon was 0.1 cc per 100,000 cc of water, which the report considers enough to sustain fry growth.
- **Benthic and invertebrate food:** [[Chironomids]] are abundant but small, so trout must feed nearly continuously. [[Scuds]] (*Gammarus* sp.) and snails are also present. [[Freshwater Shrimp]] and [[Crayfish]] are recommended as more mobile alternatives that convert filamentous algae to animal protein.
- **Silt:** Boulder Dam and Lake Mead trap nearly all sediment, so very little silt enters Lake Mohave.

**Predation and mortality**
- [[Bonytail]] were the most abundant species in gillnets, and their diet eliminated planted rainbow fry smaller than about 2.5 inches.
- Only 20 percent of piscivorous birds collected had game fish in their stomachs.
- Largemouth bass were scarce above Willow Beach, and the few below Boulder Dam were being removed by anglers and were not reproducing successfully.
- Depredation by bonytail, piscivorous trout and birds caused mortality beyond what could be economically absorbed.

**Migration and dam effects**
- Davis Dam releases more than 16,000 cfs, creating a bottom current that carries fish downstream. Rainbow trout tagged at Boulder Dam, Willow Beach and El Dorado were recovered below Davis Dam, some with wounds possibly from turbine blades.
- Neither dam has a fish ladder, so upstream migration is blocked. The last Colorado squawfish was reported in 1952 and none were captured during the survey.

## Key Findings

**Fishing pressure and harvest**
- Bass fishermen outnumbered trout fishermen by at least 2:1 in 1953, partly due to rumors that the Willow Beach trout fishery had collapsed.
- Trout were harvested at 19,782 fish in 14,973 angling hours, mainly in Black Canyon.
- Bass were harvested at 9,398 fish in 22,795 angling hours, with the Basin the most productive area.
- Landing use: El Dorado accounted for about 44 percent of visits in 1952 and 35.8 percent in 1953. Willow Beach accounted for 36.3 percent in 1952 and 24.3 percent in 1953. Lake Mohave Resort rose from 6.6 percent to 28.1 percent.
- Creel-based spending estimates were over $10.00 per day, twice a national Fish and Wildlife Service estimate.
- Monthly visits were highest in winter and early spring, and the catch per angler hour at Willow Beach varied from roughly 0.4 to 2.6 fish.

**Rainbow trout**
- The fishery depends almost entirely on hatchery fish. Natural recruitment is negligible because spawning is extended and water-level changes kill juveniles.
- Stocking records from January 1945 to November 1953 list many plantings, ranging from 1,500 to about 1.15 million fish, mostly fry 0.75–1 inch. Fish stocked at 4–6 inches and 9–10 inches were also planted.
- A tagging study released 750 fish before October 21, 1953. About 10 percent were returned when caught under 15 inches, but only one fish over 15 inches was recovered.
- Scale studies tentatively suggested ages: 12-inch fish are yearlings, 15-1/4 to 17-3/4 inch fish are up to 3 years, and 18-1/2 to 28 inch fish are 6–9 years.
- An estimated 1,250 rainbow trout spawned on a gravel bed one mile below Boulder Dam.

**Largemouth bass**
- Spawning in Lake Mohave occurs several weeks later than in Lake Mead, often in May or June, and males guard nests for 1–2 weeks.
- The average bass caught in 1953 was about 14 inches (third year of life).
- Growth is slower than in Lake Mead. Yearling bass are probably no more than 8 inches, with Basin fish possibly about 2 inches larger and Pyramid Canyon fish not reaching this size.
- Forage is limited after six to eight years of impoundment, a pattern seen in other Arizona reservoirs.

**Other species**
- [[Channel Catfish]] occur only incidentally, mostly under 2 pounds.
- [[Black Crappie]] are present in most of the lake but absent from Black Canyon, though fish from Boulder Dam have been seen drifting downstream.
- [[Bluegill]] growth may be about half that of Lake Mead bluegill. [[Green Sunfish]] make up only about 15 percent of the bluegill population.
- Razorback sucker is eaten by bass only rarely, and its importance to the sport fishery is doubtful.

**Recommendations**
1. Establish and maintain annual plantings of 2-1/4 to 3 inch rainbow trout fingerlings (about 150 per pound) at 600 to 750 per surface acre in Black Canyon.
2. Establish trout food organisms, such as [[Freshwater Shrimp]] and [[Crayfish]], that are compatible with present conditions. Stock only cool, flowing, rubble-bottomed habitats.
3. Establish a rainbow forage-fish fishery by annual plantings of Kokanee, at least 50,000 per plant. Kokanee are planktivorous, pelagic and four-year fish, and fall spawning may allow natural propagation.
4. Introduce [[Threadfin Shad]] as a more suitable forage fish for largemouth bass, crappie and catfish.
5. Continue [[Limnology]] studies until fishery conditions stabilize.

**Summary conclusions**
- Only rainbow trout and largemouth bass support a fishery among the five game fish present.
- The trout fishery is mainly in Black Canyon. Small trout grow well until about 12 inches, but larger fish grow slowly because of limited food.
- The bass fishery is well established, and the growth rate is comparable to other good waters. Introducing a suitable forage fish is still recommended.
- Fluctuating water levels are the central management problem and should be reduced where prior commitments allow.
