# Physiographic, Geological and Hydrographic Bases of Mexico

#physical-geography #hydrography #mexico

## Executive Summary

This excerpt from [[Vegetacion De Mexico]] (J. Rzedowski, Escuela Nacional de Ciencias Biologicas, IPN; coastal marine vegetation section by Laura Huerta M.) provides the physical framework for the country's vegetation. It covers the national territory's shape and coastline, the main mountain systems, lowlands and plateaus, geological history, and surface hydrography. The text does not address any fish, invertebrate or other organisms directly. Its ecological relevance lies in describing the landforms, rock types, climates and drainage patterns that structure the habitats of the country.

## Background & Description

**Territory and coastline**
- Area of about 2 million km², spread roughly equally on either side of the Tropic of Cancer.
- Southern and northern extremes at 14°30'N and 32°42'N.
- Borders total about 13,000 km; roughly three-quarters of this is coastline.
- The country is bordered by the Pacific to the west and south, the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean to the east, and the Gulf of California (Mar de Cortés) along part of its northwest side.
- The Pacific coastline is more than twice as long as the Atlantic coastline, yet the marine influence is stronger on the Gulf of Mexico side.
- The Isthmus of Tehuantepec is about 140 km wide at its narrowest.

**Peninsulas and islands**
- [[Baja California Peninsula]]: a narrow strip more than 1,200 km long, averaging about 100 km wide, with island-like conditions over much of its area.
- [[Yucatan Peninsula]]: trapezoidal, sharing borders with Guatemala and Belize.
- Gulf and Caribbean islands are mostly small, reef-derived, and of low relief.
- Pacific islands are mainly continental-origin hills rising from the seafloor.
- The only truly oceanic islands are the volcanic [[Revillagigedo Islands]], which lie more than 400 km from Cabo San Lucas and more than 500 km from Cabo Corrientes and rise from depths greater than 3,000 m.

**Relief and altitude**
- Less than 35% of the territory lies below 500 m; more than half lies above 1,000 m.
- Elevations above 4,000 m line up largely along about 19°N.
- The highest peak is [[Pico De Orizaba]] at 5,653 m.

**Major mountain systems**
- [[Sierra Madre Occidental]]: the longest and most continuous range, more than 200 km wide in places, with summits slightly above 3,000 m and plateau levels of 2,000–2,500 m; deeply incised by barrancas.
- [[Sierra Madre Oriental]]: rises steeply from the northeast coastal plain; includes Cerro Potosí (3,650 m) and Cerro San Antonio (3,450 m).
- [[Eje Volcanico Transversal]]: a discontinuous volcanic range near 19–20°N, separating the Altiplano from the Balsas Depression, with peaks including Popocatépetl (5,450 m), Ixtaccíhuatl, Nevado de Toluca (4,560 m), Nevado de Colima (4,340 m), Tancítaro (4,160 m) and Cofre de Perote (4,090 m).
- [[Sierra Madre Del Sur]]: runs parallel to the Pacific coast from Jalisco to the Isthmus, generally above 1,000 m, with Cerro Teotepec (about 3,400 m) in Guerrero.
- Northern Oaxaca system: highly dissected terrain, mostly above 1,000 m; Zempoaltépetl at 3,400 m.
- Central Chiapas Massif: reaches about 2,860 m near San Cristóbal.
- Sierra Madre de Chiapas: a Pacific-side range with a narrow coastal plain; Tacaná at 4,064 m on the Guatemalan border.

**Lowlands and plateaus**
- [[Altiplano Mexicano]]: an elevated, interrupted plateau occupying at least a quarter of the national territory, mostly near 2,000 m in the south and 1,000–1,500 m in the north.
- Northwest Coastal Plain: extends from the Colorado River delta to central Nayarit, widest along Sonora.
- Northeast Coastal Plain: widest in the north, bounded by the Sierra Madre Oriental.
- [[Depresion Del Balsas]]: lowlands between the Eje Volcanico Transversal and the Sierra Madre del Sur, with lowest parts at 300–500 m.
- Southeast Coastal Plain: Isthmus, most of Tabasco, parts of Chiapas and the Yucatan Peninsula.
- Central Depression of Chiapas: drained by the [[Rio Grijalva]], with lowest parts at 400–800 m.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Landform processes**
- Folded and faulted structures dominate the Sierra Madre ranges.
- Truncated volcanic cones are common in recently active volcanic areas, especially northern Michoacán.
- Young lava flows are so permeable that surface drainage often fails even on steep slopes.
- Mesas capped by horizontal strata are typical of arid and semi-arid areas (San Luis Potosí, Zacatecas, Baja California Sur).
- Karst landscapes (dolines, sinkholes, lapiés, "fuentes vauclusianas") develop on limestone under humid or semi-humid conditions.

**Arid-zone landscapes**
- Bajadas, alluvial fans and "bolsones" and "semibolsones" characterize the Altiplano and Sonoran plain.
- Sand dunes are uncommon but locally important, for example at Samalayuca and in the Desierto de Vizcaíno.
- The Yucatan Peninsula is a calcareous plateau with almost no surface drainage and a thin soil layer.

**Geological history**
- Sedimentary rocks of Paleozoic age are scarce, so detailed reconstruction is limited.
- During the Paleozoic, large marine transgressions and regressions repeatedly affected the country, which was for long periods a peninsula.
- Permian seas occupied parts of Sonora and linked west Texas with the Gulf of Mexico through Coahuila, southern Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí and Veracruz.
- The Coahuilan Orogeny (late Permian to first half of the Triassic) affected much of the country from Chihuahua to Chiapas.
- In the Jurassic and Cretaceous, large areas were submerged. In the Late Cretaceous the sea covered nearly all of Mexico, until the [[Hidalguense Orogeny]] (also called Laramide) began definitive emergence.
- The Eocene Hidalguense Orogeny uplifted the Sierra Madre Oriental and parallel ranges.
- Volcanism peaked in the Oligocene–Miocene (Sierra Madre Occidental, Altiplano) and again in the Pliocene–Pleistocene (central Mexico, Eje Volcanico Transversal, which is the youngest range).
- The Gulf of California appeared in the Oligocene–Miocene and was larger during part of the Pliocene.
- Connections with Central America were made and broken several times during the Cenozoic; the northern land connection was continuous.
- Pleistocene glaciations cooled the climate enough to shift some thermal zones southward and downslope. Their effect on the country's water balance is unknown.

**Surface lithology**
- Volcanic rocks (Cenozoic and Pleistocene) dominate the northwest, west and center, and are absent from the Yucatan Peninsula.
- Marine sedimentary rocks (mainly limestones, with marls, shales and calcareous sandstones) dominate the east and southeast, including the Sierra Madre Oriental and the Yucatan Peninsula.
- Metamorphic rocks (gneiss, slates and schists) run along the Sierra Madre del Sur and Sierra Madre de Chiapas.
- Intrusive rocks, mainly granites, dominate northern Baja California.
- Alluvial deposits cover large parts of the northwest coastal plain, the Altiplano, and the region from southern Veracruz to southwestern Campeche.

**Hydrography**
- Major Pacific basins: Yaqui, Fuerte, Mezquital, Lerma-Santiago, Balsas.
- Major Gulf of Mexico basins: Bravo, Pánuco, Papaloapan, Grijalva, Usumacinta.
- Endorheic basins include the Nazas, Aguanaval and Casas Grandes; the Bolsón de Mapimí and Bolsón del Salado are closed basins.
- Most of the Yucatan Peninsula is arheic, with groundwater as the main water movement.
- Karst areas in the east and southeast have low surface runoff but defined stream channels.
- Coastal plains with poor drainage (parts of Baja California, Sonora, Tamaulipas, the Marismas Nacionales of Nayarit, and the lower Pánuco, Papaloapan and Istmo-Tabasco plains) are the most flood-prone.
- Many Altiplano closed basins hold intermittent saline, often alkaline, lagunas; the south of the Altiplano has permanent freshwater lakes.
- Coastal lagunas range from fresh to saline, vary seasonally in salinity, are shallow, protected from strong waves, and can be affected by tides.
- The region richest in permanent interior lakes is northern Michoacán and central Jalisco.
- Deforestation and soil erosion increase runoff and reduce infiltration, and dams and irrigation make many originally permanent rivers intermittent.

**Notable gorges**
- Barranca del Cobre (Chihuahua), the Río Santiago canyon system, Río Bravo canyon, Río Moctezuma gorge (Hidalgo), Papaloapan rupture (Oaxaca), and the "Sumidero" canyon carved by the [[Rio Grijalva]] in Chiapas.

## Key Findings

- Mexico covers about 2 million km², has a coastline making up roughly three-quarters of its roughly 13,000 km of borders, and is shaped by its narrowing southward and the clockwise twist of the continent.
- The [[Baja California Peninsula]] and the [[Yucatan Peninsula]] create island-like or distinctive conditions, and the Revillagigedo Islands are the only truly oceanic islands.
- Mountain systems dominate the landscape. The Sierra Madre Occidental is the longest and most continuous, and the Eje Volcanico Transversal, the youngest, contains the country's highest peaks, including [[Pico De Orizaba]] at 5,653 m.
- Over half the territory lies above 1,000 m, and less than 35% lies below 500 m, so elevated terrain strongly shapes climate and vegetation zones.
- The geological record is patchy for the Paleozoic but shows repeated marine incursions, several orogenies (Coahuilan, Hidalguense/Laramide) and intense Cenozoic volcanism.
- Rock type strongly controls landforms and drainage: volcanic rocks produce impermeable-to-permeable young terrain, limestone produces karst and arheic zones, and marine sediments dominate the east.
- Hydrography is highly variable: major Pacific and Gulf basins are contrasted with extensive endorheic basins of the Altiplano and the arheic Yucatan Peninsula.
- Coastal lagunas and interior lakes are numerous, with salinity that varies seasonally and with connection to the sea, and the Michoacán–Jalisco region has the most permanent interior lakes.
- Human alterations (dams, irrigation, deforestation, soil erosion) have made many rivers intermittent and increased flood impacts in low-gradient coastal plains.
