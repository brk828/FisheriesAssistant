# Technical Proposal: The Effects of Interim Flows from Glen Canyon Dam on the Fishery Resources of the Lower Colorado River from Diamond Creek to Lake Mead

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This technical proposal, dated April 14, 1992, was prepared by [[BIO/WEST Inc]] for the [[Hualapai Tribe]] Wildlife Management Department. It requests a fishery investigation of the [[Colorado River]] from [[Diamond Creek]] (RM 226) to [[Pearce Ferry]] (RM 286) to monitor the effects of [[Interim Flows]] from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] on fish population structure, aquatic habitat, non-native fish interactions, and food resources. The work would follow methods used by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and BIO/WEST under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES), with the Hualapai Tribe providing primary program leadership. The proposal defines five study objectives, a sampling design combining permanent stations with random sampling, and a reporting schedule ending with a Final Report due February 15, 1994.

## Background & Description

- **Native fish assemblage:** Before [[Glen Canyon Dam]] was completed in 1963, the region supported eight native species: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*).
- **Extirpations:** Colorado squawfish, roundtail chub, and bonytail have been extirpated from the Grand Canyon.
- **Interim flow criteria:** Implemented in August 1991 to minimize damage to the Grand Canyon ecosystem from previous operations (Department of Interior 1988).
- **Study area:** Intensive fishery studies previously extended only to Diamond Creek (RM 226). The proposal extends evaluation to the lower Grand Canyon and Lake Mead Inflow.
- **Proposing team:** Richard A. Valdez, Ph.D., and Paul B. Holden, Ph.D. (BIO/WEST, Inc., Logan, Utah). The request was addressed to Donald E. Bay, Director, Hualapai Wildlife Management Department.
- **Field resources:** Three BIO/WEST biologists, two research boats (one 16-foot electrofishing Achilles and one 17-foot netting Achilles, each with a 40 hp Yamaha outboard), two Hualapai Tribe representatives, and one 23-foot support vessel with a boatman and swamper. The source text is partly garbled at this point, so the support vessel type is uncertain.
- **Data management:** Field data on Hualapai-approved sheets, entered into dBASE IV files. [[Datasondes]] data are downloaded at GCES; Hydrolab field data go to a water quality notebook and then to dBASE IV.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Rationale for monitoring:** [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Powell]] responses to interim flows are needed to determine whether the criteria reduce adverse effects and to identify detrimental effects that may require criteria adjustment.
- **Baseline gap:** The absence of comparable pre-interim data, particularly from Diamond Creek to Lake Mead, prevents comparison with previous operations. The [[Research Flows]] from June 1, 1991 through July 26, 1992 further complicate comparison because they altered flow regimes.
- **Expected outcome:** Monitoring will characterize the present ecosystem and its "new dynamic equilibrium" in terms of fish and macroinvertebrate composition and abundance, nutrient dynamics, productivity, and water quality. The proposal states the team will be unable to conclude whether these conditions differ from pre-research flow conditions.
- **Sampling design:** A combination of permanent stations (to characterize structure and dynamics of key ecosystem components across seasons) and random sampling of intervening areas (to assess fish composition, detect movement of piscivorous non-natives into native and endangered species' habitat, and find species absent from permanent sites).
- **Key threats named:** Piscivorous non-native fishes preying on native species; water level fluctuation affecting spawning, nursery, and macrohabitat availability.

## Key Findings

**Study objectives and methods**

1. **Objective 1 (adult fish):** Monitor distribution, abundance, and behavior of native and non-native adult fish.
   - Gill nets: 100 to 300 feet long, 6 to 10 feet deep, stretch mesh 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0 inches, multifilament material; set for 2 to 6 hours depending on sensitive species presence and water temperature.
   - Hoop nets: 4 to 6 feet in diameter, three to five hoops, set in small bays, tributary mouths, and isolated pools.
   - Electrofishing: 16-foot Achilles sportboat, 40 hp outboard, Coffelt CPS Mark XX pulsed DC output to spherical electrodes.
   - Minnow traps and seines for shorelines.
   - Relative abundance as CPE: fish per 100 feet of net per 100 hours; fish per 10 hours of electrofishing; fish per 100 square meters seined.
   - Native fish receive PIT tags ([[Passive Integrated Transponder]]) and are released; accidental mortalities go to AGFD.
   - Stomach contents collected from non-natives and, by stomach pump, from natives (non-lethal). A subsample of key species, including catfish, is sacrificed for stomach analysis and aging by otoliths and spines.

2. **Objective 2 (larval and juvenile native fish):** Monitor distribution and abundance and ascertain spawning and nursery areas.
   - Drift nets (spring and early summer, mornings and evenings), seines, and light traps.
   - Samples preserved in 5 percent formalin for laboratory identification.

3. **Objective 3 (piscivorous non-natives):** Monitor reproduction, food habits, and habitat use of non-native predators of native fish.
   - Ripe and gravid fish locations mapped as likely spawning habitat; temporary bench marks relate water level to spawning and nursery areas.
   - Seasonal stomach sampling; [[Threadfin Shad]] monitored as a traditional prey species.
   - Subsamples aged to identify age-specific habitat use.

4. **Objective 4 (tributary mouths and shallow shorelines):** Monitor water quality and sediment degradation or aggradation.
   - Level A: fish sampling plus detailed habitat measurements along three equally spaced transects; habitats mapped by plane table onto aerial photographs.
   - Level B: Level A plus continuous water quality monitoring by Datasondes and remote cameras for change over time.

5. **Objective 5 (food base):** Monitor productivity and algal standing crops.
   - Photosynthesis and productivity measured at mainstem, tributary mouth, and backwater sites.
   - Chlorophyll a filtered from water samples (held in liquid nitrogen for transfer).
   - Benthic metabolism chambers to estimate planktonic versus benthic contributions.
   - Nutrients: dissolved organic carbon, phosphorus (all forms), nitrogen (nitrate, nitrite, ammonia), water hardness, alkalinity.
   - [[Macroinvertebrates]] sampled with Hess samplers and Ekman or Ponar dredges; densities expressed as organisms per square meter.

**Sampling intensity and logistics**

- Main channel sites sampled intensively each quarterly field trip; selected tributaries at least three times annually.
- Two days per study site; most sampling in morning and evening.
- Efforts located on 1:1200 aerial photographs with macrohabitat (runs, pools, riffles, rapids, eddies) noted; depth, substrate, and cover recorded for each sample.
- Microhabitat (depth, velocity, substrate, cover) recorded for each humpback chub, bonytail, and razorback sucker captured.
- Fish measured as total length and standard length (mm) and weighed (g).

**Schedule and deliverables**

- Quarterly reports: July 1 and October 1, 1992; April, July, and October 1993.
- Annual report for 1992: January 1993.
- Final Report: February 15, 1994, including data files for the Hualapai Tribe.

**Gaps and limitations**

- The document is a proposal and reports no collected data, sample sizes, or results.
- No budget or cost figures are included in the source text.
