# FAO/EIFAC Symposium and Working Party Reports on Recreational Fisheries, Aquaculture, Lagoons and Fish Nutrition (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation (FAO Fisheries Report No. 541, Supplement, 1997 publication of 1996 meetings) gathers reports from the [[European Inland Fisheries Advisory Commission]] (EIFAC) and its partner [[Food and Agriculture Organization]] (FAO) bodies. It covers five areas: a Symposium on Social, Economic and Management Aspects of [[Recreational Fisheries]] (Dublin, 1996); an ad hoc working party on water use in [[Aquaculture]]; a meeting on [[Brackishwater Aquaculture]] and [[Lagoon Management]] (Messolonghi, Greece); a workshop on fish and crustacean nutrition for semi-intensive pond farming (Szarvas, Hungary); and a draft framework on introduced species. The main message is that recreational fisheries in Europe are stable in angler numbers but face rising conflict with pollution, other users, aquaculture and poaching, and that management must become more participatory, integrated and data-driven.

## Background & Description

The Symposium was held in conjunction with the Nineteenth Session of EIFAC, convened in Dublin, Ireland, and was attended by 119 participants from 23 countries. It was organised into five topic sessions: current status and trends; interactions with outside influences; interactions with other user groups; socio-economic and legal aspects; and management criteria. Its findings are summarised in a country-level status table covering 23 countries, with recreational fisher numbers, licence sales and percentages of population.

The aquaculture working party met in Rome on 8-9 May 1995 with seven participants from six member countries. It was renamed from the Working Party on Fish Farm Effluents to broaden its remit to multiple water use. A brackishwater working party met in Messolonghi, Greece, on 6-8 February 1996, focusing on Mediterranean lagoons. The nutrition workshop was held at the Fish Culture Research Institute, Szarvas, on 3-5 April 1996, with 58 participants from 14 countries presenting 5 review papers, 14 experience papers and 11 posters. The introduced species framework appears only as a table of contents (Preamble, Introduction, Basic Code, Implementation Guidelines) in the supplied text; its body is not included.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Several sections emphasise how external pressures shape fish stocks:

- **Eutrophication and pollution.** In Irish alkaline lakes, rising phosphorus changed fish community composition enough to require restoration. The report suggests total phosphorus should not exceed 20 mg/m³ to maintain present trophic status.
- **Hydrological modification.** Damming, river engineering, gravel extraction and abstraction interrupt migration and destroy spawning and rearing habitat. The Kaunas Hydroelectric Power Plant on the River Nemunas is linked to declines in salmon and vimba stocks in the [[Kurshiu Lagoon]].
- **Fish-eating birds.** [[Cormorant]] populations have multiplied and expanded over two decades, causing high losses to commercial, recreational and aquaculture fisheries.
- **Water scarcity.** Using Engelman and LeRoy (1993), Poland and Cyprus are already under water stress (below 1,667 m³ per capita), and some European countries may fall below 5,000 m³ per capita by 2025. Per-capita availability ranges from about 700,000 m³ in Iceland to 460 m³ in Israel.
- **Lagoon ecology.** The Messolonghi-Etoliko lagoon (about 15,000 ha) is a RAMSAR-protected wetland with temperature 5-30°C and salinity fluctuating widely. The Kurshiu Lagoon shows strong cyprinid dominance and high eutrophication near the Nemunas mouth, with the northern part cleaner due to aeration and Baltic influence.

## Key Findings

**Recreational fisheries (Symposium)**

- Angler numbers across Europe have been broadly stable since the 1980s. The earlier growth ceased for different reasons: diversification of leisure activities in Western Europe and economic hardship in Eastern Europe.
- Area of waters managed for angling is increasing, driven largely by [[Put-and-Take Fisheries]], liming of acidified waters in Sweden and Norway, and privatisation in Poland.
- Constraints include pollution, ageing anglers, conflicts with protection movements, poaching, lack of data, and access restrictions (for example, forest roads and riparian fencing).
- In France, river angling declined as pressure shifted to ponds and reservoirs. In Lower Lough Erne, Ireland, pike and perch fisheries and competition angling grew while game trout fishing declined.
- Recommendations: establish standard data collection across member countries; launch a Joint European Survey on Recreational Fisheries planned for 2000; promote education and youth participation.
- Integrated management, full user consultation, and inclusion of long-term restoration costs in project appraisals are recommended. Codes of practice for anglers, such as that in Baden-Württemberg, are supported but hard to standardise.
- Conflicts between commercial and recreational fishing depend on whether they exploit the same stocks. Hungary's licence revenue shifted from a 90/10 split between the National Anglers' Union and government to a development fund after 1993.
- Management should be interactive, with catchments as the primary unit, and should respect traditional knowledge. Decisions should be taken on the best available knowledge rather than delayed.
- Economic methods discussed include contingent valuation and willingness to pay for striped bass in the US (using MRFSS data), travel cost and random utility models, and [[Cost-Benefit Analysis]] for environmental quality decisions in England and Wales.
- Management criteria: sustainable exploitation of naturally recruiting fisheries; artificially maintained fisheries should not harm others; Germany's mandatory angler training is cited as a model.
- Finland's fisheries regions, with elected councils that can collect levies and regulate fishing, are described as successful in promoting angling tourism and coordinating stocking.

**Aquaculture and water use (Working Party)**

- The working party recommended integrating aquaculture into catchment-based [[Integrated Water Resource Management]] and adopting flexible, ecosystem-based regulation instead of rigid effluent standards.
- A ranking method was developed for nutrient and organic loading from net cages, still ponds and recirculation systems (scored 1 to 4), and for effects on small rivers, big rivers, lakes and coastal waters.
- Proposed pre-treatment for intake water includes aeration, sand filtration, iron removal and liming. Indicative oxygen thresholds are 8-10 mg/l for salmonids and 6-8 mg/l for cyprinids, with non-ionised ammonia limits of <0.0125 mg/l (salmonids) and <0.05 mg/l (cyprinids).
- Country reports should follow an outline covering water supply, technology, interactions with other users, water quality, farm management, outgoing water treatment, and a SWOT analysis.

**Brackishwater fisheries and lagoons (Messolonghi)**

- The Messolonghi-Etoliko lagoon yields an estimated 100 kg/ha, mostly from traditional fish-barrier traps. Earlier estimates were 1,500-2,000 t/year (1960) and 1,300-1,500 t/year (1983).
- Estimated catch composition: mullets 46%, sea breams 28%, bass 11%, eels 9%, other 6%.
- Only 4% of sea breams and 5% of mullets declared were of commercial size. Subsized fish are described as the central fisheries problem, and their proper management could potentially double or triple production.
- The lagoon supports about 700 fishermen (200 cooperative, 500 independent). Tension between them, and illegal fishing, are key social problems. Tax-rent systems encourage underdeclaration, so official data are unreliable.
- A project was proposed to the EU FEDER Programme linking six regions in Greece, Italy, France and Spain, with a satellite-based lagoon inventory and a standard set of eight data forms.
- Other lagoon reports: Turkey has 24 lagoons (about 300 km²) producing an estimated 1,200-1,500 t, with trammel nets and fixed barriers; Israel's brackishwater aquaculture (carp, tilapia, mullets, hybrid bass) averages 4,800 kg/ha in deep ponds and 20,000 kg/ha in recirculated systems; in the Lithuanian Baltic zone, 31 coastal species are recorded.
- Kurshiu Lagoon commercial catches fell from 60-80 kg/ha historically to 30-45 kg/ha. Forty-six species are recorded in the Lithuanian lagoon (1981-1994), versus 43 in 1949-1957. Fish species composition is changing because of pollution, dam effects and the loss of traditional fishing grounds after the lagoon's central part fell under Russian jurisdiction.

**Fish and crustacean nutrition (Szarvas Workshop)**

- Total freshwater finfish aquaculture production in the 12 EIFAC countries attending rose from 108,330 t (1984) to 117,512 t (1989), then fell to 98,019 t (1993).
- Key recommendations: determine dietary nutrient allowances for pond systems using hydrobiological data; develop mass culture of live food (rotifers, cladocerans); evaluate agricultural by-products as feed; standardise product quality methods; and build clinical and histological tools to assess fish health.
- Dose-response results: for 95% of plateau protein retention, [[Rainbow Trout]] required 27.7 g/kg dietary lysine, 2.0 g/kg tryptophan, 5.8 g/kg histidine, 15.7 g/kg valine, 13.6 g/kg leucine and 13.7 g/kg isoleucine.
- [[Dietary Protein Requirements]] for the Mexican cichlid (*Cichlasoma urophthalmus*) were about 453 g/kg under fixed-rate feeding but about 325 g/kg under satiation feeding. Optimal phosphorus was 1.5% with a Ca:P ratio of 1.33:1.
- [[African Catfish]] fed 35% crude protein plus amino acid supplementation achieved the highest protein retention (51.3%), reducing nitrogen loading to water.
- [[Nile Tilapia]] fed animal-protein-rich diets had an FCR of 1.89 versus 1.23 for soybean-based diets in a recirculation system.
- Wheat gave a feed conversion similar to artificial feed for [[Common Carp]] in ponds. Higher dietary protein improved gill condition.
- In [[Mekong Giant Catfish]] cage culture in Vietnam (1,200 cages and 37,000 t in 1995), raising on-farm feed from 10-12% to 20-22% crude protein shortened the culture period from 10 to 8 months and reduced body fat from 25% to 10%.
- Other experiments tested legume seed screenings as soybean replacements in carp feed (small leguminous screenings performed nearly as well as the control; bitter lupine screenings performed poorly), [[Gilthead Sea Bream]] and [[European Sea Bass]] lipid sources, and vitamin C requirements of various fish species.
