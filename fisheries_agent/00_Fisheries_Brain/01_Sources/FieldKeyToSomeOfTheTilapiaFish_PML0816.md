# Identification Key to Sarotherodon (Tilapia) Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sarotherodon

## Executive Summary

This document is a two-page field identification key (dichotomous key) for distinguishing members of the *Sarotherodon* "Tilapia" group. It separates five named species using external and internal morphological characters: gill raker counts, caudal fin pattern, fin margin color, dorsal fin spine count, body coloration, and the shape of the [[Hypopharyngeal Bone]]. The key is presented as a sequence of numbered couplets that lead to a species name. The text is a fragment of a larger work, with no sampling data, distribution records, or ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

The key is organized as a branching sequence. At each step the user chooses between two contrasting character states and follows the indicated number to the next couplet or to a species. Characters used include:

- **[[Gill Rakers]]:** counts on the lower part of the first gill arch (for example, 7-10, 12 or more, and 21-28).
- **[[Caudal Fin]]:** presence or absence of vertical strips, lines, blotches, or cross-bands, and fin transparency or color.
- **[[Dorsal Fin]]:** spine count (15-16, rarely 17, or 17-18) and margin color (black, red, or brown-reddish).
- **[[Hypopharyngeal Bone]]:** shape, described as triangular or rounded and heart-shaped.
- **Body and head coloration:** overall tone, iris color, and dark marks on the operculum or throat.
- **[[Courtship Coloration]]:** changes in male color during breeding, including red-black, blue, and black tones on the throat, fins, or ventral parts.
- **Juvenile versus adult differences:** several characters, such as caudal strips and the [[Tilapia]] mark, apply only to juveniles or to some adults.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not report habitat, geographic range, water chemistry, diet, reproductive behavior beyond courtship coloration, or population data. Its ecological relevance is limited to the use of breeding coloration and juvenile patterns as diagnostic traits, which matters for field identification when fish are in different life stages.

## Key Findings

The key separates five species. The main diagnostic features for each are summarized below.

- **[[Sarotherodon Galilaeus]]:** Caudal fin transparent, without strips or blotches, with a slightly concave margin. Body high and silvery-gray, iris yellow, with a dark, large area on the operculum. The [[Hypopharyngeal Bone]] is rounded and heart-shaped. During courtship, the throat and fins of males become dark.
- **[[Sarotherodon Niloticus]]:** Numerous narrow pale lines cross the entire caudal fin. Body dark-gray to red-black in breeding males. Reached through the branch with 21-28 gill rakers and black dorsal and caudal fin margins.
- **[[Sarotherodon Mossambicus]]:** Dorsal and caudal fin margins brown-reddish, with the caudal fin lacking or having very few strips. Body pale-gray to blackish, sometimes with a white throat. Courting males are usually black.
- **[[Sarotherodon Aureus]]:** Dorsal and caudal fin margins red. Caudal fin with numerous small blotches and interrupted strips. Body bluish-gray or silvery, becoming blue in courting males. A branch in the key uses 12 or more gill rakers, with the [[Tilapia]] mark present only in juveniles.
- **[[Sarotherodon Hornorum]]:** Caudal fin transparent and reddish, with few or no cross-lines. Body reddish-gray.

Branching logic from the key:

1. Gill rakers and the hypopharyngeal bone shape separate the first major branches.
2. Caudal fin pattern (strips and lines versus none or blotches) separates the next step.
3. Dorsal fin margin color (black versus red or brown-reddish) and spine count distinguish the remaining taxa.
