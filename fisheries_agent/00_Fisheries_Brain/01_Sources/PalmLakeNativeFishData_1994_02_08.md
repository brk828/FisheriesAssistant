# Palm Lake Native Fish Data

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #native-fish-conservation #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This internal Arizona Game and Fish Department memorandum (dated February 8, 1994) documents a multi-year chronology of stocking, removal, and monitoring efforts for imperiled "big river" native fishes at Palm Lake, part of broader recovery efforts for endangered Colorado River basin fishes. The core species of concern are the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*, code GIEL) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, code XYTE), both federally listed endangered species, alongside the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, code PTLU, historically called "Colorado River squawfish") and the non-native [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*, code AMNA). The document tracks hatchery stocking events from Dexter National Fish Hatchery (DNFH), subsequent removal/salvage operations, PIT-tag-based individual tracking, and transfers to refugia and research facilities (Page Springs Hatchery, ASU research ponds, Horsethief refugia ponds near Grand Junction, CO), reflecting standard ex-situ conservation and translocation practices for these species.

## Background & Description

Size data are reported for stocked cohorts and recaptured individuals:

- **Bonytail Chub (GIEL)**: Initial 1989 stocking cohort averaged **203 mm TL**; a supplemental 1991 stocking cohort (250 individuals, PIT-tagged) was released at DNFH. Recaptured individuals in the 1993–1994 sampling period ranged approximately **270–426 mm TL** and **56–430 g** in weight, indicating substantial growth since initial stocking (consistent with 2–5 years of growth in a reservoir environment).
- **Razorback Sucker (XYTE)**: Initial 1989 stocking cohort averaged **250 mm TL** (larger at stocking than bonytail, consistent with typical hatchery release sizes for this species).
- **Colorado Pikeminnow (PTLU)**: Initial 1990 stocking cohort averaged **101.6 mm TL**, considerably smaller than the other two species at time of release, reflecting a younger stocked age class.
- **F1 Recaptures**: Several GIEL individuals were identified as **F1 bonytail** (first-generation offspring, likely of hatchery-origin parents or naturally produced from stocked fish), distinguished through PIT-tag history and recapture records — notable because F1 production/survival is a key metric of conservation success for this species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Palm Lake functions as an impounded reservoir environment within the lower Colorado River basin drainage, serving as a recipient site for reintroduction of extirpated/endangered big-river fishes as well as a monitoring site to assess post-stocking survival, growth, and reproduction.
- **Co-occurring species**: The native assemblage of conservation interest (bonytail chub, razorback sucker, Colorado pikeminnow) co-occurs with the non-native [[Yellow Bullhead]] (AMNA), an introduced ictalurid that appears to have established a resident population within the lake (~125 individuals caught/released by USFWS in 1992; ~20 sampled in 1993–1994).
- **Life history relevance**: All three focal native species are historically riverine, adapted to large, turbid, main-stem Colorado River basin habitats; their presence in a lake (rather than flowing river) reflects a translocation/refugia strategy typical of endangered fish recovery programs, where reservoirs or isolated ponds are used to protect populations from non-native predators/competitors present in the main river channel.
- **Trophic/community interactions**: Yellow bullhead is a known predator on small fish and eggs and is generally considered a threat to native fish recruitment; its co-occurrence with native cyprinids and catostomids at Palm Lake is ecologically notable in the context of [[Native Fish Recovery Programs]] in the Colorado River Basin.

## Key Findings

- **Stocking History**:
  - April 20, 1989: 688 GIEL (avg. 203 mm TL) + 35 XYTE (avg. 250 mm TL) stocked from DNFH.
  - February 3, 1990: 1,136 PTLU (avg. 101.6 mm TL) stocked from DNFH.
  - April 23, 1991: Additional 250 PIT-tagged GIEL stocked from DNFH.

- **Removal & Translocation Events**:
  - April 20, 1992: 10 GIEL mortalities salvaged/preserved in formalin; 22 GIEL live-captured and removed — 2 sent to the Hassayampa River Preserve Visitor Center aquarium, 20 sent to Page Springs Hatchery (PSH).
  - At PSH: 2 of the 20 GIEL died; the remaining 18 were transferred (July 24, 1992) to Arizona State University (ASU) research ponds. Of these, 16 were newly PIT-tagged; 2 had prior tags and are presumed **F1 bonytail**.
  - November 19–20, 1992: USFWS PIT-tagged and removed 13 GIEL to Horsethief refugia ponds (Grand Junction, CO); also captured and released 12 PTLU and 125 AMNA back into Palm Lake.

- **Monitoring Results (Jan. 31, 1993 – Feb. 3, 1994)**:
  - Total catch: 39 GIEL (including **4 F1 recaptures**), 2 XYTE, 2 PTLU, and ~20 AMNA.
  - Individual PIT-tag records document specific weights (e.g., 356–430 g), total lengths (e.g., 340–460 mm), and disposition — many fish were released back into **Lake Havasu**, while confirmed F1 recaptures were routed to **ASU research ponds**.

- **Programmatic significance**: This dataset illustrates a coordinated inter-agency effort (Arizona Game and Fish Department, USFWS, Dexter NFH, Page Springs Hatchery, ASU, Hassayampa River Preserve) to establish, monitor, and manage a refugial population of endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], alongside reintroduced [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], as part of broader [[Colorado River Basin Fish Recovery]] initiatives, with PIT-tagging enabling individual-level tracking of survival, growth, and F1 reproduction over a multi-year period.
