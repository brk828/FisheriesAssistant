# Job Completion Report: Fisheries Problems Determination, Region 1-A, Texas (1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Job Completion Report from the [[Parks And Wildlife Department]] (Austin, Texas, dated July 26, 1963) covers Job No. D-1, Fisheries Problems Determination, under [[Federal Aid In Fisheries Restoration Act]] Project No. F-7-R-10, [[Fisheries Investigations And Surveys]] of the Waters of [[Region 1-A]]. The report covers January 1 to December 31, 1962. Its objective was to analyze data from all project jobs to identify specific fisheries problems and decide whether further study or corrective action was needed.

The report identifies four main problem areas: difficulty designing a dustproof hopper for [[Rotenone]] and other powdered toxicants; staffing shortages after the project lost its assistant leader in September 1961; limited access to sandy-bed river and stream survey sites; and declining natural reproduction of game fish, which depends on hatchery supplementation. It also flags the effect of planned [[Impoundment]] projects on downstream minnow supply.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** [[Federal Aid In Fisheries Restoration Act]] Project No. F-7-R-10, Job No. D-1 (the cover page reads "D-l1", corrected here to D-1 per the body text).
- **Project leader:** [[Lonnie Peters]]. Prepared by Lonnie Peters; approved by the Coordinator ([[Marion Toole]]) and the Regional Supervisor ([[Leo D. Lewis]]).
- **Administration:** [[J. Weldon Watson]] (Executive Director) and [[Eugene A. Walker]] (Director, Program Planning).
- **Procedure:** Data from all jobs under the project were reviewed to decide whether further study or a resolution method was needed.
- **Scope:** Lakes and streams of Region 1-A, including [[High Plains]] lakes, lakes below the [[Caprock]], and major rivers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Rough fish dominance:** Rough fish dominate most waters. Some lakes have stunted game species from overcrowding, and in other areas natural reproduction is very limited.
- **Regional species patterns:** [[Carp]] and [[Black Bullhead]] dominate high plains lakes. [[River Carpsucker]] is usually more abundant in lakes below the caprock.
- **Aquatic vegetation:** Serious vegetation problems occur at [[Buffalo Springs Lake]] and [[Lake Marvin]], both very clear lakes with thick beds of [[Najas Flexilis]] and [[Potamogeton Pectinatus]]. [[Coontail]] is abundant only at Lake Marvin.
- **Downstream minnow production:** Planned or under-construction dams on the [[Canadian River]], [[White River]], and [[Salt Fork Of The Red River]] will curtail minnow production for 40 to 50 miles downstream and greatly reduce it for a considerable distance beyond.
- **Natural reproduction:** Game fish reproduction has been very light in some lakes, caused by falling water levels during spawning season and a lack of spawning habitat. Habitat can be improved in some cases, but water level fluctuation cannot be controlled.
- **Streambed access:** Broad streambeds, quicksand, and shallow water with sand bars make travel by ordinary vehicle or boat impossible in many areas during normal flow.
- **Temperature effects on spawning:** Spawning is delayed on the cooler high plains. Warmer conditions at the [[Dundee Hatchery]], located off the caprock near Wichita Falls, let hatchery production begin well before natural spawning at higher elevations.

## Key Findings

1. **Dustproof hopper design problem.** A double-door hopper separating the interior from the toxicant loading opening was field tested and proved ineffective. The root cause is packaging: powdered [[Rotenone]] was previously packed in 40 or 50 pound paper bags, which are often ripped or punctured in shipping and are unwieldy and nonuniform in shape. The report concludes that rotenone or other powdered toxicants will likely need to be packaged in cardboard drums, which reduces puncture risk, simplifies hopper design, and eases loading.

2. **Staffing shortage.** The project has been without an assistant leader since September 1961 because a qualified biologist could not be hired at the salary offered. The Project Statement and Estimate (P. S. & E.) assumed an assistant would soon be available, so some planned work could not be completed.

3. **Survey access limitations.** Stream surveys have been limited by inaccessible areas. Past collections were mostly made at highway crossings, which are too few and poorly located to give good distribution and abundance data. A "[[Sand Buggy Vehicle]]" equipped with large balloon tires is recommended for unrestricted travel down sandy streambeds.

4. **Impoundment effects on minnows.** New dams will likely reduce minnow production downstream. Changes to minnow regulations, possibly bag limits or further seining restrictions, may be needed after completion to prevent serious depletion. Considerable survey work will be required, and recent adequate survey data for all streams is lacking.

5. **Natural reproduction failure and stocking.** Where natural reproduction of game species fails, supplemental stocking with hatchery-reared fish is necessary. A key problem is timing: [[Hatchery Stocking]] of [[Largemouth Bass]] fry must be planned before natural production is detectable. On high plains lakes, a failed spawn may not be detected until hatchery fry have already been distributed, which can leave a lake without young-of-the-year bass for a year. Stocking the following year may then be futile if good natural reproduction follows immediately.

6. **Recommended reserve system.** The report recommends an arrangement with hatcheries to reserve a number of fish for supplemental stocking, not tied to a specific water body. Reserved fish would be stocked only after a survey confirms natural reproduction has failed. Unneeded fish would be released in other waters where needed. In new or renovated waters, stocking needs are usually known well in advance and pose no such problem.

7. **Continuation.** The report recommends continuing Job D-1 to produce a summary of general and specific fisheries problems across the project.
