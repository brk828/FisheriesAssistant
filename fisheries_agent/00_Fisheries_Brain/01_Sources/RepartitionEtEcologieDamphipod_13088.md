# Distribution and Ecology of Amphipods (Mainly Gammarids) in the Dourduff Basin and Estuary (Brittany)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

*Note: the source is a crustacean (amphipod) study, not a fish study; the tags above are adjusted accordingly.*

## Executive Summary

This 1974 study by J. Marion (Zoologisch Museum, University of Amsterdam) documents the distribution and ecology of amphipods, mainly gammarids, in the [[Dourduff River]] basin and estuary in Brittany, France. Monthly sampling at five estuarine stations (May–October 1971), plus broader surveys of estuary side-streams, non-estuarine tributaries and neighbouring catchments, was used to examine how species shift upstream and downstream with the seasons. The central finding is that [[Echinogammarus berilloni]] persists in the estuary head while [[Gammarus chevreuxi]] and [[Gammarus zaddachi]] are scarce, but is displaced when those competitors repopulate the upper estuary under autumn spring tides. Salinity tolerance alone does not explain this displacement; interspecific competition is proposed as the limiting factor.

## Background & Description

The work was split into three parts: (A) monthly sampling at estuarine stations 1–5 during spring tides, to track species changes and their relation to salinity; (B) sampling of the rest of the estuary and small freshwater inflows; and (C) a microgeographic analysis of the non-estuarine Dourduff and its branches. Sampling used a hand net at low tide, with simultaneous measurement of temperature, pH, Ca and Cl⁻ (the latter by titration and chloride meter; dissolved O₂ was measured at some sites). Site positions were fixed with compass, tape and clinometer, referenced to Michelin map No. 58 and the 1:50,000 état-major sheet VI-15.

The Dourduff enters the estuary of the Morlaix river. A fish farm on the right bank discharges waste into the river, and an old water mill with a roughly 1.50 m waterfall marks the start of the tidally influenced estuary. The waterfall is the only obstacle to tidal penetration upstream, as there are no locks.

Nine amphipod species were recorded: *Gammarus chevreuxi*, *G. zaddachi*, *G. crinicornis*, *G. duebeni celticus*, *G. duebeni duebeni*, *Echinogammarus berilloni*, *Chaetogammarus marinus*, *Melita palmata* and *Corophium volutator*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Estuary head (stations 1–5):** Upstream sites were fresh at high tide in June and October, while downstream stations had higher and more variable salinity. On 9 August 1971, station B was sampled hourly for 12 hours to track Ca, Cl⁻, pH and temperature through the tidal cycle.
- **Salinity extremes:** Cl⁻ reached 19,300 mg/l at station 4 during the October spring tide, and 19,400 mg/l at station B at high tide. A Cl⁻ difference of about 14,000 mg/l was recorded between high and low tide at the Horn stations 1–2 in May.
- **Calcium:** The author cites a limiting value of 15 mg/l Ca for normal carapace calcium recovery after moulting in *E. berilloni*. Most sites with this species met that value, though a few fell below it.
- **Temperature:** Water in the upper estuary stayed above 4.5 °C in winter 1971/1972, above the level (3 °C) that the literature associates with the species' absence.
- **Small streams:** Brooklets were typically 0.20–1.20 m wide and 0.03–0.10 m deep, with clear, rapidly flowing water. Their beds grade from gravel and pebbles to silt downstream. Vegetation zones run from *Enteromorpha* through mixed *Enteromorpha*/brown algae to brown algae (*Fucus*, *Pelvetia*) and bare silt. A Cl⁻ rise from the brown algal zone to the river bed is attributed to mixing of freshwater with saltier interstitial water.
- **Salicornia:** Salicornia was the first colonising plant of bare estuarine mud, and its upper limit on the right bank marks the upstream limit of this habitat type.
- **Tributaries:** Gammarids were absent from sites with no cobbles or vegetation, probably because they cannot hold on in fast currents over soft sediment.

## Key Findings

- **Seasonal displacement:** *E. berilloni* occupied the estuary head from May to August while *G. chevreuxi* and *G. zaddachi* were scarce or absent. In September the two competitors repopulated the upper estuary and *E. berilloni* disappeared from that section, persisting only farther upstream. In October *G. chevreuxi* was found at station 1, where *E. berilloni* had been abundant. The author links this to autumn spring tides and the reproductive timing of the three species.
- **Salinity is not the main constraint:** Citing Vincent (1966), the author notes *E. berilloni* can survive up to 80% seawater, so increased autumn salinity cannot explain its disappearance. Competition with the more salt-tolerant *G. chevreuxi* and *G. zaddachi* is proposed as the limiting factor, consistent with Pinkster et al. (1970).
- **Reproduction:** *E. berilloni* breeds mainly in May–June (ovigerous females and juveniles found then). *G. chevreuxi* was found in precopulation throughout the study, which the author reads as year-round breeding. *G. zaddachi* was in precopulation only in May–July. *G. duebeni celticus* was in precopulation in May–June; *M. palmata* ovigerous females were found in June–July, and *G. duebeni duebeni* showed no precopulation from July to October.
- **Migration:** *G. zaddachi* was noted by Dennert et al. (1969) to move upstream only in September–December, with downstream movement dominating the rest of the year. This matches the scarcity of *G. zaddachi* at the upper stations in May–August.
- **Species-specific habitat:** *Chaetogammarus marinus* occurred throughout the estuary under stones and algae, and was also found on muddy substrate, which the author notes differs from earlier reports. *Corophium volutator* was often the sole amphipod on muddy estuarine bottoms. *Gammarus crinicornis* was found at only one station near the estuary mouth, at 15,000 mg/l Cl⁻ and 262 mg/l Ca in August at low tide.
- **Melita palmata** occurred in a brooklet at 61 mg/l Cl⁻ (low tide), far below the 7,000 mg/l lower limit cited for the genus by Den Hartog (1964). The author suggests the species may tolerate the higher salinity that brooklets reach at high tide.
- **Regional comparisons:** Only one of the sampled Doufine sites, upstream of Pont-de-Buis-lès-Quimerch, yielded *E. berilloni*, and the river was heavily polluted just downstream. The Horn was dropped after June because its estuarine part was heavily polluted.
- **Sourcing:** The Dourduff's headwaters could not be located and appear to be diffuse springs.

*Study limitations:* sampling was monthly at only five estuarine stations, and at a single spring tide per month. Several conclusions are explicitly provisional, and water-quality measurements could not be taken in brooklets at high tide.
