# Minutes of the June 6, 1990, Native Fish Work Group Meeting

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #native-fish-conservation #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This document records the minutes of a June 6, 1990, interagency **Native Fish Work Group** meeting held at Katherine's Landing (Lake Mohave, Colorado River), hosted by the [[National Park Service]] and attended by representatives from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW), [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. The meeting focused on management actions supporting native fish conservation in the Lake Mohave system, specifically: (1) an Environmental Assessment (EA) for berm improvements at **Yuma Cove** to create/protect a rearing backwater; (2) a review of fish tagging/tracking methodologies for monitoring reared native fish; and (3) a survey of additional candidate backwater sites, notably **Davis Cove**, for expanding rearing efforts. Although the specific fish taxa are not named explicitly in the OCR text, the institutional context (ASU's Dr. Paul Marsh and Dr. W.L. Minckley, backwater rearing, Lake Mohave) strongly aligns with concurrent conservation programs for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] in the Colorado River Basin.

## Background & Description
The source text contains **no direct morphological data** (e.g., meristics, body proportions, coloration) for any fish species. This is administrative/programmatic material rather than a biological survey. Ancillary details relevant to physical handling of fish include:
- **Tagging technology discussed**: Magnetic, coded **PIT tags** (Passive Integrated Transponder) were recommended by ASU as "less detrimental to fish and more reliable than other more common techniques," implying prior use of external tags (e.g., fin clips, streamer tags) considered more invasive.
- Target tagging cohort: **2,000 fish** planned for tagging, at an estimated cost of **$18,500** (tags + 2 tag detectors + labor).
- Equipment (detectors) was expected to be needed by **1992**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Isolated **backwater coves** off the main channel/reservoir of Lake Mohave, used as protected nursery/rearing habitat for native fish, separated from predatory non-native fish populations via **berms** or barriers.
- **Yuma Cove**: Bottom elevation surveyed at only **629 feet**, indicating a shallow backwater at risk of desiccation; monitored for water elevation with contingency fish removal if conditions worsen. Potential future deepening by 5–10 feet via small dredge if the berm survives storm erosion.
- **Davis Cove**: Located between Katherine's Landing and Davis Dam; identified as the most promising of 10 candidate sites (from aerial photo review, 7 field-visited) based on **size, depth, and isolability**. Proposed for a temporary (later possibly permanent) barrier structure to exclude non-native predators/competitors, mirroring the [[Yuma Cove]] model.
- **Renovation via chemical treatment**: AGFD planned two treatments of Yuma Cove (Aug–Nov 1990) — standard rotenone-type renovation practice to remove non-native fish prior to stocking natives; timed to coincide with berm construction, with dead fish to be buried using a front-end loader.
- **Population monitoring**: ASU maintains a **tri-annual monitoring effort** of the Lake Mohave adult native fish population, intended to be paired with PIT-tag data to determine whether backwater-reared fish are actually recruiting into the adult population — a core question of the entire rearing program's efficacy.
- **Regulatory/legal habitat context**: Uncertainty over whether a **Clean Water Act Section 404 permit** (Corps of Engineers) is required for the Yuma Cove berm work, reflecting the wetland/aquatic habitat modification implications of backwater engineering.

## Key Findings
- The interagency group **endorsed the Yuma Cove EA**, anticipating a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) by early August 1990, with Alternative No. 5 (off-site hatchery cost comparison) recommended for deletion.
- **PIT tagging** was agreed upon as the most reliable and cost-effective method (~$18,500 for 2,000 fish, tags, and 2 detectors) for tracking backwater-reared fish and assessing recruitment success into the wild adult population — critical to validating the backwater rearing strategy as a conservation tool.
- **Davis Cove** was identified as the top-priority candidate for a **second backwater rearing site**, with Reclamation tasked to design both temporary and permanent barrier options; a temporary barrier would be attempted first given vandalism/recreational-use concerns, with a permanent structure (similar to Yuma Cove) as fallback.
- The group recognized **agreement on production methods** (backwater rearing) but **divergence on programmatic strategy going forward**, prompting a commitment (Mr. Gordon Mueller, Reclamation) to draft a formal **interagency "Plan"** within 6 months addressing objectives, methods, and potential need for a formal interagency agreement.
- A detailed **schedule of commitments** was established spanning July 1990–September 1991, covering the 404 permit application, EA/FONSI finalization, cove renovation, berm construction, spawner stocking, PIT tag procurement, barrier design, draft plan submission, and Davis Cove barrier construction — distributed across NPS, Reclamation, AGFD, ASU, and FWS leads.
- This meeting exemplifies **multi-agency collaborative management** of native Colorado River fishes threatened by reservoir impoundment and non-native species introduction, using isolated backwater habitat creation as a core recovery technique — a strategy closely tied to broader [[Colorado River Native Fish Recovery Program]] efforts of this era.
