## Executive Summary

This document is a formal recommendation letter from the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** (Arizona State University) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Regional Director, Albuquerque office), dated 22 June 1993. It addresses two conservation policy matters arising from the Team's 24 February 1993 annual meeting: (1) a recommendation to add five native fish taxa from the [[Gila River Basin]] to the federal Candidate Species list (Category 2) due to significant habitat loss and ongoing threats, and (2) a unanimous recommendation to retain the genus name *Tiaroga* for the [[Loach Minnow]] rather than adopting a proposed reclassification to *Rhinichthys*. The letter underscores the severely degraded ecological status of the Gila River basin aquatic ecosystem, noting that with these additions, nearly all native fishes in the basin would be either federally listed or candidates for listing.

## Background & Description

The source document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions. Taxa referenced include:

- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — small-bodied cyprinid minnow.
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) — small-bodied cyprinid minnow, noted as having multiple distinct evolutionary units ("or some unit(s) thereof").
- **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*) — member of family [[Catostomidae]].
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*) — catostomid sucker adapted to desert stream conditions.
- **[[Little Colorado Sucker]]** (*Catostomus* sp.) — undescribed/undetermined species status at time of writing.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — taxonomic status under dispute; a proposed reclassification would place it under genus *Rhinichthys*, but the Team's species experts questioned the validity of this proposed generic name change.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All species discussed inhabit the **[[Gila River Basin]]** aquatic ecosystem spanning Arizona and New Mexico, characterized as a desert riverine system.
- The document explicitly states that these species "have all lost substantial portions of their historic habitats," indicating range contraction consistent with broader patterns of habitat degradation in [[Desert Fishes]] ecosystems from water diversion, groundwater pumping, non-native species introduction, and habitat modification (implied threats typical of this system, though not itemized in the letter).
- The cumulative effect of listing recommendations would result in **all native fishes of the Gila River basin** being either federally listed or candidate species, with the sole exception of a species noted as already **extinct** (unnamed in text, likely referencing the Gila River basin's history of native fish extirpations).
- This near-total candidate/listed status is presented as an indicator of severe ecosystem degradation across the basin, linking single-species conservation status to basin-wide ecological health assessment.

## Key Findings

- The **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** recommended (with one dissenting vote) that the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service add five fish taxa to **Category 2 Candidate Species** status under the Notice of Review:
  1. [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
  2. [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
  3. [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
  4. [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*)
  5. [[Little Colorado Sucker]] (*Catostomus* sp.)
- Category 2 candidate status was intended to formally acknowledge the need for further status-defining research and the potential future need for federal protection under the Endangered Species Act.
- The Team **unanimously opposed** the proposed taxonomic reclassification of *Tiaroga cobitis* (Loach Minnow) to *Rhinichthys cobitis*, recommending the Service retain the original genus *Tiaroga* pending further scientific review and publication, citing expert skepticism about the proposal's validity.
- The letter frames these recommendations within a broader conservation narrative: nearly the entire native fish fauna of the [[Gila River Basin]] is now either listed or candidate for listing, reflecting an ecosystem in serious ecological decline.
- This document illustrates the collaborative science-policy interface between academic recovery teams (based at [[Arizona State University]]) and federal regulatory bodies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) in managing [[Endangered Species Act]] candidate status determinations for imperiled desert aquatic fauna.
