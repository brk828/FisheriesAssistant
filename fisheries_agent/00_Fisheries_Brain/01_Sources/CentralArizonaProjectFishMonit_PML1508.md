# Central Arizona Project Canal Fish Monitoring: FY 2002 Survey Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report, submitted by Paul C. Marsh to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office), summarizes fish surveys in [[Central Arizona Project]]-related canals during fiscal year 2002 (1 October 2001 to 30 September 2002). Sampling followed the [[Long-Term Monitoring Plan]] for the Gila River basin. Surveys covered the [[Salt River Project]] Arizona (North) Canal (SRPN) and the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG), including its [[Pima Lateral Canal]] and [[Pima Lateral Feeder Canal]] system. The SRP South Canal was not sampled because no water outage occurred.

Fourteen taxa were captured or observed, none of them new to the study. Only two were native: [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]]. The remaining taxa were non-native. Canal dewatering and communication lapses between operators and researchers constrained sampling, and the report recommends better coordination and routine sampling in years without dry-ups.

## Background & Description

The monitoring program has run for nearly seven years. Canal samples are described as opportunistic and qualitative, so catches are not generally comparable among sites. Catch totals varied with flow, sampling effort, habitat, gear type and fish availability.

Gear used included:
- [[Electrofishing]] (backpack and boat-mounted units)
- [[Seine Netting]] (straight seines, typically 15 x 3 ft and 6 x 4 ft)
- Hoop and entanglement netting (recommended, not reported as used)

The species list (Appendix 1) comprises 14 taxa across eight families. Non-native fishes include [[Common Carp]], [[Grass Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Blue Tilapia]], [[Threadfin Shad]] and [[Rainbow Trout]]. Two species were recorded for the first time in these canals during this monitoring: green sunfish, flathead catfish, Sonora sucker and threadfin shad were captured in FCG or the interconnected Pima Lateral Feeder Canal and were not in the 1996 site records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Canal conditions varied sharply. Water flow ranged from none to full capacity, and sites included concrete-lined ditches, sand and silt bottoms, deep pools (over 3 m) and seasonally isolated pools. Water clarity ranged from clear to turbid or milky.

Dewatering for maintenance drives both sampling access and fish mortality. In January 2002, the Arizona Canal was partly dewatered from 14 January. Researchers suspected that fish richness and abundance were reduced by the drawdown, based on lower richness than prior samples from the same reach. Canal management is no longer expected to require annual dewatering, so routine sampling is likely to become feasible.

The [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]] and [[Ashurst-Hayden Diversion Dam]] are electric fish barriers or diversion structures that affect fish movement. The [[China Wash Fish Barrier]] was also sampled.

## Key Findings

**Overall totals**
- 14 taxa captured or reliably observed; no new species.
- SRPN yielded 11 species; FCG yielded 9.
- Native taxa made up under 15 percent of total species (reported as 11% at FCG and 19% at SRPN; the report's own wording is internally inconsistent here).
- Non-native species: 9 at SRPN and 8 at FCG.

**Salt River Project Arizona (North) Canal (January 2002)**
- Seven species were captured or observed at the two shallow sites.
- Sonora sucker was abundant and desert sucker common; red shiner and young-of-year channel catfish were common among non-natives.
- A boat-mounted electrofisher survey at Indian Bend Wash (24 January 2002, about 1.7 miles) recorded 124 fish. Sonora sucker was most numerous (64), followed by desert sucker (14) and red shiner (13).
- At CM 0.5 (seine), four species were taken, two of them native.

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal (October 2001)**
- Five species at the Ashurst-Hayden site (CM 0.2) by backpack electrofisher in 455 seconds. Yellow bullhead and channel catfish were common.
- Seine haul at CM 0.5 (five hauls): red shiner abundant; hundreds of young-of-year channel catfish and yellow bullhead observed.
- China Wash site above the barrier (CM 2.3): five species; common carp abundant; Sonora sucker represented by one individual.
- Downstream of the barrier (CM 2.6): six species; young-of-year channel catfish abundant.
- Sites 5 to 9 (Pima Lateral and Feeder canals) were sampled qualitatively, with no counts recorded. Pools were isolated and fish were often moribund.
- The flathead catfish record in the Pima Lateral Feeder Canal is the first known occurrence in that system.

**Management recommendations**
1. Improve communication between canal operators (SRP, SCID) and monitoring staff so sampling can coincide with scheduled and unscheduled outages. Communication lapses have been a concern for nearly seven years.
2. Modify the protocol to allow routine sampling in years without dry-ups, using boat-mounted electrofishing combined with hoop and/or entanglement netting. This was partially implemented in the last two monitoring periods.

**Limitations**
- Canal samples are qualitative and non-comparable.
- Most sites covered only a small proportion of available habitat (sometimes under 5 percent or 10 percent).
- Some fish were not retained or were not fully counted.
