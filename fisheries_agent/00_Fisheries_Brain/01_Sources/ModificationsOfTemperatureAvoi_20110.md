## Executive Summary

This methodological paper describes an improved design for a **temperature avoidance trough**, a laboratory apparatus used to test the behavioral responses of small fishes to thermal gradients. The design builds upon earlier troughs (Meldrim and Gift 1971; Hill 1968; Sprague 1964; Jones 1952; Shelford and Allee 1913) by introducing two key engineering improvements: (1) turbulence-deflecting pools at each water inlet, and (2) an enhanced drainage system incorporating a funnel-shaped center drain. These modifications were designed to reduce turbulence in the test area and to better maintain stable temperature differentials — both critical for producing reliable [[Thermal Avoidance Behavior]] data used in setting [[Thermal Effluent Standards]] for aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

*(Not applicable — this paper concerns apparatus design rather than fish morphology. Structural/engineering specifications of the apparatus are detailed below in lieu of biological morphology.)*

**Apparatus Construction Materials:**
- 1.9-cm-thick exterior grade plywood (framing board: 5.1 × 10.2 × 396 cm)
- Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) pipe fittings
- Stainless steel hose clamps
- Flexible plastic tubing
- Nontoxic epoxy paste (joinery) and nontoxic epoxy paint (coating)

**Structural Design — Two-Level Trough:**
- **Support floor**: forms structural base, bottom of inlet and center drain areas
- **Test floor**: elevated on four blocks (30.5 cm long × 8.9 cm high × 3.3 cm wide), creating turbulence-deflecting pools at each inlet port
- **Side drains**: two, 1.4 cm diameter, positioned 3.8 cm above test floor (drain warmer, less dense water)
- **Center drain**: funnel-shaped, formed by two blocks cut at 45° angle, recessed into support floor (drains cooler, denser water rapidly)
- **Baffle plates**: removable, perforated, placed at each end of test floor to exclude fish from inlet pools; a fixed perforated baffle covers the center drain; nylon screening covers side drains
- **Water level control**: maintained via adjustable valve apertures on all drains
- **Temperature monitoring**: temperature-sensing probes distributed evenly throughout trough

Material choices (PVC, plastic tubing, epoxy) were deliberately selected to **avoid introducing dissolved metals** into test water, a known confound in toxicology/behavior studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

This apparatus is not tied to a specific ecological niche but rather serves as a standardized tool for simulating thermal gradients relevant to real-world [[Thermal Pollution]] scenarios, such as those created by power plant effluents and other industrial heated discharges. The trough is used to assess [[Temperature Preference]] and [[Temperature Avoidance]] responses in small fishes, informing environmental impact assessments (Gift 1977; Stauffer et al. 1975) and the derivation of protective thermal criteria for aquatic ecosystems (Coutant 1977; Beltz et al. 1974; Raney and Menzel 1973).

The apparatus is designed for use with **small fishes**, broadly applicable across freshwater and estuarine taxa rather than a single species or family, consistent with prior avoidance-testing work on estuarine fishes (Meldrim and Gift 1971) and other taxa such as the spring cavefish, *[[Chologaster Agassizi]]* (Hill 1968).

## Key Findings

- **Turbulence reduction**: Dye injection tests showed that while substantial turbulence persisted in the lower one-third of the inlet port area (due to high water velocity needed to maintain water levels), the **test area itself exhibited laminar flow** — confirming elimination of turbulence problems present in earlier trough designs.
- **Improved thermal stratification maintenance**: The funnel-shaped center drain design allowed **cooler, denser water to be removed more rapidly** than in previous designs, while side drains effectively discharged **warmer, less dense water** — together enabling better maintenance of stable temperature differentials required for valid avoidance testing.
- **Practical problem solved**: Researchers at the Appalachian Environmental Laboratory had previously found it difficult to maintain temperature differentials using the original modified trough of [[Meldrim And Gift 1971]]; the new modifications significantly reduced this difficulty.
- **Material design choice**: Use of PVC, plastic tubing, and epoxy coatings (rather than metal fixtures) prevented dissolved metal contamination of test water, safeguarding the validity of behavioral bioassays.
- **Design lineage**: This trough represents an iterative refinement of a long lineage of avoidance/preference testing apparatuses dating back to [[Shelford And Allee 1913]], through [[Jones 1952]], [[Sprague 1964]], [[Hill 1968]], and [[Meldrim And Gift 1971]].
