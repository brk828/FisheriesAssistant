# Proposed Endangered Status for the Independence Valley and Clover Valley Speckled Dace

#ichthyology #marine-biology #rhinichthys

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Federal Register]] notice from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (the Service) announcing a public hearing on the proposed listing of two closely related fish, the [[Clover Valley Speckled Dace]] and the [[Independence Valley Speckled Dace]], as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Both fish are restricted to springs in northwestern Nevada. The hearing was scheduled for April 7, 1988, in Wells, Nevada, and written comments were accepted until late April 1988. The scan also contains two unrelated notices (an Interstate Commerce Commission rail exemption extension and a Corps of Engineers fragment), which are summarized only briefly below.

## Background & Description

- **Taxa:** Two subspecies of the speckled dace, *Rhinichthys osculus*. The Clover Valley form is *R. o. oligoporus* and the Independence Valley form is *R. o. lethoporus* (OCR-garbled names corrected).
- **Range:** The Clover Valley dace is known from only **two small springs**. The Independence Valley dace is known from **one spring**. Both are in northwestern Nevada.
- **Summary of threats:**
  1. Extremely limited distribution.
  2. Vulnerability of habitat to disturbance from [[Irrigation Practices]] (human irrigation).
  3. Introduction of [[Non-Native Aquatic Species]].
- **Document scope:** The notice does not provide population counts, body measurements, life-history data, or habitat chemistry. Its focus is regulatory and procedural.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish depend on very small spring habitats. Because each population occupies a single spring or two springs, any change to water supply, through irrigation diversion or drawdown, or any establishment of non-native species could affect the entire population. The notice describes these habitats as highly vulnerable to perturbation but gives no quantitative data on water flow, temperature, or species assemblages.

## Key Findings

**1. Proposed listing and procedural timeline**
- A proposal to list both fish as endangered was published in the Federal Register at 53 FR 35282 in September 1987.
- The comment period was extended by notices at 52 FR 45976 (December 1987) and 53 FR 5434 (February 24, 1988).
- The comment period was set to close in late April 1988. The exact closing day is partly illegible in the scan.

**2. Public hearing**
- **Date and time:** Thursday, April 7, 1988, 7:00 to 9:00 p.m.
- **Location:** Wells High School, Wells, Nevada.
- **Purpose:** To allow all interested parties to submit comments on the proposed determination.
- **Requests:** Local residents Norman Sholes and Robert Wright requested the hearing on December 15, 1987, and January 15, 1988.

**3. Contact and submission**
- Written comments go to the Regional Director, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, 500 N.E. Multnomah Street, Suite 1692, Portland, Oregon 97232.
- Contact person: Wayne S. White, Chief, Division of Endangered Species.

**4. Legal authority**
- The action is authorized under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended by several public laws, including Pub. L. 93-205 and Pub. L. 95-632.
- The proposed rule would amend 50 CFR Part 17 (Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants).

**5. Unrelated items in the scan**
- **Interstate Commerce Commission (Ex Parte No. 346, Sub-No. 24):** The comment deadline for a rulemaking on rail general exemption authority for miscellaneous manufactured commodities was extended from March 28, 1988, to May 10, 1988. The document also gives a later date of May 16, 1988. The scan is inconsistent on this point, and the most recent date stated is May 16, 1988.
- **Corps of Engineers:** A fragmentary signature block is included. No substantive content is given.
