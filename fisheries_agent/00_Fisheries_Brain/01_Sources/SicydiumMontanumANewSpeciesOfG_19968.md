# Sicydium montanum, a new species of goby from Venezuela

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Gobiidae #taxonomy #species-description

## Executive Summary

This 1920 taxonomic description by Carl L. Hubbs establishes [[Sicydium montanum]] as a new species of goby collected from a mountain brook in [[Macuto]], [[Caracas]], [[Venezuela]]. The species is distinguished from related congeners [[Sicydium punctatum]] and [[Sicydium buscki]] by its larger scales, distinctive coloration pattern, and other morphological characters. Eleven specimens ranging from 21-29 mm in length were collected by Ned Dearborn on February 1, 1908.

## Background & Description

[[Sicydium montanum]] is a small freshwater goby belonging to the family [[Gobiidae]]. The holotype specimen measures 29 mm in total length (to caudal base) and is catalogued as Cat. No. 9053 in the Field Museum of Natural History. Ten paratypes measuring 21-28 mm were collected alongside the holotype.

**Meristic characters:** Dorsal rays VI-I, 10; anal rays I, 9 (9 or 10). Scales comparatively large, approximately 48 (to 46) in median series from upper edge of pectoral to end of last vertebra. Scales deeply sculptured with long basal radii and bearing strong hastate spines in single series near posterior margin. Scales obsolescent above pectoral fins and on nape, absent from belly.

**Morphometric characters:** Body slender, scarcely compressed anteriorly; greatest depth 5.4 (to about 7.0) in body length. Caudal peduncle depth 1.6 in its length from end of anal base, 2.3 (to 2.5) in head. Head blunt and slightly depressed, flattened above; head length 4.25 (4.0 to 4.5) in standard length. Eye placed high with upper edge entering head profile; eye length about 4.0 in head. Interorbital slightly narrower than eye. Snout blunt, overhanging inferior mouth, length 2.8 in head (shorter in young specimens at about 4.0 in head). Upper jaw 2.5 in head, extending horizontally backward slightly beyond vertical from middle of pupil (mouth smaller in young).

**Dentition:** Premaxillary teeth brownish, arranged comb-like in single series along wide arch. Mandible included with entirely pale teeth that are fewer, larger, and less regularly arranged than upper jaw teeth. Gill-slit approximately as wide as gape.

**Fin characteristics:** Spinous dorsal rather high with second and third spines longest at 1.35 (1.25 to 1.7) in head, equal to anal base length or pectoral fin length. Interdorsal space approximately as long as eye. Second dorsal originates before vertical from anus, height about 2.0 in head. Caudal fin rounded, as long as head. Pelvic disc length 1.7.

**Coloration:** Pattern rather sharply defined with six cross bars or blotches extending downward across middle of sides between middle of spinous dorsal and caudal base, more or less fused medially to form longitudinal streak. Three narrow irregular bars diverging downward on each side from nape. Sides of head and snout darkened; under surface of head not punctate. First dorsal spines dark; second dorsal conspicuously spotted or blotched with dark pigment. Dark spot on caudal base widely fused with last body blotch. Anal fin with blackish subterminal streak. Pectoral fins with basal mottling. Pelvics unmarked. All fins more or less dusky.

Variation in color pattern noted among paratypes: some specimens show more sharply distinct bars than holotype, while others display more conspicuous longitudinal dark band formed by bar fusion along middle of sides, continuing forward around snout. In some paratypes, bars diverge upward to enclose light area between two arms of V-shaped mark, and bars before spinous dorsal divide to form five or six zebra-like markings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Sicydium montanum]] inhabits mountain brooks in the coastal mountain range near [[Caracas]], [[Venezuela]]. The species was collected at [[Macuto]], indicating adaptation to freshwater stream environments at elevation. The genus [[Sicydium]] comprises amphidromous gobies typically found in fast-flowing streams and rivers.

The collection locality represents tropical montane freshwater habitat. The presence of eleven specimens in a single collection suggests the species may occur in aggregations or schools in suitable habitat.

## Key Findings

1. **New species establishment:** [[Sicydium montanum]] is described as distinct from congeners [[Sicydium punctatum]] Perugia and [[Sicydium buscki]] Evermann and Clark based on larger scale counts (approximately 48 vs. fewer in related species), distinctive coloration pattern, and morphological differences.

2. **Diagnostic scale characteristics:** The species exhibits comparatively large scales (about 48 in median series) that are deeply sculptured over most of their surface by long basal radii and bear strong hastate spines in single series near posterior margin. Scales are obsolescent above pectoral fins and on nape, and absent from belly.

3. **Distinctive coloration pattern:** Six cross bars or blotches between middle of spinous dorsal and caudal base, often fused medially to form longitudinal streak; three narrow irregular bars diverging from nape; conspicuously spotted or blotched second dorsal fin; blackish subterminal streak on anal fin.

4. **Type series:** Holotype (29 mm) and ten paratypes (21-28 mm) collected February 1, 1908, by Ned Dearborn from mountain brook at [[Macuto]], [[Caracas]], [[Venezuela]]. Holotype deposited as Cat. No. 9053, Field Museum of Natural History.

5. **Ontogenetic variation:** Young specimens show proportionally shorter snout (about as long as eye, contained about 4.0 times in head vs. 2.8 in adults) and smaller mouth compared to adults.

6. **Color pattern variation:** Paratypes exhibit variation in bar distinctness, longitudinal band prominence, V-shaped markings, and zebra-like patterns anterior to spinous dorsal (five or six markings in some specimens).
