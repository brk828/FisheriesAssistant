## Executive Summary

This document is a formal letter from the [[ASIH Environmental Quality Committee]] to the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] responding to the Draft Environmental Assessment for the proposed [[Strawberry Valley Fishery Restoration]] project (Publication 87-14). The Committee declines to endorse the renovation plan for [[Strawberry Reservoir]] and its tributaries, reaffirming the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists' 1987 resolution urging caution. The letter raises concerns regarding non-native species introductions, chemical piscicide use ([[Rotenone]]), sterility claims for introduced [[Rainbow Trout]], and the absence of consideration for restoring native fish stocks, including the [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]]. While not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, the letter contains substantive scientific commentary on fish taxonomy, genetics, and stocking practices relevant to salmonid conservation.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (meristics, coloration, body proportions) are presented in this source. The document is a policy/management critique rather than a taxonomic or anatomical description. Species mentioned are referenced only by common and scientific name for taxonomic/subspecies identification purposes:

- **Bear Lake cutthroat trout** — a subspecies of [[Cutthroat Trout]] native to the Bear Lake drainage, being proposed for introduction into the Strawberry Valley/Colorado River drainage system, outside its native range.
- **Colorado River cutthroat trout** (*Salmo clarki pleuriticus*) — the subspecies historically native to the Strawberry Valley/Colorado River drainage, notably absent from restoration planning.
- **Rainbow trout** — proposed as a sport fish introduction; claims of induced sterility (presumably via triploidy) are questioned.
- **Triploid grass carp** — referenced only as a comparative case study of previously overstated sterility claims in fisheries management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System affected**: [[Strawberry Reservoir]] and its tributary streams, part of the Colorado River drainage basin in Utah.
- **Native fish history**: The Draft Environmental Assessment reportedly fails to review the historical native fish assemblage of the system, an omission flagged as a significant scientific gap.
- **Non-native introduction risk**: Introducing [[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]] (native to a different Utah drainage) into the Colorado River drainage risks **genetic miscegenation/hybridization** with the native [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] subspecies (*Salmo clarki pleuriticus*), which historically occupied the system.
- **Chemical treatment ecology**: The proposed use of large-scale [[Rotenone]] treatment to eliminate target (undesirable) fish species is highlighted as ecologically risky — rotenone can behave unpredictably, causing unintended kills beyond the treatment zone. Sodium cyanide is noted as a theoretically more complete/faster-degrading alternative piscicide, though publicly and politically unacceptable due to toxicity perception (particularly among livestock ranchers).
- **Reintroduction dynamics**: No guarantee exists that eradicated "target species" (undesirable non-native or hybridized fish) will not recolonize the system, undermining the long-term efficacy of the renovation.
- **Broader stocking intent**: Bear Lake cutthroat trout stocks raised in Strawberry Valley are intended as **broodstock sources for further introductions** into other Utah waters and potentially other states — extending impacts well beyond the immediate ecosystem and historical native range.

## Key Findings

1. The ASIH Environmental Quality Committee **does not endorse** the Strawberry Valley Fishery restoration/renovation project, upholding the Society's 1987 cautionary resolution.
2. The Draft Environmental Assessment is criticized for being dominated by **recreational and economic considerations**, at the expense of ecological/native species considerations.
3. The assessment fails to address the history of **native fish species** in the Strawberry Valley system.
4. Introducing [[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]] into the Colorado River drainage constitutes introduction of a **non-native subspecies**, risking hybridization with native [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] stocks.
5. **Sterility claims** for introduced rainbow trout are questioned, drawing a direct parallel to previously disproven claims of universal sterility in triploid [[Grass Carp]].
6. Large-scale **rotenone** application is acknowledged as having unpredictable off-target ecological effects; sodium cyanide is mentioned as a chemically superior but publicly unacceptable alternative.
7. There is **no guarantee** that eradication efforts will permanently resolve the underlying fishery management problem, historically demonstrated by the general ineffectiveness of non-native species introductions as long-term solutions.
8. The Committee notes the plan does **not consider reintroducing native fish species**, including the historically native Colorado River cutthroat trout subspecies, as an alternative restoration strategy.
9. Long-term genetic consequences of planned inter-basin trout transfers and hybridization are unpredictable, though existing literature suggests hybridization as a **predictable outcome** of such introductions.
10. Broodstock development from this project may facilitate further non-native introductions **beyond Utah**, compounding potential ecological risks regionally.
