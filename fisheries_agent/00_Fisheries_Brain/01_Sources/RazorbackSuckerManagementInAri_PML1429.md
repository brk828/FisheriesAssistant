# Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen texanus) Management in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a management summary of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) restoration in Arizona, written by Paul C. Marsh of the Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University. A Spanish translation of the same text follows the English version. Management began in 1981 under a 10-year memorandum of understanding between the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (GFD) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Over 10 million juveniles, fingerlings, and fry had been stocked by 1985. The main threat identified is predation by introduced catfishes, and the paper recommends modified stocking strategies to improve survival.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a native Arizona fish. The document gives no morphological or life-history description beyond size data for stocked fish.
- **Program start:** 1981, under a 10-year memorandum of understanding between GFD and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Production:** Fish were raised at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico. Grow-out to larger sizes occurred there and at [[GFD Page Springs Hatchery]].
- **Stocking scale:** By 1985, more than 10 million juveniles, fingerlings, and fry had been stocked.
- **Study authorship:** Paul C. Marsh, Center for Environmental Studies and Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe, Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study systems:** Stocking took place at sites on the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Verde River]] and their tributaries. The English text gives 23 sites; the Spanish text gives 28. The discrepancy is unresolved in the source.
- **Monitoring:** Stocking sites and downstream riverine reaches were monitored annually. Reservoirs on each system were sampled in 1985.
- **Survival and recapture:** Few stocked fish were recaptured more than a few weeks after introduction.
- **Dispersal:** Fish of 62–213 mm total length moved downstream, mainly at night.
- **Predation:** Introduced catfishes, [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) and [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), can remove a significant percentage of stocked fish. Predation is therefore the principal documented threat to stocked populations.

## Key Findings

1. **Predation is a major constraint.** Introduced catfish predation has the potential to remove significant percentages of stocked [[Razorback Sucker]].
2. **Post-stocking dispersal is downstream and nocturnal.** Studies in 1984 and 1985 found that 62–213 mm TL fish moved downstream at night.
3. **Recapture rates are very low.** Few stocked fish were recaptured beyond a few weeks.
4. **Recommended stocking strategy:**
   - Stock at upstream locations where predator populations are absent or small.
   - Stock in winter, when predation may be reduced.
   - Stock where predators have been removed or at least depleted.
   - Introduce a few individuals over a wide time span rather than large groups at once.
5. **Recommended monitoring strategy:** Restrict intensive monitoring to a single river system to make better use of limited resources and produce clearer information on successes and failures.
6. **Research priorities:** Investigate the possible differential survival of [[Raceway Rearing]] versus [[Pond Rearing]] fish, and the potential for [[Predator Avoidance Training]] before release.
