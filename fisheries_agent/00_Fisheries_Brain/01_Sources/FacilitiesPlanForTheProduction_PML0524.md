# Facilities Plan for the Production of Endangered Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This plan identifies the hatchery and grow-out facilities needed to produce the numbers of [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] called for in the revised stocking plans of the State of Colorado and State of Utah. It was prepared for the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] (Recovery Program), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 6, Denver, Colorado, by Thomas E. Czapla, Ph.D., Propagation, Life History, and Research Coordinator.

The revised plans were driven by recovery goals that call for adult populations sufficient for self-sustaining populations. The plan concludes that the Recovery Program has sufficient facilities and grow-out ponds to meet the revised stocking plans. Revised plans call for fewer but larger fish, which adds about one year of culture for bonytail and possibly two years for razorback sucker. The estimated annual cost of PIT-tagging 40,280 bonytail, 2,680 Colorado Pikeminnow, and 34,940 razorback sucker is approximately $300,000.

## Background & Description

The plan covers three endangered Upper Colorado River Basin fishes, each with a distinct culture approach:

- **Bonytail** (*Gila elegans*) are essentially extirpated from the system. The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] broodstock was founded from 10 wild fish collected in [[Lake Mohave]] in 1981. [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] has developed a separate broodstock from different year classes since 1996.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) are reared intensively in tanks. The Colorado River population is estimated at about 700 adults (Osmundson 2001). The Colorado Division of Wildlife produced a culture manual (Schler 2001).
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is the only remaining riverine population in the Upper Colorado Basin, in the middle [[Green River]], with fewer than 500 adults. Experimental stocking in the Upper Colorado River, including the [[Gunnison River]], has been ongoing since 1996.

Stocking size targets are: bonytail about 200 mm total length (TL) and about 150 g; Colorado Pikeminnow 150 mm TL; razorback sucker 300 mm TL and about 250 g.

Stated objectives are to (1) retain as much genetic diversity as possible under [[Genetic Management]] guidelines, and (2) provide sufficient facilities and space to produce and stock the required numbers and sizes of each species.

### Assumptions and Uncertainties

- Hatchery managers estimate production at 1,000 lb of fish per acre in lined hatchery ponds, and 500 lb per acre in leased grow-out ponds, which are highly variable.
- [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] ponds are estimated to produce about 2,000 lb per acre, owing to feeding, warm water, and a longer growing season.
- Survival in hatcheries and grow-out ponds is highly variable. Survival estimates in the state stocking plans are assumed accurate.
- Growth depends on food availability and fish density. With sufficient food and low density, stocking sizes could be reached a year earlier.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan's rationale rests on river habitat and recovery targets. The Colorado Pikeminnow stocking plan recommended augmenting reaches of the Gunnison River and the upper Colorado River above Palisade, Colorado. Razorback sucker stocking has included larval and juvenile fish released into flooded bottomlands, where wetland juveniles showed significant growth and survival. Some stocked fish reached spawning bars. Stocked razorback suckers also passed through the [[Redlands Fish Ladder]] on the Gunnison River: one fish stocked in 1996 and four others stocked the following spring used the ladder in the summer of the reporting year. Successful spawning and recruitment of stocked fish had not yet been documented.

Broodstock sources span the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison rivers, and both the Colorado River and San Juan River arms of [[Lake Powell]]. Breeding follows a matrix designed to maintain genetic diversity and limit inbreeding.

## Key Findings

### Bonytail

- **Colorado stocking plan:** 24,000 bonytail annually, to be raised at [[Mumma Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] (Alamosa, Colorado) with associated local grow-out ponds in the San Luis Valley.
- **Utah stocking plan:** The Background section gives 15,280 bonytail per year, but the Approach section and cost totals use 16,280 (24,000 + 16,280 = 40,280). The discrepancy should be checked against the source plan.
- **Dexter request:** 70,000 fry annually from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Mumma production:** Fry are reared in tanks through the first fall, then moved to grow-out ponds for a second season. At 500 lb per acre, 15.9 acres are needed. Mumma and affiliated facilities have over 35 acres of ponds.
- **Wahweap production:** At 2,000 lb per acre, 2.6 acres are needed for 16,280 bonytail. The text gives current availability as 2.8 acres in the Approach section and 3.2 acres in the Summary.
- **Historical capacity:** Wahweap once raised up to 80,000 bonytail, sized 120 to 180 mm TL.

### Colorado Pikeminnow

- **Stocking need:** 2,680 fish tagged in total; the Recovery Program will request 10,000 fry annually from Dexter.
- **Distribution:** Fry are split equally between the [[Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facility]] (Grand Junction, Colorado) and Mumma.
- **Rearing:** Fish are raised intensively in tanks and fed through winter to reach 150 mm TL sooner.
- **Surplus:** Fish grown beyond the Colorado plan will be offered to the [[San Juan River Recovery Implementation Program]].
- **Stream-side spawning:** Attempts continue in the Colorado River at Grand Valley, with limited past success.

### Razorback Sucker

- **Colorado stocking plan:** 16,440 fish annually, produced at the Grand Valley facility with leased grow-out ponds. Fish are reared intensively in 4-ft and 8-ft diameter tanks in the first season, then moved to grow-out ponds in spring. At 500 lb per acre, 18.1 acres are needed for two years of growth (36.2 acres if a third season is required). About 98 acres of grow-out ponds are available in the Grand Valley area.
- **Utah stocking plan:** 18,500 fish annually, produced at [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] with leased grow-out ponds in the Uintah Basin. Hatchery ponds (about 0.2 acre each in the past) are being modified with a water re-use system for indoor culture in the first calendar year.
- **Ouray acreage:** Hatchery ponds yield 9,100 fish, needing 5.0 acres at 1,000 lb per acre. Leased grow-out ponds yield 9,400 fish, needing 10.4 acres for a second year class (20.4 acres for a third). Ouray has 10.8 acres of hatchery ponds and 18 acres of leased grow-out ponds, with 12 to 15 more acres available from private landowners.
- **Breeding:** A 25 male × 25 female breeding matrix is used, and the Green River stock has 25 female parents maintained to develop F1 fish for augmentation.

### PIT Tagging and Costs

All stocked fish will be PIT-tagged ([[PIT Tagging]]) to evaluate survival, growth, stocking location, and lot survival.

| Species | Fish tagged | Timing | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonytail | 40,280 | Autumn of second growing season | $151,050 |
| Colorado Pikeminnow | 2,680 | Autumn after one full year of growth | $10,050 |
| Razorback Sucker | 34,940 | Second or third production year | $131,025 |

The three costs total $292,125, consistent with the approximately $300,000 cited in the Executive Summary.

### Summary Conclusions

- The Recovery Program has sufficient facilities and grow-out ponds for the revised stocking plans.
- Revised plans call for fewer, larger fish, requiring about one extra year for bonytail and possibly two for razorback sucker.
- Remaining acreage needs, shown above, are met by existing and leased ponds, with additional private-pond options in the Uintah Basin.
