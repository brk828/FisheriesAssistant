# Virgin River Monitoring: Arizona Summary Report (1994)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #virgin-river

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] memorandum (dated May 16, 1994) summarizing fish monitoring data collected from two stations on the [[Virgin River]] in Arizona: **Cedar Pockets Station** and **Beaver Dam Wash Station**. Sampling consisted of seine hauls conducted on May 3, 1994, and supplemental hoop-net sets deployed at Beaver Dam Wash from May 4–5, 1994. The report is part of a broader multi-agency monitoring effort for the Virgin River drainage, with distribution to biologists from [[Utah State University]], [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]], [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 6), and [[Arizona State University]] — reflecting the interstate, multi-jurisdictional management interest in this reach, likely tied to conservation of the endangered [[Virgin River Chub]] and other native Colorado River Basin fishes.

Note: The source OCR text for the seine-haul catch tables (Cedar Pockets and Beaver Dam Wash) is significantly degraded, precluding reliable extraction of per-species, per-sample counts. Only aggregate sample totals and the narrative hoop-net catch summary are extractable with confidence.

## Background & Description
The memo does not provide morphological descriptions of individual specimens; it is a catch-tally monitoring report. However, life-stage and reproductive condition were noted for hoop-net captures:

- **GIROSE (likely [[Virgin River Chub]], *Gila seminuda*, syn. *Gila robusta seminuda*)**: 6 adults captured in Hoop Net #1, including **2 ripe males** (indicating active spawning condition at time of capture, early May). 1 additional adult captured in Hoop Net #2.
- **CALA (likely [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], *Catostomus latipinnis*)**: 2 adults captured in Hoop Net #1.
- **CACL (likely [[Desert Sucker]], *Catostomus clarkii*)**: 3 adults captured in Hoop Net #1.
- **AMME (likely [[Black Bullhead]], *Ameiurus melas*)**: 6 adults in Hoop Net #1; 2 adults in Hoop Net #2 — a non-native ictalurid.

All hoop-net-captured fish were classified as adults; no juvenile or larval size-class data are reported in the legible portion of the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat sampled**: Two distinct mesohabitat types were targeted with hoop nets at the Beaver Dam Wash confluence with the mainstem Virgin River:
  - **Hoop Net #1**: set along a submerged log in the mainstem, ~50 ft below the mouth of [[Beaver Dam Wash]] — structured, cover-associated habitat.
  - **Hoop Net #2**: set in an eddy behind a large boulder, ~225 ft downstream of the confluence — low-velocity refuge habitat.
- **Species assemblage**: The co-occurrence of native cyprinid ([[Gila Seminuda|Virgin River Chub]]) and catostomids ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]]) alongside a non-native ictalurid ([[Black Bullhead]]) reflects the mixed native/non-native fish community typical of the lower [[Virgin River]] and broader [[Colorado River Basin]] desert stream systems.
- **Spawning activity**: The presence of ripe (reproductively active) male GIROSE in early May suggests this reach and time period correspond to active spawning behavior for this taxon, an important consideration for habitat protection timing.
- **Sampling design**: Combined use of seine hauls (multiple timed samples per station, up to 10 samples at Cedar Pockets and 11 at Beaver Dam Wash) and passive hoop-net gear indicates an effort to characterize both mobile/shallow-water and structure-associated/deeper fish assemblages.

## Key Findings
- Fish monitoring was conducted at two Arizona stations on the [[Virgin River]] (Cedar Pockets and Beaver Dam Wash) on May 3, 1994, with supplemental hoop-net gear deployed May 4–5, 1994.
- Cedar Pockets seine sampling total across all hauls was reported as **224** individuals (aggregate; species-level breakdown illegible in source OCR).
- Beaver Dam Wash seine sampling totals were substantially higher, with multiple species rows summing into totals in the hundreds (values partially legible, e.g., a row total of "958" and another of "450"), consistent with Beaver Dam Wash being a richer or more productive sampling station than Cedar Pockets — though exact species identities per row could not be reliably reconstructed from the OCR.
- Hoop-net sampling at the Beaver Dam Wash confluence documented four taxa: probable [[Virgin River Chub]] (GIROSE), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (CALA), [[Desert Sucker]] (CACL), and [[Black Bullhead]] (AMME) — all captured as adults.
- Two ripe male GIROSE were documented, providing direct evidence of spawning-condition adults present in the mainstem Virgin River near the Beaver Dam Wash confluence in early May.
- The report's distribution list underscores that Virgin River fish monitoring in this period was a coordinated interstate/interagency effort spanning Arizona, Utah, and Nevada wildlife agencies, academic researchers, and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], consistent with recovery planning for federally listed Virgin River fishes.
- **Data limitation**: The seine-haul catch tables for both stations are extensively corrupted by OCR errors (garbled species codes and misaligned columns), and numeric values should be treated as approximate or unreliable pending review of the original source document.
