# Status Report and Recommendations for Water Acquisition Activities: Recovery Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This February 21, 1990 status report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]] presents recommendations from the Water Acquisition Ad Hoc Subcommittee of the Management Group. Its goal is to expedite the acquisition of water rights, mainly by converting them to [[Instream Flow]] for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The report covers seven topics: general recommendations, the Yampa River, the Little Snake River, the 15-mile reach and Colorado River below the Gunnison River, the White River, the [[Farm Foreclosure Program]], and [[Conditional Water Rights]]. The document contains policy and administrative recommendations, not research data. No fish population, sampling, or biological measurements are reported.

## Background & Description

The [[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin]] is the framework for this work. At its August 1989 meeting, the Recovery Implementation Committee directed the Management Group to develop recommendations to speed up water acquisition. An ad hoc subcommittee was formed on September 13, 1989. It met on October 3 and November 3, 1989, and again in January 1990 (the source gives this date inconsistently). The Management Group adopted the recommendations for committee review. Subcommittee members came from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau of Land Management]], the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]], the [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]], the [[The Nature Conservancy]], and the Colorado Department of Natural Resources, among others.

Water rights are the main tool for securing flows. [[Absolute Water Rights]] are senior and can be converted to instream flows. [[Conditional Water Rights]] are unperfected decrees that carry legal risk. Before the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] can file for an instream flow appropriation, it must show that water is available, that the natural environment will be preserved to a reasonable degree, and that the flows can be met without material injury to other rights.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report is organized by river reach, and the reasons for each choice are habitat-based:

- **Yampa River:** The [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Conditional Water Right]] controls the mainstem. Acquiring it could greatly benefit endangered fish in the Lower Yampa River. The Yampa is ranked the top priority for acquisition investigation.
- **Little Snake River:** Conditional rights (Pothook, Three Forks, Powderwash) could affect flows at the Yampa confluence.
- **15-mile reach (Palisades to Gunnison):** Flow recommendations exist for July through September. Year-round recommendations are being developed. Ruedi Reservoir water is a possible source.
- **Colorado River below the Gunnison River:** Flow recommendations are pending the [[Aspinall Unit Section 7 Consultation]].
- **White River:** No flow recommendations exist. Research is needed on habitat and life-stage use by endangered fishes. The [[White River Dam]] 404 Permit is also an issue.

The Yamcolo Project return flows could reach the 15-mile reach, but only in June and July. Those flows are not large enough to benefit habitat there.

## Key Findings

**General recommendations**
- The Management Group will report on water acquisition status before each Committee meeting.
- Water rights engineer and appraiser services will be retained on an as-needed basis.
- All acquisitions (purchases, donations, instream flow appropriations) must be evaluated so that Recovery Program spending matches expected benefit.

**Yampa River**
- Phase II interim flow recommendations may be used for case-by-case acquisitions if the acquisition is smaller than the interim flow. Refined Phase III recommendations are needed before any appropriation filing.
- The Yampa Basin Feasibility Study is jointly funded for FY90 and FY91, with completion due April 1992. It will identify alternative upper-basin projects that meet local needs while protecting endangered fish.
- The Cross Mountain Ranch - Winter Unit (about 37,868 acre-feet of senior absolute irrigation rights; about 27,080 deeded acres; offered at $2.5 million) is being pursued by TNC, FWS, and BLM. The appraisal is due by March 1996.

**Little Snake River**
- The FY90 budget includes $37,588 in Section 7 funds for a technical evaluation and appraisal. The work is done through a cooperative agreement with Wyoming.
- Phase I models the hydrology. Phase II appraises rights using probability, use type, dollar value, discount rates, and present worth analysis.
- Target dates: agreement executed by March 1, 1996 (the text also gives March 1, 1998); project completed by August 1, 1998 (the text also gives August 1, 1999). The source is inconsistent on these dates.
- The Colorado River District may donate the Pothook and Powderwash conditional rights.
- Acquiring senior conditional rights to firm up junior filings may serve Recovery objectives, but the approach needs credit for "sufficient progress" to be negotiated.

**15-mile reach and Colorado River below the Gunnison**
- First priority is buying existing absolute rights that could be converted to instream flows.
- A water availability study is to begin in 1996 (contingent on funding) and be finished in FY91. A decision on an instream appropriation follows.
- Ruedi Reservoir leasing should be evaluated for a short- or long-term supply, with a target of December 1998. Congressional cancellation of Ruedi repayment debt was judged unacceptable on the West Slope.
- Industrial Resources, Inc. offered a 1965-decree conditional right of 2,998 cfs (nine years in ten) in Horsethief Canyon. The Management Group recommended no action.
- The Bureau's report on alternate water sources is due April 1996 (the recommendations also say April 1998).

**White River**
- A work plan for research proposals is due May 1, 1996, starting with a literature review.
- A status report on the 404 Permit for the White River Dam and its biological opinion is due by March 1, 1996.

**Farm Foreclosure Program**
- Under a recent federal law, FWS may acquire water rights tied to foreclosed farm properties, possibly at no cost. Stripping rights could affect adjacent wetlands.
- FWS will develop screening criteria (location, consumptive yield, minimum quantities). Action on each right requires consultation with the relevant state agency.

**Conditional water rights**
- Colorado courts have not settled whether conditional rights for municipal, industrial, or agricultural use can be changed to instream flows. The Division 5 Water Court has issued such decrees, but the Colorado Supreme Court has not ruled. Resolution may take one to two years.
- The Recovery Program should not actively pursue such rights until the court issue is resolved, or until technical and CWCB review is complete.
- Donations or purchases are acceptable if the rights have significant priority value and volume, do not interfere with higher-priority work, and do not impose heavy diligence burdens.
