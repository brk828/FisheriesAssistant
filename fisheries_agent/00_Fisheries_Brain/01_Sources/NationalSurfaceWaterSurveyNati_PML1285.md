# National Stream Survey Pilot Survey: Field Operations Report

#freshwater-ecology #water-chemistry #stream-survey

## Executive Summary

This EPA Project Summary reports on the field operations of the [[Pilot Survey]] for Phase I of the [[National Stream Survey]] (NSS), a component of the [[National Surface Water Survey]] (NSWS). The NSWS supports the [[National Acid Precipitation Assessment Program]] by assessing the chemical status of surface waters that may be at risk from [[Acid Deposition]]. The pilot survey sampled 61 stream reaches in the [[Blue Ridge Region]] and tested the research design, equipment, protocols, and logistics before full-scale work.

The pilot survey was completed on schedule and showed that a large-scale synoptic stream survey is logistically feasible. The authors also concluded that the research design, quality assurance plan, and data analysis plan would meet the NSS objectives. The document is a field report, not a fish study. It does not address fish, other aquatic organisms, or biological communities.

## Background & Description

The NSWS is a three-phase program run by the U.S. [[Environmental Protection Agency]]. Its main goal is to provide quantitative, regional-scale assessments of the present chemical status of surface waters. It has two component projects: the [[National Lake Survey]] and the NSS.

The pilot survey tested all aspects of the proposed NSS Phase I plan, including:
- data quality objectives
- sampling design
- sampling and analytical methods
- equipment
- field logistics
- data analysis

Field operations were planned and run by the [[Environmental Monitoring Systems Laboratory]] in Las Vegas, Nevada (EMSL-Las Vegas). Field personnel and support came from [[Lockheed Engineering And Management Services Company]]. A mobile [[Field Laboratory Trailer]] was set up at Southwest Technical College in [[Sylva North Carolina]].

Personnel with prior NSWS experience were recruited as field samplers or field laboratory analysts. Each group received 5 days of training, and additional training was held at the field station before sampling began.

**Site selection:**
- 61 stream reaches were targeted. A reach is a stream length between two tributary confluences.
- 56 were "regular" reaches chosen by the NSS statistical design.
- The remaining reaches were "special interest" sites, selected for historical water quality data or for ongoing studies by other investigators. The report says six, but 61 minus 56 gives five, so this count is inconsistent in the source.
- A site dossier with maps and access information was compiled for each location. Each site was visited just before sampling to verify access, record characteristics, and install hydrologic staff gauges.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey addressed the chemistry of stream water in relation to acidic deposition. The measured parameters were:
- pH, measured in both open-system and closed-system aliquots
- dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC)
- conductivity and dissolved oxygen, measured in situ
- turbidity and true color
- monomeric aluminum species, which are important for acidification effects

Water samples were collected with a portable [[Peristaltic Pump]]. The field laboratory processed bulk samples into eight aliquots. Two were prepared for monomeric aluminum analysis using methyl isobutyl ketone (MIBK) extraction, and one of these first passed through an ion-exchange column to remove inorganic aluminum complexes. Preserved aliquots were shipped overnight to a contract analytical laboratory. Data were sent to the data management center at [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] and to quality assurance staff at EMSL-Las Vegas.

## Key Findings

**Operations and effort**
- Field operations ran from March 1 to April 30 and from June 26 to July 17, 1985. The report states that 57 days of operation were needed to complete the required sampling.
- Spring sampling visited each reach at its downstream end three times at two-week intervals. On the third round, 23 reaches were also sampled at their upstream end.
- Summer sampling visited all reaches once at both the downstream and upstream ends.
- Three two-person teams used four-wheel-drive vehicles, with some sites reached on foot, by boat, or on horseback. Each team sampled one to three streams per day.
- A total of 759 samples were analyzed, including quality assurance samples.
- The field effort covered almost 45,000 miles of vehicle travel and required 8 to 14 hours of field time per day.
- The per-team cost range in the source is garbled by OCR and cannot be reported reliably.

**Method evaluations**
- The equipment and protocols met NSS requirements.
- Filtering samples during collection was not feasible.
- The peristaltic pump was more suitable than discrete grab sampling.
- Streamside pH measurements showed no apparent effect of CO2 outgassing. The open-system measurement was recommended for future use.
- Conductivity measurement in the field laboratory was feasible in terms of bench space and equipment.
- Preparing the two aluminum extracts was very time-consuming. The authors recommend investigating alternative methods.

**Holding-time experiments (two appendices)**
- Sealed syringes were impermeable to carbon dioxide for at least 7 days when kept refrigerated.
- Unpreserved water held in refrigerated plastic containers with no headspace showed no substantial chemical change for at least 84 hours after collection.
- Sample holding times could therefore be extended without compromising data accuracy or quality.

**Problems and corrective actions**
The report summarizes problems and corrective actions in four areas: field safety, planning and training, field sampling operations, and field laboratory operations. It also gives recommendations for future work and cost information for groups planning similar projects.

**Reporting notes**
- The document gives conflicting dates. The abstract says the pilot survey ran in spring and summer 1986, while the introduction says 1984. Field operations are dated 1985, and the report covers January to July 1985, with work completed in September 1986.
- The report was prepared under a contract with Lockheed Engineering and Management Services Company, Inc., under EPA sponsorship. The EPA project officer was Wesley L. Kinney.
