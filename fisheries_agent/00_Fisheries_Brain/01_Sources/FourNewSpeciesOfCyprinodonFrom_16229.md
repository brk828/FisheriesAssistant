# Four New Cyprinodon Species from Southern Nuevo León, Mexico, with a Key to the C. eximius Complex

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes four new pupfish species of the genus [[Cyprinodon]] from springs on the floor of a previously unreported dry lake bed, which the authors name [[Bolsón de Sandia]] (= Pluvial Lake Sandia), in southern [[Nuevo León]], Mexico. The four species are *C. longidorsalis*, *C. inmemoriam*, *C. veronicae* and *C. ceciliae*. All belong to the [[Cyprinodon eximius Complex]] and are considered a monophyletic lineage closely related to [[Cyprinodon alvarezi]]. Two of the four species are presumed extinct after their springs dried during the study, and the remaining two are rated endangered. The paper also provides morphometric and meristic comparisons, a discriminant analysis, and a revised identification key for the complex.

## Background & Description

The Central Plateau of Nuevo León has few integrated fish faunal studies, and until 1983 only two cyprinodontids were known from the state: [[Megupsilon aporus]] (a monotypic genus) and *C. alvarezi*, both from [[Ojo del Potosí]]. Four new springs were located between 1983 and 1988, each harboring an undescribed endemic pupfish belonging to the *C. eximius* complex as defined by [[Miller (1976)]].

The study area is an arid basin with a fossil lake bed that was photographed from the air in 1983. Climate is classed as BSo hx' (dry, semi-hot) on the lake floor and BS1kx' (semi-dry temperate) at Potosí. Soils at Sandia are sodic-haline. The area lies at the southeastern limit of the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] biotic province and the western piedmont of the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]].

Specimens are deposited in several institutions, including AMNH, IPN-ENCB, TNHC, TU, UAIC, UANL, UMMZ and UNAM. Counts and measurements follow [[Miller (1943)]] and [[Hubbs and Lagler]], with multivariate analysis performed in SPSS.

The four new species share a group diagnosis: four lachrymal pores (three in some *C. alvarezi*), two mandibular pores, a rough tubercled head and anterior body in mature males, metallic blue nuptial coloration, and an iris with two color rings (inner golden yellow, outer pale yellow), in contrast to the all-blue eye of *C. alvarezi*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The region is dominated by groundwater-fed springs in an otherwise intermittent, endorheic drainage system; many streams are dry. The authors attribute the decline of the surviving endemics to a lowering of the water table caused by agricultural overexploitation of groundwater. Habitat degradation is also linked to housing development around springs, a non-functional outlet, and drying. Associated biota include an undescribed endemic crayfish, *Cambarellus* sp., which occurs at Charco La Palma (not noted there), La Trinidad and La Presa. Aquatic vegetation at Charco Azul includes *Ceratophyllum*, *Potamogeton*, *Typha*, *Chara* and *Utricularia*. Surrounding vegetation is mainly acacia, cacti and succulents, with cedar (*Cupressus* sp.) around some springs.

Water temperatures recorded were 21 °C at Charco La Palma, 19 °C at La Trinidad, and 19–20 °C at Charco Azul, with muddy or loamy bottoms.

## Key Findings

- **Cyprinodon longidorsalis** (Charco La Palma): unique in the genus for a long depressed dorsal fin reaching between three-quarters of the caudal peduncle and caudal base; dorsal origin in front of pelvic fins; 0–2 teeth on the fourth ceratobranchial; and a strongly folded cleft of the lower lip. Holotype UANL 8281, 40.6 mm SL. Endangered.
- **Cyprinodon inmemoriam** (La Trinidad): known from a single adult male (holotype UANL 8279, 57.3 mm SL); no females were collected. Characterized by up to four teeth on the fourth ceratobranchial, the shortest maxilla in the group (0.68 times anal base), and head 3.0 times SL. Presumed extinct after the spring dried; no captive stock exists.
- **Cyprinodon veronicae** (Charco Azul = Barreno): head large (2.7–2.9 times SL), maxilla 0.70–0.77 times anal base, males with 6–7 diffuse dark bars, and females with a half-moon shaped ocellar spot smaller than the pupil. Endemic to Charco Azul, 27 km SW of La Ascensión. Endangered.
- **Cyprinodon ceciliae** (La Presa, San Juan de Avilés): nuptial males have 7–8 well-marked black bars extending to the venter; females have a round black ocellar spot smaller than the pupil and a blackish dorsal fin. The spring nearly dried in winter 1991, and attempts in early 1992 failed to find specimens; presumed extinct.
- **Diagnostic traits:** No single character distinguishes all four species; the authors stress that many traits are shared and diagnostic only in combination. Discriminant analysis (37 characters for males, 36 for females) produced well-separated clusters with no overlap, though the closest pairs (males of *C. longidorsalis* and *C. veronicae*; females of *C. veronicae* and *C. ceciliae*) were nearly in contact. Reciprocal comparisons indicated these populations were the most divergent.
- **Conservation:** Both *C. longidorsalis* and *C. veronicae* are listed as Endangered (Lozano and Contreras, 1989) and of special concern (Williams et al., 1989). Two of the four species are probably extinct, and the two Potosí species are also strongly endangered.
- **Identification key:** A revised 10-step key to the *C. eximius* complex is provided, incorporating the new species and modified from [[Miller (1976)]]. It separates species using caudal bar width, pelvic fin length, gill raker counts, ocellus presence, dorsal fin length and the teeth on the fourth ceratobranchial.
- **Future work:** Relationships within the *C. eximius* complex and the wider genus are being investigated using morphological and electrophoretic data by Anthony and Alice Echelle and the authors.
