# Relocation of 1974 Cohort Humpback Suckers from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery to West Pond

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #fish-management #marking-techniques

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents the relocation of 20 [[Humpback Sucker]] (HBS) from the 1974 cohort to [[West Pond]] in [[Imperial County]], California on July 16, 1980. The fish, originally received from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], were marked and tagged using multiple methods including freeze-branding, disc-dangler tags, and fin ray removal to evaluate marking effectiveness for individual identification. The relocation was part of ongoing fish management activities by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]].

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Sucker]] specimens were part of a larger group of 80 HBS received at the [[Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Base]] (CFWB). [[West Pond]] is approximately 50 acres in surface area with an average depth of 1 meter (maximum 2 meters) and maintains good oxygenation during warmer months, making it suitable habitat for the relocated fish.

Multiple marking and tagging techniques were employed in combination to allow evaluation of their effectiveness:
- Freeze-branding on the nuchal keel and caudal peduncle area using #8 copper wire branding irons cooled with liquid nitrogen (2 liters supplied by Cal-Poly, Pomona)
- Numbered disc-dangler tags placed below the dorsal fin
- Partial removal of specific dorsal fin rays using a paper punch

Total and fork lengths and wet weights were recorded prior to marking to enable future growth rate estimates. Three larger specimens (503, 596, and 600 mm) from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] served as controls in an ultrasonic transmitter implant procedure experiment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Transport conditions were carefully managed to minimize stress and disease:
- Fish were transported in a 400-gallon tank
- Water treated with 0.25 ppm (0.5 ounce/100 gallon H₂O) Pfizer Soluble Powder Concentrate Terramycin to deter bacterial infections
- CFWB pond temperature at loading: 28.5°C (1530 hrs)
- Tank temperature: 22.5°C
- [[West Pond]] surface temperature at release: 32.0°C (2330 hrs)
- Water temperature in tank was gradually tempered with West Pond water prior to release

The freeze-brands were not immediately visible upon immersion after branding but appeared as defined black marks by 2330 hrs the same night. Eight of 16 HBS receiving paper punch marks on dorsal fin rays had the remaining fin ray stub seared with the freeze-branding iron.

## Key Findings

- Successfully relocated 20 of 80 HBS from CFWB to [[West Pond]] on July 16, 1980
- Freeze-branding technique showed delayed visibility, with marks becoming apparent as defined black marks within approximately 8 hours
- Multiple marking methods (freeze-branding, disc-dangler tags, fin ray removal) were applied in combination to evaluate long-term individual identification effectiveness
- Transport protocol included antibiotic treatment and gradual temperature acclimation to minimize stress
- Plans indicated for relocating an additional 20-40 HBS from CFWB to [[West Pond]]
