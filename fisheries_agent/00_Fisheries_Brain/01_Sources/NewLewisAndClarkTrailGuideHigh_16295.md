# Passage of Discovery: The American Rivers Guide to the Missouri River of Lewis and Clark

#river-ecology #freshwater-ecology #conservation-history

## Executive Summary

This document is a promotional and review piece for *Passage of Discovery: The American Rivers Guide to the Missouri River of Lewis and Clark*, a trail guide by ecologist and professor [[Botkin]]. The guide follows the route of the [[Lewis and Clark Expedition]] along the [[Missouri River]] from Saint Louis to Three Forks. It combines natural history, human history and habitat restoration information. The forewords by [[Stephen Ambrose]] and [[Robert Redford]] present the river as heavily altered but still capable of partial restoration. The text contains no fish species data, survey results or quantitative ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

- **Publication:** Reviewed in the *Missouri Monitor*, July 1999.
- **Author:** Botkin, described as a scientist and professor. He previously wrote *Our Natural History: The Lessons of Lewis and Clark* (cited in the text).
- **Structure:** The guide covers natural and human history at 42 locations along the river and highlights 82 additional sites. Content includes historic sites, [[National Wildlife Refuge]] lands, riverside towns and habitat restoration projects.
- **Towns and cities featured:** Saint Louis, Saint Charles, Weston, Kansas City, Atchison, Omaha, Sioux City, Vermillion and Fort Benton, among others.
- **Forewords:** Stephen Ambrose (foreword) describes the work as a balance between human needs and natural resources. Robert Redford (afterword) emphasizes hope for restoration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the river's condition in broad terms:

- **Human alteration:** The river was dammed and channelized, and its [[Floodplain Forest]] and [[Prairie]] were converted to farmland and urban development.
- **Loss and remnants:** Ambrose notes the bleakness of this transformation. Redford states that some remnants of the original river cannot be fully restored, but significant portions could be brought back toward their original condition.
- **Scale:** The Missouri is described as the nation's longest river.

No species lists, fish community data, water chemistry, flow records or other measured environmental parameters are provided.

## Key Findings

- The guide documents **42 primary locations** and **82 additional sites** along the Missouri River, linking natural and human history.
- The river has been significantly altered through **damming, channelization and floodplain conversion**.
- Full restoration of the river's original condition is not possible, but **partial restoration** of significant reaches is considered achievable (Redford).
- The guide emphasizes **habitat restoration projects** and **refuge lands** as key components of river management and public understanding.
- The text frames the river as a case study in balancing **human development with natural resource needs** (Ambrose).
