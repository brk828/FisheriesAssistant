## Executive Summary
This study investigates the sex ratio of wild [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) populations across 12 streams in Pennsylvania, sampled from May through December using electrofishing, poisoning, angling, and recovery of pollution-killed specimens. Because no reliable secondary sex characteristic exists for distinguishing male and female brown trout externally, and because live-fish stripping methods are unreliable due to asynchronous ripening, researchers relied on lethal sampling with dissection and macroscopic gonad examination (supplemented by microscopy for small age-0 fish) to determine sex definitively. Analysis of 810 specimens across seven age groups (0–VI) and two stream chemistry types (hard-water and soft-water) found that the sex ratio does not deviate meaningfully from a hypothetical 1:1 (50% female) ratio, challenging the commonly reported pattern in other fish species of declining male proportions in older age classes.

## Background & Description
- **Sex determination challenge**: [[Brown Trout]] lack any dependable external or secondary sex characteristic allowing live differentiation of males from females.
- **Dissection-based sexing**: Sex was determined via dissection and macroscopic examination of gonads.
- **Microscopic verification**: Very small age-group 0 individuals required examination under a binocular microscope due to gonad immaturity/size.
- **Age determination**: Fish age was established via [[Scale Analysis]], allowing stratification of sex ratio data into age-groups 0 through VI.
- **Spawning-season caveat**: Stripping of eggs or sperm during spawning season is explicitly noted as an unreliable method for sex determination at the population level, since individuals ripen asynchronously (some immature, some spent, some green at any given sampling point).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat types sampled**: Populations were drawn from two distinct stream chemistry categories:
  - **Hard-water streams**: total alkalinity >100 p.p.m. (Spruce Creek, Spring Creek, Slab Cabin Creek).
  - **Soft-water streams**: total alkalinity <40 p.p.m. (remaining 9 streams, including Four Mile Run, Six Mile Run, Brown's Run, Allegheny River, Lewis Run, South Branch Kinzua Creek, East Branch Tionesta Creek, Crossforks Branch of Kettle Creek, Shaver Creek).
- **Geographic distribution**: Streams spanned Warren, McKean, Potter, Huntingdon, and Centre Counties, Pennsylvania.
- **Collection methods**: Multi-method sampling approach — electrofishing, chemical poisoning, angling, and opportunistic recovery of specimens killed by pollution events — used to maximize representativeness across the full population rather than relying solely on angler-caught or spawning-run fish.
- **Cross-reference**: Stream-type characteristics and broader population comparisons are detailed in a companion paper, [[McFadden and Cooper 1962 Ecological Comparison]] (An ecological comparison of six populations of brown trout).
- **Relevance to fisheries management**: Accurate sex ratio estimates inform potential spawning female counts and help detect sex-based mortality differentials, both critical to [[Brown Trout]] population management and stocking assessments.

## Key Findings
- **Overall sex ratio**: Combined across all streams and age groups, 52% of 810 specimens were female (n=810), consistent with a 1:1 sex ratio.
- **By water type**:
  - Soft-water streams: 49% female (n=511).
  - Hard-water streams: 56% female (n=299).
- **By age group** (percentage female, combined water types): Age 0 = 53% (n=215); Age I = 54% (n=327); Age II = 45% (n=167); Age III = 57% (n=68); Age IV = 32% (n=22); Age V = 57% (n=7); Age VI = 75% (n=4).
- **Statistical testing**: Of 37 samples/subsamples (≥10 fish each) tested via chi-square against a hypothetical 50% female ratio, only **2** differed significantly at the 0.05 probability level, and **none** at the 0.01 level — consistent with chance variation.
- **No age-related trend**: Unlike the commonly reported pattern in other fish species of a progressive decrease in male proportion with increasing age, no statistically significant age-related trend in sex ratio was detected in [[Brown Trout]].
- **No differential mortality**: No evidence was found for a mortality differential between sexes across age classes.
- **No stream-specific differences**: No statistically significant differences in sex ratio were detected between individual streams.
- **Conclusion**: The sex ratio of wild [[Brown Trout]] populations in both hard-water and soft-water Pennsylvania streams is essentially 50% female across age-groups 0–VI, supporting the use of a 1:1 ratio assumption in subsequent population and fisheries studies where direct sex-ratio data are unavailable.
