# Hyale gulbenkiani sp. n.: A New Amphipod from the Mediterranean

#marine-biology #crustacea #amphipoda #hyalidae

## Executive Summary

This 1961 taxonomic paper, published in February 1962 in [[Vie et Milieu]] (tome XII, fasc. 4), describes a new marine amphipod, *Hyale gulbenkiani* sp. n., by [[Amilcar Mateus]] and [[Emilia Mateus]]. The species was collected at the Plage de la Digue, [[Banyuls-sur-Mer]], France, during the authors' stays at the [[Laboratoire Arago]]. Specimens were gathered in 1957 and 1958 from wet sand and under stones. The authors distinguish the species from the two French congeners [[Hyale Perieri]] and [[Hyale Grimaldii]] by morphological characters, mainly the antennae, gnathopods, telson and uropods. The holotype and paratypes are deposited at the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]], Faculdade de Ciências do Porto. The paper was printed as an offprint in Porto and carries a publication list of the institute on its front matter.

## Background & Description

The paper names the species in honour of [[Calouste Gulbenkian]], whose foundation ([[Fundação Calouste Gulbenkian]]) supported the development of culture in Portugal and funded Emilia Mateus's collaboration on the work. Additional support came from the Instituto de Alta Cultura and the French Service de la coopération technique. The authors thank Professors Petit and Vachon for laboratory hospitality at Banyuls-sur-Mer and Paris.

**Material.** The description is based on a collection of several dozen animals of both sexes.

**Morphological description (as reported):**
- **Body:** length 8 mm; greenish with some brown spots; no appreciable difference in colour or size between male and female.
- **Head:** sub-pentagonal profile; large, black, reniform eyes.
- **Mesosome and metasome:** coxal plates I–IV overlapping and taller than their segments; coxal plates V–VII smallest; segments of the metasome glabrous.
- **Urosome and telson:** urosome segments glabrous, free and rectangular; telson with separate lamellar lobes that slightly exceed the peduncle of uropods III, unarmed.
- **Antennae I:** shorter than one-third of body length; peduncle one-third the length of the flagellum; flagellum with 8–13 articles in males and 8–10 in females; no accessory flagellum or calceoli.
- **Antennae II:** more robust than antennae I; flagellum about one and a half times the peduncle, with 9–13 articles bearing setae on distal margins.
- **Mouthparts:** mandibles without palp, with a 6-toothed cutting edge; molar process robust. Maxillipeds have a four-segmented palp and three strong, short spines on the inner lobe.
- **Gnathopods I (male):** carpus with a well-developed lobe bearing long setae; propod subquadrangular with a transverse palmar margin defined by two spines. Females are nearly identical to males.
- **Gnathopods II (male adult):** propod ovoid to obpiriform, with a very oblique palmar margin defined by two robust spines; long, curved dactyl. Juvenile males have an intercalary carpal lobe; females resemble gnathopods I.
- **Pereiopods and uropods:** pereiopods III–IV subequal; V smallest; V–VII strongly spinose. Uropods III are small, with a terminal group of spines and no inner branch.

**Taxonomic comparison.** The species is closest to *H. perieri* and *H. grimaldii*, considering only species recorded from France. A comparison table (summarised here) contrasts the body length, eyes, mouthparts, antennae, gnathopods and telson:

| Character | *H. gulbenkiani* | *H. perieri* | *H. grimaldii* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 8 mm | 13 mm | 6 mm |
| Antennae II | Less robust | Robust | Less robust |
| Gnathopods II (male) palmar margin | Very oblique | Oblique | Not very oblique |

The authors conclude that these differences justify a new species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors report that *H. gulbenkiani* is abundant at suitable sites: submerged locations that are not far from the water surface. At the Plage de la Digue it is very abundant in wet sand that remains beneath stranded algae. Individuals swim actively and jump when placed on a solid surface. The authors searched other localities and beaches and did not find the species there, so its known distribution rests on a single beach. No water chemistry, depth or temperature measurements are reported.

The paper is an amphipod systematics paper, so the broader community is not described. The publication list appended to the document (about 80 numbered titles) covers mainly arachnids, isopods, cave fauna and Lepidoptera, with a few marine and fisheries items (for example, the Atlantic sardine, *Sardina pilchardus*, and parasitic copepods of hake). These are not analysed in this paper.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Hyale gulbenkiani* sp. n., from wet sand and beneath stones at the Plage de la Digue, Banyuls-sur-Mer, France, collected in 1957 and 1958.
- **Diagnostic characters:** antennae I shorter than one-third of body length, with 8–13 flagellar articles in males; gnathopods I with a transverse palmar margin; gnathopods II with a voluminous obpiriform propod and a very oblique palmar margin in males; telson lobes exceeding the uropod III peduncle.
- **Size and sexual dimorphism:** 8 mm total length; males and females are not visibly different in colour or size, and female gnathopods are nearly identical to male gnathopods I.
- **Relationships:** closest to *H. perieri* (13 mm) and *H. grimaldii* (6 mm) among French species, differing in antennal robustness, gnathopod form, maxilliped setation and uropod proportions.
- **Distribution:** known from a single beach (Plage de la Digue); the species was not found at other sites searched.
- **Type material:** holotype and paratypes deposited at the Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre, Porto.
- **Earlier record:** the authors had previously reported a species new to France, *Orchestia ghigii*, from Banyuls-sur-Mer (A. et E. Mateus, 1958, 1959).
- **Publication context:** the work appeared in [[Vie et Milieu]] and is accompanied by an institute publication list of roughly 80 titles, largely terrestrial and cave biology.
