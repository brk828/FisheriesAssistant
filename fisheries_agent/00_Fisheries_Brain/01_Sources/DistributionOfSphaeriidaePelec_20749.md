# Distribution of Sphaeriidae (Pelecypoda) in Michigan, U.S.A.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sphaeriidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 malacological study by William H. Heard, based on nearly 5000 lots in the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] collections, reviews the distribution of freshwater sphaeriid clams (fingernail and pill clams) in Michigan. The author recognises 32 species, reduced from 73 species and 36 varieties listed by [[Winslow]] (1926), and follows the nomenclature of [[Herrington]] (1962). About half of the sphaeriids are broadly distributed across the state, while the rest have geomorphologically or ecologically restricted ranges. The distribution is explained mainly by Pleistocene colonisation from the [[Mississippi River]] basin via post-glacial drainage confluences, later fragmented by the formation of the present [[Great Lakes]] and by the [[Glacial Grand River]].

## Background & Description

Sphaeriids are small freshwater bivalves in two subfamilies. The subfamily Pisidiinae includes the genus *Pisidium* (pill clams), divided into subgenera such as *Neopisidium*, *Eupisidium* and *Pisidium s.s.*, distinguished by siphon development, gill structure and nephridia. The subfamily Sphaeriinae includes the genus *Sphaerium* (fingernail clams), with distinct anal and branchial siphons and a nearly equipartite shell. Taxonomy is described as redundant and in need of revision; the classification used follows that currently accepted by most malacologists.

Several Michigan taxa are highlighted as morphologically distinctive:
- *Pisidium idahoense* is large (over 5 mm) and typical of cold, deep waters.
- *Pisidium dubium* is also over 5 mm but has coarse striae absent from the beaks and more terminal beaks.
- *Sphaerium sulcatum* is one of the largest sphaeriids, sometimes reaching one inch (about 25 mm).
- *Pisidium cruciatum* is minute, usually less than 2.0 mm.
- *Sphaerium rhomboideum* has a distinctive rhomboid shape.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Michigan's streams drain six principal watersheds: Lake Superior and Lake Michigan (Upper Peninsula); Lake Michigan, Lake Huron, the St. Clair River, Lake St. Clair, the Detroit River and Lake Erie (Lower Peninsula). Major streams listed in Table 1 include the Ontonagon, Menominee, Manistee, Muskegon, Grand, Kalamazoo, Saginaw, Au Sable and Huron rivers, among others.

Habitat preferences vary by species. Some taxa are lake dwellers (e.g., *Pisidium lilljeborgi*, *Pisidium nitidum*), some are stream-dwelling (e.g., *Sphaerium transversum*, *Pisidium compressum* stream forms), and others inhabit temporary woods pools (*Sphaerium occidentale*). Deep, cold-water taxa such as *Pisidium conventus*, *P. idahoense* and *Sphaerium nitidum* are largely confined to the Great Lakes and their deep inland relict lakes, including [[Isle Royale]] in [[Lake Superior]].

Dispersal occurred mainly through active upstream migration during flood and low-water phases, with passive transport by crayfishes, frogs, salamanders, fishes, aquatic insects and aquatic birds. Endozoic dispersal of juveniles through fish intestines was also proposed (Odhner, 1951).

## Key Findings

**Species status and distribution categories**
- Of the 31 species and forms, about half are of general distribution. Broadly distributed taxa include *Pisidium adamsi*, *P. casertanum*, *P. compressum*, *P. dubium*, *P. ferrugineum*, *P. milium*, *P. subtruncatum*, *P. variabile*, *Sphaerium lacustre s.s.*, *S. occidentale*, *S. partumeium*, *S. rhomboideum*, *S. securis*, *S. striatinum* and *S. sulcatum*.
- Restricted ranges fall into five categories (a) to (e), covering northern, southern, Lake Michigan-only, and Great Lakes-endemic patterns.

**Introduced and Great Lakes species**
- *Pisidium henslowanum*, *P. amnicum* and *Sphaerium corneum* are considered introduced from Europe and restricted to the Great Lakes and their downstream drainage. *P. henslowanum* was found in Saginaw Bay, Lake Huron (Heard, 1961; Hooper dredging records).

**Barriers and dispersal routes**
- Three Pleistocene routes from the Mississippi basin are identified: the [[Illinois-Des Plaines Channel]] (glacial Lake Chicago), the [[Maumee River]] (glacial Lake Maumee), and the [[Fox River Valley]] in eastern Wisconsin, which gave entry to the Upper Peninsula.
- The glacial Grand River acted as a barrier. *Pisidium cruciatum*, *P. punctiferum* and *Sphaerium transversum* did not spread north of the Grand-Saginaw Valley, and *P. insigne* is absent from the southern Lower Peninsula.
- The rupture of the glacial Grand River into the westward-flowing Grand River and the eastward-flowing Saginaw River occurred before *P. cruciatum* and *P. punctiferum* could enter the Saginaw drainage from the west. This supports the conclusion that a single drainage rupture can create an effective dispersal barrier.
- The Fox River confluence break isolated *P. fallax* in the Lake Michigan watershed of the Upper Peninsula and prevented *P. cruciatum* from reaching Lake Michigan drainage.

**Original versus masked distribution**
- Original distribution patterns remain clearly discernible for *P. cruciatum*, *P. punctiferum* and *S. transversum*. They are partly masked for *P. fallax*, *P. insigne*, *P. obtusale*, *P. walkeri* and *S. fabale*.

**Isle Royale fauna**
- Isle Royale holds an assemblage of 13 sphaeriid taxa, larger than the Lake Superior fauna itself, presumed to derive from western Ontario via a northward route through the Mississippi.

**Data gaps**
- The author notes the Michigan fauna cannot yet be fully correlated with surrounding territories due to fragmentary records. Possible spurious locality records (e.g., *P. aequilaterale* from Reed's Lake, Grand Rapids) are flagged as doubtful, and further collecting is expected to reveal species such as *P. lilljeborgi s.s.* in the Lake Michigan watershed of the Upper Peninsula.

**Taxonomic outlook**
- Forms with limited ranges may represent true subspecies, but insufficient data currently exist to test this. The author hopes the report will stimulate further study of Sphaeriidae distribution.
