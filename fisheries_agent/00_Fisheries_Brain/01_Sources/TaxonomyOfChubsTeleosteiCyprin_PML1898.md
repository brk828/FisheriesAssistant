# Taxonomy of Chubs (Genus Gila) in the American Southwest with Comments on Conservation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic review addresses the long-debated classification of chubs of the genus [[Gila]] in the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico. The authors recognize three full species: [[Gila Robusta]], [[Gila Intermedia]], and [[Gila Nigra]]. They argue that G. nigra may have arisen through more than one hybridization event between robusta and intermedia. Following resolution of a nomenclatural problem, G. nigra is characterized in a dichotomous key. The paper recommends listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]] for all Gila species in the [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Background & Description

Chubs of the Gila River basin vary widely in body form, from elongate, thin-bodied fish with large fins and many scales and fin rays to stubby, thick-bodied fish with short fins and reduced meristics. Historically they were classified as several nominal species, as subspecies of [[Gila Robusta]], or as a single "Gila robusta complex." For more than 20 years, two species were generally recognized: a polytypic G. robusta and a monotypic G. intermedia. Problematic populations continued to cause confusion.

The name *Gila grahami* was applied to a form now treated as a subspecies of robusta. Re-examination of syntypes showed that *grahami* is a synonym of robusta and is therefore unavailable. The earliest applicable name for the form was *Gila nigra* Cope 1875 (in [[Cope And Yarrow 1875]]), based on specimens from Ash Creek and San Carlos, Arizona. Because these chubs were officially or unofficially under consideration for listing under the Endangered Species Act, explicit nomenclature was considered especially important.

The species accounts provided in the paper are as follows:

- **[[Gila Elegans]] (Bonytail):** Formerly widespread across the Colorado River basin, occupying open, strong-flowing channels of large rivers. It is now essentially restricted to a single mainstem reservoir in the lower Colorado basin. Its body is streamlined, markedly elongate, and attenuate, with more than 90 lateral-line scales.
- **[[Gila Intermedia]] (Gila Chub):** Restricted to the Gila River basin. It favors deep pools, marshes, and cutoff floodplain pools in small to middle-sized streams, and is strongly associated with cover such as woody debris and undercut banks.
- **[[Gila Robusta]] (Roundtail Chub):** Distributed through much of the Colorado River basin, from Colorado and Wyoming to northern Sonora. It uses cover less than Gila or headwater chubs and typically frequents open areas in the deepest pools and eddies of middle-sized to larger streams.
- **[[Gila Nigra]] (Headwater Chub):** Restricted to the Gila River basin in Arizona and New Mexico. It occupies middle to headwater reaches of middle-sized streams and is associated with deep places near obstructions, large pools, or undercut banks.

The paper also notes two other species of the genus, [[Gila Seminuda]] and [[Gila Jordani]], along with [[Gila Cypha]]. Together, these form the seven recognized taxa of the Colorado basin Gila group.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three Gila River basin forms show **[[Parapatry]]**. Morphologically distinguishable populations have been documented repeatedly since the 1920s, and no two of the three were ever collected at the same locality. Intermedia or nigra typically lives upstream or in smaller tributaries, while robusta lives downstream or in the mainstem of the same creeks or rivers. A single historic coexistence was recorded from the vicinity of "Salt River at Tempe," though the authors suggest this may reflect collection from nearby sites.

The authors propose an ecological explanation for this distribution. Robusta favored large rivers, whereas intermedia and nigra favored smaller creeks. This may have promoted persistence of the forms in the increasingly arid American Southwest, where lower reaches of rivers were less reliable than upstream sections.

There is no evidence that the three forms differ morphologically in relation to habit or habitat. Morphological variation does not reflect ecophenotypy or ecotypy, and no coherent geographic intergradation was found between adjacent populations.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic conclusions**

- Three species are recognized: G. robusta, G. intermedia, and G. nigra.
- G. nigra is proposed as a species of **[[Hybrid Origin]]**. Its morphology is intermediate between robusta and intermedia, and the authors suggest it may have arisen more than once.
- Intraspecific morphological variation in nigra is no greater than in other Gila species.
- Under the [[Biological Species Concept]], the parental forms interbred but did not merge into a single entity, so the authors judge species-level recognition appropriate.

**Genetic evidence**

- Robusta showed relative [[Allozyme Variation]] homogeneity (based on 16 variable loci), with most variation found within populations.
- Intermedia and nigra had most of their variation among populations, and each population tended to be distinctive.
- No fixed allozyme differences were detected, and the pattern of variation did not differ between intermedia and nigra.
- A common [[Mitochondrial DNA]] haplotype was shared by all 54 chubs examined from the Gila basin, including all three forms.
- Genetic data could not distinguish between shared ancestral polymorphism and hybrid origin.

**Morphological evidence**

- [[Table 1]] summarizes meristic data (post-Weberian vertebrae, anal and dorsal fin rays, and related counts) for populations of each form. The key was built from these data, supplemented by [[Rinne 1976]], [[Minckley 1973]], and [[DeMarais 1986]].
- The key separates the four forms through couplets that consider body shape, caudal peduncle ratio, lateral-line scale counts, post-Weberian vertebrae, gill rakers, and fin ray counts.

**Nomenclature**

- *G. grahami* is unavailable and is a synonym of G. robusta.
- *G. nigra* Cope 1875 is the earliest applicable name for the headwater form.
- The original San Carlos townsite was inundated by a reservoir, and its name was later transferred to a community upstream.

**Conservation recommendations**

- Conservation of Gila as units is strongly favored, extended to all native species in the Colorado River basin.
- Of the seven recognized taxa, [[Gila Cypha]], [[Gila Elegans]], [[Gila Seminuda]], and [[Gila Jordani]] are already listed as endangered under the ESA (USFWS, 1996).
- Intermedia is a formal candidate for listing (USFWS, 1997), and surveys were underway to assess robusta populations in the Gila River drainage before a possible listing proposal.
- Three taxa (elegans, intermedia, and robusta) are on Mexico's list of species of special concern under the [[Mexican Listing Of Species Of Special Concern]] (SEDESOL, 1994).
- The authors argue that taxonomic recognition at the species level carries significant political weight in efforts to prevent extinction, because both countries' legislation focuses on named entities.
