# 2010 Lake Mohave March Razorback Roundup

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a field report from the 2010 Lake Mohave March Razorback Roundup, an annual long-term interagency monitoring effort run by the [[National Park Service]] (NPS) and the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW) from March 15 to March 19. Crews netted the Tequila Cove spawning zone on the Nevada shoreline with trammel nets and also collected larvae. The trip captured 114 fish from eight species. The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally endangered native catostomid, made up the largest share of interest, with 54 individuals contacted, almost all of them recaptures of previously tagged, hatchery-reared fish.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Roundup]] is a recurring spring program that monitors the [[Razorback Sucker]] population in [[Lake Mohave]]. The 2010 camp was based at Bill Gay's Cove, with capture work centred on Tequila Cove. Each capture was tied to its tagging and stocking history, so the roundup doubles as a check on the survival and recapture records of stocked fish.

The document also records the non-native and sport species caught alongside the target fish, which matters because they share the spawning area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Netting effort.** Seven to ten 300 ft × 6 ft trammel nets (1.5-inch mesh) were set from Tequila Cove south to Mesquite Cove over four nights. On three nights the nets were set at 5:30 pm and pulled at 12:30 pm. On the third night they were set at 5:30 pm, checked at 10 pm, and pulled at 8 am the next morning. Larval capture was carried out on three of the nights.

**Catch composition.** A total of 114 individuals from eight species were captured:

The counts for the minor species were partly garbled in the scan and should be checked against the original table. The percentages match the razorback and carp totals. Several of the non-native species compete with or prey on native fish, so their co-occurrence with razorbacks in the spawning zone is ecologically relevant.

**Razorback recaptures and stocking.** Of the 54 razorbacks, 53 were recaptures from the years 1996–1999, 2001, and 2004–2010. Only one fish was untagged. Seven fish that had only an older 400 PIT tag (unit partly garbled in the scan, likely 400 MHz) were given a 134 kHz PIT tag. Seventy-two percent of all fish caught had been stocked in 2009 or 2010. Forty-five fish had not been caught since their initial release.

**Handling and release.** Forty-nine razorbacks were fin-clipped for genetic analysis. All captured razorbacks were returned to Lake Mohave, either at Bill Gay's Cove or at the USBR camp. A few were held for the collection of gametes.

**Rearing origin.** Examination of past capture data showed that most fish caught in 2010 had been reared at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] or [[Achii Hanyo National Fish Hatchery]]. The authors attribute this mainly to the high number of recaptures of recently stocked fish. Other rearing sources included Lake Mohave backwaters and bubbling ponds (the scan garbles the full list).

**Larval collection.** A total of 1,250 larvae were collected from Tequila Cove over the sampling nights. A subset was retained for genetic analysis. The document also mentions an attempt above the Nevada shoreline, but the details are unclear in the scan.

## Key Findings

- **Catch:** 114 fish from eight species were captured over four nights of trammel netting.
- **Razorback dominance of the catch:** 54 razorbacks were contacted, the largest single share.
- **Recapture-dominated:** 53 of 54 razorbacks were recaptures from 1996–2010 stocking and tagging records.
- **Recent stocking dominates:** 72% of razorbacks caught had been stocked in 2009 or 2010.
- **Tagging gaps:** Seven fish were retagged with 134 kHz PIT tags because their earlier tags were of a different type; one fish was untagged.
- **Recent release gap:** 45 razorbacks had not been recaptured since their first release.
- **Genetic sampling:** 49 razorbacks were fin-clipped for genetic analysis.
- **Larval capture:** 1,250 larvae were collected from Tequila Cove, with a subset kept for genetic analysis.
- **Hatchery origin:** Most recaptured fish came from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] or [[Achii Hanyo National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Management implication:** Recaptures and fin-clip genetic sampling suggest the program is tracking survival and genetic diversity of stocked razorbacks, and the high recent-stocking share reflects recent hatchery releases.
