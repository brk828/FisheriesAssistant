# Preliminary Observations on the Toxicity of Antimycin A to Fish and Other Aquatic Animals

#ichthyology #fish-toxicology #fishery-management #antimycin-A #multiple-families

## Executive Summary

This 1964 Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife circular reports preliminary laboratory and field investigations of [[Antimycin A]], an antifungal antibiotic, as a potential fish toxicant for fishery management. The study evaluated toxicity at concentrations of 0.01 to 120 p.p.b. on 24 freshwater fish species in laboratory bioassays and 25 species in outdoor pools and hatchery ponds. [[Antimycin A]] proved to be a powerful and selective fish toxicant, killing [[Carp]] and other rough fish at low concentrations in both cool and warm water. Notably, the compound degraded rapidly in water (within 24-96 hours), showed minimal toxicity to non-target organisms (plankton, aquatic plants, bottom fauna, salamanders, tadpoles, turtles), and appeared to leave no harmful residues. Susceptibility varied widely among species, with [[Longnose Gar]], [[Bowfin]], [[Black Bullhead]], and [[Yellow Bullhead]] showing high resistance. Water chemistry, particularly pH and hydroxyl ion concentration, significantly influenced toxicant efficacy and degradation rate.

## Background & Description

[[Antimycin A]] is an antifungal antibiotic first isolated from *Streptomyces* sp. bacteria by Dunshee, Leben, Keitt, and Strong (1949) at the University of Wisconsin. At least seven *Streptomyces* species produce antimycin, including *S. kitazawaensis* (Japan) and *S. blastmyceticus*. The compound consists of multiple active fractions (A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄) differing only in alkyl side chain composition. The A₂ fraction, though comprising only 20% by weight, accounts for approximately 60% of biological activity.

The crystalline material used in these studies was supplied by Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation from Kyowa Fermentation Company, Tokyo, with composition: A₁ (40%), A₂ (20%), A₃ (20%), A₄ (10%). The empirical formula for A₁ and A₂ fractions is C₂₈H₄₀N₂O₉; for A₃ and A₄ is C₂₆H₃₆N₂O₉.

**Physical-Chemical Properties:**
- Soluble in polar organic solvents (ethanol, acetone, chloroform)
- Slightly soluble in nonpolar solvents (petroleum ether, benzene)
- Relatively insoluble in water
- Susceptible to alkaline degradation via hydrolytic cleavage at lactone carbonyl sites
- Degradation accelerated by light, high alkalinity, and warm temperatures

**Mechanism of Action:**
[[Antimycin A]] is a powerful inhibitor of electron transport in oxidative phosphorylation systems, specifically blocking the cytochrome b-(Coenzyme Q)-cytochrome c pathway. This retards cellular respiration. Derse and Strong (1963) hypothesized that antimycin is absorbed through gills and interferes with fish respiration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Laboratory Test Conditions:**
Bioassays conducted in slightly alkaline, medium-hard reconstituted water at three temperatures: 12°C, 17°C, and 22°C. Twenty-four species representing nine families were tested in 1- or 5-gallon vessels with observations at 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours.

**Field Test Environments:**

*Wading Pools (1,000-gallon vinyl):*
- Two bottom types: 3 inches sand or silt loam
- Established aquatic vegetation: *Sagittaria latifolia*, *Elodea canadensis*, *Myriophyllum heterophyllum*, *Potamogeton nodosus*, *P. pectinatus*, *Spirogyra* spp., phytoplankton
- 4-8 week establishment period before fish stocking
- Natural temperature variation and light conditions

*Hatchery Ponds (Delafield, Wisconsin):*
- Pond No. 2: 0.47 acre surface area, 0.78 acre-feet volume
- Pond No. 5: 0.78 acre surface area, 1.90 acre-feet volume
- Stocking rates: 225-240 pounds fish per acre
- Applications: 10 p.p.b. using two formulations (emulsifiable concentrate vs. acetone solution)

**Water Chemistry Influence:**
A critical finding was the profound effect of water chemistry on antimycin efficacy. Alkaline shifts in wading pools (pH rising from 7.5 to 10+) due to plant photosynthesis and calcium depletion caused rapid detoxification. Free hydroxide ions accelerated molecular breakdown. Hatchery ponds with higher buffer capacity and lower reserve alkalinity maintained antimycin activity longer, achieving complete kills at 10 p.p.b.

## Key Findings

**Species Sensitivity Hierarchy (Most to Least Sensitive):**

*Highly Sensitive Group:*
- [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[White Sucker]], [[Iowa Darter]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Walleye]]
- All killed by 0.8 p.p.b. in 24 hours at 12°C
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: 0.6 p.p.b. (24h) or 0.08 p.p.b. (96h) at 12°C
- [[Brown Trout]]: 0.4 p.p.b. (24h) or 0.08 p.p.b. (96h) at 12°C
- [[Gizzard Shad]]: 0.04 p.p.b. (24h) at 22°C caused complete mortality

*Intermediate Sensitivity:*
- [[Northern Pike]], [[Stoneroller]], [[Carp]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Bigmouth Buffalo]], [[Brook Stickleback]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Bluegill]], [[Longear Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[White Crappie]]
- Killed by 0.1-1.6 p.p.b. in 24 hours at 12°C
- [[Carp]]: 2 p.p.b. (24h) or 0.6 p.p.b. (96h) at 12°C; 1 p.p.b. (24h) or 0.4 p.p.b. (96h) at 17°C
- [[Northern Pike]]: 0.8 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C, 0.2 p.p.b. at 17°C, 0.1 p.p.b. at 22°C

*Resistant Group:*
- [[Goldfish]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]]
- [[Channel Catfish]]: 20 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C
- [[Yellow Bullhead]]: 80 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C
- [[Goldfish]]: 100 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C
- [[Black Bullhead]]: 120 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C (most resistant species tested)

**Temperature Effects:**
Higher temperatures significantly increased toxicity. [[Goldfish]] toxicity increased tenfold at 17°C vs. 12°C. Catfish toxicity enhanced approximately twofold at 17°C. At 22°C, bullheads remained 10× more tolerant than goldfish.

**Non-Target Organism Effects:**

*Invertebrates:*
- Water fleas (*Daphnia magna*): killed by 100 p.p.b. (24h) at 12°C; 10 p.p.b. (24h) at 22°C
- Crayfish (*Cambarus* sp.): no mortality at 10 p.p.b. (96h)
- Damselfly nymphs (*Ischnura* sp.): survived 50 p.p.b. (48h) at 12°C; required 500-1,000 p.p.b. for mortality

*Amphibians and Reptiles:*
- Tiger salamander (*Ambystoma tigrinum*): survived 80 p.p.b. (96h) at 12°C
- Bullfrog tadpoles (*Rana catesbeiana*): survived 20 p.p.b. (24h)
- Turtles: no observed effects in field trials

*Aquatic Communities:*
- Plankton: no significant changes in quantity (0.0036 to 0.0040 cc/L in treated pools vs. controls)
- Bottom fauna: 15 taxonomic groups unaffected; midges increased 55-65% during experimental period (natural variation)
- Aquatic plants: no gross toxicity to filamentous algae, submersed or emergent vegetation

**Detoxification and Degradation:**
- Complete detoxification within 24-96 hours under field conditions
- Degradation accelerated by free hydroxide ions, light, and warm temperatures
- Derse and Strong (1963) reported 10 p.p.b. detoxified within 7 days
- No harmful residues detected in bioassays with fish following degradation
- Stock solutions in acetone remained stable up to 24 days
- Crystalline material stable at room temperature for 2 years

**Hatchery Pond Results:**
At 10 p.p.b., [[Antimycin A]] killed 18-19 species within 48 hours. [[Northern Pike]] showed distress first (4-6 hours), followed by [[Rainbow Trout]], [[White Sucker]], [[Carp]], [[Walleye]], and sunfishes. Only [[Longnose Gar]], [[Bowfin]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], and [[Brown Bullhead]] survived (70% recaptured alive after 20 days). Notably, [[Goldfish]]--laboratory-resistant--died within 24 hours in ponds, suggesting field conditions enhanced toxicity.

**Formulation Comparison:**
Emulsifiable concentrate formulation produced faster initial response (fish surfaced 4-6 hours post-application) compared to acetone solution (10 hours). By 24 hours, no significant difference in mortality between formulations.

**Behavioral Observations:**
Affected fish exhibited narcosis-like condition with little response to motion or handling. [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Perch]] surfaced within 2 hours. Larger bullheads showed distress and developed unidentified fungus-like condition before death. All fish displaying distress symptoms eventually died, suggesting irreversible action.

**Management Implications:**
The discovery that [[Carp]]--a widely undesirable exotic species difficult to control--is highly vulnerable to low antimycin concentrations represents a significant finding for fishery management. The compound's rapid degradation, minimal non-target effects, and effectiveness in cool and warm water make it a promising selective piscicide.

**Research Recommendations:**
Further studies needed on: effects on various fish life stages (egg to adult); additional species; minimum killing concentrations; performance in diverse water chemistries; appropriate formulations for standing vs. flowing waters; degradation factors; residue fate; comprehensive field trials.
