# Summary of Commercial Fisheries Catch in Oklahoma For the Years 1958-1960

#ichthyology #fisheries-biology #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae #commercial-harvest

## Executive Summary

This report documents commercial fish harvest in [[Oklahoma]] waters from 1958-1960, continuing a survey initiated by Ronald E. Elkin in 1957. The study tracked catch data from licensed commercial fishermen through monthly reports and personal interviews. Between 84 fishermen (1958) and 56 fishermen (1959-1960) harvested 384,975-526,550 pounds annually, valued at $45,432-$52,406. [[Buffalofish]], [[Carp]], and [[Flathead Catfish]] dominated catches, comprising 79.5-92.5% of total catch value. [[Grand Lake]] was the most productive water body, contributing 37.1-44.3% of annual harvests across all three years. The report identifies restrictive fishing regulations as the primary limiting factor preventing greater harvest of rough fish populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Oklahoma Fishery Research Laboratory]] conducted this survey to determine the extent of commercially harvested fish in Oklahoma waters. Data collection methods included:

- Monthly catch record reports from individual commercial fishermen
- Reports tabulated numbers, pounds, species, and average price per pound
- 1960 featured enhanced methodology with personal interviews and periodic mail contact
- All fishing operations were discussed and observed when possible
- Individual reports maintained confidential

**Licensed Fishermen:**
- 1958: 84 licensed commercial fishermen
- 1959: 56 licensed commercial fishermen  
- 1960: 56 licensed commercial fishermen
- Approximately one-half fished full-time in 1960, deriving total income from commercial fishing

**Regulatory Framework:**
Oklahoma commercial fishing laws required:
- Three-inch square mesh minimum for all gear
- Nets set at least 100 yards from any shoreline
- Gear operated at least four feet below surface
- No fishing on Saturday, Sunday, or legal holidays
- These restrictions made only [[Trammel Nets]] and [[Gillnets]] practical
- 100-yard shoreline restriction effectively eliminated commercial operations in rivers and streams (except special contracts in 1958)
- Weekend/holiday restrictions limited fishermen to four-day work weeks

**Gear Usage:**
- Part-time operators: as little as 300 linear feet of net
- Full-time operators: up to 200,000 linear feet of various mesh sizes
- Large operators did not deploy all nets simultaneously even during peak harvest

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water Bodies Fished:**
- 1958: 11 lakes and 4 rivers produced 419,802 pounds
- 1959: 11 lakes produced 526,550 pounds
- 1960: 10 lakes produced 384,975 pounds

**Major Production Waters:**
[[Lake Texoma]], [[Grand Lake]], and [[Fort Gibson Lake]] combined produced:
- 1958: 53.7% of statewide catch
- 1959: 81.4% of statewide catch
- 1960: 82.7% of statewide catch

**Grand Lake** (46,300 surface acres):
- Largest contributor: 37.1% (1958), 39.3% (1959), 44.3% (1960)
- Fished continuously for three years

**Lake Texoma** (93,080 surface acres):
- Largest lake by surface area
- Closed approximately 6 months in 1958
- Closed 4 months in 1960 due to public demand
- Closures significantly reduced annual harvest

**Fort Gibson Lake** (19,100 surface acres):
- Fished continuously three years
- Contributed 13.3% (1958), 14.7% (1959), 20.6% (1960)

**Environmental Factors:**
- Fluctuating market demands affected annual harvest
- Special closed seasons on some waters due to public resentment
- Inclement weather strongly reflected in harvest variations
- Public resentment forced closed seasons on some lakes
- Comparisons between waters difficult due to variable closure periods

**Rough Fish Populations:**
The report notes tremendous populations of rough fish exist in many Oklahoma waters, providing potential for expanded commercial fishery, particularly as new federal reservoirs are completed.

## Key Findings

**Annual Harvest and Value:**

*1958:*
- Total harvest: 419,802 pounds
- Estimated value: $45,432.00
- 84 licensed fishermen

*1959:*
- Total harvest: 526,550 pounds
- Estimated value: $52,406.00
- 56 licensed fishermen

*1960:*
- Total harvest: 384,975 pounds
- Estimated value: $45,500.00
- 56 licensed fishermen

**Species Composition and Value:**

*[[Buffalofish]] (Ictiobus spp.):*
- 1958: 156,918 lbs (37.4% harvest), $23,538 (51.7% value)
- 1959: 153,867 lbs (29.2% harvest), $23,080 (44.0% value)
- 1960: 111,599 lbs (29.0% harvest), $16,740 (36.8% value)

*[[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio):*
- 1958: 119,522 lbs (28.4% harvest), $5,976 (13.2% value)
- 1959: 197,219 lbs (37.5% harvest), $9,861 (18.8% value)
- 1960: 109,308 lbs (28.4% harvest), $5,465 (12.0% value)

*[[Flathead Catfish]] (Pylodictis olivaris):*
- 1958: 50,174 lbs (12.0% harvest), $12,543 (27.6% value)
- 1959: 58,155 lbs (11.0% harvest), $14,539 (27.7% value)
- 1960: 55,943 lbs (14.5% harvest), $13,986 (30.7% value)

*[[Drum]] (Aplodinotus grunniens):*
- 1958: 12,141 lbs (2.8% harvest), $1,821 (4.0% value)
- 1959: 13,831 lbs (2.6% harvest), $2,075 (4.0% value)
- 1960: 18,385 lbs (4.8% harvest), $2,758 (6.1% value)

*[[Paddlefish]] (Polyodon spathula):*
- 1958: 3,233 lbs (0.8% harvest), $647 (1.4% value)
- 1959: 3,646 lbs (0.7% harvest), $729 (1.4% value)
- 1960: 6,068 lbs (1.6% harvest), $1,213 (2.7% value)

*[[White Bass]] (Morone chrysops):*
- 1958: 4,467 lbs (1.1% harvest), $893 (2.0% value)
- 1959: 10,611 lbs (2.0% harvest), $2,122 (4.1% value)
- 1960: 26,691 lbs (6.9% harvest), $5,338 (11.7% value)

*[[River Carpsucker]] (Carpiodes carpio):*
- 1958: 11,861 lbs (2.8% harvest), no market value
- 1959: 7,883 lbs (1.5% harvest), no market value
- 1960: 4,979 lbs (1.3% harvest), no market value

*[[Gar]] (Lepisosteus spp.):*
- 1958: 61,393 lbs (14.6% harvest), no market value
- 1959: 81,338 lbs (15.5% harvest), no market value
- 1960: 52,001 lbs (13.5% harvest), no market value

*[[Blue Sucker]] (Cycleptus elongatus):*
- 1958: 93 lbs (trace harvest), $14 (trace value)

**Key Species Contribution:**
Buffalofish, Flathead Catfish, and Carp contributed:
- 1958: 92.5% of total catch value
- 1959: 90.5% of total catch value
- 1960: 79.5% of total catch value

White Bass and Paddlefish accounted for remaining market values. Carpsucker and Gar added considerable weight but had no market value.

**Management Implications:**

The report concludes that:
- Tremendous rough fish populations provide potential for important commercial fishery
- Large federal reservoirs already support sizeable fishery
- Resource will increase as additional reservoirs are completed
- Current harvests substantial but potential for much greater harvests exists
- **Most significant limiting factors are restrictive laws**
- More flexible laws needed to develop efficient program for rough fish harvest
- Current regulations prevent effective utilization of rough fish resource

**Data Quality:**
- Cooperation from fishermen considered "only fair" in 1957-1959
- 1960 cooperation "very good" due to intensive personal interview approach
- Enhanced 1960 methodology provided more reliable data
