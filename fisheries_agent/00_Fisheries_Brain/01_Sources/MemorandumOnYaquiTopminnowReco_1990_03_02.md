## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific/morphological study but an **administrative memorandum** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, 1990) reassigning lead recovery responsibility for the [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*). The memo consolidates management authority for three at-risk Yaqui-basin fishes—[[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], and [[Yaqui Chub]]—under a single field station, the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], reflecting the species' limited U.S. distribution occurring on or near the refuge. While no morphological or population data are presented, the memo is a key administrative record for the species' conservation history and is relevant to the broader [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] efforts in the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat/Range**: The known historical range of the [[Yaqui Topminnow]] in the United States is limited and occurs on or near the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (Arizona borderlands, part of the Yaqui River drainage system).
- **Co-occurring imperiled taxa**: The memo implies sympatric or nearby occurrence of [[Yaqui Catfish]] and [[Yaqui Chub]] within the same refuge system, indicating a shared desert-aquatic ecosystem under conservation management.
- **Conservation context**: The species inhabits a fragile desert spring/stream ecosystem characteristic of the [[Desert Fishes]] fauna of the Southwestern U.S. and northern Mexico, an ecosystem type widely recognized for endemism and vulnerability to habitat loss.

## Key Findings
- Lead responsibility for [[Yaqui Topminnow]] recovery was formally transferred from the **Phoenix, Arizona, Ecological Services Office** to the **San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge**, effective upon receipt of the memo (March 2, 1990).
- This transfer consolidates recovery lead responsibility for three Yaqui-basin fish species—[[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], and [[Yaqui Chub]]—at a single field station, streamlining conservation management.
- The Phoenix Ecological Services Office was redesignated as the **primary support field station** for Yaqui topminnow restoration efforts, retaining a secondary technical role.
- Scientific recovery recommendations for the species remain the responsibility of the **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]**, led by W.L. Minckley, decoupling policy/administrative authority from technical/scientific guidance.
- The decision was justified as being **consistent with the founding purposes** of the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], underscoring the refuge's role as a designated conservation unit for endemic Yaqui-basin aquatic fauna.
