## Executive Summary

This March 1988 field memo from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) reports on targeted electrofishing and trammel-net sampling of the "middle lake" at the ASURF facility to assess the reproductive status of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) previously stocked as hatchery larvae. The core management question was whether these ~2.5-year-old fish had reached reproductive maturity, which would inform a decision on whether to stock additional larvae (a move that would confound future ability to distinguish hatchery-origin from naturally produced fish). Sampling confirmed that both sexes of razorback sucker were in active spawning condition — including running-ripe males, egg-expressing females, and a female with an eroded anal fin (a physical indicator of active spawning activity) — leading to the decision to **withhold additional stocking**. The sampling also incidentally documented [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) parasitized by *Lernaea cyprinacea*, and a new lake record for [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*).

## Background & Description

- **Study system**: The ASURF lakes are artificial water bodies managed cooperatively among Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD), Arizona State University (ASU), and other partners as a refuge/research population for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Stocking history**: Approximately 500 hatchery-reared razorback sucker larvae/juveniles were stocked into the middle ASURF lake on 3 November 1985, sourced through a hatchery in cooperation with AZGFD.
- **Rationale for sampling**: By March 1988, the stocked fish would have been roughly 2.5 years (~29 months) old — potentially old enough to have reached reproductive maturity. Determining reproductive status was essential before deciding whether to add more larvae, since mixing cohorts would eliminate the ability to differentiate wild-spawned from hatchery-stocked individuals in future monitoring.
- **Target species**:
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — family Catostomidae, a federally listed endangered Colorado River Basin native fish.
  - [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) — family Cyprinidae, also a Colorado River Basin native, incidentally netted.
  - [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) — incidentally captured; noted as a **new species record** for the ASURF lake system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat features**: Razorback suckers were captured near stands of [[Cattail]] (*Typha domingensis*) along the western shoreline, suggesting association with emergent aquatic vegetation, potentially for spawning substrate or cover.
- **Co-occurring fish community**: The lake supports a mixed native/non-native assemblage including [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Green Sunfish]] — all non-native sportfish/baitfish species incidentally caught alongside the target natives.
- **Parasitism**: All five captured bonytail were infected with *Lernaea cyprinacea* (the anchor worm, a parasitic copepod), though fish otherwise appeared healthy and in good body condition. Razorback suckers, by contrast, showed no external signs of parasitism, injury, or disease.
- **Comparative spawning ecology**: The observed sex ratio of ripe razorback sucker (2.50:1, male:female) was noted as similar to spawning ratios documented in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada — a key reference population for this species' reproductive biology.
- **Larval fish survey**: A nocturnal spotlight survey (18 March, 2.0 hrs) near the productive trammel-net site failed to detect any larval fish, indicating that active spawning had not yet produced free-swimming larvae, or larvae were not yet present in detectable numbers/locations. Repeat surveys were planned at ~2-week intervals through the end of April.

## Key Findings

**Electrofishing (15 March, 1104 sec / 0.31 hr real-time):**
- 2 razorback suckers captured (450 mm, 475 mm TL), both **male**, neither tuberculate nor ripe.
- Incidental catch: threadfin shad (n=1), largemouth bass (n=26), bluegill (n=6), green sunfish (n=22).

**Trammel netting (3 nets, 30–50 m, set 15 March, fished ~10 hrs overnight):**
- **34 razorback suckers** captured total:
  - 22 males: 385–475 mm TL (mean ± SD = 428 ± 19 mm)
  - 14 females: ~374–495 mm TL (mean ± SD = 455 ± 27 mm)
- **Reproductive condition**: 15 of 22 males were "running ripe"; 6 of 14 females expressed eggs with little/no abdominal pressure — all ripe fish taken from the same net.
- **Sex ratio of ripe fish**: 2.50:1 (male:female), comparable to spawning aggregations in Lake Mohave.
- One female exhibited an **eroded anal fin**, a physical sign typical of fish actively engaged in spawning behavior.
- All razorback suckers were free of injury, external parasites, and disease.
- **5 Bonytail** captured: 245–308 mm TL (mean ± SD ≈ 300 ± 20 mm); weight 198–350 g (mean ± SD = 236 ± 49 g). All were parasitized by *Lernaea cyprinacea* but otherwise healthy.
- Incidental catch: threadfin shad (n=8), **roundtail chub (n=1, new lake record)**, largemouth bass (n=3), bluegill (n=146), green sunfish (n=26).

**Management decision:**
- Because razorback suckers of both sexes showed clear evidence of reproductive capability (ripeness, egg expression, spawning-related fin erosion), the decision was made **not to stock additional hatchery-reared larvae** into the lake at that time, preserving the ability to later distinguish wild-produced from hatchery-origin fish.

**Larval survey (18 March):**
- Spotlight search conducted for 2.0 hours at multiple sites near the productive spawning net location; **no larval razorback suckers detected**.
- Follow-up surveys planned at approximately 2-week intervals through the end of April.

**Planned future work:**
- More extensive sampling using electrofishing, trammel nets, and seines planned for April or early May 1988, with further data analysis and distribution of results to follow.
