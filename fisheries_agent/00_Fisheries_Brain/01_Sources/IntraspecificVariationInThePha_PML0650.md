# Intraspecific Variation in the Pharyngeal Tooth Formulae of Minnesota Cyprinid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines how much [[Intraspecific Variation]] occurs in the [[Pharyngeal Teeth]] of 42 cyprinid species from Minnesota waters. Variation differs widely among species. The percentage of arches bearing variant [[Half-Formula|half-formulae]] ranges from 0% to 49.2%. The 0,4-4,0 formula is nearly invariable, while the 2,4-4,2 and 1,4-4,1 conditions are the most variable. The authors attribute part of this variation to evolutionary trends that reduce [[Minor Row]] teeth or add [[Major Row]] teeth, and part to faulty [[Tooth Replacement]]. Because variation is common, they recommend that pharyngeal arch and tooth characters be included in species descriptions and keys.

## Background & Description

Cyprinid [[Pharyngeal Teeth]] are used as taxonomic characters, which requires knowing how much variation occurs within species. Earlier work reported uneven results. [[Berinkey]] found many abnormal formulae in small populations of *Phoxinus phoxinus* and proposed that this variation might precede speciation. Shutov found 69 of 500 *Abramis brama* from Lake Seliger with variant formulae. Penczak reported that 83% of 164 *Leuciscus leuciscus* had the usual 2,5-5,2 arrangement. Little work had been done on New World cyprinids. Evans and Deubler found the usual 2,4-4,2 formula in all 150 *Clinostomus elongatus* examined, but 11 variants among 150 *Semotilus atromaculatus*. Schwartz and Dutcher found only one deviant among 504 *Notropis cerasinus*.

**Terminology and tooth-row structure**
- The large fifth ceratobranchials are called pharyngeal arches or pharyngeal bones.
- New World cyprinids usually have one or two tooth rows per arch. The introduced carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) has three.
- The major row usually has four or five teeth. The minor row, if present, has one or two teeth.
- Teeth are numbered from the most anterior-ventral tooth, which is tooth number one.
- Variation is expressed as half-formulae for the left and right arches, because variation on the two sides proved independent.

**Methods**
- Specimens were collected from Minnesota waters. Some *Carassius auratus* were raised in breeding ponds in Oklahoma and donated by Dr. Jack Young. Rare species were borrowed from the James Ford Bell Museum of Natural History.
- The goal was at least 50 individuals per collection, and this was usually exceeded.
- Arches were removed fresh where possible, cleaned in hot water or after boiling, and counted under a dissecting microscope. Missing teeth, identified by pits, and broken teeth were included in counts. Replacement crowns embedded in the mucosa were excluded.
- About 4,000 pairs of arches were processed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper relates tooth formulae to feeding function and to the biogeography of cyprinids. Relevant points include:

- **Feeding function:** Major-row teeth lie in the best position for mastication. Minor-row teeth sit lateral to the main occlusal area in most New World minnows and are less involved in chewing. The authors suggest that minor-row teeth are not functionally essential and are therefore not maintained by selection in some species, whereas major-row teeth are functional.
- **Biogeography:** Chu (1935) and Vladykov (1934) found that three-rowed genera are more common in Africa, India and eastern Asia, while one- and two-rowed genera predominate in Europe and North America. Several authors consider two or three rows to be the primitive condition, with later reduction in some lineages.
- **Phylogeny:** *Notropis* and *Hybopsis* are polyphyletic, so a single ancestral tooth formula cannot be assumed. Earlier New World stocks presumably came across the Bering Sea connection from East Asia with one or two rows of teeth.
- **Tooth-bud mechanism:** Tooth buds form from the dental lamina, and selection may act on bud anlagen early in development, possibly through mesectoderm of neural crest origin.

## Key Findings

**Overall variation**
- The percentage of arches with variant half-formulae ranged from 0% to 49.2%.
- Tooth replacement was observed in 14% to 82% of arches, with a mean of 37% across the 42 species.
- Variation was uneven: some formulae were stable, while others were plastic.

**0,4-4,0 formula (17 species, most invariable)**
- 13 of the 17 species had no variants.
- *Hybognathus hankinsoni* showed 0.8% (2 of 247 arches), *Notropis anogenus* 0.9% (1 of 115), and *N. stramineus* 0.6% (1 of 147). In all three, the deviation was a single added tooth on the left major row, giving 0,5-4,0.
- *Notropis lutrensis* was the most variable 0,4-4,0 species, at 7.4% (16 of 215). Fifteen of the 16 deviants had a single minor-row tooth, giving 1,4 or 4,1. The authors suggest that minor-row loss is nearly complete in Minnesota populations, and that the species may be a complex of several species.

**0,5-5,0 formula (four species)**
- *Opsopoeodus emiliae*: 94 arches, no deviants.
- *Notemigonus crysoleucas*: 1.9% (3 of 155).
- *Phoxinus erythrogaster*: 2.0% (5 variants), mostly 0,4-5,0.
- *Phoxinus eos*: 8.0% (11 of 137), mostly 0,5-4,0.
- The authors interpret these variants as evidence that an extra, smaller major-row tooth is being added in *P. erythrogaster* and *P. eos*. Supporting evidence included the smaller, less specialized tooth crown, a shortened anterior edentulous process, and most variants having only four major-row teeth.

**2,4-4,2 formula (four species, with variation in some)**
- Four species had the 2,5-4,2 arrangement with low variation: *Phoxinus neogaeus* 1.4% (1 of 71), *Semotilus atromaculatus* 3.9% (5 of 130), *S. margarita* 5.2% (7 of 134), and *Clinostomus elongatus* 11.2% (19 of 170).
- In *C. elongatus*, all variants were of one type, 2,4-4,2, suggesting the 2,5-4,2 condition had not yet been fixed in all populations.
- The authors conclude that the extra tooth in *Semotilus* and *Phoxinus* is largely established in the left major row.

**1,4-4,1 formula (six species)**
- *Notropis spilopterus* showed marked variation, with a sample of 13 arches yielding seven different deviant half-formulae and no discernible pattern.

**2,4-4,2 formula (ten species)**
- Six species were relatively invariant, including *Rhinichthys cataractae*, *R. atratulus*, *Couesius plumbeus*, *Notropis atherinoides*, *N. cornutus* and *N. umbratilis*. Variation in the *Notropis* species ranged from 1.4% to 3.5%.
- Four species showed greater variation:
  - *Notropis rubellus*: 10 of 174 (5.7%)
  - *N. blennius*: 6.8% (10 of 147)
  - a fourth *Notropis* species (name garbled in the scan): 9.6% (15 of 156)
  - *N. hudsonius*: 49.2% (818 of 1,663)
- In *N. hudsonius*, 775 of the 818 deviants were 1,4 or 4,1, and 19 different deviant half-formulae were recorded. The data came from 13 collections, 12 in Minnesota and one in South Dakota, so sampling bias does not explain the result. The authors interpret this as the species evolving toward the 1,4-4,1 condition, with almost half of specimens already there.

**Evolutionary and developmental conclusions**
- Trends in New World cyprinids are toward fewer minor-row teeth (from two to one or zero) and toward keeping or adding major-row teeth. The authors note these trends are not universal.
- Left arches showed more variants than right arches. When an extra major-row tooth appeared, it was always on the left arch.
- Additions were more common than subtractions. The authors attribute many small deviations to faulty tooth replacement, in which a tooth destined for replacement was not shed.
- The authors recommend including pharyngeal arch morphology and tooth shape in species descriptions and keys, because tooth formulae alone are not reliable taxonomic characters in some species.
