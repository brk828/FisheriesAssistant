# Familias de Peces Marinos Costarricenses y de Aguas Contiguas: A Key to Marine Fish Families of Costa Rica

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-families

## Executive Summary

This document is a Spanish-language identification manual by William A. Bussing, published by the Universidad de Costa Rica as Serie Ciencias Naturales No. 6 (1969). It is a revised edition of a mimeographed key first produced in 1965 for the course Ictiologia B-416. It provides a dichotomous key to the marine fish families expected along both Costa Rican coasts, from the littoral zone to about 100 fathoms (brazadas), and includes an illustrated glossary of ichthyological terms. The key is based on [[Meek and Hildebrand Key]] (1923), updated for taxonomic changes from [[Greenwood et al. Classification]] (1966), and the author states that many of its couplets are original. The document covers fish only; it does not address species-level data, environmental measurements, or ecological observations.

## Background & Description

- **Author and context:** William A. Bussing, Departamento de Biologia, Facultad de Ciencias y Letras, Universidad de Costa Rica, Ciudad Universitaria "Rodrigo Facio."
- **Revision:** The main change from the 1965 edition is the addition of 14 families and the updating of nomenclature for others.
- **Scope:** Families known or expected from both Costa Rican coasts between the littoral zone and 100 brazadas. Oceanic families are included when they often occur in coastal waters. Bathypelagic families and families generally living deeper than 100 brazadas are excluded.
- **Geographic applicability:** The key can also be used for fish of Central American coasts in general, not only Costa Rica.
- **Terminology:** Simplified where possible, though many technical terms remain. A glossary of 27 terms is included, and illustrations (Figs. 1–4 and Lámina I) were drawn from various sources by Carlos Mata Rojas.

The main document structure is:

1. **List of families (Una lista de las Familias):** About 120 families grouped under roughly 40 orders, with common Spanish names given for many (for example, "Tiburones," "Rayas," "Macabí," "Robalos," "Pargos," "Barracudas," "Caballitos").
2. **Clave de Familias (Key to Families):** Numbered couplets (1A/1B through 103A/103B and beyond) that lead to a family, using characters such as gill-opening count, body shape, fin arrangement, nictitating membrane, barbels, dentition, and lateral line.
3. **Glossary (Glosario):** Definitions of terms such as adipose fin, cirri, compressed and depressed bodies, cycloid and ctenoid scales, interorbital region, isthmus, lateral line, nictitating membrane, preorbital, soft ray, and vomer.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document provides no field data, water chemistry, habitat measurements, sampling methods, or population information. Its ecological relevance is limited to:

- **Depth range:** Coverage extends from the littoral zone to about 100 brazadas, with deeper-water families deliberately excluded.
- **Habitat inclusion:** Oceanic families are kept in the key because they frequently enter coastal waters.
- **Geographic range:** Both the Pacific and Caribbean coasts of Costa Rica, and by extension Central American coasts.

## Key Findings

- **Coverage:** The key covers the marine fish families expected in Costa Rican waters from the shore to about 100 brazadas, including oceanic families that also occur near the coast.
- **Structure of the key:** It starts by separating fish with five external gill openings (Chondrichthyes, sharks and rays) from those with a single gill opening (bony fishes), then proceeds through successive couplets to individual families.
- **Shark and ray entry points:** Early couplets separate families such as [[Sphyrnidae]] (hammerhead sharks, identified by a laterally expanded head), [[Triakidae]] and [[Carcharhinidae]] (separated by the nictitating membrane), [[Alopiidae]] and [[Lamnidae]] (separated by caudal fin length), and [[Pristidae]] (sawfishes, with a saw-like snout).
- **Ray key:** Rays are separated by disc shape, tail presence, and spine or sting features, leading to families such as [[Dasyatidae]], [[Gymnuridae]], [[Myliobatidae]], and [[Mobulidae]].
- **Bony fish entry points:** The key separates families by body form and fin structure. Examples include the eel-like families ([[Muraenidae]], [[Ophichthidae]], [[Congridae]]), the flatfish families ([[Bothidae]], [[Soleidae]], [[Cynoglossidae]]), and the seahorse family [[Syngnathidae]].
- **Nomenclature:** Family names follow the 1966 revision by Greenwood et al. Many Spanish common names are given alongside, which makes the key useful for local fishers and students.
- **Stated limitations:** The author notes that some technical vocabulary could not be removed, that the key is intended primarily for a course, and that responsibility for original sections rests with the author.
- **Geographic scope:** Although written for Costa Rica, the key is presented as applicable to the wider Central American coastline.
