# Adult Bonytail Request for Stomach Pumping Studies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the principal investigator of the [[Grand Canyon Humpback Chub Studies]] (Rich Valdez, BIO/WEST, Inc.) to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] propagation coordinator requests up to 100 adult [[Bonytail]] from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The bonytail would serve as a surrogate species to test [[Nonlethal Stomach Pumping]] as a way to assess the food habits of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon. The request was prompted by the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]], which asked for an evaluation of possible damage to the pharyngeal mill and of the technique's efficiency. The detailed study design was still in development, so the final fish numbers were not yet set.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Evaluate nonlethal stomach pumping for humpback chub food habit studies, using bonytail as a surrogate.
- **Rationale for species choice:** Adult bonytail were chosen because of their similarity to humpback chub in pharyngeal tooth structure and intestinal system.
- **Origin of request:** The Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) of Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) asked BIO/WEST to assess (1) possible damage to the pharyngeal mill and (2) the efficiency of the technique.
- **Requesting organization:** BIO/WEST, Inc., Logan, Utah.
- **Recipient:** Holt Williamson, Propagation Coordinator, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Copy recipients (12 listed):** GCES program management, a Bureau of Reclamation contracting officer's representative, the Dexter hatchery manager, several [[Arizona State University]] researchers, and an [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] staff member.

The memo is a notice of anticipated need rather than a finished proposal. It does not give a budget, schedule, or final study design.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study system:** [[Grand Canyon]] and the [[Colorado River]], where humpback chub are the focal species.
- **Surrogate rationale:** Bonytail share key anatomical traits with humpback chub, which is why they were proposed in place of the endangered focal species.
- **Fish sourcing:** The investigator proposed mixing wild [[Roundtail Chub]] from the [[Dolores River]] with hatchery bonytail to reduce the number of bonytail that would need to be sacrificed.
- **Study site:** Not yet determined. Fish might be taken to Dexter or shipped to the [[Logan Experimental Fish Hatchery]].

## Key Findings

1. **Fish request:** Up to 100 adult bonytail were requested from Dexter National Fish Hatchery. The investigator did not expect to need more than 100.
2. **Sacrifice limit:** The investigator hoped that no more than 10 bonytail would be sacrificed, leaving at least 90 unharmed. This is an aim, not a fixed commitment.
3. **Mixed-source plan:** Wild roundtail chub from the Dolores River would be combined with hatchery bonytail to lower the number of bonytail needed.
4. **Study objectives:** The ACT asked for (1) an assessment of possible pharyngeal mill damage and (2) an assessment of technique efficiency.
5. **Status:** The detailed study design, final fish numbers, and study location were all still undetermined at the time of writing. The memo is an advance notice to the propagation coordinator.
6. **Logistics options:** Either staff would travel to Dexter to collect fish, or the fish would be shipped to the Logan Experimental Fish Hatchery.
