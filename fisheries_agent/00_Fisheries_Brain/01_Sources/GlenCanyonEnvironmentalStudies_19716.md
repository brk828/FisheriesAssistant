# Humpback Chub Research Coordination Under Glen Canyon Environmental Studies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-cypha

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of management correspondence from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, dated January 1992. It transmits an external review of the four endangered-fish projects on the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), organized by [[Wendell Minckley]], and sets out the proposed program changes. The review was also informed by a [[National Academy of Sciences]] review held in October 1991. The reviewers' goal was to fine-tune the programs, not to end them. Their central concerns were the need for integration among projects and the lack of a planned final synthesis. The letters propose consolidating and redirecting work, forming a coordinating committee, and holding a Principal Investigators meeting in early February 1992 before any contracts are modified.

## Background & Description

The humpback chub is an endangered fish of the Colorado River system. Its management is tied to the operation of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and to conservation actions in the mainstem and tributaries of the Colorado River through Grand Canyon. Studies covered three main areas:

- **Reproduction and spawning:** spawning sites and pre-larval ecology.
- **Early life history:** larval and juvenile recruitment, dispersion, and dispersal.
- **Adult life history:** adult demography, dispersion, and dispersal.

Four research projects were active in 1991–1992:

| Project | Lead | Main Area |
|---|---|---|
| Bio/West Studies | [[Bio/West Inc]] | Adult ecology and demography, mainstem Colorado River |
| Habitat program | [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) | Habitat in Grand Canyon tributaries, including the [[Little Colorado River]] |
| Larval/juvenile program | [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF) | Larval and juvenile demography, mainstem and Little Colorado River |
| LCR program | [[Arizona State University]] / [[Navajo Heritage Program]] (ASU/NN) | Adult demography and spawning in the Little Colorado River |

A separate monitoring program, run by AGF, provides the baseline data against which the other projects are tested.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents focus on the ecological processes that control humpback chub populations. Three themes stand out:

- **Recruitment:** Larval and juvenile survival was treated as the key question for managing an endangered species. The reviewers judged that little specific larval fish expertise existed in the Southwest.
- **Habitat and individual behavior:** Reviewers saw value in identifying individual fish, which allows comparisons of individual movement and habitat use within a site rather than only averages across sites.
- **Thermal ecology:** Experimental work on temperature and larval survival was identified as relevant to dam operations and possible management.

The Little Colorado River confluence zone with the mainstem was identified as a key area for integrated sampling. Tributaries other than the Little Colorado River were considered as possible sites for new breeding populations. The [[Yampa River]] was suggested as a comparison habitat, since data were available there.

## Key Findings

**1. Program-wide integration is required.** The reviewers stressed that the four projects must work toward a common goal of recovery. Key questions remain about whether data bases are current and interrelated, which is required for both annual and final synthesis.

**2. Responsibilities were reassigned by focus area.**
- Bio/West: mainstem adult demography. Habitat work is integrated with FWS, and food studies are performed only incidentally.
- FWS: habitat support for other projects. Its tributary work is to be consolidated into one program, and the representativeness of its study reaches must be documented before any redesign.
- AGF: larval and juvenile demography in both rivers. This includes a single consolidated project, a common analytical basis, and emphasis on recruitment, dispersion, and dispersal from known or presumed spawning sites.
- ASU/NN: adult demography and spawning in the Little Colorado River. Thermal ecology work is transferred to AGF, and the emphasis shifts toward recapturing tagged fish.

**3. Several studies are to be de-emphasized or deleted.** These include:
- Food and food availability studies (to be pursued only incidentally).
- Chemical characterization of the Little Colorado River and the confluence zone.
- Detailed studies of algal and invertebrate standing crops, except where directly linked to larvae and juveniles.
- Detailed assessment of otolith edge chemistry. Careful size-frequency analysis and otolith aging for hatching dates and cohort strength are still required.
- Thermal ecology work at ASU/NN, which moves to AGF.

**4. A larval fish expert should be retained.** AGF was asked to acquire the services of a qualified larval fish expert to advise its principal investigator.

**5. Monitoring must continue.** The monitoring program led by AGF was excluded from the redesign. All reviewers agreed it is essential. Methods are not to be changed unless data from before and after a change are shown to be reconcilable. AGF must articulate its objectives, study plan, and data management before the effort resumes. Monitoring funding is to be determined separately.

**6. Coordination structure proposed.** The letters propose:
- A biological committee of principal investigators and primary field staff, chaired by the Senior Scientist, meeting twice a year (about February, before the field season, and early September, after it). All principal investigators reacted favorably. An informal meeting was also suggested as an alternative.
- A second group of contract and other administrators handling money, permits, logistics, and politics.

**7. Next steps.** A Principal Investigators meeting was scheduled for early February 1992 at Arizona State University, with [[Wendell Minckley]] attending. Contract modifications will follow the meeting. Requests from the [[National Academy of Sciences]] review committee members for copies of the review were also noted, with the 4 December 1991 letter and the 17 January 1992 letter to be sent at a minimum.

**8. Synthesis is still undefined.** The reviewers noted there was little apparent planning for the final synthesis of information. The letters also state that the eventual products should feed into the [[Environmental Impact Statement]] process and into management of [[Glen Canyon Dam]].
