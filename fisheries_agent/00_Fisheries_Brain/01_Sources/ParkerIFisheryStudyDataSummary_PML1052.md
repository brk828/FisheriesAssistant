# Parker I Fishery Study: Data Summary for October and December 1985 Survey Trips

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #cyprinidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum (Applied Sciences Referral No. 86-2-2, dated March 20, 1986) summarizes the first two sampling trips (October 1985 and December 1985) of the [[Parker I Fishery Study]] on the highly modified [[Lower Colorado River]]. The study collected quantitative [[Electrofishing]] data, limited [[Limnology]] data, and aquatic macrophyte observations across 15 sites representing several [[Habitat Types]]. Its purpose is to inform future river modification planning for the downstream, unmodified Parker II section. Statistical analysis and temporal comparisons were deferred to later reports, and larval fish, seine, and invertebrate samples were collected but not yet analyzed.

## Background & Description

The Parker I Division is about 14 miles (approx. 22.5 km) long and has a highly modified channel created by rock training structures, some of which create backwaters. Following bankline stabilization, commercial and residential development were established on parts of the division. The study was conducted by the [[Environmental Sciences Section]] of the [[E&R Center]] (Division of Research and Laboratory Services) under contract to the [[Yuma Projects Office]].

Key definitions and methods:
- **Backwater:** an area with little or no flow, effectively cut off from the main channel except at its downstream end.
- **Electrofishing:** a direct-current pulsed shocking unit (usable up to 3000 µS/cm) mounted on an 18-foot aluminum boat with six anodes. Each 300-meter bankline transect received two passes.
- **Beach seining:** planned for shallow water but no seine sites were sampled on these trips.
- **Larval fish:** paired towed 505 µm nets, towed 5 minutes in the main channel and no-flow backwaters, with flowmeters.
- **Macrophytes:** assessed using a 1-meter quadrat along transects, scored for abundance, coverage and sociability using [[Braun-Blanquet Method]] classes.
- **Limnology:** temperature, pH, conductivity and dissolved oxygen measured with a Hydrolab 4000 at 10 sites; light extinction via a limnophotometer; Secchi depth recorded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sites were chosen to represent the main habitat types in Parker I: vegetated natural bankline, cattail/bulrush adjacent to riprap, riparian vegetation growing from old riprap, unvegetated riprap, and backwater natural bank vegetation. Sites included:
- **Main river sites** (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 13): high-velocity riprap, cobble and cattail banks (velocities commonly 1 to over 2 ft/s).
- **Backwater sites** (2, 8, 9, 11, 14, 15): classified by size as small (site 11), medium (site 15) and large (site 14), with natural structure such as rootwads, fallen limbs and cattail stands.
- **Deer Island Lake sites** (9, 10): flow-through conditions caused by a breached upstream dike, repaired in November 1985. A semipermeable dike was subsequently installed.

Water quality was broadly similar between main river and backwater habitats, but backwaters showed more variation. Macrophytes in site 2 included [[Eurasian Watermilfoil]] (about 25% cover in October) and [[Curlyleaf Pondweed]], which were absent in December. Site 8 had curlyleaf pondweed covering 80% of the upper one-fourth of the site in October.

## Key Findings

**Limnology**
- October 20–22, 1985: mean temperature was 18.9 °C in midriver sites versus 22.2 °C in backwaters; the Deer Island main channel measured 19.0 °C.
- pH averaged 8.17 in the main river and 7.94 in backwaters.
- Conductivity averaged 923.5 µS/cm in the main river and 1232.2 µS/cm in backwaters; Deer Island Lake lower end was 930 µS/cm.
- December 17–18, 1985: midriver temperature averaged 11.9 °C and backwater 12.9 °C. Dissolved oxygen averaged 11.07 mg/L (main river), 9.9 mg/L (backwaters) and 11.7 mg/L (Deer Island channel), all higher than October values.
- Conductivity decreased from October to December by about 5.2% (main river), 17.7% (backwaters) and an average of 9.4% in the Deer Island channel, per the report's percentages.
- Light extinction coefficients (October): about 0.63 to 2.62 across sites; December main river 0.6 to 0.9 at two sites, backwaters averaged 1.0300, medium backwater 1.5243 and large backwater 0.7723.

**Electrofishing and Catch Results**
- October 1985: 828 fish collected across the sites, and 615 in December (the report notes the total counts and that the October trip included all 15 sites; site 3 was not sampled in December).
- Total effort: 4,500 meters of bankline shocked (300 m per site) and roughly 2 to 27 minutes of shocking per site.
- Nongame and game fish were nearly equal in number (1.1:1 nongame to game), but by weight the ratio was 5.95:1 nongame to game.
- Nongame fish were dominated by threadfin shad (36% average) and carp (56% average). Carp totaled 434 individuals in the sampling, with 6 mirror carp.
- Bluegill comprised about 41% of game fish captured; largemouth and smallmouth bass, redear sunfish, green sunfish and black crappie were also present in lower numbers.
- Eight flathead catfish were captured in October (none in December), with the largest taken from main river cattails. Seven channel catfish averaging 1.02 kg came from natural backwater bankline in October.
- Redbelly tilapia were restricted largely to backwaters and vegetated riprap, with 90% from backwaters in October and 100% from site 2 in December.
- One armored catfish (Loricariidae, *Hypostomus* sp., 402 mm) was captured at site 2 in December and preserved as a museum specimen.

**Habitat Preferences (Tentative, Based on Two Trips)**
- The highest fish catch occurred in backwater natural-structure bankline habitat for both game and nongame species; this habitat was also described as the most diverse and abundant.
- Flathead catfish, smallmouth bass and large carp appeared to prefer nonvegetated main-river riprap.
- Bluegill and largemouth bass were absent from unvegetated riprap and cobble habitats, suggesting a preference for vegetative structure.
- The largest game-to-nongame weight ratio (1:17) was in unvegetated riprap.

**Tagging**
- 81 Floy anchor tags were placed in larger centrarchids and ictalurids. Of 55 tagged in October, 5 were recovered in December and one was reported by an angler; recovered largemouth bass had returned to backwaters where they were tagged.

**Management Notes and Conclusions**
- Results are preliminary; statistical comparisons over time and habitat area proportions (from aerial photographs) were planned for future reports.
- A new stocking boat and pulsator performed well in December.
- The semipermeable dike at Deer Island Lake provided slight flow (about 0.3 ft/s at site 10), which may improve fish abundance according to [[Kennedy]] (1974).
