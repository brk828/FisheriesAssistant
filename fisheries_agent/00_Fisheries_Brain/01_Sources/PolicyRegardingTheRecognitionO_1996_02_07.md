## Executive Summary

This 1996 joint policy notice from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] establishes a standardized framework for interpreting the term "distinct population segment" (DPS) within the definition of "species" under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The 1978 ESA amendment restricted the recognition of population-level conservation units to vertebrates, and Congress instructed the Services to exercise this authority "sparingly" — reflected by the fact that only ~30 of over 300 listed native vertebrate species carry separate DPS status. The policy formalizes a three-part test (discreteness, significance, and conservation status) for evaluating whether a vertebrate population segment warrants listing, delisting, or reclassification independent of the full species or subspecies. It explicitly acknowledges consistency with the pre-existing [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] "Evolutionarily Significant Unit" (ESU) policy for [[Pacific Salmon]] (Oncorhynchus spp.), which remains the operative sub-policy for salmonid population units in the Pacific.

## Background & Description

This document is a legal/regulatory policy notice rather than a taxonomic or morphological study; it does not describe specific anatomical, meristic, or morphometric traits of any species. However, it explicitly identifies **quantitative measures of genetic or morphological discontinuity** as permissible (though not mandatory) lines of evidence for establishing "markedly separated" population status under the discreteness criterion. Notably, the policy states that:

- Genetic distinctness is **not required** to recognize a DPS, though it may support such a finding.
- Morphological/genetic divergence is one of several possible indicators of separation from other conspecific population units, alongside physiological, ecological, and behavioral factors.
- The [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] Evolutionarily Significant Unit (ESU) policy for Pacific salmonids (56 FR 58612–58618; November 20, 1991) similarly does not mandate genetic data to identify an ESU, though such data are relevant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not a species-specific ecological account, the policy addresses population-level ecological considerations relevant to conservation biology:

- **Discreteness criteria** include separation due to physical, physiological, ecological, or behavioral factors, or delimitation by international governmental boundaries reflecting differing management/exploitation/regulatory regimes.
- **Significance criteria** explicitly recognize ecological context, including:
  - Persistence of a population in an ecological setting **unusual or unique** for the taxon.
  - Loss of the population segment producing a **significant range gap** for the taxon.
  - The segment representing the **only surviving natural occurrence** of a taxon otherwise persisting only as an introduced population outside its historic range.
  - **Marked genetic divergence** from other conspecific populations.
- The Services explicitly reject ecosystem-role importance (i.e., a population's functional importance to its local community/ecosystem) as sufficient grounds for significance, restricting significance evaluation to importance *to the taxon itself* rather than to the surrounding ecosystem — a notable philosophical distinction from broader biodiversity conservation frameworks.
- The policy acknowledges tension between conserving genetic resources and conserving natural systems/biodiversity, framing DPS recognition as serving both goals without prioritizing either.

## Key Findings

- **Legal Origin of DPS Concept**: The 1978 ESA amendment restricted "distinct population segment" recognition strictly to **vertebrate** species; plants and invertebrates were explicitly excluded, a limitation the Services affirm they cannot override despite acknowledging the inconsistency (as also noted by the National Research Council).
- **Historical Usage**: Of over 300 native vertebrate species listed under the ESA as of 1996, only ~30 carried distinct DPS status — supporting the Congressional directive that this authority be used "sparingly."
- **Three-Part DPS Test**:
  1. **Discreteness** — markedly separated from other conspecific populations (physical/physiological/ecological/behavioral factors) OR delimited by international boundaries with differing conservation/management regimes.
  2. **Significance** — evaluated via evidence such as unique ecological setting, range-gap consequences of loss, sole-surviving-natural-population status, or marked genetic divergence.
  3. **Conservation Status** — standard ESA endangered/threatened determination applied as if the DPS were a full species.
- **Salmonid-Specific Precedent**: The [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] Evolutionarily Significant Unit (ESU) policy (1991) predates and is affirmed as consistent with this general policy; an ESU requires (1) substantial reproductive isolation from other conspecific units and (2) representing an important component of the evolutionary legacy of the species — making it the primary operative framework for [[Pacific Salmon]] population segment determinations.
- **Rejection of Absolute Reproductive Isolation**: The Services explicitly reject requiring complete reproductive isolation as a prerequisite for discreteness, noting this standard would be unmet even by some recognized full species that experience low-frequency interbreeding.
- **International vs. Domestic Political Boundaries**: International boundaries may define discreteness (consistent with CITES implementation), but sub-national boundaries (e.g., U.S. state lines) are explicitly excluded as a basis for discreteness, reserved instead as a potential incentive tool for delisting/exclusion decisions.
- **Subspecies Priority**: Under FWS Listing and Recovery Priority Guidelines (48 FR 43098; 1983), subspecies are given higher listing priority than DPS when numerical priority is equal, reflecting a conservation hierarchy: **species > subspecies > DPS**.
- **Retroactive Application**: Previously listed vertebrate DPS will be reevaluated case-by-case against this new policy framework during periodic 5-year status reviews under ESA section 4(c)(2).
- **Non-Regulatory Status**: The Services classify this as a non-regulatory interpretive policy, not subject to formal APA rulemaking procedures, despite some commenter objections.
