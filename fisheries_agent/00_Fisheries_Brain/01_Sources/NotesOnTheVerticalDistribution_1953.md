# Notes on the Vertical Distribution of Organisms in the Profundal Sediments of Douglas Lake, Michigan

## Executive Summary
This 1953 study by Gerald A. Cole investigates the **vertical stratification of benthic organisms** within profundal lake sediments — a dimension of benthic ecology largely neglected in North American limnology at the time (in contrast to European work by [[Lenz 1931]] and [[Berg 1938]]). Ninety vertical core samples were collected from two profundal stations (16 m and 19.5 m depth) in the South Fish Tail Bay depression of **Douglas Lake, Michigan**, during August 1950. Cores were sectioned at fine (1–2 cm) intervals near the surface and coarser (4 cm) intervals at depth, then screened and examined microscopically. The study quantified both **microbenthos** (copepods, ostracods, nematodes) and **macrobenthos** (oligochaetes, chironomid and chaoborid larvae, sphaeriid clams), documenting how deep into the sediment column each taxon penetrated. While the paper is not fish-focused, it provides critical **benthic food-web context** relevant to profundal fish feeding ecology, since tubificids and chironomid/chaoborid larvae are key prey items for benthivorous and planktivorous fish in temperate lakes.

## Background & Description
Not directly applicable to fish; however, taxon-specific identification features and life-stages noted include:
- **Limnodrilus spp.** (Tubificidae) — dominant macrobenthic oligochaete; specific identity not resolved (cf. *L. hoffmeisteri* and *L. claparedianus* per [[Eggleton 1931]]).
- **Tendipes plumosus** (Chironomidae) — dipterous larva, second most abundant macrobenthic form (n=10).
- **Chaoborus punctipennis** (Chaoboridae) — phantom midge larva (n=8), a taxon known for diel vertical migration behavior in the water column, though here examined only in sediment residency.
- **Canthocamptus staphylinoides** and **Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi** — harpacticoid/cyclopoid copepods exhibiting both **active and encysted (resting) life stages** within the sediment; encystment allows survival deep in anoxic ooze.
- **Ostracods**: *Candona simpsoni*, *Physocypria pustulosa*, *Cypria turneri* — small crustaceans recovered via fine-mesh screening.
- **Sphaerium sp.** (Sphaeriidae) — single juvenile bivalve mollusk recorded at 3–4 cm depth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat/Depth**: Two stations sampled — 16 m (near lower thermocline limit) and 19.5 m (well within hypolimnion), both classified as **profundal zone** per Eggleton's (1931) zonation of Douglas Lake.
- **Physico-chemical conditions**: Cold, low-oxygen hypolimnetic water (6.8–8.4°C; O2 near 0–0.01 ppm; CO2 ~15–16.5 ppm; pH ~7.0; alkalinity 125–135 ppm; conductivity 225–240 µmho) — characteristic of oxygen-depleted profundal habitat that constrains which taxa can persist in bottom sediments.
- **Sediment stratigraphy**: Upper ~15 cm blackened, fine, watery ooze; transition to brown, compacted, gelatinous material below ~20 cm — mirroring findings of [[Moore 1939]].
- **Microbenthos niche partitioning**: *Canthocamptus staphylinoides* was common at 16 m but nearly absent at 19.5 m, while *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi* was more abundant at the deeper station — suggesting **depth-related niche differentiation** among microcrustaceans, likely tied to oxygen tolerance.
- **Vertical migration/penetration**: Encysted *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi* penetrated deepest among microbenthos (>16 cm), while active individuals of *Canthocamptus* reached 14–16 cm.
- **Macrobenthos vertical distribution**: 93.4% of all macrobenthic individuals occurred within the upper 14 cm of sediment; deepest record was a single *Limnodrilus* at 20–24 cm.
- **Comparative lake ecology**: Contrasts drawn with [[Crystal Lake Minnesota]], where oligochaetes (mostly naidids) were concentrated near the surface (60% in top cm) and profundal oligochaetes/tendipedids were absent except during fall overturn — highlighting **inter-lake variability in vertical benthic stratification**, likely driven by differing hypolimnetic oxygen regimes and substrate composition.
- **Trophic relevance**: Though not discussed by the author, taxa such as *Limnodrilus* and chironomid larvae constitute classic **profundal prey resources for benthivorous fish** (e.g., ciscoes, whitefish, sculpins) inhabiting deep, cold lake strata — underscoring the paper's indirect relevance to [[Profundal Fish Feeding Ecology]].

## Key Findings
- **90 total core samples** (30 collections × 3 cores) taken from profundal depths (16 m, n=24 cores; 19.5 m, n=66 cores), plus 8 additional littoral/sublittoral comparison cores.
- **Microbenthos**: 8 species identified overall; **7 species (5 abundant)** at the shallow (16 m) station vs. only **4 species (2 common)** at the deep (19.5 m) station — indicating a decline in microbenthic diversity with depth.
- *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi* cysts found down to the **16–18 cm** stratum — the deepest microbenthic penetration recorded.
- **Macrobenthos totals**: 106 individuals across ≥5 species; dominated by **Limnodrilus spp.** (tubificid oligochaetes), followed by **Tendipes plumosus** (n=10), Tanypodinae larvae (n=4), **Chaoborus punctipennis** (n=8), and single records of *Sphaerium* and an unidentified oligochaete.
- **91%+ of Limnodrilus individuals** occurred in the upper 14 cm of sediment; only 7 individuals found below this level; deepest single record at **20–24 cm**.
- Notably, only **7.4% of tubificids** were found in the very top centimeter — contrasting sharply with Crystal Lake data where **60% of oligochaetes** occurred at the surface/top cm, suggesting Douglas Lake profundal tubificids preferentially aggregate slightly below the sediment-water interface (e.g., pronounced clustering in the 2–3 cm stratum at 19.5 m).
- **Chaoborus punctipennis**: 7 of 8 larvae found at the deeper (19.5 m) station; distribution ranged from the surface stratum down to 9–10 cm — no clear evidence of diel vertical migration detected from morning/afternoon sample comparisons due to small sample size.
- **Tendipes plumosus** showed no significant distributional difference between the two stations; most individuals occurred between 2–6 cm depth, with only one larva in the topmost centimeter.
- Overall, **93.4% of macrobenthos** occurred between the surface and 14 cm, consistent with [[Berg 1938]]'s finding of ~90% of Danish lake macrobenthos within 0–15 cm, though Berg documented some populations penetrating to ~30 cm in Esrom Lake.
- Author suggests **seasonal vertical migration** of profundal benthos is plausible in Douglas Lake, based on Berg's (1938) observation of shifting population maxima (spring: 5–10 cm; summer/autumn: 0–5 cm) — a hypothesis untested in the current August-only sampling.
- Study reinforces methodological value of the **plastic-tube vertical core sampler** (previously described in [[Cole 1949]]) for fine-scale benthic stratigraphy work, while acknowledging its limitation in detecting motile-organism displacement during sampling.
