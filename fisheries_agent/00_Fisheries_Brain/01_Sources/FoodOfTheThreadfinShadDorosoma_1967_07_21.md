# Food of the Threadfin Shad, *Dorosoma petenense*, in Lake Chicot, Arkansas

## Executive Summary
This 1967 study by Robert Victor Miller examined the feeding ecology of 49 [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), ranging from 36–119 mm SL, collected from Lake Chicot, Arkansas, across eight sampling dates in 1959. The study found that the diet consisted of an almost equal split between plant material (54.1%, dominated by [[Chlorophyta]]) and animal material (45.9%, dominated by [[Protozoa]] and invertebrate eggs), spanning over 60 identified genera. Statistical analysis (rank correlation) between stomach/foregut contents and concurrent plankton samples demonstrated that the species is largely a **non-selective, opportunistic planktivore**, consuming organisms in proportion to their environmental abundance rather than exhibiting specific prey preference. This contrasts with some other threadfin shad populations (e.g., Salton Sea) that show selective feeding on fish eggs/fry.

## Background & Description
- **Size range examined:** 36–119 mm Standard Length (SL); weights ranged from 0.5–27.0 grams depending on collection date.
- **Age classes:** Only young-of-year and yearling age groups were represented in the sample; no qualitative dietary differences were found between these two age groups or across the size range studied.
- **Foregut structure:** The species possesses a specialized digestive foregut divided into two functional sections:
  - **Pharyngeal organs:** Filter/process fine food particles; lower quantities of food found here relative to the stomach.
  - **Stomach-esophagus:** Ratio of organisms here to the pharyngeal organs was typically 3:1 to 4:1 for most taxa.
- **Differential digestion/retention rates (Rotifera):** A chi-square test revealed genus-specific retention patterns in the foregut segments:
  - *Conochilus*, *Polyarthra*, *Trichocerca*: Most abundant in pharyngeal organs (implies rapid digestion upon reaching stomach).
  - *Brachionus*: Roughly equal distribution between the two segments.
  - *Keratella* and *Monostyla*: Most numerous in the stomach (implies these genera are less digestible and accumulate).
- **Feeding apparatus:** Possesses numerous gill rakers used for filter feeding; however, the presence of planktonic organisms small enough to pass through raker spacing in the gut suggests **clumping of food particles** or intermittent blockage of raker gaps during feeding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Collected via seine in a shallow (~1.5 m) embayment of [[Lake Chicot]], Arkansas; comparative plankton samples were also taken in deeper, open water (6–8 m) with no major compositional differences found between the two zones.
- **Feeding Strategy:** Classified as a generalist, pelagic planktivore that appears to feed on "any available organism of suitable size."
  - **Filter feeding (Primary):** Non-directed filtering of water column plankton (algae, rotifers, protozoans, crustaceans).
  - **Bottom feeding (Secondary):** Evidenced by occasional ingestion of *Chaoborus* and tendipedid (chironomid) larvae, alongside unusual amounts of sand and mud in the gut.
  - **Sight feeding (Occasional):** Evidenced by the presence of adult insects and Hydracarina (mites), organisms unlikely to be captured via non-directed filter feeding alone.
- **Schooling behavior:** Kendall's coefficient of concordance confirmed that individual shad within the same collection sample ate nearly identical proportions of prey items, a pattern attributed to the schooling behavior of the species.
- **Trophic Role:** Serves as a critical [[Forage Fish]] species for piscivorous predators (e.g., Largemouth Bass) due to its schooling behavior, small size, and easy capture.
- **Thermal Tolerance:** The species is noted to be intolerant of cold temperatures, with historical mortalities recorded when water temperatures dropped below approximately 45–54°F.

## Key Findings
1. **Diet Composition:** The diet was volumetrically dominated by **Chlorophyta** (green algae, 50.6% of volume) and **Protozoa** (33.6% of volume). Other significant contributors included invertebrate eggs (5.3%) and Chrysophyta (1.9%).
2. **Non-Selective Feeding:** Rank correlations between the foregut contents and plankton samples were significant (often at the 1% level) in 6 of 8 sampling events, strongly indicating that *D. petenense* in Lake Chicot are **non-selective feeders**, consuming prey largely in proportion to its natural abundance in the water column.
3. **Comparison to Other Populations:** This non-selective behavior contrasts with findings by Kimsey (1958) in the [[Salton Sea]], where threadfin shad were found to selectively target eggs and fry of the gulf croaker (*Bairdiella icistius*).
4. **Consistency with Related Species:** The non-selective foraging strategy aligns with findings for the related [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) studied by Kutkuhn (1958) and the [[Pacific Sardine]] (*Sardinops caerulea*) studied by Hand and Berner (1959), suggesting this may be a common trophic strategy among planktivorous Clupeids.
5. **Management Implications:** Because threadfin shad exhibit high dietary flexibility (adapting to whatever plankton/detritus is available), the authors suggest that introduction of this species to new water bodies for forage stocking purposes should prioritize matching the correct **thermal range** over specific food availability, provided moderate levels of plankton or bottom organisms exist.
