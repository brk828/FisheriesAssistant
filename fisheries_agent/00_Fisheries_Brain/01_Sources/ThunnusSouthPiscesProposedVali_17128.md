# Thunnus South, 1845: Proposed Validation Under the Plenary Powers (Z.N.(S.) 1652)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Scombridae #Thunninae #Thunnus #Zoological-Nomenclature

## Executive Summary

This 1964 case submitted to the [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]] (ICZN) by Bruce B. Collette and Robert H. Gibbs, Jr. requests the use of plenary powers to **validate the generic name *Thunnus* South, 1845** for the tuna genus, and to suppress the senior but little-used name *Thinnus* S.D.W., 1837. The case centers on resolving a chain of homonymy and emendation issues stemming from *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817, which is itself invalid as a junior homonym of *Thynnus* Fabricius, 1775 (a hymenopteran). Because *Thunnus* is the name in nearly universal use among fishery biologists and systematists for six commercially important tuna species, the authors argue that strict application of the [[Law of Priority]] (which would favor *Thinnus* S.D.W., 1837) would cause needless nomenclatural instability in an economically important fishery science context.

## Background & Description

The paper is a nomenclatural/taxonomic ruling and does not provide direct morphological descriptions of *Thunnus* species. However, it identifies the genus as encompassing six commercially important tuna species:

- *Thunnus alalunga* (Bonnaterre, 1788) — [[Albacore]]
- *Thunnus albacares* (Bonnaterre, 1788) — [[Yellowfin Tuna]]
- *Thunnus atlanticus* (Lesson, 1830) — [[Blackfin Tuna]]
- *Thunnus obesus* (Lowe, 1839) — [[Bigeye Tuna]]
- *Thunnus thynnus* (Linnaeus, 1758) — [[Atlantic Bluefin Tuna]] (type species)
- *Thunnus tonggol* (Bleeker, 1852) — [[Longtail Tuna]]

The type-species designation is established **by tautonymy**: *Scomber thynnus* Linnaeus, 1758 (*Systema Naturae*, ed. 10, 1: 297–298), linked to *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817 (*Règne Animal* 2: 313).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Direct ecological data are not provided in this taxonomic note. Contextually, the genus *Thunnus* comprises large pelagic [[Scombridae|scombrid]] fishes distributed across tropical, subtropical, and temperate oceanic waters worldwide, and represents some of the most economically significant fisheries species globally — a point the authors use to argue for nomenclatural stability. The genus falls within the subfamily [[Thunninae]], as formally recognized by E. C. Starks (1910).

## Key Findings

**Nomenclatural history and conflict:**
- *Thunnus* South, 1845 was proposed as a substitute/emendation for *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817.
- *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817 is invalid, being a **junior homonym** of *Thynnus* Fabricius, 1775 (an insect genus, Hymenoptera).
- Gill (1894) first pointed out South's 1845 proposal; since then, most ichthyologists have used *Thunnus* South for the type species and related tunas.
- Whitley (1955) rediscovered an even earlier emendation, **Thinnus S.D.W., 1837** (in *The Analyst*, possibly authored by "S. D. Wood" of Derbyshire), which predates *Thunnus* South by 8 years. Whitley proposed reviving *Thinnus* under priority rules.
- *Thinnus* S.D.W. saw minimal usage — only by Abe (1955); Abe himself later (1963) reverted to using *Thunnus*.

**Rationale for plenary action:**
- Because tuna taxonomy is used extensively by fishery biologists (not just systematists), the authors argue a nomenclatural switch to *Thinnus* would create widespread instability and confusion in commercially vital fisheries literature.
- The 1962 FAO World Scientific Meeting on the Biology of Tunas and Related Species (La Jolla, California) had already provisionally endorsed the Collette & Gibbs generic scheme using *Thunnus*.

**Formal requests to the ICZN:**
1. Suppress *Thinnus* S.D.W., 1837 under plenary powers for purposes of the [[Law of Priority]] (but not [[Law of Homonymy]]).
2. Place *Thunnus* South, 1845 (masculine gender), type species *Scomber thynnus* Linnaeus, 1758 (by tautonymy via *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817), on the **Official List of Generic Names in Zoology**.
3. Place the specific name *thynnus* Linnaeus, 1758 (as in *Scomber thynnus*) on the **Official List of Specific Names in Zoology**.
4. Place on the **Official Index of Rejected and Invalid Generic Names in Zoology**:
   - *Thynnus* Cuvier, 1817 (junior homonym of *Thynnus* Fabricius, 1775)
   - *Thinnus* S.D.W., 1837 (as suppressed above)
5. Place the family-group name **THUNNINAE** Starks, 1910 (type genus *Thunnus* South, 1845) on the **Official List of Family-Group Names in Zoology**.
6. Place the family-group name **THINNINAE** Whitley, 1955 (type genus *Thinnus* S.D.W., 1837) on the **Official Index of Rejected and Invalid Family-Group Names in Zoology**, as invalid due to suppression of its type genus.

**Significance:** This case exemplifies the use of ICZN [[Plenary Powers]] to preserve nomenclatural stability for a name in prevailing and long-established usage over an older, rarely-used competing name — a common conservation mechanism in zoological taxonomy.
