## Executive Summary

This 1954 paper by W. E. Ricker (Pacific Biological Station, Nanaimo, B.C.) presents a series of **nomenclatorial corrections and taxonomic revisions** within the insect order **Plecoptera (stoneflies)**, updating his earlier 1944 and 1952 works. The paper resolves synonymies among several genera — *Capnia*, *Nemoura*, *Leuctra*, *Isoperla*, and *Alloperla* — based on renewed examination of type specimens (including material at the Museum of Comparative Zoology, MCZ). It also erects two new subgenera, **Calineuria** and **Attaneuria**, within *Acroneuria*, distinguished by nymphal occipital spinule patterns and male sternal "hammer" morphology. Although focused on aquatic insects rather than fish, the paper is relevant to freshwater stream ecology, as Plecoptera nymphs are key benthic macroinvertebrates and a food source for stream-dwelling fish such as [[Rainbow Trout]] and other [[Salmonidae]].

## Background & Description

Diagnostic features discussed are primarily **nymphal and adult genitalic/sclerite morphology**, used to distinguish closely related stonefly species and subgenera:

- **Capnia labradora** (new species): distinguished from *Capnia columbiana* by the shape of the supra-anal processes — in *columbiana* the tips are more divergent and the hind border is wavy, whereas *labradora* lacks this waviness.
- **Nemoura decepta**: females previously described as *N. rossi* are now synonymized with *decepta*; distinguished from *N. delicatula* by more extensive dusky pigment along the wing cord, a character clearer in females than males.
- **Leuctra (Despaxia) augusta**: characterized by reduced abdominal sclerotization and a distinctive subgenital plate shape, matching the synonymized *L. glabra*.
- **Leuctra (Paraleuctra) occidentalis / bradleyi / sara**: separated largely by subgenital plate morphology in males and a highly variable subterminal spine on the male cercus (size and shape vary even between adjacent streams); this variability undermines recognition of *L. projecta* Frison as distinct.
- **Isoperla petersoni**: penial sclerite and body markings match the synonymized *I. fontium*; short-winged (brachypterous) individuals represent an isolated southern population rather than a distinct taxon.
- **Alloperla severa**: identified by body size (~13 mm to wing tips) and absence of dark mesothoracic/metathoracic markings; the supra-anal process shape (straight-sided vs. dumbbell-shaped) and female subgenital plate shape (rounded vs. subacute) intergrade within populations, invalidating *Alloperla thalia* and reaffirming synonymy with *A. elevata*.
- **Calineuria** (new subgenus of *Acroneuria*): elongate-rectangular hammer on the ninth sternite (vs. transversely oval); nymph has a fairly complete but midline-interrupted transverse row of occipital spinules; subanal gill tufts absent.
- **Attaneuria** (new subgenus of *Acroneuria*): complete but wavy transverse occipital spinule row in nymphs; transversely oval hammer on the ninth sternite; subanal gill tufts absent.

These characters — supra-anal process shape, subgenital plate form, cercal spine morphology, sternal hammer shape, and occipital spinule patterns — are the primary tools used throughout for species-level and subgenus-level discrimination in [[Plecoptera]] taxonomy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: All taxa discussed are **lotic freshwater** stonefly nymphs/adults inhabiting streams and rivers, ranging from Arctic/subarctic Labrador (*Capnia labradora*, *C. nearctica*) to the western North American cordillera (*Isoperla petersoni*, *Leuctra occidentalis*, *Alloperla severa*) and eastern North America (*Attaneuria ruralis*, *Leuctra sara*).
- **Geographic distribution**: Notably wide-ranging taxa include *Alloperla severa* (synonymized with *A. elevata*), common along the entire Pacific Coast of North America, extending north to the Matanuska Valley, Alaska — indicating broad coastal cordilleran distribution with an "Interior" ecotype (formerly misidentified as *A. thalia*) in Montana.
- **Population variation**: Several species show **clinal or population-level morphological variation** (e.g., cercal spine shape in *Leuctra occidentalis/bradleyi*, wing length in *Isoperla petersoni*, supra-anal process shape in *Alloperla severa*) that the author argues does not warrant separate taxonomic status — an important cautionary note for stream insect biogeography and cryptic diversity assessment.
- **Trophic role**: Though not explicitly discussed in the excerpt, Plecoptera nymphs occupy a foundational role in freshwater food webs as detritivores/grazers and predators, and serve as prey for [[Freshwater Fish]] communities — relevant to habitat notes on stream fish diet composition.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described**: *Capnia labradora* Ricker, distinguished from *C. columbiana* by supra-anal process morphology; type locality Nain, Labrador.
2. **New synonymy**: *Capnia hantzschi* Ricker = *Capnia nearctica* Banks, confirmed via re-examination of a paratype at MCZ.
3. **New synonymy**: *Nemoura rossi* Ricker = *Nemoura decepta* Frison, based on newly available mature female specimens.
4. **New synonymy**: *Leuctra glabra* Claassen = *Leuctra augusta* Banks, confirmed via cotype re-examination (correcting a prior count error — two cotypes exist, not one).
5. **Lectotype correction**: The 1952 lectotype designation for *Leuctra occidentalis* was based on a misidentified specimen; the true identity of that lectotype is *Leuctra bradleyi* Claassen, requiring reassignment of names — *L. occidentalis* (Banks) properly includes *bradleyi* Claassen and *projecta* Frison as synonyms, while the species long called "*occidentalis*" by Needham, Claassen, and subsequent authors is actually *Leuctra sara* Claassen.
6. **Species roster clarified for *Paraleuctra***: Four valid species recognized — *forcipata* Frison, *occidentalis* Banks, *purcellana* Neave, and *sara* Claassen.
7. **New synonymy**: *Isoperla fontium* Neave = *Isoperla petersoni* Needham & Christenson; nomenclatural priority issues around authorship ("Claassen" vs. "Needham") clarified per International Rules.
8. **New synonymy**: *Alloperla elevata* Frison and *Alloperla thalia* Ricker (1952) both = *Alloperla severa* (Hagen, 1861), based on type specimen re-examination and demonstrated intergradation of diagnostic characters.
9. **Two new subgenera erected** within *Acroneuria*: **Calineuria** (type species *A. californica* Banks, cordilleran) and **Attaneuria** (for *A. ruralis* Hagen, eastern North American), differentiated by nymphal occipital spinule rows and male sternal hammer shape.
10. **Broader taxonomic principle emphasized**: Intraspecific and interpopulation morphological variability (wing length, cercal spine shape, pigmentation, subgenital plate form) is common in Plecoptera and should not automatically justify new species/subspecies designations — a methodological caution applicable to freshwater invertebrate systematics generally, and by extension to [[Species Complex]] identification challenges relevant to fish taxonomy as well.
