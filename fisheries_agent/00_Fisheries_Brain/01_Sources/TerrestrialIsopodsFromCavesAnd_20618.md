# Terrestrial Isopods from Caves and Mines in Texas and Northern Mexico

#crustacea #isopoda #oniscoidea

## Executive Summary

This 1965 reprint from the *Texas Journal of Science* by [[George A. Schultz]] of the [[Duke Marine Laboratory]] identifies isopods collected from caves and mines in Texas and northern Mexico. Specimens were sent by [[James Reddell]] of the [[Texas Speleological Survey]]. The collections comprise 8 isopod species from 23 sites. Seven species are terrestrial oniscoids; the eighth, [[Cirolana Pelaezi]], is a blind cirolanid from a Mexican cave. The paper's main contribution is the first description of the male allotype of [[Venezillo Tanneri]], a key to the species, and the observation that nearly all specimens are surface fauna that were not modified for cave life.

## Background & Description

The isopods were collected by several investigators between 1963 and 1964. Most came from sites in Texas, with a smaller number from Coahuila, Nuevo León and San Luis Potosí, Mexico. Nearly all specimens belong to the suborder [[Oniscoidea]]. The exception is [[Cirolana Pelaezi]], a blind species of the suborder Flabellifera from Sótano del Arroyo, San Luis Potosí.

Only one oniscoid showed true cave-dwelling traits. It was a blind, pigmentless member of the Trichoniscoidae, described by Schultz (1964a) as the new genus [[Mexiconiscus Tlamayaensis]], from Tlamaya near Xilitla, San Luis Potosí. The other specimens, apart from some albino [[Porcellio Laevis]], had the typical colour and morphology of their species. The author notes that albinism is common outside caves in this species, so it is probably not a cave adaptation.

The paper describes the male allotype of [[Venezillo Tanneri]], first described from a single female as *Cubaris* by Mulaik and Mulaik (1942). Van Name (1936) later placed it in a group of that genus, and *Venezillo* has since been raised to full generic rank. Key morphological points include:

- Body about twice as long as wide and comparatively smooth.
- About 8 ocelli per eye.
- Strongly produced endopod on male first pleopod, with a small triangular exopod.
- Distal third of the male second endopod abruptly narrower than the proximal two-thirds.
- Greatly produced exopod apices on pleopods 2, 3 and 4.
- Grayish-brown colour, with males darker than females, both bearing small irregular white spots.

Mouthpart and appendage figures (maxillipeds, maxillae, mandibles, hypopharynx, second antenna, seventh leg) are referenced but not reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most specimens were collected near cave or mine entrances, in twilight zones, on organic debris, rotting wood or silt, and under rocks. The author concludes that these animals were probably surface fauna that were transported into the caves or mines and then adapted to underground conditions. The sites were varied: abandoned mines, bat caves, molasses bat caves, drip rooms and sinks. Several collection records are noted as having guano or rotting material present.

The paper also reports that cave populations of [[Metoponorthus Pruinosus]] show year-round breeding. Gravid females were collected in January, August and September, which the author interprets as evidence of a year-round breeding season. The species was the most widespread in the study and was previously recorded as widespread by Hatch (1947), Lindroth (1957) and Schultz (1964b).

## Key Findings

**Species distribution (summarised from Table 1 and Table 2):**

- [[Metoponorthus Pruinosus]] was the most widespread species, found at 13 of the 23 sites.
- [[Armadillidium Vulgare]] was next, found at 7 sites.
- [[Porcellio Laevis]] was found at 4 sites.
- [[Venezillo Tanneri]] was found at 3 sites, including the type locality at Edinburg, Hidalgo Co., Texas (listed as site 24 in Table 2). Most of its records are from [[Diablo Cave]], Val Verde Co., and nearby caves.
- [[Porcellio Gertschi]] and [[Cylisticus Convexus]] were each recorded from only one location.
- [[Mexiconiscus Tlamayaensis]] and [[Cirolana Pelaezi]] are described from Mexican localities. The former is a new genus and the latter is blind and cave-restricted.

**Main findings and conclusions:**

- Only one oniscoid, *Mexiconiscus tlamayaensis*, shows true cave-adapted traits (blindness and loss of pigmentation).
- Albinism in *Porcellio laevis* is not considered a cave modification because it occurs outside caves.
- Gravid *Metoponorthus pruinosus* females occur in several months, indicating year-round breeding in cave populations.
- The newly described male allotype of *Venezillo tanneri* is documented with detailed morphological characters, and the female is partly characterised.
- A [[Dichotomous Key]] to the eight species is provided, separating them using features such as the ability to roll into a ball, uropod length, pleotelson shape, blindness, and flagellar segment proportions. The author notes it has limited use because more caves and mines are expected to yield additional species.
