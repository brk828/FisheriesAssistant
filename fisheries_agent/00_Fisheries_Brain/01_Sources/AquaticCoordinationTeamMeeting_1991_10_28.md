# Aquatic Coordination Team Meeting, December 3, 1991

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program (Bureau of Reclamation, Upper Colorado Region) announcing a meeting of the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT) scheduled for December 3, 1991, at Arizona State University. The memo does not present primary scientific, morphological, or ecological data; rather, it documents the coordination of an expert review of ongoing [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]] research efforts within the Glen Canyon/Grand Canyon system. The review was organized by Dr. [[Wendell Minckley]] and involved a multi-institutional panel tasked with evaluating study plans and recommending refinements to endangered fish research methodology.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic descriptive data are present in this source document. The text is administrative in nature and does not describe body form, coloration, meristic counts, or other physical characteristics of any fish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While explicit ecological data are absent, the memo situates the research context within the **Colorado River ecosystem** downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], an area of long-standing concern for native and endangered fish conservation due to altered flow regimes, temperature changes, and sediment dynamics caused by dam operations. The convening of the ACT and an independent review panel implies an active, multi-agency research program monitoring:
- Population status of endangered native fishes in the Grand Canyon reach
- Effects of dam-regulated flows on fish habitat
- Study design adequacy for long-term endangered species monitoring

Participating agencies and institutions reflect the interdisciplinary and interjurisdictional nature of this ecological monitoring effort, including the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[National Park Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], [[Oregon State University]], [[University of Oklahoma]], [[University of Nevada]], and the [[Navajo Nation Heritage Program]] (NNHP), as well as Hopi tribal representation.

## Key Findings
- The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program scheduled an [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] meeting for **December 3, 1991**, at Arizona State University to discuss the status of endangered fish research.
- Dr. [[Wendell Minckley]] led an independent review of the endangered fish research effort, assisted by a panel of prominent aquatic ecologists: Dr. [[Hiram Li]] (Oregon State University), Dr. [[William Matthews]] (University of Oklahoma), Dr. [[James Deacon]] (University of Nevada), and Dr. [[Duncan Patton]] (Arizona State University).
- The review's stated purpose was to **assess study plans and completed work** across all endangered fish studies and provide **recommendations for refining research methodology**.
- The review was expected to be completed by **late November 1991**, with results to be discussed at the December ACT meeting alongside findings from a related **National Academy of Sciences review**.
- The distribution list indicates broad institutional involvement in Colorado River endangered species research, spanning federal (BOR, USFWS, NPS), state (AGF), academic (ASU), and tribal (Hopi, Navajo Nation) stakeholders.
- No quantitative data, population estimates, or species-specific findings are included in this document; it functions solely as a scheduling and coordination notice for the broader [[Glen Canyon Dam]] adaptive management and endangered species research program.
