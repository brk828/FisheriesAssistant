# Reclamation Research in the Seventies: First Progress Report (Research Report No. 26)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1974 progress report from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (U.S. Department of the Interior) summarizing its Congressionally funded water resources research. The report covers the Bureau's seven regional offices and the Engineering and Research Center in Denver. Work is grouped under five Committee for Water Resources Research (COWRR) categories, plus the separately presented [[Project Skywater]] weather modification program.

The document is primarily a water resources, engineering, and planning report. Fish and aquatic life appear as secondary concerns: lake trout fisheries, salmon spawning gravels, estuary productivity, and aquatic weed and algae control in canals. Most projects are described in progress terms, and many results are preliminary.

## Background & Description

The report sets out the Bureau's research goals: more efficient water management, improved hydroelectric reliability, measurement of social, economic, and environmental impacts, mitigation of ecological and water quality effects, better project planning, engineering design, and construction methods. It is organized into the following categories:

- **Water Supply Augmentation and Conservation:** phreatophyte control, on-farm irrigation efficiency, and irrigation management services.
- **Water Quantity Management and Control:** aquatic pest control, sediment and siltation studies, reservoir operations models, flood hydrology, and ground water analysis.
- **Water Quality Management and Protection:** pollutant sources, salinity monitoring, nitrate removal, herbicide residues, reaeration, and reservoir temperature prediction.
- **Water Resources Planning:** economic evaluation, land classification, soil chemistry, boron leaching, ecological impact assessment, and estuary and reservoir ecology.
- **Engineering Works:** structures, hydraulics and sedimentation, hydraulic machinery, electric power, soil and rock mechanics, concrete and other materials, and tunneling.

Facilities are made available to qualified scientific and academic users on request to the Chief, Division of General Research, Denver.

Project Skywater was authorized by the 87th Congress in 1961 to study increasing precipitation through cloud seeding. About 90 percent of its work is contracted to universities, private firms, state agencies, and other federal agencies. The Division of Atmospheric Water Resources Management at the Denver center coordinates it.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report treats ecological effects mainly as impacts to be measured and mitigated:

- **Aquatic weeds and algae in canals:** More than half of irrigation and multiple-use channels in the 17 Western States have aquatic weed infestations. Control can cost up to 40 percent of annual operation and maintenance. Some herbicides may harm nontarget organisms, including fish and wildlife.
- **Asiatic clams and canal sedimentation:** In the [[Delta-Mendota Canal]], [[Asiatic Clam]] activity was linked to the precipitation of inorganic deposits that trap fine sediment. Calculations suggest a single clam may produce 5.5 grams of particles per year. Clam numbers and sedimentation rates rose sharply during the 1969-70 dewatering.
- **Phreatophytes:** Deep-rooted water-using plants such as [[Saltcedar]], cottonwoods, and willows transpire large volumes of water and displace beneficial land use.
- **Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta:** The Delta covers 740,000 acres and more than 700 miles of waterways. It serves as a water supply, waste disposal system, transportation route, recreation area, and fish and wildlife habitat. Ocean salinity intrusion is a continuing concern.
- **Twin Lakes Reservoir:** The reservoir supports one of the best lake trout fisheries in the western United States. Its success depends on [[Mysis Shrimp]], a key food for young fish, and on cold water temperatures. The [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]] was being assessed for possible harm to trout and shrimp.
- **Tehama-Colusa salmon facilities:** Planned [[Salmon Spawning Beds]] monitoring will measure gravel water velocity and dissolved oxygen. Fine sediment cleaning will be modeled to protect spawning gravels and meet turbidity limits.
- **Estuary and backwaters:** Studies address biological productivity, the effects of the San Luis Drain on estuary productivity, and fish concentrations in four dredged backwater areas of the lower Colorado River.

## Key Findings

### Water Supply and Conservation
- Field analysis across over 5,000 acres at 17 locations in 11 Western States found that an average of **44 percent** of delivered irrigation water was stored in the root zone for crop use. The report states that **70 to 90 percent** efficiency is achievable with better management and farm infrastructure.
- The [[Irrigation Management Services]] program served **96,000 acres** in 11 irrigation districts during the 1972 season.
- In phreatophyte research, 72 herbicide combinations were tested on greenhouse saltcedar. [[Silvex]] was judged the best cost-effective option. A 2,4-D polymer and MSMA were ineffective. Bromacil was the only soil sterilant showing promise, though its persistence may be undesirable.

### Water Quantity and Control
- Under [[Aquatic Pest Control]], a copper sulfate and diquat combination was found more effective at lower concentrations and relatively harmless to fish. [[Hydrothol 191]] gave excellent results in the Corning Canal. No compound yet controls mat-forming blue-green algae completely, and copper sulfate remains the only practical management method.
- The [[Central Valley Project]] pilot model optimizes Trinity and Shasta reservoir operations on a daily basis for 30 days.
- A [[Lake Mead]] survey (1963-64) measured reservoir capacity at **29.8 million acre-feet**, with **2.72 million acre-feet** of sediment accumulated since 1935. Trap efficiency was judged 100 percent, and reservoir life is estimated at **500 years** after Glen Canyon Dam closure.
- The [[Hybrid Computer Model]] for the Upper Jordan River drainage has completed Utah Lake and Provo River models.

### Water Quality
- Salinity surveillance on the lower Colorado River (Parker to Imperial Dams) was designed to keep sampling error below 5 percent, 95 percent of the time.
- Three nitrate removal methods were judged technically feasible. [[Algae Stripping]] was economically unattractive. [[Bacteriological Denitrification]] was technically feasible but costly. The [[Symbiotic Process]] reduced drainage water from **16 mg/l** to **2 mg/l** nitrate-nitrogen on a 6-acre grass plot, at one-third to one-half the cost of the other processes.
- Herbicide residue monitoring found 2,4-D below 100 ppb in 16 of 18 Columbia Basin treatments. Arsenic from organic arsenical herbicides ranged from 0 to 0.6 ppm in the Rio Grande Project.
- [[Reservoir Temperature Prediction]] models agreed with Folsom Reservoir data to about ±5°F. They are being used for Shasta and Trinity operations to protect downstream fisheries.
- A study of [[Las Vegas Wash]] effluent concluded it contributed to eutrophication of Lake Mead through high dissolved solids and nutrients.
- At Cheney Reservoir, **42 percent** of total inflow was lost to evaporation, increasing salinity.

### Planning and Ecology
- The [[Battelle Environmental Evaluation System]] scores project plans across **4** categories (ecology, aesthetics, pollution, human interest), **18** subcategories, and **78** measurable parameters, with "red flag" warnings for sensitive topics.
- Economic findings from Illinois weather modification research indicate an average gain of about **one bushel per acre** for corn and soybeans, worth roughly **$2 to $3 per acre**.
- Boron in arid soils was found to be leaching slowly at Hollister, so some areas may need long leaching periods or more tolerant crops.

### Engineering and Technology
- Forced-vibration tests on Morrow Point and Monticello Dams are providing data on earthquake loading for arch dams.
- The [[Power System Stabilizer]] was recommended by the Western Systems Coordinating Council for generators larger than **75 megawatts**.
- A [[Hyflo Method]] canal control system was tested on the Corning Canal, with later refinements planned.
- Asphalt emulsion grouting at Morrow Point reduced abutment leakage from **429 gpm** to **37 gpm** when combined with cement grout.
- [[Polymer-Impregnated Concrete]] reached compressive strengths over **25,000 psi** and tensile strengths of **1,700 psi**, with durability improvements of 1,000 percent or more against acid and freeze-thaw exposure.

### Project Skywater
- Pilot project results remain preliminary. The South Dakota experiment suggested precipitation decreases on seeded days, though the authors caution that five seasons are needed for reliable statistics.
- Early North Dakota and Utah results indicated increased precipitation in target areas. Northern Utah airborne and ground-based seeding were both associated with precipitation increases.
- Social surveys found favorable attitudes toward weather modification correlated with education and conservation concern. The report concludes that increased precipitation is unlikely to cause catastrophic ecosystem change, though long-term ecological studies are still needed.
