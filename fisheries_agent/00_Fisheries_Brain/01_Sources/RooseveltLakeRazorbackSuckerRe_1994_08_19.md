## Executive Summary
This is a 1994 Bureau of Reclamation memorandum proposing the construction of a **razorback sucker (Xyrauchen texanus) rearing facility** at Rock House Farm, an ~100-acre irrigated pasture parcel on the Salt River arm of [[Roosevelt Lake]], Arizona. The document outlines the historical decline of the [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Gila River Basin]], references existing rearing infrastructure at [[Lake Mohave]], and solicits feedback from resource agencies and native fish authorities on project feasibility. The facility's stated goal is to rear juvenile razorback suckers to a **predator-protective size** for release into designated critical habitat and Roosevelt Lake, supporting species recovery under the **Endangered Species Act of 1973**.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological measurements are provided in this correspondence; the document is administrative/regulatory rather than a biological survey.
- Implicit ecological note: juvenile razorback suckers must reach a **"predator protective size"** before release, indicating a life-history bottleneck where small/young individuals are highly vulnerable to predation (likely from non-native predatory fishes) in these reservoir systems.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historical range**: Once one of the most abundant and widely distributed fishes in the mainstem rivers of the [[Colorado River]] system, including the [[Gila River Basin]].
- **Habitat types occupied**: Mainstem river channels, reservoirs (e.g., [[Saguaro Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]]), and associated backwater/lake habitats such as [[Peck's Lake]] near Clarksdale on the [[Verde River]].
- **Population history**:
  - Maintained populations in [[Salt River]] reservoirs until at least the early 1950s.
  - Caught in **commercial quantities** from Saguaro Lake in 1949; absent from collections since.
  - Disappeared from Roosevelt Lake just prior to the 1930s.
  - Persisted longer in the Verde River Basin (Peck's Lake), documented at least until 1954.
- **Critical habitat designation** (Final Rule, March 21, 1994):
  - Salt River and its 100-year floodplain, from the old U.S. Highway 60/State Route 77 bridge to the Roosevelt Lake diversion dam.
  - Verde River and its 100-year floodplain, from the Forest Service boundary (T.18N., R.2E., Sec. 31) to Horseshoe Dam.
- **Current recovery infrastructure**: Multiple rearing ponds already established at natural spawning/growing areas on [[Lake Mohave]], where predator-protective-sized fish are released into the wild.
- **Threat context**: Species listed as **endangered** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973; decline attributed to habitat alteration (dam construction, reservoir formation) and likely predation/competition from introduced species (implied by the emphasis on rearing to predator-protective size before release).

## Key Findings
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] has been extirpated from large portions of its historic range in the [[Gila River Basin]], including Roosevelt Lake (since ~1930s) and Saguaro Lake (since ~1949), while persisting longer in Verde River tributary habitats (Peck's Lake, until at least 1954).
- The Bureau of Reclamation proposes converting part of the newly acquired **Rock House Farm** (~100 acres of irrigated pasture on the Salt arm of Roosevelt Lake) into fish-rearing ponds modeled after the successful [[Lake Mohave]] razorback rearing program.
- The project's dual conservation goals are: (1) **reintroduction** into formerly occupied critical habitat and Roosevelt Lake, and (2) **maintenance of additional genetic stock** for the species.
- Legal/regulatory driver: the March 21, 1994 Final Rule formally designated critical habitat for the razorback sucker along specific reaches of the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]], directly motivating this facility proposal.
- The proposal was at an early planning stage, with Reclamation soliciting input from key stakeholders including [[Arizona State University]] researchers (Dr. W.L. Minckley, Dr. Paul Marsh), the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the [[USDA Forest Service]] (Tonto National Forest).
- This document illustrates a broader regional strategy of using multiple geographically distinct rearing facilities (Lake Mohave, proposed Roosevelt Lake) to support genetic diversity and redundancy in razorback sucker recovery efforts across the [[Colorado River]] and [[Gila River Basin]] systems.
