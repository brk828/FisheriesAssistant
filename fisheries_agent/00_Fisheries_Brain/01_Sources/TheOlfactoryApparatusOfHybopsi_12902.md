# Olfactory Apparatus of Hybopsis gelida and Hybopsis aestivalis (Pisces: Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 morphological study by [[Branley A. Branson]] describes the olfactory organ of two North American minnows, [[Hybopsis Gelida]] and [[Hybopsis Aestivalis]], collected by seine from the turbid [[Kaw River]] in Kansas. Using gross dissection, skeletal clearing, and serial histology, the author documents nostril and nasal flap anatomy, the olfactory rosette and lamellae, sensory and sustentacular cell types, and the associated nervous system. The author explicitly frames the work as a comparative baseline for future physiological and behavioral research, given how little is known about cyprinid olfaction. The paper also proposes a new term, "valance," for the curtain-like nasal flap extension, and reports compound olfactory lamellae not previously described in the literature.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Eight *H. gelida* (21.5–38.5 mm standard length) and 70 *H. aestivalis* (12.0–33.0 mm standard length).
- **Collection:** Seines in the Kaw River on August 26, 1962. The river is described as very turbid and low-running for most of the year, typical habitat for both species, although some *H. aestivalis* races occupy clear water ([[Moore 1950]]).
- **Methods:** Specimens were preserved in 10% formalin or modified Bouin's solution. Skeletal preparations used alizarin clearing and staining. Heads were decalcified, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned sagittally, transversely, and horizontally at 10 and 15 µm, then stained with iron hematoxylin and Mallory's triple stain. Measurements used a calibrated ocular disk.
- **External anatomy:** Paired anterior and posterior nostrils lie anterodorsal to the eye. The anterior nostril is smaller and opens through a nasal flap that is more pronounced in *H. gelida*. Jacobsen's organ is visible through the posterior nostril only. The flap is concave anteriorly, deflecting water downward into the anterior nostril.
- **Nasal flap and valance:** The grooved flap extends downward as an epithelial-lined cord, the "valance," which spreads laterally and mesially into the olfactory chamber. The groove arises from an accessory flap.
- **Water flow:** A constant current, driven largely by ciliation, passes through the apparatus. Additional flow is forced through during active swimming. The authors place these fish in Pipping's third category, with water entering the anterior nostril and leaving the posterior one.
- **Olfactory sac:** Nearly circular, about 1.0 mm in diameter, and housed in a fossa formed mainly by the lateral ethmoid, with protection from the frontal, premaxilla, and lachrymal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper situates the anatomy within the ecology of Great Plains streams. Many fishes in these practically lightless, turbid waters show reduced eyes and other modifications. Feeding, predator avoidance, and sensory-cue-dependent behaviors that work in clear water may be less effective in silt-laden water ([[Hubbs 1940]]; [[Hubbs 1941]]; [[Moore 1950]]). In both species, the eyes are only moderately developed, while taste buds and lateral-line neuromasts are very abundant. Based on lamellae counts relative to other sense organs, both species fall into Teichmann's first category, where eyes and olfaction are roughly equally important for environmental adaptation. The author suggests that *H. aestivalis*, which is more ecologically plastic and occurs in both clear and turbid water, relies more on its larger eyes, which may partly explain its fewer olfactory lamellae.

## Key Findings

- **Nasal flap and valance:** The author proposes "valance" for the curtain-like nasal flap extension, noting it was also described in [[Tinca]] (Burne 1909) and other forms (Teichmann 1954).
- **Olfactory rosette structure:** The central lamella lies at about 45° to the body axis. Radial lamellae per side average 12 (9–13) above and below in *H. gelida*, versus 8 (7–9) above and 9 (7–11) below in *H. aestivalis*.
- **Lamellar dimensions:** Radial lamellae average 43.1 µm (31.2–56.0) in length. Width is 5.7 µm (3.9–9.1) apically and 8.0 µm (6.5–11.7) basally. Wall thickness averages 3.0 µm basally and 2.2 µm apically.
- **Compound lamellae:** The author reports compound lamellae, in which one wide base gives rise to two or three lamellae, and states these had not previously been described.
- **Sensory cells:** Olfactory hair cells come in at least three types, all polarized, with long, multiple sensory hairs averaging about 15 µm. Sustentacular cells are columnar, with cilia at their distal ends.
- **Accessory sacs:** Empty anterior accessory sacs are subdivided into three smaller chambers. The author notes similar empty sacs in Perciformes, sticklebacks, and flatfishes.
- **Nervous system:** Three first-order olfactory nerves join each side. The olfactory crura are very closely approximated, about 0.75 mm long in a 30.0 mm fish, a condition the author compares to reptiles. Olfactory bulbs and everted cerebral hemispheres with well-developed olfactory tuberculi are present, and the author calls for further study of mitral cells and ganglionated cells.
- **Interspecific difference:** *H. aestivalis* has fewer lamellae, which the author links to its larger eyes and more plastic habitat use. He plans further comparisons of geographic races and of clear- versus silt-water species.
- **Conclusion:** The paper is a foundational comparative anatomy description intended to guide future physiological and behavioral olfaction research in cyprinids.
