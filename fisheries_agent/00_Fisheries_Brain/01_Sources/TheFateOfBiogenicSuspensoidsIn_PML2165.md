# The Fate of Biogenic Suspensoids in a Desert Reservoir

#limnology #phytoplankton #reservoir-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study, by [[J.C. Priscu]], [[J. Verduin]] and [[J.E. Deacon]], examines what happens to organic material produced within [[Lake Mohave]], a 115.3 km² multipurpose impoundment on the lower [[Colorado River]]. The authors combined vertical light extinction, hypolimnetic nitrogen accumulation and areal [[Hypolimnetic Oxygen Deficit]] measurements at five stations sampled from October 1976 to September 1977. They conclude that biogenic suspensoids (living and dead particulate organic matter) settle from the sunlit surface layer into the [[Hypolimnion]] and are carried downstream toward [[Davis Dam]] by a cold underflow, where they are mineralized. The hypolimnetic oxygen depletion equalled about 39% of the mean phytoplankton oxygen production during spring and summer, and most of it occurred near the dam. The authors argue that reservoir organic matter dynamics differ fundamentally from those of natural lakes and matter for water-quality management and the timing of downstream releases.

## Background & Description

The paper was reprinted from the Proceedings of the Symposium on Surface Water Impoundments (ASCE, June 2-5, 1980, Minneapolis, Minnesota). It is a research article rather than a proposal or budget.

**Motivation.** Reservoirs in a series of multipurpose projects must be managed with knowledge of where materials produced within each reservoir end up. The authors note that the depth of discharge is fundamental to predicting water quality within a reservoir and in its outflow. The stated objective was to evaluate the fate of biogenic suspensoids in Lake Mohave by examining:
- spatial variation in vertical light extinction;
- hypolimnetic accumulation of nitrate-nitrogen (NO₃⁻-N) and ammonium-nitrogen (NH₄⁺-N);
- hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits.

**Study system.** [[Lake Mohave]] lies between Hoover Dam and Davis Dam and was filled in 1953. It is one of six reservoirs between the [[Grand Canyon]] and Mexico. It was created largely to reduce flow variability of the Colorado River caused by irregular power demands at [[Hoover Dam]], and it also stores water for hydroelectric production at Davis Dam.

**Methods (summary).**
- **Sampling:** Five stations (M1-M4 in the lake, M5 on the Colorado River) were sampled monthly or semi-monthly from October 1976 to September 1977.
- **Light:** Irradiance was measured at 1 m intervals with a submarine photometer. Suspensoid dry weights were measured on filters at 0, 5 and 10 m. An extinction coefficient of 0.07 m² g⁻¹ was derived using a modified [[Verduin Extinction Model]] (based on Verduin 1954, 1964), with an extinction of 0.03 m⁻¹ attributed to pure water after [[Whitney]] (1938). Suspensoid concentrations at other depths were then calculated from light profiles.
- **Nutrients:** NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N were measured at 10 m intervals with a detection sensitivity of 20 µg N L⁻¹.
- **Oxygen:** Dissolved oxygen was measured with a Hydrolab instrument at the four lake stations from April to July 1977. Hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits were calculated from these data.
- **Primary productivity:** Measured with the [[Carbon-14 Method]] (¹⁴C) at 0, 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 m, and converted to oxygen production assuming a photosynthetic quotient for comparison with oxygen deficits.
- **Currents:** A Marsh-McBirney electromagnetic current meter took readings at 2 m intervals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and stratification.** Water entering Lake Mohave comes from about 61 m depth in upstream [[Lake Mead]], so inflows stay cold (11-14 °C) throughout the year. During summer [[Thermal Stratification]], this cold water underflowed the warmer lake water and stayed in the hypolimnion before exiting through deep penstocks at Davis Dam. The hypolimnion held about half the lake volume (the thermocline averaged 10 m). The estimated hypolimnetic retention time was about 40 days, roughly half the theoretical retention time for the whole lake given in Table 1.

**Currents.** Current velocity fell from 35.5 cm s⁻¹ to 5.4 cm s⁻¹ where the river entered the lake proper, as the basin widened. This reduction in velocity is one reason the sinking material could be retained and transported.

**Light and suspensoids.** During isothermal periods (February and April), suspensoid concentrations were fairly uniform with depth, and vertical differences were mostly related to the depth of the [[Euphotic Zone]]. After stratification began in May, the epilimnion held much higher suspended material than the hypolimnion at most stations. Station M4 had high epilimnetic suspensoids, high [[Primary Productivity]] and high phytoplankton biomass (chlorophyll *a*) that month. In June and July, hypolimnetic concentrations increased toward the dam and in July exceeded epilimnetic concentrations at the upstream stations. The highest recorded concentration, 12.71 mg L⁻¹, was measured at the impounding dam. Station M5 never stratified because of its lotic nature, and its low turbidity indicated that the downstream hypolimnetic suspensoids originated from the Lake Mead inflow rather than the river.

**Nitrogen dynamics.** Nitrate-N in the river station (M5) remained high all year, reflecting nutrient-rich hypolimnetic water from Lake Mead. NH₄⁺-N at M5 stayed below 40 µg L⁻¹. During isothermal months, surface and bottom NO₃⁻-N declined from about 350 to 150 µg L⁻¹. During the summer, surface NO₃⁻-N and NH₄⁺-N were depleted to below 20 µg L⁻¹ in the epilimnion. Epilimnetic NH₄⁺-N was reported to rise to 169 µg L⁻¹ at station M1 and to remain high over the lake, possibly because of decomposition of a blue-green algal bloom in June.

**Oxygen.** Hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits increased downstream, from 0.002 mg O₂ cm⁻² day⁻¹ at M4 (upstream) to 0.126 mg O₂ cm⁻² day⁻¹ at M1 (near Davis Dam). The authors observed that the lowest deficit occurred where primary production was highest, which weakens the applicability of the [[Hutchinson Trophic Index]]. That index, developed for natural lakes, would classify the upper reaches as extremely oligotrophic and the dam area as highly eutrophic. The authors suggest that in a through-flow system, deficits instead indicate areas of high organic buildup and degradation.

## Key Findings

1. **Suspensoid accumulation in the hypolimnion.** Evidence from light extinction, hypolimnetic nutrient build-up and oxygen deficits all pointed to biogenic suspensoids accumulating in the hypolimnion near the impounding dam.

2. **Oxygen budget.** Total hypolimnetic oxygen depletion was about 39% of the mean phytoplankton oxygen production in the lake during spring and summer. Over 63% of this deficit occurred at the dam, according to the abstract.

3. **Deficit gradient (Table 2).** Hypolimnetic areal oxygen deficits (mg O₂ cm⁻² day⁻¹, April-July 1977) were:

| Distance below Hoover Dam (km) | Station | Deficit |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | M4 | 0.002 |
| 61 | M3 | 0.019 |
| 73 | M2 | 0.050 |
| 108 | M1 | 0.126 |

4. **Nitrogen regeneration.** About 330 µg L⁻¹ of NO₃⁻-N was depleted from the epilimnion, and about 180 µg L⁻¹ of dissolved inorganic nitrogen reappeared at M1. With no other inputs, the authors estimated that about 50% of the depleted nitrate was regenerated in the hypolimnion. The independent oxygen-based estimate, using the ratio of oxygen deficit to production, is described as broadly consistent with this figure.

5. **Downstream DIN trends.** Hypolimnetic [[Dissolved Inorganic Nitrogen]] increased in spring and summer at M1-M3 and decreased at M4. Reported slopes were +14.57 to +47.74 µg L⁻¹ per month at M1-M3 and −12.57 at M4. The authors attribute the difference to downstream stations holding older water, where decomposition of accumulated organic matter has had more time.

6. **Mechanism sequence.** The authors propose the following chain during stratification:
   - phytoplankton is produced in the epilimnion;
   - these organisms sink into the hypolimnion;
   - they are mineralized by bacteria, with ammonium accumulating and then being nitrified to nitrate ([[Nitrification]]), which consumes oxygen;
   - the material is carried downstream by the through-flow and exits at the dam.

7. **Sedimentation is limited.** Earlier natural-lake studies (Frink 1967, Hasler 1963) found that particulate organic matter settles to the deep center of the basin. In Lake Mohave, high through-flow velocities carried most settling matter downstream, so sedimentation was not the main fate.

8. **Management implications.** The authors state that:
   - the depth of release from the impounding dam needs more study, since it affects reservoir and outflow water quality;
   - hypolimnetic release can carry oxygen-consuming organic matter and nutrients, with consequences for downstream [[Water Quality Management]] and downstream water allocation;
   - reservoir stratification and associated oxygen depletion limit the usable volume for fish.

9. **Downstream effects.** Based on earlier literature cited by the authors, cities below major impoundments have reported greater [[Taste And Odor Problems]] tied to increased organic loads, and hypolimnetic releases can reduce the oxygen available for downstream BOD assimilation.

10. **Research need.** The authors conclude that more study is needed on the nature and fate of biogenic suspensoids and their by-products in reservoirs, and that the research supported this work through a grant from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (contract 14-06-300-2718).
