# Fresh-water Fishes of Baja California: Origins and Affinities

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a compiled extract from the *Systematic Zoology* Baja California Symposium. It contains a short review item by Jay M. Savage (an Associate Professor of Biology, University of Southern California, Los Angeles), with a reference list on herpetofauna, followed by the main paper, "The Fresh-water Fishes: Their Origins and Affinities," by W. L. Follett (Curator of Fishes, California Academy of Sciences). Follett's paper is the substantive content. It catalogues the indigenous and introduced fishes of fresh water in [[Baja California]], traces their dispersal origins, and assigns each species a salt-tolerance category. The headline conclusion is that Baja California has no discrete freshwater fauna: its freshwater fishes are peripheral, mostly marine derivatives.

## Background & Description

Follett reports that the indigenous freshwater fishes of Baja California belong to **17 families, 25 genera and 29 species**. All of these are of marine affinity except *Xyrauchen*, *Gila*, *Ptychocheilus* and perhaps *Cyprinodon*. The only endemic species is *Fundulus lima* (see [[Fundulus Lima]]).

Follett gives four reasons for the absence of a true freshwater assemblage:

1. A discontinuity between southern California coastal streams and the Tijuana River. The [[Santa Ana River System]] fauna did not extend south of the San Luis Rey River, which suggests these streams were not connected during [[Pleistocene Sea-Level Lowering]].
2. Long-standing aridity, which prevented coastwise dispersal through estuaries.
3. An abrupt escarpment along the eastern shore, which largely precludes lagoons that could have aided dispersal.
4. The [[Gulf of California]] as a barrier to freshwater fishes from Middle America.

The only strictly freshwater species were a few that, until extirpated, made up the depauperate but specialized fauna of the lower [[Colorado River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Follett identifies five faunal sources for the freshwater fishes:

1. **Northeastern Pacific** (three euryhaline species): [[Salmo Gairdneri]], [[Leptocottus Armatus]] and [[Gasterosteus Aculeatus]] (subspecies *microcephalus*).
2. **Lower Colorado River** (southward dispersal): [[Xyrauchen Lexanus]], [[Gila Robusta Elegans]] and *Ptychocheilus lucius*.
3. **Atlantic seaboard lowlands** (westward dispersal): *Fundulus parvipinnis*, *Fundulus lima* and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]].
4. **Gulf of California** (occasional entry via Colorado River distributaries): *Micropogon megalops*, *Cynoscion xanthulus* and [[Cynoscion Macdonaldi]], each known from a single or few reports.
5. **Panamic region** (northwestward dispersal): the text states 12 species, five of which have passed beyond Baja California. The enumerated list, however, contains 17 species (the OCR reads "1Z"). Examples include [[Elops Affinis]], [[Mugil Cephalus]], [[Agonostomus Monticola]], [[Gillichthys Mirabilis]], [[Gobiomorus Maculatus]] and [[Dormitator Latifrons]].

Follett describes the indigenous fauna as "essentially peripheral." Several families and genera reach their range limits here: *Salmo* at Arroyo del Rosario, *Leptocottus* at Arroyo San Miguel, and Gasterosteidae at about El Rosario. Tropical genera such as *Agonostomus*, *Dormitator* and *Awaous* reach their northwesterly limits near Cabo San Lucas.

Follett uses six freshwater-status categories adapted from Myers' salt-tolerance classification: **Primary** (strictly intolerant of seawater), **Secondary** (confined to fresh water but able to cross narrow sea barriers), **Diadromous** (regular migration at a defined life stage), **Vicarious** (nondiadromous freshwater representatives of marine groups), **Complementary** (marine groups that dominate freshwater only where primary, secondary and vicarious faunas are absent), and **Sporadic** (fishes that enter fresh water without true migration).

Many Pacific representatives of the Elopidae, Bothidae, Centropomidae, Lutjanidae, Eleotridae and Gobiidae are "geminate" species paired with Atlantic relatives (see [[Geminate Species]]). Follett suggests these reflect a wider, continuous ancestral distribution that predates the closure of the Central American seaway at the end of the Miocene.

## Key Findings

- **No discrete freshwater fauna**: Baja California's freshwater fishes are 29 species across 17 families and 25 genera. Only *Fundulus lima* is endemic.
- **Marine origins**: All but four genera (*Xyrauchen*, *Gila*, *Ptychocheilus* and perhaps *Cyprinodon*) have marine affinities.
- **Range limits**: Several taxa reach their southern or western limits in the peninsula (e.g., *Salmo*, *Leptocottus*, Gasterosteidae, *Fundulus*, *Cyprinodon*).
- **Salmo gairdneri status**: Trout from the Sierra San Pedro Mártir have been treated variously as a distinct species, a subspecies or an undifferentiated form. Follett notes that Baja California populations are landlocked in some localities (e.g., Arroyo San Antonio, Arroyo Santo Domingo).
- **Cyprinodon macularius**: Once widespread in the lower Colorado River basin, it is now restricted in the Baja California range. Follett reports that the Pozo del Tule spring population was no longer present in the cited observations, as the spring had become cooler and the population was re-established from the Colorado River.
- **Introduced species**: The list includes *Cyprinus carpio*, *Gila orcutti*, *Siphateles mohavensis*, *Notropis lutrensis*, *Ictalurus punctatus punctatus*, *Gambusia affinis affinis*, *Micropterus salmoides salmoides*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Lepomis macrochirus*, *Pomoxis annularis* and *Pomoxis nigromaculatus*. *Cynoscion xanthulus* in the Salton Sea is a recent introduction by man.
- **Erroneous records**: Follett excludes several reported species, including *Fundulus ainetus*, *Fundulus estensus*, *Catostomus rutilus*, and *Ilyodon furcidens* (type material probably collected in Colima, Mexico), as well as *Agonostomus monticola* records from Pacasmayo, Peru (which are referable to *Mugil cephalus*).
- **Taxonomic uncertainty**: Several genera, including *Agonostomus* (possibly *Joturus*) and *Gillichthys*, have unresolved generic placements. Follett explicitly defers final decisions pending further study.
