# Proasellus boui n. sp.: A New Anophthalmic, Depigmented Interstitial Isopod from the Gardon Valley, France

#carcinology #subterranean-biology #asellidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Jean-Paul Henry]] and [[Guy Magniez]] describes *Proasellus boui* n. sp., a very small, eyeless and unpigmented isopod of the genus [[Proasellus Genus]] (suborder [[Asellota]]). The species was collected from three irrigation wells in the [[Gardon River Valley]] near [[Alès]] (Gard, France) during 1967. Only male specimens were available (five adults, 1.8–2.8 mm), so the description is based on male diagnostic characters. The authors place the species within *Proasellus* on the basis of a revised generic diagnosis, distinguish it from related anophthalmic forms, and provide an updated checklist of about 45 named *Proasellus* taxa. They argue that groundwater (phreatic and interstitial) habitats remain under-explored and that targeted in situ sampling is needed to reveal the true distribution of hypogean asellids.

## Background & Description

**Collection and material.** Prospecting of the phreatic domain of the Gardon valley in 1967 yielded specimens from three tubed wells used for crop irrigation. Individuals were identical across stations but differed from all known western European *Proasellus*. The species is named after [[Claude Bou]], who first discovered the material and made it available to the authors.
- Two wells at Saint-Hilaire-de-Brethmas yielded 3 and 1 males, respectively.
- One well at Les Tavernes yielded a single male.
- The female is unknown.

**General morphology.** Despite its small size, the body retains the typical form of hypogean *Proasellus*. The elongation coefficient is about 4, which is typical for the genus. For comparison, the authors report:
- Below 4 in [[Proasellus Spelaeus]], which has a shorter, broader body.
- 5 in the interstitial [[Proasellus Albigensis]].
- Above 5 in [[Proasellus Cavaticus]] subspecies *walteri*.

Other features include a slightly concave rostral margin, rounded non-projecting anterior cephalic angles, subparallel pereonal margins, a subpentagonal pleotelson that is longer than wide, very reduced setation, and remarkably long, slender pereopods I–VII.

**Appendages (male).**
- *Antennules:* short, with a flagellum of at most five articles bearing olfactory lamellae.
- *Antennae:* flagella were broken in all five specimens, probably during collection.
- *Mouthparts:* typical of the genus, with a well-developed triarticulate mandibular palp, a maxillule inner lobe bearing five distal plumose setae, and a maxilliped with a 4–5 hook retinaculum.
- *Pereopods:* the dactylopodites of pereopods II–VII each bear a single, very long sternal spine, which resembles *[[Proasellus Slavus]]*.
- *Pleopods I:* the exopodite carries about ten short, subequal glabrous setae distally; the retinaculum has a single hook on each side.
- *Pleopods II:* the endopodite is vesicular with a short, truncate, medially split neck, an arrangement that is characteristic of the genus.
- *Pleopods III–V:* the exopodites are sclerotised and divided by sutures. The pleopod IV structure resembles that of *[[Proasellus Beroni]]* and *P. slavus*. The pleopod V structure resembles that of *P. slavus*.
- *Uropods:* broken in all specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Systematic framework.** The authors give a revised generic diagnosis of *Proasellus* (type species *Asellus meridianus* Racovitza, 1919). Key characters include reduced or absent eyes, five distal setae on the maxillule inner lobe, and male pleopods II that are fused into a subtriangular, medially inserted pair. The genus is described as homogeneous and well localised, with more than 40 described species. Many are anophthalmic and endemic to defined areas such as the Iberian Peninsula, the Maghreb, and the Balkans. The authors interpret this endemism as evidence of long colonisation of these regions.

**Checklist.** The paper lists roughly 45 named taxa in chronological order, noting eyed (+) versus anophthalmic (−) forms and their regions. Examples include *P. coxalis* (Dollfus, 1892; eyed, peri-Mediterranean), *P. cavaticus* (Leydig, 1871; anophthalmic, western Europe), and *P. meridianus* (Racovitza, 1919; eyed, Atlantic Europe). Recent French additions include *P. synaselloides* (Henry, 1963) and *P. albigensis* (Magniez, 1965). Several species described in 1968 come from Corsica and Spain, and one unnamed form is recorded from the French Basque Country. The authors note that the subgenera proposed by [[Karaman Subgenera]] (1952, 1955) are unsatisfactory for expressing the lineages within the genus.

**Habitat interpretation.** The authors suggest that small cave-dwelling populations, such as *P. spelaeus* and *P. beroni*, are secondary colonists of a broader subterranean habitat made up of interstitial environments. Cave populations are thought to occupy permanent or temporary pools fed by seepage between percolation zones and the underlying valley flows. The type locality of *P. beroni* (Sabara Cave, central Corsica) is a fossil network with no liquid circulation in summer (Remy, 1950). The authors conclude that the true habitat of this species remains to be found.

**Sampling methods.** Direct in situ sampling of interstitial fauna, using the method recommended by [[Bou And Rouch Method]] (1967), is presented as the best way to build a complete picture of the asellid fauna in a region. Recent sampling in the Ardèche and Hérault recovered three interstitial stations of *P. cavaticus walteri*, *P. synaselloides*, and *P. cavaticus cavaticus*. These sites lie well outside the previously known ranges of these forms.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Proasellus boui* n. sp. is known from five adult males (1.8–2.8 mm) from three wells in the Gardon valley, Gard. The female is unknown.
- **Distinct morphology:** The species is anophthalmic and depigmented. It is distinguished mainly by its very small size, slender pereopods, the structure of pleopods II, IV and V, and its habitat. The authors state that it cannot be confused with any other known western European form.
- **Generic placement:** The species fits the revised diagnosis of *Proasellus* but shows similarities to *P. slavus* (pereopods, pleopods V) and *P. beroni* (pleopods IV).
- **Biogeographic significance:** Each newly found interstitial station of *P. cavaticus walteri*, *P. synaselloides*, and *P. cavaticus cavaticus* extends the known ranges of these taxa considerably.
- **Under-sampling of groundwater habitats:** Interstitial and phreatic environments are described as insufficiently explored. Many new *Proasellus* forms have been found there in recent years, while surface species are no longer being added.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** The addendum notes that Romanian forms *P. danubialis*, *P. elegans*, and *P. basnosanui* were excluded from the checklist. According to Sket (1967), *P. danubialis* may be a variant of *P. coxalis*, and *P. elegans* may be synonymous with *P. deminutus*. An earlier statement that Sket had associated these Romanian forms with those species was an error caused by their alphabetical adjacency in Sket's catalogue.
- **Future work:** The authors plan a comprehensive revision of the *cavaticus* group and state that a similar revision of the whole genus is needed.
