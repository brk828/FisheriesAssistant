## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific data source** but a personal letter, dated 10 February 1989, addressed to Dr. [[W. L. Minckley]], listing local contacts in and around [[Cuatro Ciénegas]], Coahuila, México. The correspondent (apparently connected to fieldwork in the basin) compiled names of government officials, landowners, schoolteachers, and academic colleagues relevant to research access, permitting, and site stewardship in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]]. While it contains no direct morphological, taxonomic, or population data on fish, it is valuable as **metadata/context** for historical ichthyological fieldwork in this globally significant endemism hotspot, including named locations of specific springs and pools that are known habitats for endemic aquatic fauna.

## Background & Description

[[Cuatro Ciénegas]] is a desert basin (bolsón) in Coahuila, México, renowned for an unusually high concentration of endemic aquatic species — fish, mollusks, and notably living [[Stromatolites]] — sustained by numerous spring-fed pools (*pozas*), rheocrene springs, and canal systems collectively forming part of the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] aquatic ecosystem. The letter identifies key local stakeholders whose land or authority intersected with major hydrological features of the basin during the late 1980s, a period of active biological survey work by researchers such as Dr. [[Salvador Contreras-Balderas]] and Dr. Minckley.

Named individuals include:
- **Alonzo Rios Contreras** — Presidente Municipal (Alcalde) of Cuatro Ciénegas, associated with the "Centro de Investigaciones Biológicas del Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas," indicating a formal municipal research/conservation institution tied to the basin.
- **Dr. Salvador Contreras-Balderas** — of the Laboratorio de Ictiología Sistemática y Ecológica, Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León, a key regional ichthyologist collaborator.
- Various landowners controlling access to specific water bodies (detailed below).
- Federal/state tourism and ecology officials (SEDUE — Secretaría de Desarrollo Urbano y Ecología), reflecting the regulatory framework governing the protected basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter explicitly names several critical aquatic habitat features within the Cuatro Ciénegas basin, each historically significant for endemic fish and invertebrate studies:

- **[[Los Pozas Azules]]** (Rancho Quintero / "Los Vislargs"): Described as deep lakes (~43 ft / 13 m), explicitly flagged as "very important and fragile [[Stromatolite]] sites" — underscoring the basin's globally rare microbialite formations, considered analogs for Precambrian ecosystems.
- **[[Rio Mesquites]]**: A major river system in the basin; ownership of the majority of this river and an associated commercial/tourism establishment (with palapas) is attributed to Roberto Arredondo.
- **[[Rancho Tierra Blanca]]**: Site of a rheocrene spring feeding the Rio Mesquites, owned by Manuel Gonzalez — rheocrene springs (channelized, flowing-water spring types) are important distinct microhabitats within the basin's aquatic mosaic.
- **[[Pozo Orozco]]**: A spring/pool ("pozo") on Rancho owned by Manuel Gonzalez Felán.
- **[[El Mojarral]]** (West and East): Named pools within the basin; El Mojarral West and an associated artificial lake ("Los Tortugas") are owned by Dr. Jorge Narváez Lugasti's family (via wife Berta Laura); El Mojarral East possibly owned by Dolores Villareal de Garza. "Mojarral" references are notable because *mojarra* is a common regional term related to native cichlid/poeciliid fishes, suggesting these pools were historically recognized fish habitats.
- **Ejido San Marcos**: Mentioned in fragmentary handwritten notes at the end of the letter, indicating additional field site(s) or community land relevant to basin access.

These named pools and springs (Pozas Azules, Rio Mesquites, Pozo Orozco, El Mojarral East/West) collectively represent the fragmented, isolated aquatic habitats typical of the Cuatro Ciénegas basin's hydrogeology, which has produced high rates of **endemism and speciation** among fish (e.g., pupfish, cichlids) and invertebrates due to long-term hydrological isolation of individual pools.

## Key Findings

- The document itself contains **no direct species records, counts, or morphometric data**; its scientific value is entirely as a **fieldwork logistics and site-access record**.
- It confirms the existence of a formal local research institution — the **Centro de Investigaciones Biológicas del Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas** — operating under the Cuatro Ciénegas municipal government as of 1989, indicating institutional interest in basin conservation predating formal federal protection.
- It documents that access to critical, ecologically fragile sites (notably the stromatolite-bearing **Pozas Azules**) was controlled by private landowners (Rancho Quintero/Los Vislargs family), highlighting the reliance of researchers like Minckley and Contreras-Balderas on personal relationships with local ranchers for site access.
- It corroborates active involvement of Mexican regulatory bodies (**SEDUE** — federal/state ecology and urban development authorities) in the basin during this period, relevant to the regulatory history of what would later become the **Cuatro Ciénegas Protected Area**.
- Multiple named pools (Rio Mesquites, El Mojarral East/West, Pozo Orozco, Los Pozas Azules) serve as a partial gazetteer of historically studied aquatic sites within the basin, useful for cross-referencing against contemporaneous species survey papers by [[W. L. Minckley]] and [[Salvador Contreras-Balderas]].
