# Threadfin Shad Observations in the Colorado River and Lake Havasu, 1957

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a pair of 1957 intraoffice memoranda from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (signed G. W. McCammon, Fisheries Biologist II, addressed to J. B. Kimsey). They report field observations of [[Threadfin Shad]] (TFS) in the [[Colorado River]] system, covering [[Lake Havasu]] (especially the [[Bill Williams Arm]]), the river below [[Imperial Dam]], and the river below [[Davis Dam]]. The memos document sampling by [[Rotenone Sampling]] and [[Tow Netting]], fork-length measurements, and predator observations. The main questions were whether a fall spawning period occurred and how shad were distributed and sized. The second memo is dated November 18, 1957 but covers August 31 to September 10, 1957, and the first is dated November 22, 1957.

- **Document type:** Two intraoffice correspondence memos, with attached length-frequency distribution sheets.
- **Agency:** [[California Department of Fish and Game]].
- **Authors and recipient:** G. W. McCammon (Fisheries Biologist II), with field help from Chuck Seeley; memos addressed to J. B. Kimsey.
- **Focal taxa:** Threadfin shad (primary); [[Plains Red Shiner]] (noted at Imperial Dam); [[Largemouth Bass]] (predators examined for shad in stomachs).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake Havasu, Bill Williams Arm (November 12, 1957 sampling):**
- Shad were "extremely abundant" in the shallow, muddy upper end of the arm and could be dipped from the center of the arm in about one foot of water.
- No evidence of an appreciable fall spawning was found.

**Lake Havasu, coves and Bill Williams Arm (September 1957):**
- Water temperature was 85°F on September 3, 1957, when larval tow-netting produced no larvae.
- Extensive beds of [[Najas]] and [[Potamogeton]] were present at the rear of the coves. This led to speculation that large shad might have been spawning there, but no eggs were found on examined plant strands.
- Small shad (about 1.0 inch) concentrated at cove mouths, and large schools fed at the surface in late afternoon and evening. Some predators were observed slashing at these schools.

**Colorado River below Imperial Dam:**
- [[Plains Red Shiner]] were "very abundant." At one river location, 25 to 30 PRS could be dip-netted with a single swipe.
- No TFS were observed despite three rotenone applications.

**Colorado River below Davis Dam:**
- A large cove on the west side of the river, about eight miles below Davis Dam, was treated with rotenone on September 10, 1957. Shad were not affected until about two hours after application, which suggests a small school swam into the cove from the river and was killed there.

## Key Findings

**1. Lake Havasu, Bill Williams Arm, November 12, 1957 (rotenone):**
- Two rotenone samples collected a total of **1,071** shad.
- Fork length range: **1.0 to 4.6 inches**; mean: **1.5 inches**.
- No evidence of an appreciable fall spawning.
- Shad were extremely abundant in the shallow, muddy upper arm.

**2. Predation (creel census):**
- TFS were found in the stomachs of several angler-caught largemouth bass during [[Creel Census]] work.

**3. Lake Havasu, late August to early September 1957:**
- Tow-netting for larval TFS on **September 3, 1957**, at a water temperature of **85°F**, was unsuccessful.
- Two coves were sampled with rotenone, and a third cove was treated on **September 4**.
- Total collected: **330** TFS.
- Fork length range: **0.6 to 3.5 inches**; mean: **1.6 inches**.
- Some large shad (**3.0 to 3.5 inches**) were found at the back of the cove treated on the second day.
- Gonads of several large shad were checked and were **not ripe**. No eggs were found on [[Najas]] or [[Potamogeton]] strands.

**4. Colorado River below Davis Dam, September 10, 1957:**
- Rotenone was applied to a large cove about eight miles below the dam.
- Total collected: **17** TFS, all large fish compared with Havasu shad.
- Fork length range: **2.8 to 4.1 inches**; mean: **3.4 inches**.

**5. Predator stomach contents (Davis Dam area):**
- Several largemouth bass were collected.
- One bass (length partly garbled in the scan, apparently 12.0 inches) had eaten a **4.6-inch** shad.
- Another bass (length also garbled, apparently 12.0 inches) had eaten a **3.9-inch** shad.
- Both shad were judged to have been ingested before the rotenone application.

**6. Imperial Dam area:**
- Three rotenone applications produced **no TFS**.
- [[Plains Red Shiner]] were very abundant (25 to 30 per single net swipe at one location).

**7. Management relevance (inferred from the memos):**
- The memos document shad size structure, spawning evidence, and predator interactions. They do not state a formal management recommendation.
- The contrast between small Havasu shad (mean 1.5 to 1.6 inches) and larger shad below Davis Dam (mean 3.4 inches) is a recorded observation. The memos do not explain it.
