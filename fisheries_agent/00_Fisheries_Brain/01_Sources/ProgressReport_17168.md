# Colorado River Fisheries Investigations: 1979 Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 progress report from the [[Colorado River Fisheries Project]] (CRFP), prepared by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and submitted under a contract with the Water and Power Resources Service, documents the first full field season of a program studying the life history, habitat and culture requirements of endangered Colorado River fishes. The work combined field surveys on the Green, Colorado and Gunnison Rivers, hatchery propagation at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], and university contracts on tolerance, behavior and toxicology. The report also includes a 1980 addendum covering hybrid crosses, humpback chub propagation, striped bass, disease work, and radio tracking through mid-year.

## Background & Description

The project was established in April 1979 through a Memorandum of Understanding between the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the Water and Power Resources Service, with the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (Colorado Office) later providing additional funding for Upper Colorado River work. Its stated aims were to study:

1. Spawning requirements
2. Young and adult habitat requirements and preferences
3. Migration and movement
4. Interspecific competition
5. Cultural technologies
6. Tolerance to chemo-physical conditions

Studies were organized into three lines: field, hatchery and laboratory investigations. Full field activities began July 15, 1979 and ended in November when cold weather stopped work. Two field stations were established: Vernal, Utah (Green River) and Grand Junction, Colorado (Colorado and Gunnison Rivers). The Vernal station had three permanent and four temporary staff; the Grand Junction station had two permanent and two temporary staff.

Target species were the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), with bonytail chub, razorback sucker and related Gila species also handled in hatchery and laboratory work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Roughly 570 miles of the Colorado, Gunnison and Green Rivers were divided into strata: six on the Green River, eight on the Colorado and one on the Gunnison (later recommended to expand to eleven). Strata were defined by geophysical traits (channel form, substrate, gradient, depth) and biological features (productive bottomlands, sterile sand runs, productive backwaters, swift canyons, and point sources of allochthonous material).

**Habitat types.** Eight habitat categories were recorded: main channel, backwater, side channel, eddy, pool, riffle, run and shoreline. Substrates were classed as silt, sand, gravel, rubble, boulder and bedrock.

**Sampling design.** A sequential random-start design was used across strata, with 0.5-mile intensive sections sampled over 24 hours, followed by general electrofishing of the intervening 4.5-mile reach. Sampling was stratified by time of day (morning, afternoon daylight, darkness) and by runoff stage (pre-, during and post-runoff).

**Key sites.**
- [[Black Rocks]]: a deep, swift, one-mile section below Loma with substantial humpback chub and squawfish populations.
- [[Westwater Canyon]]: deep, swift water (River Miles 111–125) with habitat like Black Rocks.
- [[Walter Walker Wildlife Area]]: a 440-acre area around a flooded gravel pit, about 9 miles downstream of Grand Junction, supporting squawfish and razorback suckers historically.
- Lower Green River backwaters, where young squawfish were concentrated.

**Hatchery context.** At Willow Beach, a recirculation system with ten independent tanks was designed, and a bypass pipeline at the Logan Fishery Experiment Station was planned to cool water for adult squawfish.

## Key Findings

**Program and logistics**
- Utah State University (contract from December 7, 1979) studies temperature and TDS tolerance, preference and swimming performance of five native species, plus a striped bass field study on predation risk.
- University of Idaho (from April 1, 1980) works on northern squawfish spawning and early life history, culture techniques, and acute toxicity of 16 chemicals (eight organic, eight inorganic) to Colorado squawfish and humpback chub.
- A fish disease survey with the Fort Morgan Fish Disease Diagnostic Center was set up, with first field trip scheduled for May 26, 1980.

**Hatchery propagation**
- The first humpback chub spawning attempt (May 1979) failed; a squawfish spawn in July 1979 gave about 30,000 poorly fertilized eggs and 918 juveniles.
- Broodstock problems included too few mature females and rearing space occupied by 1,500 subadult squawfish and 828 subadult razorback suckers.
- Approximately 550 squawfish (lot 40VAWB) were set aside as future broodstock; 70 mature males showed active sperm in June 1979.
- Eight roundtail chub were captured with 19 humpback chub near Grand Junction for hybrid crosses with bonytail.

**Green River (1979)**
- Sampling ran July 16 to October 24, 1979, post-runoff only, covering about 300 river miles.
- 1,794 young-of-the-year (YOY) squawfish were collected, most in Strata A and F.
- Adult squawfish totaled 77 juvenile and adult fish in the systematic program; 26 adult humpback chub, 1 adult bonytail and 2 adult razorback suckers were also taken, and five humpback chub were recaptured.
- Backwater seining identified three of seven sampled backwaters near Ouray as producing YOY squawfish; 54 fish were marked and released, with 9 recaptured. Diurnal catches peaked around midnight.
- Young squawfish were not found in areas preferred by redside shiners.

**Colorado and Gunnison Rivers (1979)**
- Sampling ran July 20 to November 4, 1979, covering about 255 river miles of the Colorado and 37 of the Gunnison.
- 23 fish species and 4 hybrids were captured.
- Black Rocks yielded 29 Colorado squawfish and 90 humpback chub, tagged and released, with evidence of reproduction.
- Brood stock collection: 7 squawfish sent to Willow Beach, and 20 adult humpback chub captured by trammel nets with no mortalities.

**Habitat preferences**
- Adult squawfish favored shoreline, eddy and run habitats, velocities of 0.15–0.95 ft/s, and sand or silt substrates in the Green River data; on the Colorado, most adults were in water under 10 ft deep, under 2 ft/s, over boulders.
- Juvenile and young squawfish occupied backwaters with little or no current, young typically under 5 ft deep, under 1 ft/s, over silt-sand.
- Adult humpback chub occupied deep, swift, rocky channels; young were found only in shallow, calm, sand-silt backwaters (0–10 ft, under 1 ft/s).

**Significant findings**
- Westwater Canyon yielded 10 adult, 1 juvenile and 2 young humpback chub, plus 7 squawfish (1 adult, 1 juvenile, 5 young), suggesting a significant population.
- 241 young and 21 juvenile squawfish were taken from the 47-mile stratum above the Green River confluence; one seine haul at mile 34.8 gave 106 young.
- A juvenile from the 1978 year class was caught near River Mile 133, the farthest upstream young squawfish capture in several years, indicating successful spawning in 1978 and 1979.

**Recommendations for 1980**
- Sample Black Rocks and Walter Walker in May–June (chub spawning) and July–August (squawfish spawning).
- Sample strata sequentially where logistics dictate, rather than randomly.
- Reduce seines of 1/2-inch mesh or larger, replace trammel nets with gill nets, reduce wire traps, and trial a shrimp trawl and angling.
- Check all trammel and gill nets hourly to reduce squawfish mortality after trammel-net deaths in 1979.
- Tag non-endangered fish with Floy tags.
- Increase strata on the Colorado and Gunnison from 9 to 11, and establish a special study in Westwater Canyon.

**1980 addendum (mid-year)**
- Hybrid crosses: three female bonytail crossed with roundtail males produced about 300, 22,430 and 3,708 eggs; one bonytail by humpback cross on May 20 produced about 22,000 eggs. Each batch gave roughly 10,000–15,000 fry, sent to Dexter NFH.
- Humpback propagation: a Black Rocks field spawn yielded 18,000 eggs (about 13,000–14,000 fry reared at Willow Beach). Little Colorado River humpbacks produced 2,000 fry from 5,700 fertilized eggs.
- Striped bass: about 200 fish were taken below Cataract Canyon, with spawning evidence between Gypsum Canyon and Cataract Canyon; 40–45 squawfish were also found in that reach.
- Radio tracking: six squawfish and one razorback sucker were tagged. One squawfish was lost after distress and accidental death; the others showed good health and useful movement data.
- Hormonal injections of adult humpback chub at Black Rocks produced about 17,000 viable eggs.
- Many Little Colorado River humpbacks collected for culture died at Willow Beach; causes were under investigation.
