# Systematics of the Percid Fishes of the Subgenus Microperca, Genus Etheostoma

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1978 bulletin of the [[Alabama Museum of Natural History]] (Number 4, 53 pages) by Brooks M. Burr, based on a dissertation at the University of Illinois, revises the darter subgenus [[Microperca]] (genus [[Etheostoma]], family [[Percidae]]). The subgenus contains three species: [[Cypress Darter]] (*E. proeliare*), [[Fountain Darter]] (*E. fonticola*) and [[Least Darter]] (*E. microperca*). The study describes intraspecific morphological variation, maps distributions, and re-examines intra- and interspecific relationships. The author concludes that *E. proeliare* is the most primitive member, that *E. fonticola* is its closest relative (forming a species pair), and that *E. microperca* is the most derived, probably the most advanced North American percid. The bulletin also documents the endangered status of *E. fonticola* and presumed extirpations of *E. microperca* from parts of its range.

## Background & Description

The subgenus *Microperca* comprises the smallest darters and among the smallest freshwater fishes in North America. Adults are usually 25–35 mm standard length (SL), and all three species have one-year to 18-month life cycles. The study examined more than 25 meristic characters on about 1,200 *E. proeliare*, 200 *E. fonticola* and 2,000 *E. microperca* specimens, plus 16 proportional measurements on 40–70 specimens per species. Life history data (published separately) were incorporated into the taxonomic analyses.

Key diagnostic traits of the subgenus include:
- Extremely reduced body lateral line (0–9 pored scales) and degenerate cephalic lateral line, with the infraorbital canal always interrupted.
- Reduced squamation, with nape, breast and belly usually naked.
- Exceptionally long male pelvic fins with cup-like skin webs used during spawning.
- Breeding tubercles on males only, and sexual dimorphism in coloration and body proportions.
- Bilobed (*E. proeliare*, *E. fonticola*) or conical (*E. microperca*) female genital papillae.
- Mature ova indented on one side, appearing as a half donut.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Microperca species are primarily inhabitants of sluggish, low-gradient streams, lakes and springs, often with cool water and abundant aquatic vegetation. Two species are primarily spring spawners, and some populations of two species probably spawn nearly year-round in constant-temperature springs.

- **Distribution:** The three species are largely allopatric, ranging from Ontario, Michigan, Minnesota and Wisconsin south through the Great Lakes and Mississippi Valley to the Florida panhandle and the Guadalupe River of Texas. Sympatry of *E. proeliare* and *E. microperca* occurs only at Sansbois Creek, Oklahoma, with no evidence of hybridization.
- **Habitat associations:** The reductions in lateralis system in Microperca and [[Hololepis]] may relate to low-gradient habitats and behavior; the similar reductions in [[Catonotus]] are considered convergent.
- **Historical biogeography:** The authors infer that the Ozark and Blue River populations of *E. microperca* are glacial relicts persisting in cool springs after Pleistocene displacement. *E. proeliare* likely originated in Mississippi Valley lowlands and dispersed to Gulf Coast drainages via stream capture and lowland connections.
- **Conservation:** *E. fonticola* is nationally recognized as endangered and was restricted to the San Marcos River after extirpation from the Comal River. The San Marcos population was estimated at 103,000 fish (Schenck and Whiteside, 1976). Recommendations include controlling development along the Comal and San Marcos rivers and limiting pumping from the [[Edwards Aquifer]]. *E. proeliare* is not endangered, but Sisk (1973) suggested listing in Kentucky. *E. microperca* is listed as rare or threatened in Kansas, Arkansas, Oklahoma and Wisconsin, and its Iowa population has not been recorded since 1890.

## Key Findings

- **Subgeneric status:** Microperca is recognized as a subgenus within *Etheostoma*, not merged with [[Hololepis]]. The two are closely related, but the authors favor recognizing monophyletic, morphologically definable species groups.
- **Phylogeny:** The subgenus is monophyletic (supported by seven characters). *E. fonticola* and *E. proeliare* form a sister-species pair (six characters). Earlier rankings placing *E. fonticola* as the most advanced member were judged superficial.
- **Most primitive species:** *E. proeliare* retains the greatest number of primitive states, including larger size, more complete squamation, higher meristic values and the most developed lateralis system.
- **Most derived species:** *E. microperca* shows derived conditions for nearly every character, including conical papillae, reduced squamation, red-orange breeding fins and extreme sexual dimorphism in body proportions.
- **Fountain Darter population differentiation:** The Comal River and San Marcos River populations differ in left pectoral ray count (about 90% of Comal fish have nine or fewer; 88% of San Marcos fish have 10 or more) and in male second dorsal fin pigmentation. The author concludes these reflect real genetic differentiation, possibly accelerated by high spring temperatures and many generations per year.
- **Ozark populations of Least Darter:** These differ from northern populations in several meristic and pigment characters, have tubercles on anal rays and anterior dorsal fins, and reach greater maximum size. Dorsal fin tubercles are unknown in any other North American percid. The author nonetheless recommends treating *E. microperca* as monotypic, noting that subspecific recognition would be subjective.
- **Size and sex:** Maximum SL is 40 mm or less in the subgenus. *E. proeliare* and *E. microperca* females are larger, probably because they live longer. Lake Pontchartrain *E. proeliare* attain larger size and partially scaled napes than Ohio populations, interpreted as environmentally induced.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** Specimens of *E. microperca* attributed to the Saline River, Arkansas, and to several Kentucky and Oklahoma streams were reidentified as *E. proeliare* or other Catonotus species. Historical records from the Calumet and Du Page rivers, Illinois, and Iowa's Cedar River indicate local extirpation.
- **Type material:** Lectotypes were designated for *Microperca punctulata* (UMMZ 200216) and *Alvarius fonticola* (from USNM 36523, selected by Collette and Knapp 1966). The name *microperca* replaced the preoccupied *punctulata*.
- **Proposed conclusion:** The authors argue that earlier phylogenetic placements based on few characters were superficial, and that the full character set supports *E. proeliare* as the most primitive species, *E. fonticola* as its sister, and *E. microperca* as the most advanced.
