# Green River and Fontenelle Reservoir Post-Impoundment Investigations (1965–1967)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This completion report, a 1967 addendum to Progress Report No. 4 (Job No. 1B, [[Fishery Investigations]], [[Flaming Gorge Unit]]), covers physical, chemical, and biological sampling of [[Fontenelle Reservoir]] and the [[Green River]] from July 1965 to June 1967. The work was financed under Section 8 of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] as part of the [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] investigations. The study examined how the reservoir and its tailwater influence each other, especially for the fishery.

A September 1965 evacuation of the reservoir to minimum pool caused record tailwater flooding (up to 23,000 cfs) that scoured the river and reduced fish stocks. Salmonids declined in relative abundance, while cyprinids and catostomids increased. Trout were sparse below [[Big Sandy River]] confluence, and fishing use was concentrated in a few river reaches.

## Background & Description

The study area lies in a northern shrub desert with low precipitation, alkaline and easily eroded soils, and harsh climate. Earlier work included a pre-impoundment survey by [[Bosley]] (1960), a [[Rotenone Treatment]] of the Green River in 1962 to suppress undesirable fish, and pre- and post-treatment fauna studies by [[Binns]] (1965, 1967). Trout stocking was heavy after treatment. Hatchery rainbow trout were stocked at 700,000 (1963) and 921,000 (1964) in the river, and brown trout at 386,000 (1965) and 386,855 (1966).

The tailwater was divided into four sections:

- **Section I:** Flaming Gorge head to [[Bitter Creek]] mouth, near Green River City.
- **Section II:** Bitter Creek to [[Big Sandy River]] mouth, including [[Big Island]].
- **Section III:** Big Sandy River to [[Fontenelle Dam]].
- **Section IV:** Green River above the reservoir and the [[New Fork River]], a surveillance reach.

Sampling methods included seasonal plankton hauls with a Wisconsin closing net, [[Eckman Dredge]] and [[Surber Sampler]] benthos sampling, diving gill nets for the reservoir, dynamite and electrofishing for the river, and tagging with jaw, Peterson, and dart tags. A 1966 [[Creel Census]] was run by ground and aerial counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir chemistry and physics.** Fontenelle was a typical small, alkaline western reservoir. When full in 1965, it showed chemical stratification but no thermocline. Bottom waters approached anaerobic conditions (≤1 ppm O₂ below 50 ft), with CO₂ rising to 15 ppm. After draw-down, the reservoir was homothermal (about 33°F under ice) and near saturation for oxygen. Turbidity seldom exceeded 25 ppm.

**Plankton.** Mougeotia and cladocerans were the main plankters before draw-down. Afterward, diatoms (Fragillaria, Asterionella, Tabellaria) dominated the phytoplankton, and copepods, rotifers, and cladocerans dominated the zooplankton at different times. Total plankton peaked at 798 per liter in summer and fell to 10.1 per liter in winter. Aquatic plants (Elodea, Potamogeton, Myriophyllum) became established on shoals after draw-down.

**Bottom fauna.** [[Tendipedidae]] (midges) were dominant in both reservoir and river. Midge density in the reservoir fell from 526 per square foot at 5 ft to 116 per square foot at 35 ft (November 1966).

**River chemistry.** The Green River became more alkaline and mineral-laden downstream of the Big Sandy confluence. Sulfate was well above typical U.S. levels in Sections I and II. Mean conductance was about 705 µmhos in Section I, 680 µmhos in Section II, and 466 µmhos in Section III. Intermittent tributaries such as Dry Creek carried heavy spring silt loads (Dry Creek turbidity 5,875 ppm; Slate Creek 1,750 ppm).

**Fish and fishery.** Flannelmouth suckers were the dominant reservoir fish in both years. Rainbow and brown trout together made up about 25 percent of the fish fauna in FY 1967 (44 percent in FY 1966). Most of the fish in the river were taken with dynamite.

## Key Findings

**Reservoir fish community**
- Gill net catch per net-hour rose from 0.94 (FY 1966) to 1.35 (FY 1967).
- Flannelmouth sucker was the dominant species in both years. Rainbow trout ranked second, at 29 percent in FY 1966 and 22 percent in FY 1967.
- Brown trout catch per hour dropped from 0.16 to 0.035, and whitefish from 0.086 to 0.008. Whitefish were lost from the reservoir after its initial filling.
- Carp, a species that was absent in FY 1966, became more common on shoals. Mean carp condition factor was 1.971.
- Utah chub (three fish) and bonytail chub were rare.

**Growth and condition**
- Reservoir rainbow trout averaged 434 mm and 842 g (FY 1966), falling to 362 mm and 664 g (FY 1967).
- Rainbow trout reached 300 mm in 2 years and 464 mm at 5 years. The oldest fish was 6 years.
- Brown trout took about 7 years to reach roughly 500 mm.
- Mean condition factor (K) for brown trout rose from 1.021 to 1.085.

**Food habits**
- In reservoir rainbow trout (n = 50), organic debris was 41.6 percent and midge larvae 36.8 percent by volume.
- In Section II (n = 51), organic debris was 72.3 percent and snails were the main animal food.
- In Section III (n = 32), organic debris was 54.2 percent. Caddisflies, dragonfly nymphs, and snails were the main animal items.

**Tailwater fishery and movement**
- The 1966 creel census found 0.54 trout per hour in Area I (Green River City to Big Island) and 0.29 trout per hour in Area II (Big Island to Fontenelle Dam). Mean rainbow length was 283 mm in Area I and 342 mm in Area II.
- Fishing pressure was heaviest in July, on holidays, and in the upper area. Most non-resident anglers came from Colorado and Utah.
- Of 3,703 rainbow trout tagged in the river, 243 were recovered by September 1, 1967 (about 6.5 percent). Of these, 95 percent were caught within five miles of release, and the longest movement was 70 miles downstream.
- Of 794 trout tagged at the upper end of Flaming Gorge Reservoir, 27 were recovered (3 percent), all from downstream areas.

**Flood effects and spawning**
- Flood effects on the river fauna were severe, but the benthos recovered within about a year. Caddisflies (Hydropsychidae) were slow to recover from the 1962 rotenone treatment.
- Limited evidence suggested a possible upstream trout spawning run below Fontenelle Dam, centered on [[Flume Creek]].

**Management implications**
- The authors suggested that the reservoir moderates tailwater temperatures, which may draw trout toward cooler water near the dam.
- The authors also noted that flood-driven downstream emigration, stocking reductions, and the marginal summer temperatures of lower reaches may explain the sparse trout populations downstream.
