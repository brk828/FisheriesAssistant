# Barrier Net/Berm — Davis Cove Sucker Rearing Program (NFWG, 1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #freshwater-ecology #ConservationManagement

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal 1993 correspondence from the **Native Fish Work Group (NFWG)** outlining an operational plan for rearing and harvesting an unspecified species of endangered [[Catostomidae|sucker]] (contextually consistent with the [[Razorback Sucker]], *Xyrauchen texanus*, a Colorado River Basin native) in artificial and natural backwater habitats at **Lake Mohave**. The note describes propagation methods (natural spawning, larval collection, manual fertilization), juvenile rearing in isolated coves (**Yuma Cove**, **Davis Cove**), and infrastructure challenges — particularly a failing **barrier net** intended to exclude predatory/exotic fish, which is slated for replacement by an **earthen berm** in fall 1994. The correspondence includes a draft interagency schedule (NPS, BOR, ASU-FWS, NFWG) coordinating NEPA compliance, permitting, construction, propagation, and harvest activities across 1994–1995.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide direct morphometric data on the fish themselves, but it does include size-class information relevant to life-stage identification and management thresholds:

- **Larval stage**: collected directly from the reservoir for backwater transplantation.
- **Juvenile size class 1**: fish **<15 cm** — retained in initial harvest for further grow-out.
- **Juvenile size class 2**: fish **<16–17 cm** — specifically transported to [[Davis Cove]] for continued growth before eventual release.
- **Sub-adult/target stocking size**: fish **>20 cm** — considered large enough for direct stocking into the main reservoir; notably **few individuals exceeded this size** during the August 1993 harvest, suggesting poor survival or slow growth of fertilized-egg-origin fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use**: The program relies on **natural and artificial backwaters** (isolated coves cut off from the main reservoir) as nursery habitat, mimicking historic floodplain/backwater rearing conditions important for [[Catostomidae|sucker]] recruitment in regulated rivers.
- **Key sites**:
  - [[Yuma Cove]] — used for natural spawning and manual egg fertilization.
  - [[Davis Cove]] — primary juvenile rearing backwater; managed with a **barrier net** (soon a berm) to exclude exotic species and prevent fish escape/immigration.
- **Threats within the niche**:
  - **Exotic fish species** competing with or predating on native larvae/juveniles within Davis Cove.
  - **Macrophyte overgrowth** and **poor water quality**, which complicate harvest/recovery efforts.
  - **Sand accumulation** on the barrier net during summer months, degrading barrier integrity and risking breach (a recurring problem from the prior year).
- **Reservoir hydrology dependency**: Collection and construction activities are tightly constrained by seasonal **reservoir elevation drawdown**, with only a **1–2 month window** suitable for berm construction at low water.
- **Management interventions**:
  - Periodic **poisoning (rotenone-type reclamation)** and full evaluation of Davis Cove planned post-berm construction (1994).
  - Removal of **exotic and oversized native fish** prior to stocking events.
  - Decision to **delay reclamation** of Davis Cove in fall 1993 specifically to allow small juvenile suckers (<16–17 cm) additional grow-out time — an explicit trade-off between habitat renovation and cohort survival.

## Key Findings
- **Poor survival/hatching success** was observed in suckers originating from **manually fertilized eggs** placed directly in backwaters, based on preliminary August 1993 harvest data (few fish >20 cm recovered).
- **Natural larval collection and transplantation** from the reservoir to backwaters was adopted as the primary propagation strategy for the coming year, with **manual spawning retained only as a contingency**.
- Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery (NFH) was being evaluated as a potential **supplemental larval collection, incubation, and holding facility** to buffer against low natural collection yields.
- The **barrier net at Davis Cove** is failure-prone (sand accumulation, breaching) and will be **decommissioned and replaced with an earthen berm** in fall 1994, requiring tight coordination among NEPA compliance, Section 404 permitting, and construction contracting (Bureau of Reclamation-led).
- **Interagency roles** were assigned across four functional areas:
  - **Propagation** — ASU/FWS (Marsh, Divine)
  - **Harvest** — NFWG (Burke, C. Minckley)
  - **Management** (weeds, reclamation, reporting, barrier net) — NFWG (Liles, Burke, Horn, Mueller, Haley, Finegan)
  - **Construction/NEPA/Permitting** — NPS and BOR (Haley, Davison)
- The overarching program reflects a **recovery strategy for an imperiled Colorado River catostomid**, likely the [[Razorback Sucker]], integrating captive propagation, backwater nursery rearing, exotic species control, and reservoir infrastructure modification — a model relevant to broader [[Colorado River Basin Native Fish Conservation]] efforts.
