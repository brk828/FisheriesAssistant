# Virgin Spinedace 90-Day Petition Finding

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepidomeda

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of 1993 regulatory materials from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Service) concerning a petition to list the [[Virgin Spinedace]] as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The Service issued a 90-day finding, published in the [[Federal Register]] on March 16, 1993, concluding that the petitioners presented substantial information indicating that listing "may be warranted." This positive finding triggered a [[Status Review]]. The petitioners also asked that [[Critical Habitat]] be designated. Public comments were requested by June 14, 1993, for use in the 12-month finding. A supporting letter from a fish researcher at [[Arizona State University]] is included.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Virgin spinedace (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*), a freshwater fish of the [[Virgin River]] system.
- **Petition:** Dated June 15, 1992, and received by the Service on July 13, 1992. The petitioner was the President of the Bonneville Chapter of the [[American Fisheries Society]]. The petition asked for endangered listing throughout the species' range and for designation of critical habitat.
- **Scope of petitions:** The Service announced a finding on two petitions to list the species.
- **Information submitted:** Petitioners provided information on the fish's biology, distribution, and threats.
- **Legal basis:** Section 4(b)(3)(A) of the Act requires a finding on whether a petition presents substantial scientific or commercial information indicating a listing action may be warranted. The finding is due within 90 days of receipt. If positive, the Service must promptly begin a status review.
- **Date of finding:** March 1, 1993, as stated in the notice.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document gives limited ecological detail. Its main environmental content is:

- The species is a native fish of the Virgin River fauna, which the supporting letter describes as the researcher's long-term field focus.
- The letter characterizes the species' status as "perilous and declining."
- The species is described as sharing similar status with other southwestern fishes, which the letter's author has studied.
- The petitioned action concerns the species throughout its range. Specific range boundaries, population counts, habitat measurements, and threat details are not given in the text provided.

## Key Findings

1. **Positive 90-day finding:** The Service determined that the petitioners' information was substantial enough to indicate that listing the Virgin spinedace as endangered "may be warranted."
2. **Status review initiated:** A positive finding requires the Service to begin a review of the species' status. A 12-month finding will follow.
3. **Public comment period:** Comments, biological data, and questions were accepted at the Service's Utah Field Office (Salt Lake City) until June 14, 1993. Robert D. Williams, State Supervisor, was the designated contact.
4. **Critical habitat request:** The petitioners asked that critical habitat be designated along with the listing.
5. **Expert support:** In a letter dated April 6, 1993, Paul C. Marsh, Research Professor at the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies, stated that available data "confirm the perilous and declining status" of the species. He stated that this justifies federal listing as endangered with critical habitat, and he expressed support for the Service's proposal.
6. **Documents available:** The petition, finding, and supporting data were available for public inspection by appointment during business hours.
