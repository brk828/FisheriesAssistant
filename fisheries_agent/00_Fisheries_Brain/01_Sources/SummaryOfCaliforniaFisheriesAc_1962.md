# Summary of California Fisheries Activities Along the Colorado River for 1962

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Moronidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #Palaemonidae

## Executive Summary
This 1962 California Department of Fish and Game report documents the resumption of full-time biological work on the [[Colorado River]] after a 10-year hiatus, coordinated with Arizona and the Bureau of Sport Fishery and Wildlife. Key activities included: (1) continued stocking of catchable [[Rainbow Trout]]; (2) an experimental introduction of [[Striped Bass]] into [[Lake Havasu]]; (3) monitoring of a 1958 [[Freshwater Shrimp]] (*Palaemonetes paludosus*) introduction intended as a forage organism, including new transplant efforts; (4) initial fall population sampling trials using multiple gear types; (5) creel census surveys across five river stations; and (6) a cooperative [[Channel Catfish]] stocking and marking study on the [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]] to evaluate artificial population enhancement and migration patterns.

## Background & Description
- **Rainbow Trout (catchable stock):** Stocked as legal-size "catchables"; six plantings totaling 12,555 fish (2,750 lbs) between February and November 1962, from Topock Bridge to just above Needles.
- **Striped Bass:** Two size classes introduced—
  - Fingerlings: ~2 inches in length (38,000 total planted: 17,000 + 21,000)
  - Yearlings: ~7 inches in length (225 total planted: 200 + 25)
  - Largest wild-caught striped bass reported from the lower river to date: **19.5 lbs**, though no evidence of natural reproduction was observed.
- **Freshwater Shrimp (*Palaemonetes paludosus*):** Transplanted individuals ranged **12–38 mm** in length (sampled January 1963 from Imperial Bait Camp Pond).
- **Largemouth Bass (predation samples):** Two individuals examined for shrimp predation measured **7.4 inches** and **4.0 inches**, respectively.
- **Channel Catfish:** Fingerlings planted (size not specified beyond "fingerlings"); a subset of 5,020 California-planted fish were marked via **dorsal fin removal** for identification. A planned follow-up release involved ~5,000 **10-inch** catfish for migration tracking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Colorado River]] mainstem and reservoirs** (Topock to Imperial Dam, including [[Lake Havasu]]) serve as the primary habitat matrix, encompassing river channel, backwaters, oxbows, and associated irrigation drains/canals.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** were stocked as a put-and-take fishery in the cooler upper reaches near Topock/Needles, not intended for natural reproduction.
- **[[Striped Bass]]** were introduced as a predatory game species into the Havasu reservoir system, using aerial planting methods (twin-engine Beechcraft) analogous to fingerling trout stocking; collected via the Bureau of Reclamation's Tracy Fish Screen.
- **[[Freshwater Shrimp]]** were established in isolated pond habitats (Havasu Landing pond and ponds within the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]]) as a forage base, with limited natural range expansion into the river and backwaters upstream of Imperial Dam (~1 mile). Multiple new transplant sites were established in January 1963 across resorts, marina backwaters, and drainage oxbows to broaden shrimp distribution.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** in backwater habitats (e.g., "No Name #2," a small lake ~1 mile above Imperial Dam) demonstrated direct trophic linkage to the shrimp introduction, confirming at least partial forage utilization.
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** population dynamics were studied cooperatively on the [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]], with stocking designed to assess whether artificial recruitment could sustain a fishable population, and separate tagging/marking efforts planned to elucidate movement patterns within the river-canal system.

## Key Findings
- California resumed full-time biological fieldwork on the Colorado River in 1962 after a decade-long absence, with Bob Hayden appointed as Fishery Biologist II stationed at Blythe.
- **Rainbow trout stocking:** 12,555 fish (2,750 lbs) distributed across six plantings from February–November 1962.
- **Striped bass introduction:** 38,225 total fish planted in Lake Havasu (17,200 near Topock; 21,025 near Needles) via aerial drop with minimal mortality; largest recovered wild specimen was 19.5 lbs; **no reproduction confirmed** as of report date.
- **Freshwater shrimp status:** Of four ponds stocked in 1958, viable shrimp populations persisted only in the Havasu Landing pond, two Refuge ponds, and ~1 mile of river/backwater above Imperial Dam. Predation by largemouth bass was documented in two instances (March and May 1962). In January 1963, ~800 shrimp were harvested and redistributed to five new sites to expand range.
- **Population sampling (fall 1962):** Rotenone and gill nets were most effective in Lake Havasu; rotenone alone was most effective below Palo Verde Weir. Current sampling techniques were assessed as inadequate for capturing fish concentrated in bank cover (brush/logs); scuba diving identified as a promising but currently under-resourced technique (amateur divers insufficient; professional divers recommended).
- **Creel census (Oct–Dec 1962):** Conducted at 5 stations from Havasu to below Palo Verde Weir. Catch rates were generally low, ranging from **0.16 fish/angler-hour** (Parker to Headgate Dam) to **0.33 fish/angler-hour** (below Palo Verde Weir).
- **Channel catfish study:** Cooperative effort with the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife; 404,561 fingerlings planted by the Bureau, plus 37,000 by California (5,020 marked via dorsal fin removal) to assess whether artificial stocking can establish a fishable population. Follow-up migration study planned using ~5,000 tagged 10-inch catfish.
- Cross-jurisdictional cooperation among **California, Arizona, and the Bureau of Sport Fishery and Wildlife** was central to all major 1962 initiatives, reflecting a coordinated tri-state approach to Colorado River fisheries management.
