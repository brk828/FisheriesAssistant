# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Memorandum February 1986

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #desert-fishes #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] at [[Arizona State University]] addresses progress on recovery planning for multiple endangered desert fish species in Arizona. The document announces a rescheduled meeting for April 17-18, 1986, and outlines the agenda for developing the [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]]. Key species under consideration include [[Gila Nigrescens]], [[Lepidomeda Vittata]], [[Tiaroga Cobitis]], [[Meda Fulgida]], [[Gila Robusta Seminuda]], [[Cyprinodon Macularius]], and [[Xyrauchen Texanus]]. The team faces challenges in meeting deadlines and coordinating review of technical recovery plan drafts.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] operates under the [[Department of Zoology]] at [[Arizona State University]] in Tempe, Arizona. The team is responsible for developing and reviewing recovery plans for multiple endangered desert fish species in the southwestern United States. The memorandum, dated February 20, 1986, was written by W.L. Minckley to recovery team members and consultants.

The team had missed its first deadline (January 15) for reviewing the [[Gila Nigrescens]] technical draft recovery plan, with only one set of review notes received. The primary focus is developing the [[Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan]], with a draft deadline of end of March. The plan involves coordination between multiple agencies including the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery efforts focus on desert aquatic habitats in Arizona, particularly:

- [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] near Douglas, Arizona
- [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]]
- [[Upper San Pedro River]] (BLM exchange area)
- [[Yaqui Basin]]

The memorandum mentions an [[Ichthyology]] class conducting field surveys of fishes in the upper San Pedro and aquatic habitats at Buenos Aires NWR. These surveys involve sampling "muds and waters of a series of ponds" to assess aquatic habitats and fish populations.

The document addresses the broader challenge of desert fish conservation, noting concerns about "final numbers of populations (or communities, in this case) to be required before down- or delisting" of endangered species.
