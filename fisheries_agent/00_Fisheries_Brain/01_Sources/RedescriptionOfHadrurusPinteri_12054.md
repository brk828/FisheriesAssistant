> **Note:** This source is not an ichthyological paper. It is a taxonomic redescription of a scorpion species, *Hadrurus pinteri* Stahnke (Scorpionida: Vejovidae), from Baja California, Mexico. The note below has been adapted to reflect the actual taxon and ecological content of the source.

## Executive Summary
This paper by Stanley C. Williams (1970) provides a redescription of **[[Hadrurus pinteri]]**, a large scorpion species in the family **[[Vejovidae]]**, originally described by Stahnke (1969) from only four juvenile specimens. Williams argues that adult-based redescription is necessary because juveniles differ conspicuously from adults, and the original diagnostic characters were based on unstable body ratios rather than absolute measurements. The redescription is based on 21 adult specimens (plus 36 additional specimens studied for variation) collected from volcanic habitats across Baja California Norte and Baja California Sur, Mexico. The study documents detailed morphology, sexual dimorphism, ontogenetic (juvenile vs. adult) differences, and habitat associations.

## Background & Description
- **Body size:** Adults reach a total body length of up to ~120 mm (male: 119.2 mm; female: 116.2 mm).
- **Coloration:** Carapace and mesosoma dorsum brownish black with subtle marbling; metasoma, brachium, and humerus yellowish brown; walking legs pale yellow; pedipalp fingers dark brown; aculeus black.
- **Diagnostic features (vs. related [[Hadrurus spadix]]):**
  - Pair of oval, light-colored glandular patches on dorsal vesicle surface in mature males (absent in females and juveniles).
  - Dorsal telson surface not conspicuously hirsute.
  - Metasoma melanic.
  - Middle segment of chelicerae with dusky dorsal markings.
  - More swollen pedipalp hands.
  - Tendency toward higher pectinal tooth counts.
- **Pectines:** Females 28–33 teeth (predominantly 29–30); Males 39–42 teeth (predominantly 42). Redescribed male: 42/41; redescribed female: 31/31.
- **Carapace:** Finely granular surface, irregular granule dispersion; median eyes on raised ocular tubercle; three lateral eyes per group with three long conspicuous bristles above each group.
- **Mesosoma:** Tergites 1–6 finely granular; tergite 7 coarsely granular; last sternite with two pairs of incomplete granular lateral keels and ~30 brown bristles.
- **Metasoma:** Dorsal and dorso-lateral keels represented as rows of isolated granules (not raised); inferior lateral and inferior median keels present and complete on all segments, ranging from smooth (segment I) to serrate (segments III–V).
- **Telson:** Ventral surface bulbous, densely hirsute; dorsal vesicle surface relatively bald except for lateral curvature; males possess paired light oval glandular patches (~2.5 mm) at base of aculeus.
- **Chelicerae:** Movable finger with one conspicuous tooth (0.6 mm) on inferior border.
- **Pedipalps:** Moderately swollen hands; only the inferior external keel developed, irregularly granular; movable finger slightly longer than carapace.
- **Walking legs:** Densely hirsute tibia and tarsomeres; pedal spurs well-developed, deeply serrate (4–5 serrations).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Restricted to **rocky volcanic habitats**; never found in predominantly sandy substrates or non-volcanic terrain.
- **Geographic range:** From Oakies Landing to San Miguel de Comondú, spanning Baja California Norte and Baja California Sur, Mexico, including localities such as Bahía de Los Ángeles, Bahía San Luis Gonzaga, Calamajué Arroyo, Misión San Borja, and San Ignacio.
- **Abundance:** Never collected in large numbers at any locality; low-density populations.
- **Reproduction:** Adult females collected with distended mesosomas containing late-stage embryos, indicating **ovoviviparous/viviparous reproduction** typical of scorpions.
- **Detection method:** All specimens collected via **ultraviolet (UV) fluorescence detection**, a standard method for locating scorpions at night.
- **Sex ratio:** Males and females encountered in roughly equal numbers; juveniles of both sexes well represented in samples.

## Key Findings
1. **Adult vs. juvenile disparity:** The original species description (Stahnke 1969) was based solely on juvenile instars, which differ conspicuously from adults (juveniles have uniformly bright yellow brachium, humerus, vesicle, and legs, with a contrasting dark pedipalp chela; adults show more differentiated coloration).
2. **Sexual dimorphism:** Documented dimorphism includes males having significantly more pectinal teeth (39–42 vs. 28–33 in females), a distinctive pair of dorsal vesicle gland patches (males only), and slightly longer terminal metasomal segments.
3. **No genital papillae** were found in either sex, and the genital operculum is completely divided longitudinally in both sexes — contradicting reliance on such traits for sexing in related taxa.
4. **No detectable geographic variation** in color or morphology was found across the sampled range in Baja California.
5. **Taxonomic caution:** The paper underscores the risk of describing new species based on juvenile specimens and unstable morphometric ratios rather than fixed adult characters, relevant to broader **[[Scorpion taxonomy]]** and diagnostic methodology.
6. Species is taxonomically distinguished from its close relative **[[Hadrurus spadix]]** via multiple discrete morphological characters rather than ratio-based metrics alone.
