# Fertilizing Farm Fish Ponds

#aquaculture #pond-management #nutrient-cycling

## Executive Summary

This document describes the evolution of pond fertilization methods developed by [[Auburn University]] Agricultural Experiment Station from 1934-1938 and refined through continued research. The research demonstrates that older ponds (5-15 years of fertilization history) require only phosphate fertilizer rather than complete NPK formulations, due to natural accumulation of potassium and biological nitrogen fixation. Platform application methods reduce fertilizer requirements by 20-40% compared to broadcasting methods.

## Background & Description

Original fertilization methods recommended broadcasting fertilizer over shallow water areas or pouring it in a line 15-20 ft from the shoreline. Research led to improved platform application method where fertilizer is placed on a platform approximately 1 ft underwater. Wind and waves effectively distribute nutrients throughout the pond as they dissolve from the platform. This circulation of nutrients in surface waters significantly reduced annual fertilizer requirements.

The standard complete fertilizer formulation is 8-8-2 (nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium) applied at 100 lb per acre per application. For older ponds, either 40 lb of superphosphate or 15 lb of triple superphosphate per acre per treatment may be used.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Ponds develop self-sustaining nutrient cycles after several years of fertilization. After 2 years of potash fertilization, sufficient potassium accumulates in organic and inorganic forms in waters and bottom muds to support maximum production without additional inputs.

After 3-5 years of fertilization, nitrogen-fixing bacteria in bottom muds become sufficiently abundant to supply pond nitrogen needs. These bacteria use carbohydrate energy from organic deposits to convert atmospheric nitrogen dissolved in pond water into ammonia nitrogen usable by microscopic plants. Certain microscopic algae also become more abundant and use sunlight energy to convert atmospheric nitrogen to ammonia.

However, nitrogen fixation proceeds slowly in cold water. Ponds receiving only phosphate in February and March may require longer to produce necessary ammonia for rapid increase in microscopic plants and fish feed organisms compared to ponds receiving nitrogen and phosphate. Slow development of microscopic plants allows water to remain clear and [[Pond Weeds]] to begin growing.

## Key Findings

- Platform application method reduces annual fertilizer requirements by 20-40% compared to broadcasting methods
- A single platform can fertilize up to 15 acres of water
- Ponds receiving potash fertilization for 2+ years no longer need additional potassium
- After 3-5 years of fertilization, sufficient nitrogen is fixed annually within ponds through bacterial and algal processes, eliminating need for nitrogen fertilizer additions
- After 5-15 years of prior fertilization, ponds require only phosphate; nitrogen and potassium can be omitted
- Phosphate fertilization costs approximately 80% as much as complete 8-8-2 or 20-20-5 fertilizer
- Even after 15 years of phosphate fertilization, fish production declined when phosphate was omitted, demonstrating continued necessity
- Phosphate-only fertilization should only be used in ponds where [[Pond Weeds]] have been eliminated by systematic fertilization with 8-8-2 in prior years
- For phosphate fertilization: first application February 1, followed by two additional applications at 2-week intervals; subsequent applications when water clarity exceeds 18 inches depth visibility
- New ponds or ponds requiring weed control should use 8-8-2 fertilizer at 100 lb per acre per application
- Fertilization timing and frequency based on microscopic green growth visibility (water clarity at 18 inches depth)
