# Yaqui Chub and Yaqui Topminnow Subpermit Renewal and Annual Report, San Bernardino NWR (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This two-page memorandum from the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] Refuge Manager to the Technical Services Division of the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Region 2) does two things. It requests renewal of endangered species subpermit PRT-676811 under unchanged conditions, and it reports on 1992 activities for the endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] and [[Yaqui Topminnow]]. Refuge work focused on protecting, maintaining and creating habitat for these fishes. Staff ran twice-yearly status surveys, reported that all populations were doing well and reproducing, restocked suitable habitat, and assessed areas for possible reintroduction. An attached stocking table records fish movements, though much of it is illegible after scanning.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum with an enclosed annual report, dated November 24, 1992. It was received by the regional office on January 28, 1993.
- **Author:** Kevin S. Cobble, Refuge Manager.
- **Permit:** [[Subpermit PRT-676811]], an endangered species subpermit under which the refuge works on the Yaqui fishes.
- **Target species:**
  - [[Yaqui Chub]], an endangered cyprinid fish. Refuge fish are also held in aquaria at the refuge office for research, propagation and public information.
  - [[Yaqui Topminnow]], an endangered fish.
- **Other species mentioned:** [[Beautiful Shiner]] and an unspecified "Shiner," both appearing in stocking records. The memo does not assign them any management role.

The report notes that refuge staff monitored populations, restocked fish into suitable habitat, and worked to protect that habitat from harmful activities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Survey waters.** Populations were checked in the following refuge waters:
- [[Black Draw]]
- [[House Pond]]
- North, Twin, Oasis and Mesquite ponds
- Robertson Cienega
- Well outflows and cienegas associated with the Bunting, Cottonwood, Middle, Cienega and Bathouse artesian wells

Surveys were also conducted on Leslie Creek and Turkey Creek.

**Habitat management.** Chub and topminnow were seined so that suitable refuge habitat could be stocked and populations increased. The refuge also assessed areas for possible reintroduction in the San Bernardino and Sulphur Springs Valleys. The memo does not report water chemistry, habitat measurements, or non-fish taxa.

## Key Findings

**1. Survey methods and schedule**
- Status surveys were run on all refuge waters to determine presence and relative abundance of chub and topminnow.
- Methods: [[Seine Netting]], [[Minnow Traps]] and [[Electrofishing]].
- Surveys were conducted twice annually, in summer and winter, to monitor populations.
- All captured fish were released unharmed.

**2. Population status**
- All chub and topminnow populations were reported to be "doing well and reproducing."
- The memo gives no counts or abundance estimates for surveys, so no quantitative population figures can be reported.

**3. Propagation and stocking**
- Fish were seined to stock suitable refuge habitat and boost populations.
- The attached stocking table (partly legible after OCR) lists roughly a dozen records. Entries cover [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Beautiful Shiner]] and unspecified shiners. Recorded amounts include 10, 23, 30, 47, 49, 50, 80, 101, 153, 200, 212, 300 and 377 fish.
- Stocking destinations include [[House Pond]], [[Gamma Ponds]], Mesquite Pond, Robertson Cienega, North Pond, Cottonwood spring and Bathouse. Source stock was taken from locations including [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Middle spring and the Bathouse well. Some rows are too degraded to read reliably, so counts should be verified against the original scan.

**4. Habitat protection and restoration**
- Staff worked to protect habitat from harmful activities.
- Habitat was maintained and created on the refuge for these fishes.

**5. Reintroduction assessment**
- Areas in the [[San Bernardino Valley]] and [[Sulphur Springs Valley]] were assessed as possible reintroduction sites for Yaqui chub and topminnow. The memo does not state any outcome.

**6. Requests and conclusions**
- Renewal of [[Subpermit PRT-676811]] was requested with permit conditions unchanged from the previous year.
- The refuge offered to supply further information needed to meet the subpermit terms.
