# Central Arizona Project Fish Monitoring Oversight: Field Notes, Proposals and Agreements (1998–1999)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish-monitoring

## Executive Summary

This document is a compiled file of field notes, scheduling correspondence, a proposal routing form, a cover sheet, an annual report letter and a cooperative agreement modification relating to fish and habitat monitoring under the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP). Arizona State University (ASU) personnel, led by [[Paul C. Marsh]], provided field quality-control oversight to fish monitoring conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) on the San Pedro, Gila and Salt rivers during October–November 1998 and October 1999. The work was funded by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR) under cooperative agreement 1425-97-FC-32-00780, modified (Modification 002) to add oversight and reporting, increasing the total agreement value by $12,018 to $88,794.

## Background & Description

The field notes record the sampling routine at numbered stations (for example Sta 1-1-1 at Hereford, Sta 1-3-3 at Asarco Mouth on the San Pedro; Sta 2-3-1 at Asarco on the Gila) and document access logistics: gate keys, private-land permission, and GPS coordinates recorded with a Rockwell unit (typical error ±4 to ±11 m). Several sites lacked the aluminum location tags used for relocation, and station locations were sometimes uncertain.

Sampling methods named in the documents include [[Electrofishing]], seining, dip netting, entanglement (trammel) netting, and preservation of voucher specimens in 10% formalin, later transferred to 70% ethanol. Habitat data included stream width and depth, substrate and habitat type.

Other administrative material includes:
- An ASU proposal routing form (Form 204-01A.1) with checkboxes for human subjects, animal use, radiation and biosafety (all marked no).
- A 1999 cover e-mail from [[Paul C. Marsh]] estimating a "conservative first draft" cost of $11,964 for oversight in the first year, based on 200 hours of salary, travel at a university rate, and per diem.
- A list of duties for a contracted technician: field collection over up to four consecutive nights, processing voucher specimens, data entry, equipment maintenance, a photographic slide inventory system and statistical reporting.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The monitoring concerns fish populations affected by delivery of Central Arizona Project water to the Gila River basin, under the 1994 [[Biological Opinion]] referenced in the modification. The 1996 "Long-term Monitoring Plan for Fish Populations in Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, Arizona" is the stated protocol reference. Sites include dam-to-canal surveys during drawdowns for maintenance on the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]], [[Arizona Canal]] and [[South Canal]] (1998), and river reaches on the San Pedro (including Aravaipa Creek confluence and TNC Reserve land) and Gila rivers, with mesquite bosque and sugar-sand banks noted as access difficulties.

Water conditions noted include low water and drawdowns, with sampling schedules dependent on flows and on the annual maintenance shutdown of the canal system.

## Key Findings

- **Oversight quality concerns (1998 and 1999 reports):** Area coverage of sampling was highly variable and poorly estimated (form 2, variable 20). Electrofishing technique varied strongly among users, so data may not be comparable among samples and sites. Both observations were repeated in 1999.
- **Improvement noted in 1999:** Daily routine covered two or three sites per day, compared with one or two in 1998. Core crews camping near study areas started earlier and worked later.
- **Recommendations:** (1) an annual training session with on-the-ground demonstrations of each technique, with habitat, substrate and fish-encounter examples; (2) use of seining, dip netting and entanglement netting in addition to electrofishing; (3) encouragement of standardized methods across crews; (4) use of the comments section on field data forms and sketch maps; (5) maintenance of field notes; (6) immediate formalin fixation of voucher specimens in suitably sized containers to avoid crowding; (7) crews remaining in the field to reduce travel time.
- **Capacity estimate:** Under typical conditions (low water, cooperative weather, minimum crew size), the 1998 report considered at least three sites per day feasible.
- **Access and permissions:** Several sites required written owner permission (for example c-Spear Ranch, where the manager denied access in 1999), and the A-Diamond Ranch site was not sampled in 1998 pending owner contact.
- **Funding and scope:** Modification 002 adds oversight at a minimum of 10 and maximum of 15 sampling stations, plus a comprehensive Fiscal Year 1999 report on age-0 and age-1+ fish captured per station, due to Reclamation within 60 days after data delivery (final report due by July 1, 1999).
- **Annual report (21 December 1998):** ASU reported surveys during canal drawdowns on the Florence-Casa Grande Canal, Arizona Canal and South Canal, with fish captured by dip net, electroshocker or seine, identified to species and life stage, and entered into KEy3 software.
- **Budget:** The 1999 oversight request totaled $12,018 (salaries and benefits $8,595; travel 1,700 miles at $0.325/mile = $553; per diem 15 days at $26 = $390; indirect costs at 26% = $2,480).
