# Paul Colburn Marsh: Curriculum Vitae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the curriculum vitae of [[Paul Colburn Marsh]], a fisheries ecologist and research professor at [[Arizona State University]]'s Center for Environmental Studies. It covers his education, employment, professional memberships, teaching, grants, service, publications, presentations, and technical reports from 1971 through 1996. His work centers on the conservation of endangered and native fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]] and the Southwest, the ecology of irrigation canals, and the impacts of [[Non-Native Fishes]] on native communities.

## Background & Description

**Education**
- B.A., Life Sciences, University of Connecticut, 1971
- M.S., Natural Resources Conservation, University of Connecticut, 1974 (thesis on fishes of the Thames River Estuary)
- Ph.D., Fisheries, University of Minnesota, 1979 (thesis on silt and turbidity effects on benthic invertebrates)

**Career progression**
- Quality Control Chemist, Chas. Pfizer and Company (1971–72)
- Research Associate, University of Minnesota (1978–79)
- Fisheries Specialist, Minnesota Pollution Control Agency (1979–80)
- Research Ecologist, Arizona State University (1980–81)
- Assistant, Associate, and then Professor of Research at Arizona State University (1981–present), with affiliated and adjunct zoology appointments

**Professional affiliations and certifications**
- Certified Fisheries Scientist, [[American Fisheries Society]]
- Member of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], where he served as co-editor of the Proceedings, Coordinator of the Lower Colorado River Area, and Chair of the Membership Committee
- Memberships in the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, North American Benthological Society, Ecological Society of America, and Society for Conservation Biology, among others

**Teaching:** Fishery Management, Aquatic Entomology, Fishery Ecology of Polluted Waters, Techniques in Fishery Biology, and Ecology of Fishery Populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research record links several recurring themes:

- **Endangered native fishes:** Long-term work on [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]], covering population estimates, movements, early life history, larval ecology, and genetics.
- **Reservoir and river systems:** Studies at [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]], the [[Gila River]], the [[Little Colorado River]], and [[Grand Canyon]] on abundance, predation, and management of dam operations for native fishes.
- **Irrigation canals as ecosystems:** Work on the [[Coachella Canal]] and Phoenix-area canals on [[Benthic Invertebrates]], fish communities, and [[Ecosystem Metabolism]].
- **Non-native species:** Research on predation by introduced [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Grass Carp]], and red shiner on native fishes, and on the replacement and displacement hypothesis. This is linked to [[Exotic Species]] policy.
- **Genetics and captive propagation:** Mitochondrial DNA studies of razorback sucker hatchery stocks and hybridization in *Gila seminuda*.
- **Temperature and embryo survival:** Experiments on how incubation temperature affects hatchability of native Colorado River fishes.

## Key Findings

**Major funded research (1982–1999)**
- National Biological Service, research and management of native fishes of the lower Colorado River basin (1995–99)
- National Biological Service, sonic telemetry of adult and juvenile razorback suckers in Lake Mohave (1995–97)
- U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, ecology and conservation of humpback chub in the Little Colorado River (1991–96)
- U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, survival and growth of juvenile razorback suckers in an isolated Lake Mohave backwater (1991)
- U.S. Forest Service, native fishes of the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests (1994–96) and factors affecting montane trout (1988–90)
- Arizona Game and Fish Department, spikedace and loach minnow studies, flathead catfish commercial potential, and razorback sucker in the upper Verde, Salt, and Gila rivers
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, early life history of razorback sucker and temperature effects on native fishes

**Ongoing applied programs**
- Native fishes in Palm Lake, Hassayampa River Preserve (since 1993)
- Endangered species dynamics at Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge and the ASU Research Park (since 1986)

**Recovery and advisory roles**
- Consultant to the Colorado River Fishes, Desert Fishes, and Virgin River Fishes Recovery Teams (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service)
- Author of recovery plans for loach minnow and spikedace, and a recovery action plan draft for desert pupfish
- Member of the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Aquatic Coordination Team and the Lower Colorado River Endangered Species Work Group

**Publication record (1980–1996)**
- A long record of journal articles, book chapters, edited volumes, species accounts, and technical reports, including the Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council.
- Notable papers include the genetic origin of *Gila seminuda* (*PNAS*, 1992), mitochondrial DNA diversity in razorback sucker (*Copeia*, 1996; *Conservation Biology*, 1996), and predation on humpback chub by introduced fishes (*Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*, in press).
- Technical reports include the *Aquatic Resources of the Yuma Division, Lower Colorado River* (222 pp., with a Phase II report of 200 pp.) and the *Commercial Potential of Flathead Catfish* (111 pp.).

**Presentations**
- Over 60 abstracts and talks at the Desert Fishes Council, American Fisheries Society chapter and division meetings, American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, and North American Benthological Society meetings from 1979 to 1996.

**Service**
- Peer reviewer for more than ten journals, including *Copeia*, *Conservation Biology*, and *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*
- Associate Editor for Fishes, *Great Basin Naturalist*, and member of the Editorial Review Board, *Freshwater Invertebrate Biology*
- Chair of the Exotic Species Committee, Arizona-New Mexico Chapter of the American Fisheries Society (1984, 1986)
