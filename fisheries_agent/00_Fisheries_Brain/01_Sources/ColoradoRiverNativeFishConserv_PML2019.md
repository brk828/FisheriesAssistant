# Colorado River Native Fish Conservation Compilation, 2009–2010

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation gathers 2009–2010 reports and meeting materials on conservation of native fishes in the [[Colorado River]] basin. It covers five areas: a native fish sanctuary and sampling at [[Davis Cove]] in [[Lake Mohave]]; conservation of the "Three Species" (roundtail chub, flannelmouth sucker and bluehead sucker) in Wyoming; the Lower Virgin River monitoring and tilapia work plan; production at the [[Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility]]; and a keynote on the conflict between native and non-native fishes. Administrative material (database notes, hotel and meeting logistics) is also included.

## Background & Description

The document is a set of loosely linked items, not a single study:

- **Davis Cove sampling report (Nov. 2009):** Davis Cove has functioned as a native fish sanctuary since 2005. Agencies agreed to shift its role from a sanctuary pond toward a source of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] for stocking other waters.
- **Wyoming Three Species reports (2009):** A Wyoming Game and Fish Department (WGFD) summary of conservation activity across ten management categories, with contributions from the BLM, USGS, USFWS and universities.
- **Colorado River Aquatic Biologists (CRAB) meeting materials (Jan. 2010):** Agenda, presentation order and logistics for the Laughlin, Nevada meeting.
- **Virgin River work plan (2010):** Monitoring reaches and tilapia management, with a recovery implementation team agenda.
- **Achii Hanyo facility summary (2009):** Pond rearing of bonytail and razorback suckers.
- **Native Fish Work Group (NFWG) materials:** Database comments, Lake Mohave work group agenda, helicopter survey schedule, and stocking tables for [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and other sites.
- **Keynote on native vs. non-native fishes:** A talk on the decline of native fishes and the conflicts with sport fisheries.
- **Logistics:** Hotel confirmation and group agreement for the CRAB meeting.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Davis Cove and Lake Mohave.** Most razorbacks were caught where the [[Spiny Naiad]] was thickest. Fish were noted as "skinny," which the authors attributed to high fish density and competition for food. Multiple trammel nets of different mesh sizes were used to improve size representation. No non-native fish were observed in the 2009 sampling.

**Wyoming Three Species habitat.** Threats include hybridization with white suckers (identified as the leading contributor to flannelmouth and bluehead decline), barriers to fish passage, low flows, water withdrawals, and proposed oil and gas produced-water discharge into [[Muddy Creek]]. Baseline selenium and trace-element data are being collected for water, sediment, macroinvertebrates and fish tissue.

**Virgin River.** [[Tilapia]] occurrence in Nevada reaches is a main management concern, and the recovery team is planning barrier and treatment options.

**Achii Hanyo ponds.** Water is supplied by an irrigation canal carrying Colorado River water. Pond temperatures can reach 35 °C, and dissolved oxygen is a concern. The authors identify heat-related mortality as a possible major cause of losses.

## Key Findings

### Davis Cove (November 2009)

- Sampling was conducted by NPS, USBR and AZGFD representatives. Fish were captured with trammel nets, which were set for one hour.
- Razorbacks over 450 mm were released into Lake Mohave, averaging 466 mm.
- 175 razorbacks measuring 295–445 mm were returned to the backwater, averaging 353 mm.
- 13 young-of-year razorbacks averaged 104 mm. One had a right pelvic fin clip from earlier sampling.
- Bonytail over 250 mm received PIT tags, and smaller bonytail received right dorsal wire tags.
- A population estimate of about 373 adult fish (OCR-garbled, confidence interval not legible) and a young-of-year estimate of about 1,091 fish were reported.
- The report recommends continued sampling through winter and spring, with removal of some fish over 350 mm to reduce competition for food.

### Wyoming Three Species Conservation (2009)

- **Status and research:** A WGFD report summarizes native fish surveys from 2002–2006. Stomachs from over 200 lake trout (2008–2009) and about 50 brown trout (2009) are being analyzed for roundtail chub in their diets.
- **Muddy Creek baseline:** Samples were collected for selenium and other inorganic elements, with a long-term monitoring proposal in draft.
- **Database:** All data since 2002 are spatially referenced with GPS. The Utah Division of Wildlife Resources is leading development of a standardized database.
- **Genetics:** A University of Wyoming Master's thesis covers hybridization of bluehead, flannelmouth and white suckers. The cited paper is Quist et al. (2009), *North American Journal of Fisheries Management* 29:460–467.
- **Habitat and passage:**
  - Funds were awarded to Trout Unlimited for a culvert removal project.
  - BLM inventoried stream crossings in the Kemmerer, Pinedale and Rock Springs field offices, with 99% of fish-bearing crossings assessed as of October 2009.
  - In the Rawlins Field Office, 44 culverts were identified; 8 were in Three Species habitat and 6 were barriers.
  - BLM repaired and maintained 75 riparian exclosures in the Rock Springs field office and built 12 new exclosures.
- **Non-native control:**
  - About $350,000 in governor-directed funds supported large-scale white sucker removal in 2008.
  - Weirs and electrofishing were used in the Little Sandy, Big Sandy, Bitter and Muddy Creek watersheds in 2009.
  - BLM removed 332 white suckers and 234 creek chubs from Three Species habitat on Upper Muddy Creek.
  - Chemical renovation of three subdrainages is under consideration. Holding options for native fish during treatment are being evaluated, and a 2010 pilot study is planned.
- **Expanded distribution:** About 125 roundtail chub were moved from Little Halfmoon Lake to Scab Lake to create a lentic population free of trout predation.
- **Monitoring:**
  - Upper Muddy Creek flannelmouth population is very low (about 100 individuals), with all sampled fish from a single year class. Strategic sampling produced higher catch rates than random sampling.
  - BLM established 13 long-term monitoring sites on Lower Muddy Creek, to be re-sampled every three years.
  - A roundtail chub on Dry Cow Creek was the first BLM record of the species that far above the confluence.
- **Regulation:** Live bait is banned in all Three Species drainages. Bluehead sucker cannot be used as bait in the Snake River drainage. Under the Upper Colorado River Basin stocking agreement, Wyoming requires permits for stocking and hatchery operations. HACCP is required for transplants.
- **Management plan:** A draft five-year Three Species plan covers five Green River watersheds. The BLM Rawlins RMP (approved December 2008) designates about 60,000 acres of the Upper Muddy Creek/Muddy Creek area as a Wildlife Habitat Management Area.

### CRAB Meeting Agenda (January 13–14, 2010)

- Presentations were arranged by river reach, from Dam to Lake Havasu through Lake Mead and Lake Powell.
- Topics included the Lake Mead non-native fish removal work and humpback chub in the Grand Canyon.
- The meeting was held at the Aquarius Resort in Laughlin, Nevada.

### Virgin River 2010 Work Plan

- Monitoring of Long-Term reaches (Beaver Dam Wash to Riverside, RM 49.5) and gorge sites (RM 87.75 and RM 85.25) with spring and fall trips.
- Tilapia monitoring at Experimental Wash (RM 44.25) with two trips from July to September.
- Three new reaches were added (BLM).
- The entire Lower Virgin River from the new gorge barrier site to Halfway Wash will be sampled in 2010.
- The recovery team agenda covers tilapia eradication options, fish barriers, NEPA status and a draft action plan.

### Virgin River Fall 2009 Sampling

- Beaver Dam Wash: 587 red shiners, 30 mosquitofish, 7 flannelmouth suckers, 6 desert suckers, 2 Virgin chub, and 2 largemouth bass.
- Cedar Pockets: 153 red shiners, 95 mosquitofish, 14 flannelmouth suckers, 2 desert suckers, and 2 Virgin chub.
- Mean lengths: Virgin chub 141.5 mm (Beaver Dam Wash) and 205 mm (Cedar Pockets).

### Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility (2009)

- The facility is an extensive earthen pond operation in Parker, Arizona, with ponds ranging from about 0.1 to 1.1 acres.
- Stocking densities were below 250 lbs/acre-foot, with some polyculture.
- Harvest densities ranged from 58 to 1,015 lbs/acre-foot.
- Young bonytail recruitment was reported at about 35,000 fish, compared with about 5,000 in the previous year.
- Survival was poor in ponds 3, 4 and B. Pond B was stocked with 4,860 young-of-year bonytail (2007 broodstock).
- Overall, 4,000 bonytail over 300 mm were delivered, meeting the commitment.
- Planned improvements include renovating at least four ponds, a new water pump, and better water filtration to keep out rough fish.

### Native Fish Work Group and Stocking Records

- Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery requested 30,000 razorback larvae for future stocking.
- Razorbacks stocked in January 2010 totaled 4,065, with more planned for the Roundup.
- Stocking tables list dozens of releases of razorbacks and bonytail across Lake Mohave, Lake Havasu and reaches of the Colorado River.
- The 2005–2009 MSCP fish augmentation program recorded numbers stocked by source (Bubbling Ponds, Willow Beach, Achii Hanyo, Dexter and backwaters). The source tables are partly OCR-garbled, so exact totals are not reliable.
- The NFWG database note states that the primary database is not electronically linked to the online version. Backwater records are moved when fish are released to the lake, and some tags return no data or unlinked data.

### Keynote on Native and Non-Native Fishes

- About 40 native fish species were recorded in the basin roughly 150 years ago. Most introductions began in the mid-to-late 1800s, with a surge from the 1930s into the 1950s.
- Nearly 100 non-native fish introductions are documented. About twice as many non-native fishes now occur in the system as native fishes.
- The keynote states that most introduced sport fishes are predators, that predation on early life stages is the typical mechanism of native decline, and that [[Red Shiner]] has been implicated despite its small size.
- Native and non-native fishes are described as incompatible, with managers facing funding conflicts between sport and native fish programs.
