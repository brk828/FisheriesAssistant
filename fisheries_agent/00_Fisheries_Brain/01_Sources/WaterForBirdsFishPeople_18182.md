# Water for Birds, Fish and People: A Devils Lake Inlet-Outlet Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae #Percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1995 policy document by [[Gordon Berg]], chairman of the [[Devils Lake-Sheyenne-Red River Advisory Committee]], argues that [[Devils Lake]] water management must include both an inlet and an outlet, not an outlet alone. Berg contends that an outlet-only plan will concentrate salts, risk a [[Fish Kill]], and fail to satisfy downstream cities or Canada. He proposes a "comprehensive plan" centred on a dam and channel on the [[Sheyenne River]], a [[Missouri River]] water inlet treated with [[Ozone Treatment]] to remove biota, and inlets and outlets for both Devils Lake and [[Stump Lake]]. The plan is framed as serving migratory birds, fish and people at a total estimated cost of $76 million.

The fish-relevant core of the document is the [[Biota Transfer Problem]]: Canada's objection to non-native fish and fish diseases entering the [[Hudson Bay Drainage]], and North Dakota's history of introducing non-native species such as [[Striped Bass]] and [[Zander]].

## Background & Description

The document is a position paper and news release rather than a research study. It combines historical notes, water-quality sampling, hydrological figures and a cost estimate. Its author presents the plan as a response to the [[Boundary Waters Treaty of 1909]], which Berg says sets a limit of 500 mg/L total dissolved solids (TDS) for water crossing into Canada.

Key historical points cited:
- The Red River Basin was once under an ocean, leaving salt in the soil that rains and high water tables leach into runoff and lakes.
- Early reports put Devils Lake at 1453 to 1466 ft (Roche, 1820s) and 1476 ft (Nicollet, about 1830). Ancient beaches suggest the lake was at least 20 feet higher than present and has probably overflowed into the Sheyenne and Red Rivers.
- A mid-1820s overflow reportedly carried 2 million acre-feet of water and 2 million tons of TDS to Hudson Bay.
- In 1889, at an elevation of about 1430 ft, the lake was too saline to support fish. Creel Bay tested above 8,500 TDS around 1900.
- During the 1930s drought the lake dried, and wind deposited salt on surrounding farmland. Fish did not return until 1950. Current TDS is about 1,800.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Salinity gradient.** Water quality samples taken by the ND Department of Health on May 15, 1995 show strong bay-to-bay variation:

| Site (depth) | Sulfates (mg/L) | TDS (mg/L) |
|---|---|---|
| West Bay (0.5 m) | 397 | 1000 |
| Six Mile Bay (0.5 m) | 461 | 1070 |
| Creel Bay (0.5 m) | 823 | 1780 |
| Main Bay (0.5 m) | 870 | 1840 |
| East Bay (0.5 m) | 1580 | 3270 |
| East Devils Lake (0.5 m) | 3770 | 7570 |

**Inflows.** USGS data for Water Year 1993 list TDS for 11 inflowing coulees and channels ranging from 329 to 593 mg/L, with an average of 436 mg/L. The author notes this is comparable to Missouri River water, which carries 420 to 600 mg/L.

**Flow regime.** The Sheyenne River averaged about 40,000 acre-feet flowing past annually over the previous 35 years. Flows were about 150,000 acre-feet in the extreme high year 1950 and about 6,000 acre-feet in the low year 1961. The Red River carries about 10,000 cfs at TDS near 350 during a roughly 35-day spring window, rising to 40,000 cfs further north.

**Fish tolerance.** The document states that fish will reproduce in water under 800 TDS. The proposed freshening target for Devils Lake is about 500 TDS.

**Biota.** The Devils Lake Basin drains to Hudson Bay and the Missouri River Basin drains to the Gulf of Mexico, and each has distinct biota. Striped bass do not occur naturally in the Hudson Bay drainage. Canadian concerns include displacement of native fish in [[Lake Winnipeg]] and [[Lake Manitoba]], threats to commercial fisheries, and disease transmission. A [[Fish Screen at McClusky]] already exists to block Missouri River fish and eggs from entering the Hudson Bay watershed.

**Introduced species history.** The ND Game and Fish Department introduced non-native fish despite Canadian protests. Striped bass were introduced to Devils Lake in 1977. The author reports they cannot reproduce in a mud-bottomed lake and that none remain. Zander introduction also failed; further stocking was halted by Governor Sinner, and Governor Schafer continued the ban.

## Key Findings

**1. Outlet-only plans are rejected as inadequate.** The author argues that outlets alone concentrate salts, leave the poorest water in the lake, and do not provide flood control for Fargo, Valley City or Grand Forks.

**2. Dam and channel on the Sheyenne River.** The plan centres on a dam on the Sheyenne River with its crest at 1440 MSL, backing water roughly 14 miles. A channel of about 9 miles (excavation on about 7 miles, maximum cut 60 to 70 feet) would carry about 1,500 cfs in either direction without pumps. The Devils Lake Sioux Tribe is reported to favour this route.

**3. Flood control and drought supply.** Water diverted in spring is intended to save about $2 million in annual flood damages, and Sheyenne water is intended to reduce the need for treated Missouri River water, saving about $1 million per year in electrical costs.

**4. Stump Lake freshening.** Raising East Stump Lake to about 1415 MSL would reduce its TDS to permit releases to the Goose River. The document projects Stump Lake TDS falling from about 7,000 to 2,000 mg/L in 10 years, with releases of about 20,000 acre-feet per year.

**5. Ozone-treated Missouri River inlet.** A 300 cfs treatment plant at [[McClusky]] would sterilise Missouri River water for biota: 100 cfs for eastern North Dakota and western Minnesota municipal supplies and 200 cfs for migratory birds and the Lonetree Wildlife Management Area. The author believes this would remove Canada's objection.

**6. Legislative safeguard.** Berg recommends a state law barring introduction of non-native fish unless all bordering states and provinces agree, or requiring legislative approval for any introduction.

**7. Disease reduction.** The author states that maintaining water quality and levels in drought would reduce bird diseases such as botulism and Newcastle's disease.

**8. Cost.** The total project is estimated at $76 million, about one-tenth of the [[Garrison Diversion Project]] estimate, but excludes its irrigation component. Breakdown:

| Feature | Estimated Cost (millions) |
|---|---|
| Ozone treatment plant at McClusky | $30 |
| Devils Lake–Sheyenne channel | 11 |
| Devils Lake–Stump Lake channel | 3 |
| Dam and control structure, Sheyenne River | 20 |
| Property, engineering, electric line | 10 |
| 300 cfs pump and 12 miles of pipe to Goose River | 2 |
| **Total** | **76** |

**9. Institutional roles.** The author proposes that the [[US Army Corps of Engineers]] build and operate the inlets and outlets, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] build the ozone plant, and the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] manage the Lonetree Wildlife Management Area. He also proposes tying the Mayville municipal system to the Traill County Water System.

**10. Timing.** The author argues that federal emergency funding is available now for an inlet, but would be unlikely once an outlet is built alone, so the two should be pursued together.
