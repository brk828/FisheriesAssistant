# Population Estimate Methodology Correspondence for Native Fishes Database (2009)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a 2009 email correspondence, with attached reference list and scan-damaged annotations, between [[Native Fish Lab]] staff (Marsh & Associates LLC, Tempe, Arizona) and a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) staff member, Melissa Mata. Mata had questioned population estimates in the native fishes database. The lab's reply, from Paul Marsh with Carol Pacey and Brian Kesner, explains the [[Chapman Modification of the Petersen Method]] used for adult and repatriate population estimates in the [[Razorback Sucker]] program in [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]]. It also states that database numbers may change as errors are corrected, and that methods have been refined over years.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** Carol Pacey forwarded Mata's email of 12 August 2009 to Paul Marsh and Brian Kesner. Marsh replied on 17 August 2009 on behalf of the team. A later page, dated 17 December 2009, contains a forwarded copy of the exchange.
- **Focal taxon:** The reference list cites Marsh et al. (2003), "Decline of the Razorback Sucker in Lake Mohave, Colorado River, Arizona and Nevada," and Marsh et al. (2005), on [[Repatriation]] as a management strategy for imperiled fish species.
- **Data reporting:** The lab distributes reports annually to interested parties, including FWS, through the [[Lower Colorado River Fishes]] list serve. Findings are also presented at the annual [[Native Fishes Work Group]] meeting.
- **Reviewer annotation:** A margin comment by Brian Kesner notes that most fish in the "mark" sample were already marked (PIT tagged) before the marking sample. The annotation says this point needs further discussion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The text covers mark-recapture population estimation for native fishes in a regulated Colorado River reservoir system. Its main concerns are:

- **Sampling design:** Estimates use data from consecutive years, the current year and the prior marking year. Most applications use only March data. Same-trip recaptures are excluded so that sampling is without duplication.
- **Stocking effects:** Since 2005, fish stocked after the prior-year marking event have been removed from the resample count (C). The stated reason is that these fish had no opportunity to be marked and so could not appear in M. The adjustment was applied but not described in the September 2008 report.
- **Partnerships:** The lab credits data provided by several partners and cooperators in the native fishes conservation effort, including FWS, for the reliability of its results.

## Key Findings

**1. Estimation method**
- Wild adult populations use the Chapman-modified Petersen estimator, following [[Ricker]] (1975). The variables are:
  - M = number of fish captured and marked (released) in the prior year
  - C = number of fish captured in the subsequent sample year
  - R = number of fish in C that were previously marked
- Formula as given: N = (M+1)(C+1)/(R+1), with confidence limits from the Ricker (1975) appendix II table.
- **Worked example (March 2008):** M = 13, C = 19, R = 5, giving N = 47 adults. Applying the standard Chapman form to these inputs yields about 46 (45.7). The stated figure of 47 and the formula as printed in the scan differ slightly, possibly because of OCR or rounding, and this should be checked against the source data.

**2. Repatriate estimates**
- Repatriate estimates use the same formula. Confidence intervals come from the [[Chapman 1948]] tables.
- **Worked example (March 2008, all March data for 2007–2008):** M = 95, C = 166, R = 12, giving N = 1,232. Using the Chapman form with the −1 term, this gives 1,232.2, which matches the reported value.

**3. Database reliability**
- The database is under continuous revision. Figures may change as errors are found or new information arrives.
- Changes are flagged in reports when they occur, but individual database edits are not reported.
- Different methods can produce somewhat different results, which the authors consider expected.

**4. Proposed next step**
- The lab suggested a meeting in Tempe to resolve remaining questions in real time, rather than continuing with long written exchanges.

**5. Points for follow-up**
- The adjustment for stocked fish (since 2005) was not described in the 2008 report.
- The discrepancy in the 2008 example (47 reported vs. about 46 calculated) should be checked.
- The PIT-tag status of marked fish, raised in Kesner's annotation, was still to be discussed.
