# Toxicity of Dursban to Three Species of Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #toxicology #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #Centrarchidae #organophosphate-insecticides

## Executive Summary
This 1966 study evaluates the acute toxicity of **Dursban** (a novel organophosphate insecticide, chemically related to [[Chlorpyrifos]]) to three freshwater fish species: [[Golden Shiner]], [[Mosquito Fish]], and [[Green Sunfish]]. The researchers compared populations from insecticide-contaminated agricultural areas (Mississippi Delta) against populations from uncontaminated ponds (State College, MS) to assess baseline toxicity and potential resistance development. The study found that Delta populations exhibited significantly higher tolerance (3-5x) to Dursban than their uncontaminated counterparts, suggesting a cross-resistance phenomenon likely stemming from prior exposure to other agricultural insecticides like methyl parathion. Overall, Dursban was found to be less toxic to fish than chlorinated hydrocarbons but more toxic than many other organophosphates, though its margin of safety relative to target pests (mosquito larvae) remains high.

## Background & Description
While this paper is primarily a toxicological bioassay rather than a morphological study, specific size classes were selected for standardized testing:
*   **[[Golden Shiner]]:** Total length 2.5–4.0 inches.
*   **[[Mosquito Fish]]:** Total length 1.0–2.5 inches.
*   **[[Green Sunfish]]:** Total length 1.0–3.5 inches.

### Symptomology of Poisoning (Behavioral/Physiological Response)
Distinct species-specific reactions to Dursban exposure were observed, differing notably from typical chlorinated hydrocarbon poisoning symptoms (which usually involve hyperactivity and hypersensitivity):
*   **[[Golden Shiner]] & [[Mosquito Fish]]:** Loss of equilibrium, inversion (turning on backs), rising to the surface, and prolonged survival in a state of partial paralysis.
*   **[[Green Sunfish]]:** Moved to the bottom of the test container, turned on their sides, and died with minimal activity or struggling (unlike the surface-oriented behavior of the other species).
*   **Gravid [[Mosquito Fish]]:** Females in terminal gestation stages showed a tendency to abort in the presence of Dursban, corroborating previous findings regarding chlorinated hydrocarbons and methyl parathion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study population dynamics were split between two distinct environmental contexts, which served as the primary independent variable for measuring resistance:

1.  **Uncontaminated Source (State College, Oktibbeha Co., MS):** Fish collected from farm ponds isolated from known pesticide use, serving as a baseline "susceptible" population.
2.  **Contaminated Source (Mississippi Delta):**
    *   [[Golden Shiner]] and [[Green Sunfish]] collected from a bayou bordering cottonfields (Indianola, Sunflower Co.).
    *   [[Mosquito Fish]] collected from an agricultural drainage ditch (Belzoni, Humphreys Co.), directly exposed to runoff from cotton insecticide applications.

This ecological contrast highlights the selective pressure exerted by agricultural chemical runoff on native fish populations, driving physiological resistance in the Delta ecotypes independent of direct Dursban exposure (since Dursban was experimental and not yet used in the wild).

## Key Findings

### 1. Comparative Toxicity (36-Hour Median Tolerated Limit)
Dursban toxicity varied significantly by species and population origin. [[Mosquito Fish]] were consistently the most tolerant species, while [[Green Sunfish]] were generally the most sensitive.

| Population | Golden Shiners (ppb) | Mosquito Fish (ppb) | Green Sunfish (ppb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delta (Resistant) | 125 | 595 | 125 |
| State College #1 | 45 | 230 | 37.5 |
| State College #2 | 35 | 215 | 22.5 |

### 2. Cross-Resistance
*   Delta populations were **3 to 5 times more tolerant** of Dursban than genetically similar populations from non-agricultural areas, despite never having been exposed to Dursban specifically.
*   This supports the hypothesis of **cross-resistance**, likely developed via prior exposure to other organophosphates (e.g., methyl parathion) or chlorinated hydrocarbons, consistent with findings in [[Ferguson et al. 1964]].

### 3. Mortality Patterns
*   **Uncontaminated fish:** Tolerated increasing concentrations up to a threshold, after which mortality increased abruptly (steep dose-response curve).
*   **Delta fish:** Mortality was sporadic and increased slowly across a wide range of concentrations (shallow dose-response curve), mirroring patterns seen in hydrocarbon-resistant populations.
*   **Hydrolysis Effect:** A pronounced decline in mortality rates was observed after 24–36 hours, attributed to the rapid hydrolysis of the organophosphate compound reducing its bioavailability/toxicity over time.

### 4. Time-to-Death Analysis (LT-Values at 1000 ppb)
Analysis of Lethal Time values reinforced the resistance hierarchy, particularly at the LT50 and LT75 thresholds.

| Species | State College LT50 (hrs) | Delta LT50 (hrs) |
|---|---|---|
| Shiners | 3.5 | 13.75 |
| Mosquito Fish | 2.5 | 34.0 |
| Green Sunfish | 3.75 | 5.0 |

### 5. Environmental Safety Implications
*   Dursban demonstrated high target-specificity: Lethal doses for mosquito larvae (*Culex fatigans*, *Aedes aegypti*) were in the range of 2–5 ppb.
*   This creates a substantial **margin of safety** for fish populations (specifically the most sensitive species, [[Green Sunfish]], tolerating 22.5–125 ppb), suggesting Dursban could be a viable, fish-safe larvicide for mosquito control programs.
