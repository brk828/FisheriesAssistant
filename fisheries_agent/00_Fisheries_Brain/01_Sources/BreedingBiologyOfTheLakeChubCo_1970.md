## Executive Summary

This study by Brown, Hammer, and Koshinsky (1970) investigated the breeding biology of the [[Lake Chub]] (*Couesius plumbeus*) at Lac la Ronge, Saskatchewan, over three field seasons (1966–1968). Spawning occurred in two distinct habitats: the tributary [[Montreal River]] during May, and rocky lake shores/shoals during late June. Water temperature was identified as the primary regulating factor for spawning onset, with most activity occurring once water reached 10°C. Mark-recapture data (finclipping, n=4026) demonstrated migrations of up to 3 km and confirmed that river-spawning and lake-spawning populations do not intermix. The species exhibits nonadhesive egg dispersal, no nest-building, no territoriality, no parental care, and a polyandrous mating system dominated by male pursuit and nudging behavior. Critically, the behavioral findings — particularly the absence of nest-guarding — contradict patterns seen in true *Hybopsis* species, supporting the reclassification of the Lake Chub into the monotypic genus *Couesius* rather than *Hybopsis*.

## Background & Description

- **Sexual Dimorphism**:
  - Males possess larger [[Pectoral Fins]] than females year-round, a reliable sex-distinguishing trait outside the breeding season.
  - Males develop a more distinct dark lateral body stripe, which darkens further during courtship.
  - Both sexes develop fine [[Nuptial Tubercles]] (no specific pattern), but development is much greater in males:
    - Females: tubercles restricted to head region.
    - Males: tubercles also develop on body scale peripheries (up to 6 per scale) and on dorsal surfaces of larger pectoral fin rays (occasionally first few pelvic rays).
  - **Nuptial coloration (males)**: bright orange markings at pectoral fin bases (most prominent), above/behind operculum, at mouth corners (sometimes extending to eye), and at pelvic fin bases in larger males. Intensity is positively correlated with tubercle development, but not correlated with spawning aggressiveness.
  - **Nuptial coloration (females)**: some develop orange patches at pectoral fin bases and mouth corners, more pronounced in older/larger fish.
  - Females attain greater maximum size and likely greater longevity than males (largest female = 147 mm total length).

- **Egg Morphology**:
  - Developing eggs: yellow, 1.29–1.67 mm diameter pre-spawning.
  - Spawned, water-hardened eggs: averaged 2.30 mm diameter.
  - Eggs are **nonadhesive**, freely dispersed rather than deposited in nests.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Use**: The [[Lake Chub]] demonstrates habitat plasticity, spawning successfully across multiple substrate types — rocks, silt, leaves, gravel, and wood fragments — and in two distinct settings (riverine shallows and lacustrine rocky shores/shoals).
- **Thermal Regulation of Spawning**:
  - River spawning (Montreal River): initiated in May, following ice breakup upstream at Bigstone Lake; most spawning activity followed water reaching 10°C, with completion by ~16°C.
  - Lake spawning: delayed until late June when lake surface temperatures reached 10°C — a considerable time lag versus river spawning, attributed to temperature's dominant regulatory role (supporting [[Ahsan 1966b]] experimental findings on photoperiod vs. temperature effects on spermatogenesis).
- **Population Structure**: Finclip recapture data indicate **no intermingling** between river-spawning and lake-spawning (including Dog Island and South Dominion Point) populations — suggesting reproductive/spatial population substructuring within the same lake system.
- **Co-occurring Spawners**: Spawning migrations coincided/overlapped with [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) and [[Longnose Dace]] (*Rhinichthys cataractae*) runs in the same river shallows.
- **Predation/Cannibalism**: Both sexes were observed consuming eggs during spawning bouts in aquaria — no parental guarding behavior was observed to prevent this.
- **Comparative Behavior**: Courtship behavior (approach from beneath, vent-region nudging, persistent pursuit) closely parallels other cyprinids such as [[Longnose Shiner]] (*Notropis longirostris*), [[Black-nosed Dace]] (*Rhinichthys atratulus atratulus*), and [[Red-bellied Dace]] (*Chrosomus erythrogaster*), but diverges sharply from nest-building/guarding *Hybopsis* species (*H. biguttatus*, *H. micropogon*).

## Key Findings

- **Migration**: Marked individuals (finclipping, n=4026) traveled as far as 3 km to reach river spawning grounds; recapture data confirmed distinct, non-mixing lake and river spawning populations.
- **Sex Ratios on Spawning Grounds**: Males consistently outnumbered females in river samples:
  | Year | Males | Females | Total | M:F Ratio |
  |------|-------|---------|-------|-----------|
  | 1966 | 149 | 85 | 234 | 1.75:1 |
  | 1967 | 401 | 201 | 602 | 2.00:1 |
  | 1968 | 732 | 553 | 1285 | 1.32:1 |
  - The true population ratio is likely closer to 1:1; the skew is attributed to spent females departing spawning grounds earlier than males (supported by [[Ahsan 1966a]] histological work on repeated sperm discharge capability in males).

- **Mating System**: Polyandrous — multiple males pursued single females. No territoriality observed in the field (though partial territorial behavior was seen in 3 of 12 aquarium male-pair trials, likely a confinement artifact). No nests, no guarding.

- **Age/Size Structure of Spawning Population (1968)**:
  - Age-group III (2 years, 11 months): 68% of spawning females; mean total length 95.1 mm (first-time spawners); contributed 54% of potential recruitment.
  - Age-group IV: 26% of captures; mean length 110.5 mm; contributed 33% of recruitment.
  - Age-group V+: 6% of spawning females; contributed 13% of recruitment despite carrying more eggs — indicating greater female longevity does not substantially boost overall recruitment.

- **Fecundity**: Egg counts for 20 females (90–132 mm TL) fit the relation: **Log N = -3.2426 + 3.0962(Log L)**, where N = egg number and L = total length (mm).

- **Egg Viability**:
  - Field-collected eggs (silt/leaves/wood): 56% viable (519/919).
  - Field-collected eggs (gravel under flat rock): 54% viable (53/99).
  - Lab-fertilized, hand-stripped eggs: hatching success ≥65%.

- **Incubation**: Eggs hatched in ~10 days at 8–19°C in laboratory pint jars, consistent with field timing (fry first captured June 2–6 in Montreal River across study years).

- **Taxonomic Implication**: Fundamental behavioral differences (no nest-building/guarding) versus true *Hybopsis* species support **rejection of Bailey's (1951) proposal** to include Lake Chub within *Hybopsis*; findings support reversion to the monotypic genus **[[Couesius]]**, per AFS Names of Fishes Committee guidance (R.M. Bailey, pers. comm.), though this is not viewed as a permanent taxonomic resolution.
