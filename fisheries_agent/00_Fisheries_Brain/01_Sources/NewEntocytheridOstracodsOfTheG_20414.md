# New Entocytherid Ostracods of the Genus Ornithocythere and Description of a New Genus

#ostracoda #entocytheridae #taxonomy #freshwater-biology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, by [[Horton H. Hobbs III]] of Indiana University, describes three new species of [[Entocytheridae|entocytherid ostracods]] in the genus [[Ornithocythere]] Hobbs, 1967, and erects the new genus [[Hartocythere]] for a single species previously placed in [[Geocythere]]. The three new species are [[Ornithocythere Aetodes]] and [[Ornithocythere Popi]] (both from Mississippi, collected from the burrowing crayfish [[Cambarus Diogenes]]) and [[Ornithocythere Rhea]] (from Kentucky, from the same host genus). With these additions the genus contains five species, whose ranges are discontinuous. The author correlates female and male forms for all members and provides dichotomous keys for both sexes, based largely on genital structures.

## Background & Description

**Scope and taxonomic context.** The paper deals with small, freshwater, crayfish-associated ostracods of the family [[Entocytheridae]]. These animals live as commensals or ectosymbionts on [[Burrowing Crayfish]] hosts and are found in the burrows the crayfish dig. The paper is not about fish. Its main subject is morphological taxonomy.

**Genus diagnosis (Ornithocythere).** Key diagnostic traits include:
- Mandibular terminal tooth pectinate.
- Male copulatory complex with the peniferum extending ventrally beyond the clasping apparatus.
- An acute, sclerotized, beaklike prominence on the peniferum, said to resemble an inverted bird head.
- A large corneous penis directed anterodorsally.
- Clasping apparatus that may or may not divide into horizontal and vertical rami, with the horizontal ramus ending in three to five apical denticles.
- Females with a heavily sclerotized genital papilla, with or without a campanula (a bell-like enclosure), and no J-shaped rod or amiculum.

**Type species:** [[Ornithocythere Waltonae]] Hobbs, 1967.

**Female genital complex.** The genital complex is located posterodorsally in the female shell. It consists of a heavily sclerotized genital papilla, which is a simple cone or cylinder. Some species also have alate processes (wings) or a campanula. The whole structure is surrounded by an amorphous mass that collects detritus.

**Species descriptions:**

- **[[Ornithocythere Aetodes]] (new species).** Male shell subovate, eyes about one-fourth shell length from the anterior margin. The S-shaped clasping apparatus has three smooth prominences on the external border and terminates in four apical denticles. The ventral finger is about four times the length of the dorsal finger. Female genital complex has a papilla with two alate processes. The name comes from Greek *aétos* (eagle) + *-oides* (like), referring to the eagle-head-like peniferum. Type locality: burrow from a ditch along St. Rte. 28, 0.5 mi W of jct. St. Rtes. 28 and 35, Smith County, Mississippi (Leaf River drainage). Recorded from no other locality. Holotype male and allotype deposited in the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (nos. 126264 and 126265).

- **[[Ornithocythere Popi]] (new species).** Male clasping apparatus gently curved, with four annulations on the external border of the distal half and three apical denticles. The ventral finger is three times the length of the dorsal finger. Female genital papilla is enclosed in a ventrally tapering campanula. Named in honor of the author's father, Horton H. Hobbs, Jr. Type locality: burrow in drainage ditch on U.S. Hwy. 49, 0.7 mi N of Stone-Harrison County line, Stone County, Mississippi (Biloxi River drainage). Additional range record: burrows on a creek bank, U.S. Hwy. 11, 8.8 mi SW of jct. with St. Rte. 26, Pearl River County, Mississippi (Pearl River drainage). Holotype male and allotype deposited in the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (nos. 126266 and 126267).

- **[[Ornithocythere Rhea]] (new species).** Peniferum elongate and slender, extending well beyond the clasping apparatus. Clasping apparatus is always clearly divisible into horizontal and vertical rami, with the vertical ramus 2.5 times the length of the horizontal ramus, and the two rami forming an angle under 90°. The penis is very long and slender compared with other members. Female has a saclike extension directed posterodorsally from the papilla base. Named for the genus of ostrichlike birds, since the peniferum resembles an inverted ostrich head and neck. Type locality: Trammel Creek, 1.4 mi from jct. U.S. Hwy. 231 and 31W at Alvaton, Allen County, Kentucky (Barren River drainage). The only locality known.

- **[[Ornithocythere Gypodes]] Hobbs III, 1969 (redescribed).** Originally known only from the holotype. Additional specimens from two localities in Pickens County, Alabama and two localities in Noxubee County, Mississippi allowed the first description of the triunguis female (genital papilla directed posteroventrally, with surrounding amorphous mass) and a fuller account of the male clasping apparatus.

- **[[Ornithocythere Waltonae]] Hobbs, 1967 (type species).** Compared with *O. aetodes*, it has a C-shaped clasping apparatus with an entire external border. Its female genital complex is a papilla enclosed in a posteroventrally directed campanula.

**New genus: [[Hartocythere]].** This genus was erected to hold [[Hartocythere Torreya]] (originally *Entocythere torreya* Hart, 1959; reassigned to *Geocythere* by Hart, 1962). Diagnostic features are:
- Ventral portion of the male peniferum heavily sclerotized, angled anteroventrally, and deeply cleft (bifid).
- Clasping apparatus curved at approximately a right angle, not divisible into rami, with four apical denticles.
- Female genital papilla long and slender, with two posteroventrally curved alate processes dorsal to it.

Gender: feminine. Type species designated here: *Entocythere torreya* Hart, 1959.

**Taxonomic keys.** The paper provides keys to males and to triunguis females. Male key separates species by external border of clasping apparatus, rami division, prominences, and beaklike projection shape. Female key separates species by campanula presence, papilla direction, and alate processes or posterodorsal extension.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host association.** All species were collected from burrowing crayfish. Hosts recorded include [[Cambarus Diogenes]] (subspecies *diogenes* and *ludovicianus*, and an undetermined *C. diogenes* subspecies for *O. rhea*). Burrows are the sampling habitat, and the detritus-laden amorphous masses surrounding female genitalia suggest a microhabitat in sediment.
- **Drainage systems.** Records are tied to river drainages: Leaf River (*O. aetodes*), Biloxi River (*O. popi* type locality), Pearl River (*O. popi* additional record), Barren River (*O. rhea*), and the Alabama, Escambia, Choctawhatchee, and Apalachicola systems (*H. torreya*).
- **Co-occurring entocytherids.** Associated species include [[Ankylocythere Harmani]] Hobbs, 1966 (with *O. aetodes* and *O. popi*), [[Ankylocythere Hyba]] Hobbs and Walton, 1963 (with *O. rhea*), undescribed species of [[Ankylocythere]] and [[Geocythere]], and [[Uncinocythere Simondsi]] (with *H. torreya*).
- **Distribution pattern.** The genus shows a discontinuous range across the Coastal Plain of the southeastern US. Only *O. waltonae* is reported from a different region, the coastal plain of Maryland and Virginia (the paper does not explicitly state which species this refers to).

**Phylogenetic relationships.** The paper argues that the genera *Geocythere*, *Hartocythere*, *Okriocythere* ([[Okriocythere]] Hart, 1964), and *Ornithocythere* probably share a common origin:
- *O. aetodes* has excrescences on the clasping apparatus external border like *Geocythere*, and a peniferum length similar to that genus.
- Its female alate processes link it to *Hartocythere*.
- *O. gypodes*, *O. popi*, and *O. aetodes* share the "bird-head" peniferum.
- *O. rhea* is probably the most derived or remote member of the genus, based on its unique female saclike extension.

## Key Findings

1. **Three new species of Ornithocythere** were described: *O. aetodes* (Mississippi), *O. popi* (Mississippi), and *O. rhea* (Kentucky). The genus now contains five species.
2. **Female-male correlation** was established for all members of *Ornithocythere*. Keys were provided for both sexes, based largely on genital structures.
3. **Genital complex characters** distinguish the species: presence or absence of a campanula, alate processes, posterodorsal saclike extension, and the direction of the papilla.
4. **A new genus, *Hartocythere*,** was proposed for *Hartocythere torreya* (Hart, 1959), formerly *Geocythere torreya*, based on its deeply bifid ventral peniferum and right-angle clasping apparatus.
5. ***Ornithocythere gypodes*** was redescribed with new triunguis female data from additional localities in Alabama and Mississippi.
6. **Range patterns:** The genus is discontinuous across the Coastal Plain, and *H. torreya* is known from Alabama, Florida, and Georgia.
7. **Type localities and deposition:** Holotypes and allotypes were deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]] (Smithsonian Institution). Paratypes are held by the author, C. W. Hart, Jr., and the Smithsonian.
8. **Nomenclature:** *O. rhea* is named after the ostrichlike bird genus *Rhea*, and *O. aetodes* after the eagle (Greek *aétos*), both for the peniferum shape. *O. popi* honors the author's father.
