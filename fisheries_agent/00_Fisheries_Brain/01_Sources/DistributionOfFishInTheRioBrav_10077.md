# Fish Distribution Maps of the Mexican Rio Bravo Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a cartographic atlas of 158 numbered distribution maps for fish taxa in the Mexican portion of the [[Rio Bravo Basin]] (the [[Rio Grande]] drainage). Each map is captioned in Spanish and marks localities as present (●) or absent (○). The atlas covers freshwater species, along with euryhaline and marine species that enter the estuary. The text consists only of map titles and captions. It contains no locality counts, sample sizes, statistics, or narrative findings, and the referenced Cuadro 1 is not included.

## Background & Description

- **Format:** One map per taxon, each with the caption "Distribución geográfica del pez [family] [species] en la cuenca geohidrológica mexicana del Río Bravo."
- **Coverage:** Maps 1–157 each address one taxon (some are genus-level "sp." entries or paired species). Map 153 combines *Diplectrum bivittatum* and *D. formosa*.
- **Summary map:** Map 158 covers 74 euryhaline and marine species that penetrate the estuary, with details in Cuadro 1, which is not reproduced.
- **Taxonomic scope:** The atlas includes gars, eels, herrings, salmonids, characins, carps and minnows, suckers, catfishes, killifishes, livebearers, goodeids, silversides, mullets, sunfishes, perches, drums, cichlids, anchovies, sea catfishes, needlefishes, snooks, jacks, and gobies or sleepers.
- **Source-text note:** Scanning errors and a few misspellings in the source were corrected in this note (e.g., "Onchorhynchus" to *Oncorhynchus*, "Stypodon singnifer" to *Stypodon signifer*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Rio Bravo basin spans arid and semi-arid northern Mexico, and the taxa mapped reflect its varied aquatic habitats:

- **Native desert and endemic taxa:** Several genera have many mapped entries, including *Cyprinodon* pupfishes (15 map entries, Maps 79–93), *Gila* chubs (8 entries, including three *Gila* sp. maps), and *Gambusia* (8 entries, Maps 104–111). These groups are often restricted to small springs or isolated drainages.
- **Introduced and non-native taxa:** Several maps cover species commonly introduced outside their native ranges, such as [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), [[Mozambique Tilapia]] (*Oreochromis mossambicus*), [[Blue Tilapia]] (*Oreochromis aureus*), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). Map titles do not mark them as introduced, so this classification is based on general knowledge, not the document.
- **Salmonids:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) appears as a single map, consistent with a cold-water or stocked occurrence.
- **Estuarine and marine-entering fishes:** The inclusion of Map 158 and marine-associated taxa such as [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), [[Common Snook]] (*Centropomus undecimalis*), [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*), and *Arius felis* reflects connectivity between the lower Rio Grande and the [[Gulf of Mexico]].
- **Diadromous species:** [[American Eel]] (*Anguilla rostrata*) is mapped, which points to a freshwater–marine life cycle.

Each map records presence and absence by locality, but the document provides no environmental variables or sampling methods.

## Key Findings

1. **Scale of the atlas:** 158 numbered maps, covering roughly 150 species-level entries, including "sp." and paired species. The exact total depends on how the duplicated entries are counted.
2. **Family-level breadth:** Taxa come from roughly 25 families. The most heavily represented are:
   - **Cyprinidae (carps and minnows):** the largest group, with many *Notropis* (11 map entries, Maps 36–46), *Cyprinella* (11 entries, Maps 48–58), *Gila* (8 entries), and *Dionda* (3 entries, Maps 30–32). It also includes [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), and *Hybognathus amarus*.
   - **Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes):** 15 *Cyprinodon* entries, plus *Megupsilon aporus*.
   - **Poeciliidae (livebearers):** including *Gambusia* (8 entries), *Xiphophorus* (7 entries, Maps 112–117 plus *X. couchianus*), and [[Guppy]] (*Poecilia reticulata*).
   - **Ictaluridae (catfishes):** about 10 entries (Maps 69–77), including [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*), and [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*).
   - **Catostomidae (suckers):** about 9 entries, including *Carpiodes carpio*, *Catostomus* spp., *Ictiobus* spp., *Moxostoma* spp., and *Cycleptus elongatus*.
   - **Centrarchidae (sunfishes):** 7 entries, including [[Largemouth Bass]], *Lepomis* spp., and [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*).
   - **Cichlidae:** *Cichlasoma* and *Oreochromis* entries, including a cichlid endemic to the region, *Cichlasoma minckleyi*.
3. **Cyprinodon as a regional focus:** Fifteen *Cyprinodon* maps, which reflects the high number of named pupfish species in the basin.
4. **Gar and other ancient lineages:** Three gar species (*Lepisosteus osseus*, *L. oculatus*, *Atractosteus spatula*) are mapped, including [[Longnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus osseus*) and [[Spotted Gar]] (*Lepisosteus oculatus*).
5. **Notable endemic or regionally restricted taxa:** Examples include the blind cave-associated *Prietella phreatophila* (Map 78), the *Characodon* goodeids (Maps 118–120), and the *Chirostoma* silversides (Maps 123–127).
6. **Euryhaline and marine component:** Map 158 identifies 74 euryhaline and marine species that penetrate the estuary, a substantial component of the regional fauna.
7. **Limitations:** The document contains no locality numbers, sample sizes, abundance data, dates, or regulatory information. Any conclusions about abundance, status, or threats would require the referenced Cuadro 1 or the original study.
