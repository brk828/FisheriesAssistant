# Arizona Trout Recovery Team Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a presentation summary of the Arizona Trout Recovery Team, presented by John H. Rinne for Bill Silvey. It reports on recovery planning for threatened and endangered trout and related freshwater fishes in the American Southwest and adjacent states. The source is a heavily degraded OCR scan, so many sentences are incomplete or unreadable. The conclusions below rest only on legible fragments and should be checked against the original.

The main points are:
- Recovery plans and draft plans are in progress for several river reaches and species.
- [[Apache Trout]] is the most common trout on streams of the [[White Mountain Apache Reservation]], although the exact figure is illegible.
- Texas spring and river fishes, including the [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] and [[San Marcos Gambusia]], are listed as candidates or species of concern.
- Area offices were planned for Phoenix, Arizona, and Austin, Texas, under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

The report covers several freshwater systems and habitat types:
- **Arizona streams:** Apache trout habitat on the White Mountain Apache Reservation.
- **Colorado and Utah rivers:** Reaches of the Green River (from the Yampa River confluence), the Gunnison River (from a highway bridge), and the Virgin River (lower reaches, including a segment near Ash Creek). Legible fragments name these reaches, but their boundaries are partly lost.
- **Texas springs and rivers:** Spring-fed and cave-associated habitats, including Leon Springs, the Devil's River and San Marcos systems.

Critical habitat was recommended for some of these reaches. The text indicates that critical habitat designations were to be finalized by year-end, but the affected species and the exact deadline are unclear.
