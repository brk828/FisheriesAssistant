# Zoological Notes from the Irish Naturalists' Journal (1966)

#marine-biology #freshwater-biology #copepoda #amphipoda #cetacea #lepidoptera #ornithology

## Executive Summary

This reprinted issue section (Vol. 15, No. 7, July 1966) gathers short zoological notes and correspondence from Irish naturalists. The main items are: a first probable Irish record of the copepod [[Diaptomus Castor]]; a southerly range extension of the amphipod [[Gammarus Lacustris]] in County Cork; two stranded [[Cuvier's Whale]] specimens on the west coast; a juvenile [[White-Sided Dolphin]] landed at Killybegs; a [[Humming-Bird Hawk Moth]] record from County Antrim; and an announcement of a five-year [[Sea-Bird Breeding Survey]]. No fish are covered. The material is mainly marine mammal and freshwater invertebrate distribution.

## Background & Description

The issue is a compilation of independent notes, each with its own author and institution:

- **Copepod note** by J. N. R. Grainger, Department of Zoology, Trinity College Dublin.
- **Amphipod note** by Geoffrey Fryer, Freshwater Biological Association, Ambleside.
- **Whale note** by David Cabot, Department of Zoology, University College Galway.
- **Dolphin note** by J. P. Hillis, Fisheries Division, Department of Agriculture and Fisheries, Dublin.
- **Correspondence** by Michael P. Walters (moth) and Oscar J. Merne (seabird survey).

The notes rely on earlier literature, including Brady's copepod monographs, Scourfield's Clare Island Survey, Gurney's *British Freshwater Copepoda*, Hynes, Macan and Williams (1960) on freshwater amphipods, and Fraser and Parker's *Stranded Whales and Turtles* (1949).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater invertebrates:** [[Diaptomus Castor]] is a winter-active species of temporary pools in southern England and much of Europe. It disappears in summer, probably as resting eggs. Other Irish Diaptomus species (*D. gracilis*, *D. laticeps*, *D. wierzejskii*, *D. laciniatus*) are mostly plankton-dwelling and recorded from lakes, mainly in the west and south-west. [[Gammarus Duebeni]] is the common Irish freshwater amphipod and is widespread.
- **Isolated lakelet habitat:** The lakelet at Three Castles Head sits on a promontory with sea cliffs up to 387 ft, but its water is fresh. The author describes it as possibly the most southerly body of fresh water in Ireland apart from ponds.
- **Marine mammal strandings:** Both whales and the dolphin were found dead or landed, linking these records to the Atlantic coast and fishing activity (trawler landing at Killybegs).
- **Seabird colonies:** Ireland supports 22 breeding seabird species, whose colonies lie on the mainland coast and offshore islands.

## Key Findings

**1. Diaptomus castor, County Meath (Grainger)**
- Collected in large numbers on 20 March 1965 from a pond one mile north of Rathmolyon, Co. Meath.
- Not found on repeated searches June to November 1965; specimens reappeared in January 1966.
- The author considers this the first unequivocal Irish record. Earlier Irish mentions probably confused it with *D. gracilis*, as Brady's figures were shown by Gurney to be mostly of *D. gracilis*.
- Five of the six British *Diaptomus* species are now known from Ireland. *D. vulgaris* (an eastern English species considered a recent immigrant) is the only one not recorded.

**2. Gammarus lacustris, County Cork (Fryer)**
- Two adults and three juveniles collected in June 1965 from a small lakelet at Three Castles Head, extreme south-west Co. Cork.
- This is the most southerly station for the species in the British Isles, extending its known range considerably southward.
- Irish records lie near the west coast, and the British distribution appears northern and western (Scotland including the Hebrides, northern England, north Wales, and western Ireland).

**3. Ziphius cavirostris, Co. Galway (Cabot)**
- A 30 ft specimen was found washed up dead at Dooloughan, near Slyne Head, on 17 January 1965.
- It was confirmed as *Ziphius cavirostris* by Dr F. C. Fraser (British Museum, Natural History) from photographs.
- The lower jaw tip was broken, so teeth could not be taken for ageing or sexing.
- Local reports suggest it arrived around August 1964, about six months before discovery.
- This follows an earlier specimen from Achill Island, Co. Mayo (November 1964).

**4. Lagenorhynchus acutus, Co. Donegal (Hillis)**
- A juvenile White-sided Dolphin landed by the trawler *Undaunted* at Killybegs on 9 July 1965.
- Overall length 4 ft 2 in and weight 53 lb. Adult length for the species is 9 to 10 ft.
- Identified by its black-and-white pattern. The teeth had not erupted, and their positions were marked by red spots along the gums.
- Retained for preservation by Michael Rogers.

**5. Macroglossa stellatarum, Co. Antrim (Walters, correspondence)**
- The author took a specimen at Ballintoy in 1955 and saw one in a Portrush garden the same year.
- He had not seen the moth on the north Antrim coast in any other year. He comments on a recent Rathlin Island report.

**6. Sea-Bird Breeding Survey (Merne, correspondence)**
- A five-year survey of breeding sea-bird populations began in Ireland in 1966.
- It covers 22 breeding species and aims to map the location and size of all colonies on the coast and islands.
- Sponsored by the Sea-bird Group (BOU, BTO and RSPB). Observers' information is treated as confidential.
