# ESA Vegetation Classification Panel: Scope of Work 1996-97

#vegetation-ecology #vegetation-classification #plant-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is not an ichthyological or aquatic study. It is an administrative packet from the [[Ecological Society of America]] (ESA) that covers the [[Vegetation Classification Panel]]'s scope of work for 1996-97, its July 1996 annual report, and a [[Federal Geographic Data Committee]] (FGDC) request for public comment on a draft [[National Vegetation Classification Standard]]. The Panel's long-term goal is a national classification of U.S. vegetation. The Governing Board was asked to authorize joint exploration work and to receive a definitive long-term proposal by July 1997. The packet also contains unrelated Federal Register notices from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] Office of Management Authority, which concern marine mammal permit applications and do not bear on the classification.

## Background & Description

- **Memo date and parties:** July 15, 1996; from Orie Loucks, Chair, ESA Vegetation Classification Panel, to the ESA Governing Board.
- **Panel origin:** Authorized at the end of 1994 and appointed in January 1995. Its role is service-oriented, supporting ESA and the FGDC.
- **Panel mission:** Facilitate and support a standardized, scientifically credible vegetation classification system. Objectives included:
  - providing a neutral forum for reviewing goals and standards (nomenclature, hierarchy, structure, definitions);
  - promoting standardization of named vegetation units;
  - providing ongoing review of modifications or additions to named units;
  - facilitating public access to classification information;
  - identifying areas for further research and development.
- **Authorization requested:** The Board was asked to authorize joint exploration studies with partners, prepare reports on data and classification standards for publication by ESA, develop proposals for long-term institutional structures, and deliver a definitive long-term proposal by July 1997.
- **Sponsoring/partner institutions:** [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[National Biological Service]], [[Fish And Wildlife Service]], [[Environmental Protection Agency]], USDA Forest Service, and the FGDC.
- **Panel membership (as listed):** 18 signatories, including Orie L. Loucks (Chair, Miami University) and Michael Jennings (Vice Chair, National Biological Service). Other affiliations include Pennsylvania State University, University of California at Davis, Bureau of Land Management, Arizona State University, University of Connecticut, World Wildlife Fund, University of North Carolina, University of Montana, USDA Agriculture Research Service, National Park Service, University of Colorado, and California State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Scope of the classification:** The FGDC standard is meant to support national-level statistics on vegetation resources. Agencies that map or inventory vegetated land cover are expected to collect enough data to translate their results for national aggregation and comparison. The end goal is a quantitative, geo-referenced basis for vegetation cover modeling, mapping, and analysis at the field level.
- **Classification structure:** The classification has a physiognomic (structural) portion and two lower floristic levels, the [[Alliance]] and the [[Community Association Type]]. The floristic levels depend on plot data, analysis, and standards.
- **Draft standard questions for public comment:** Reviewers were asked about:
  - the threshold separating vegetated from non-vegetated areas;
  - threshold criteria for defining tree-dominated land and separating tree cover classes;
  - parallelism between tree-dominated classes (open and closed canopy) and other life-form cover classes, including whether shrub and herbaceous classes need similar divisions;
  - whether shrub height classes should be modeled on other life forms.
- **Review logistics:** Comments were to go to the FGDC Secretariat in hardcopy and softcopy on one 3.5-inch diskette. The notice was signed by Richard Wikner, Acting Chief, National Mapping Division.

## Key Findings

1. **Phase I is largely complete.** The Panel's physiognomic recommendations were largely incorporated into the FGDC's proposed standards, which were published in the [[Federal Register]] on July 3, 1996 for public comment. The Panel met in June 1995 (Houston) and in March 1996 (near Cincinnati) and completed a third review of the proposed standards.
2. **Phase II focuses on floristic-level standards.** The Panel's work now centers on the Alliance and Community Association Type levels. These require plot data standards, a possible common database, and consensus approaches to quantifying and reviewing floristic units.
3. **Draft Initiative.** A drafting subgroup met in Denver, CO in early June 1996. Its document, *Initiative for a Standard Classification of Vegetation for the United States*, proposes a baseline classification system and standards for terminology, nomenclature, plot data, and documentation. It also covers data management and archiving, peer review, and a long-term institutional framework, possibly within ESA. The draft remained open for comment, with community discussion planned at the ESA Annual Meeting in Providence, RI, in August 1996.
4. **Immediate tasks (next eight months).** These include:
   - developing a standard glossary and terminology;
   - developing standards for plot databases;
   - reviewing and recognizing a generic classification system for the floristic levels;
   - creating a standard peer review process for proposed unit names;
   - formalizing a documentation format for recognized units;
   - articulating a preliminary institutional arrangement and budget, with a proposal due the following year.
5. **Timeline.** 1996-97 is for standards and review guidance documents. 1997-98 is for creating review mechanisms and a long-term institution with its budget, on behalf of the vegetation classification community and the FGDC.
6. **Guiding principle.** ESA's role is to facilitate and advocate standards and review and to provide professional advice. The Panel stated that the effort will be better received as a forum for reviewing community standards and outcomes than as a classification created by a committee.
7. **Support.** Staff and logistical support came from the ESA Sustainable Biosphere Initiative Project Office in Washington, D.C., with funding from the Fish and Wildlife Service, the National Biological Service, the Environmental Protection Agency, and the [[National Science Foundation]].
