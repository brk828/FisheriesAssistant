# Osteology and Classification of the Fishes of the Family Blenniidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #blenniidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. National Museum Bulletin (No. 284, Victor G. Springer, 1968) defines the family [[Blenniidae]] osteologically and revises its suprageneric classification. The study uses a detailed skeletal description of [[Entomacrodus Nigricans]] as an osteological standard for the family, then compares jaws, dentition, caudal and dorsal fin structure, pectoral and pelvic girdles, and circumorbital bones across the family. Springer recognizes four suprageneric taxa: subfamilies [[Blenniinae]] (tribes [[Blenniini]] and [[Omobranchini]], with new tribe Omobranchini) and [[Nemophidinae]], plus tribe [[Salariini]]. The work also proposes a hypothetical phylogeny and a set of diagnostic characters for the family.

## Background & Description

- **Family:** Blenniidae, a family of small, scaleless, shallow-water fishes, distributed circumglobally mainly in tropical and subtropical seas, with about 400 species. Most are benthic; a small group (Nemophidinae) has adopted a free-swimming habit.
- **Motivation:** No reasonably comprehensive osteological description existed for any blenniid species, earlier piecemeal osteological data were misleading, and a sound classification of the suborder [[Blennioidei]] requires osteology of every included family.
- **Higher classification context:** [[Gosline]] (1968) placed blennioids in the perciforms, separating them by pelvic fins anterior to pectoral fins and a one-to-one ratio of vertebrae to dorsal and posterior anal soft rays. All blenniids except the monotypic *Plagiotremus* (pelvics absent) share these traits.
- **Earlier schemes:** [[Hubbs]] (1952) split the Blennioidei into Zoarcicae and Blenniicae; [[Makushok]] (1958) split northern blennies into three superfamilies; Gosline (1968) recognized five superfamilies. [[Norman]] (1943) provided the last comprehensive infrafamilial classification, recognizing subfamilies Ophioblenniinae, Blenniinae and Salariinae. Springer relegates Norman's Salariinae to tribe rank and places the Nemophidinae as a subfamily.
- **Material:** Cleared-and-stained specimens from the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM) and other collections, examined with radiographs; 41 nominal genera are used in the material list, with a comparative series of Chaenopsidae, Clinidae, Tripterygiidae and several other families.
- **Methods:** Enzyme clearing and staining ([[Taylor]], 1967), dissection, radiography, and camera lucida or micro-projector drawings. Bone names follow [[Starks]] (1901) with specified exceptions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Blenniids are benthic and shallow-water, and most are reef or coastal forms. The study notes that the benthic habit explains the functional role of pelvic fins as a pedestal for support.
- The Nemophidinae are described as having adopted a free-swimming habit; reduced or malformed pelvic fins are common in individuals of many Nemophidinae genera, which the author attributes to loss of the pelvic support function.
- Feeding habits are inferred from morphology: the median ethmoid's brace function in Blenniini, Omobranchini and Nemophidinae is linked to a carnivorous habit; reduced median ethmoid and weakened premaxillary processes in Salariini are linked to a primarily herbivorous, grazing habit.
- Entomacrodus is described as a benthic genus that uses the caudal fin less for propulsion than free-swimming fishes.
- Geographic separation supports the distinction between northern blennies (Zoarcicae) and tropical blennies (Blenniicae): there is very little overlap in distribution.

## Key Findings

- **Standard osteology:** The skeleton of *Entomacrodus nigricans* (male, 53 mm SL, Havana, Cuba; USNM 192195) is described in detail and proposed as the family standard, with the caveat that the Nemophidinae need more study.
- **Vertebrae and fins:** 34 vertebrae in *E. nigricans* (range 33–35); 13 spines and 15 rays in the dorsal fin; 2 anal spines and 17 anal rays; 13 segmented caudal rays. Blenniid vertebrae range from 29 (rarely 28) in *Stanulus* to 134 in *Xiphasia*.
- **Pectoral girdle:** The coracoid is reduced and fused (synostosis) to the cleithrum, a key family character. Four pectoral radials are present in all blenniids.
- **Tooth counts:** In Blenniini, premaxillary comblike teeth number 14–56; Omobranchini 14–30; Salariini 30–250; Nemophidinae 16–42 (upper jaw).
- **Caudal fin:** Four basic caudal types are identified. The most primitive blenniid caudal (two epurals, autogenous minimal hypural, nine branched rays) occurs in most Salariini and some Blenniini.
- **Circumorbitals:** Usually five in blenniids; four in Nemophidinae (except *Meiacanthus* with three) and in *Ecsenius* and one undescribed Salariini genus. Their number is often diagnostic for genera.
- **Basisphenoid:** Present in Blenniinae except Nemophidinae, where it is absent; a "pseudobasisphenoid" formed by pterosphenoid outgrowths occurs in some Nemophidinae.
- **Vomerine teeth:** Characteristic of *Entomacrodus* but not universal within species; found in some Blennius, Rhabdoblennius, Stanulus and other genera.
- **Kinethmoid:** A rostral cartilage ossification (named "kinethmoid" provisionally) found in several Blenniini, Omobranchini and Salariini species.
- **Phylogeny:** The author proposes that Salariini and Omobranchini derive from Blenniini, and that Nemophidinae derive from Omobranchini or a related group, based on jaws, dentition, caudal type and gill opening.
- **Taxonomic actions:** The new tribe Omobranchini is established; Blennius is recommended to be restricted, but status quo is maintained until revision; Ophioblennius and several other genera are discussed as synonyms or substitute names.
- **Distinctive characters:** Blenniids are diagnosed by at least three characters: coracoid-cleithrum synostosis; the interopercle–epihyal–interhyal relationship; and a single row of close-set comblike teeth anteriorly with no functional teeth behind it. Other supporting characters include the low dorsal spine count, nonprotractile premaxillaries, lack of scales, and absence of branched pectoral rays.
- **Diagnoses:** Tribe and subfamily diagnoses are given with ranges for vertebrae, rays, caudal and pelvic counts, jaw structure and circumorbital number.
