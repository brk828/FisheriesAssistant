# ASU Research Park Fish Survey, April 1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

Arizona State University personnel surveyed fish in the three ponds of the [[ASU Research Park]], Tempe, on 10–11 April 1997 using seven entanglement nets. The catch totaled 67 fish representing 10 species, all previously recorded at the park. [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]] dominated the catch. Two marked [[Razorback Sucker]] were recaptured, and no [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Colorado Squawfish]] were caught. The survey memo also announces a tentative follow-up survey for Fall 1997.

## Background & Description

The memo is a trip report from Brian Bagley of the [[Center for Environmental Studies]] at Arizona State University, dated 16 April 1997 and addressed to interested parties. Its purpose was to assess the status of fishes in the research park's ponds.

Sampling details:
- **Gear:** Seven entanglement nets (described in Table 2 as trammel and experimental gill nets; see [[Trammel Net]] and [[Gill Net]]).
- **Set time:** Nets were deployed between 1930 and 2145 hrs on 10 April 1997.
- **Pull time:** Nets were run and pulled between 0500 and 0730 hrs on 11 April 1997.
- **Results:** Summarized in Table 2, which is garbled in the source. Its catch-per-unit-effort values could not be recovered and are not reported here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The park's three ponds hold a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fishes. The catch included:
- **Native or conservation-relevant species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a federally listed species in the Colorado River basin, plus the Colorado River natives [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]], which were absent this year.
- **Introduced or non-native species:** [[Grass Carp]], [[Blue Tilapia]], [[Tilapia Hybrid]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Goldfish]], [[Yellow Bass]], and the sportfish [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]].

Two recaptured razorback suckers provide a record of individual growth and persistence in the ponds:
- **Male:** Total length (TL) 544 mm. Originally tagged on 5 April 1996 at 530 mm, so it grew 14 mm in roughly one year.
- **Female:** TL 600 mm. Originally tagged on 29 September 1995 at 602 mm. The memo reports this as a 2 mm decrease, which may reflect measurement error or a data-entry issue. The original value is reported here as given.

The memo notes that one or two bonytail chub are usually encountered during this sampling effort, but Colorado squawfish "have been conspicuously absent for the last several years."

## Key Findings

- **Total catch:** 67 individuals, 10 species, all previously recorded in the research park.
- **Dominant species:** Largemouth bass made up 51% of the total catch and bluegill 16%.
- **Minor species:** The remaining eight species each made up 10% or less of the catch. They are grass carp, blue tilapia, threadfin shad, razorback sucker, green sunfish, goldfish, yellow bass, and tilapia hybrid (*Tilapia* sp.).
- **Razorback sucker:** Two individuals were captured, both previously tagged recaptures (see Ecological & Environmental Context).
- **Absent species:** No bonytail chub or Colorado squawfish were caught. The memo flags the squawfish absence as a multi-year pattern.
- **Future plans:** The next survey is tentatively scheduled for Fall 1997. Interested parties were invited to participate or ask questions by contacting the author.
