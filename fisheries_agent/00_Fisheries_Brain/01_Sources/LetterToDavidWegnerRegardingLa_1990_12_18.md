## Executive Summary
This 1990 letter from Arizona State University researchers Paul C. Marsh and Michael E. Douglas to David Wegner (Glen Canyon Environmental Studies) outlines logistical and biological requirements for obtaining larval [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) for laboratory-based predation and ecology studies tied to the "Ecology and conservation biology of humpback chub in the [[Little Colorado River]]" project. The letter details a proposed captive broodstock program, larval production targets, historical fecundity data from a 1981 spawning event, and concerns regarding the suitability of a proposed hatchery facility. Secondary native fish species are also flagged for inclusion in predation research.

## Background & Description
- **Target life stage:** Swim-up larvae, defined as approximately **10 days post-hatch** and **8.5 mm total length (TL)** at 20°C rearing temperature.
- **Adult broodstock reference data (wild [[Little Colorado River]] population):**
  - Total length (TL) range: **355–409 mm**
  - Weight range: **350–730 g**
- **Reproductive output (historical benchmark, 1981 spawning by Hamman, Willow Beach):**
  - Sample size: 9 wild adults
  - Fecundity per female: ranged from **0 to ~5,000+ eggs**
  - Total eggs produced: **20,200**
  - Fertilization rate: ranged from **0% to 92%**
  - Total larval hatch: reported as **~12,469**

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat:** [[Little Colorado River]], a major tributary spawning ground for the mainstem [[Colorado River]] [[Humpback Chub]] population within the Grand Canyon ecosystem.
- **Reproductive timing:** Spring–summer spawning season, consistent with typical *Gila cypha* life history in the LCR system.
- **Community context:** The letter identifies co-occurring native fishes proposed for predation studies alongside humpback chub:
  - [[Speckled Dace]]
  - [[Bluehead Sucker]]
  - [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]
  - These species are of secondary research priority relative to *Gila cypha* but are relevant to understanding predator-prey dynamics within the same native fish assemblage.
- **Conservation framework:** Explicit distinction is drawn between fish collected for **experimental/research purposes** versus those needed for **refugium stock establishment and maintenance**, reflecting management under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of Endangered Species.

## Key Findings
- **Research timeline constraints:** Due to funding/authorization delays, ASU field studies could not begin before **June 1991**, pushing experimental laboratory work to **autumn 1991** at the earliest, with larval material needed by **spring 1992**.
- **Larval production targets:** A minimum of **5,000 viable larvae** requested per year for **two consecutive years (1992 and 1993)** to support planned experiments.
- **Broodstock recommendation:** Minimum of **15 adult humpback chub (10 females, 5 males)** should be collected from the [[Little Colorado River]] into captivity as early as possible (preferably winter 1990–91) to:
  - Allow acclimation to hatchery conditions
  - Enable culturists to refine propagation techniques during spring 1991
  - Ensure readiness to meet spring 1992 research demand
- **Hatchery site concern:** Significant reservations are raised about **Page Springs State Hatchery** as an appropriate facility for holding/propagating *Gila cypha*, despite the Arizona Game and Fish Department's (AZGFD) willingness to manage the species. Coordination with the **USFWS Office of Endangered Species (Albuquerque)** is recommended to determine the appropriate rearing station.
- **Species prioritization:** Humpback chub propagation and research needs take precedence over work with [[Speckled Dace]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], which will be secondary efforts undertaken by ASU personnel.
- **Institutional distribution:** Copies of the correspondence were sent to USFWS Albuquerque, USFWS Phoenix, and AZGFD, indicating a multi-agency coordination effort for endangered species management under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies program.
