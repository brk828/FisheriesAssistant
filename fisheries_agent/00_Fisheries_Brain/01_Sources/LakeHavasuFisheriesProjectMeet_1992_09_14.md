# Lake Havasu Fisheries Project Meeting (1992)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ConservationManagement

## Executive Summary
This memorandum documents an interagency meeting (14 September 1992) among the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Bureau of Reclamation (USBR), and Ecological Services regarding the **Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program**. The core issue concerns the potential adverse impact of habitat-structure installation on the last remaining populations of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] in Lake Havasu, both federally listed endangered [[Colorado River Native Fishes]]. Rather than halting the broader fisheries project, participants proposed establishing native fish "grow-out" facilities — modeled on the successful [[Yuma Cove]] and [[Davis Cove]] programs at [[Lake Mohave]] — as a mitigation and species-enhancement strategy. Initial planning steps, including site-selection criteria and a joint inspection trip, were agreed upon.

## Background & Description
Direct morphological data on individual specimens is not provided in this memo; however, key size/growth-related details relevant to management are noted:
- Bonytail stocked in a prior Lake Mohave trial were **larger juveniles, 3–8 inches (3–8 in.)** in length at release.
- Recaptured tagged bonytail from this stocking cohort (released ~3 years prior to this meeting) demonstrated **successful post-stocking survival and growth**, supporting the viability of grow-out-then-release strategies for [[Bonytail]].
- Fish raised in [[Yuma Cove]], [[Davis Cove]], and Phoenix-metropolitan-area facilities reportedly showed **increased growth rates** relative to open-lake or hatchery-standard conditions, though specific quantitative growth metrics are not detailed in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type discussed:** Isolated, protected littoral coves within a larger reservoir system, functioning as semi-natural "grow-out" nurseries that reduce predation and competitive pressure on juvenile native fish before lake-wide release.
- **Preferred cove characteristics** (per Tom Burke, USBR):
  - Narrow-mouthed entrance (limits predator/competitor ingress and facilitates seining)
  - Depth greater than 20 feet
  - Seinable shallow area for fish capture/sampling
  - Total area of **1–3 acres**
- **Habitat conflict identified:** Standard habitat-structure placement (e.g., artificial structures added for general/sport [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]] goals) was flagged as potentially detrimental if placed within or near native-fish grow-out coves, due to likely enhancement of habitat for predatory or competing non-native sportfish.
- **Refugia considered and rejected:** Exclusive reliance on existing designated refuges — [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Bill Williams National Wildlife Refuge]] — as the sole conservation mechanism was discussed and dismissed as insufficient on its own.
- **Regional precedent:** The [[Lake Mohave]] cove-rearing model (Yuma Cove, Davis Cove) is treated as the proof-of-concept ecological framework being replicated at Lake Havasu.

## Key Findings
- **Endangered species at risk:** [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] populations in Lake Havasu are the remaining wild stocks threatened by continued habitat modification under the fisheries improvement project.
- **Regulatory status:** Ecological Services (Lesley Fitzpatrick) indicated the project would require **full Section 7 consultation** if proceeding without adequate mitigation for these listed species.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Development of up to **10 native fish grow-out coves** in Lake Havasu, mirroring the Lake Mohave model.
- **Management stipulation:** No artificial habitat structures should be placed in or near coves designated for native fish grow-out, to minimize competitive/predatory pressure from sport fish.
- **Evidence for stocking efficacy:** Recapture of tagged, previously-stocked larger bonytail (3–8 in. at release) in Lake Mohave after ~3 years supports the conclusion that **grow-out-and-release of larger juveniles is an effective recovery strategy**.
- **Next steps agreed upon:**
  1. Establish a cooperative agreement between USFWS and partner agencies (funding/manpower still undetermined).
  2. Set numeric goals for fish stocking over a defined time period (proposed by Bill Grossi, BLM).
  3. Conduct a joint site-selection trip to Yuma, AZ (7–9 October 1992) involving USFWS, BLM, and AGFD to review maps/photographs and shortlist candidate coves.
  4. Perform physical, on-site inspections before final cove selection.
- **Outcome:** All represented agencies agreed in principle to the native fish grow-out research framework, marking the initial administrative step toward implementation within the broader [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]].
