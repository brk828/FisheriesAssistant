# Space Requirements for Gila Taxonomy Research Proposal at Arizona State University

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #taxonomy #molecular-biology #administrative

## Executive Summary

This administrative memorandum documents space allocation negotiations at [[Arizona State University]] for a proposed multi-year research project on the taxonomy of the fish genus [[Gila]]. The proposal, submitted to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], requests $993,693 over 3 years (including $302,583 indirect costs) and requires significant additional space: 600 ft² storage for field equipment, 600 ft² laboratory space for molecular biology work, and 300 ft² office space for personnel. The memoranda outline a strategy involving the Vice President for Research and College of Liberal Arts and Sciences to provide storage sheds and laboratory renovation funding contingent on project award.

## Background & Description

The research team consists of [[W.L. Minckley]] (coordinator), [[T.E. Dowling]] (genetics), [[M.E. Douglas]], and [[P.C. Marsh]] (collections) from the Department of Zoology and [[Center for Environmental Studies]]. The proposed project encompasses three research subareas: morphological studies, protein electrophoresis, and mitochondrial DNA studies. If fully funded, the project would support two postdoctoral associates, an administrative assistant, and several technicians. The Department of Zoology already faces space constraints, spending approximately $1,000 annually to rent trailer storage at Price Road for current field equipment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research focuses on taxonomic clarification of the genus [[Gila]], a group of cyprinid fishes. The project employs multiple methodological approaches including traditional morphological analysis, [[Protein Electrophoresis]], and [[Mitochondrial DNA]] studies to resolve taxonomic questions. The work requires extensive field equipment for specimen collection and specialized laboratory equipment including incubator, ultracold freezer, ultracentrifuge, high-speed centrifuge, and gel driers for molecular analyses.

## Key Findings

**Space Requirements Identified:**
- 600 ft² storage space needed for new field equipment, to be housed in metal storage sheds with concrete floors and roll-up doors along the north wall of the Aquatic-Terrestrial facility
- 600 ft² laboratory space required for molecular biology component, potentially through renovation of Life Sciences room 281 (441 ft²) plus additional cold room removal
- 300 ft² office space for two postdoctoral associates and administrative assistant, to be provided through departmental reallocation

**Funding Strategy:**
- Office of Vice President for Research committed to provide storage space either through space allocation or funding purchase/assembly of new storage structures
- VPR to fund renovation of approximately 600 ft² in Zoology for molecular biology laboratory through building renewal or other mechanisms
- Total project budget: $993,693 over 3 years with $302,583 overhead cost recovery

**Institutional Coordination:**
- Staging area strategy developed for south end of Life Science compound during construction
- Chain-link fence with green plastic screening to separate compound areas during construction phase
- Future block wall construction contingent on area not being needed for new Animal Care Facility
- Space and Resources Committee charged with developing plan for new faculty space allocation
