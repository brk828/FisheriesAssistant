## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 correspondence from a researcher at [[Arizona State University]] (Department of Zoology) to the Vice President for Conservation Programs at [[American Rivers]], outlining proposed first-year deliverables for a project funded through the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]]. The letter is administrative in nature rather than a primary data report, but it establishes a data-classification framework for documenting native freshwater fish occurrence records across the [[Gila River Basin]]. The core scientific content concerns a tiered locality-data system (first-, second-, and third-order records) intended to support basin-wide distribution mapping and a preliminary gap-analysis for future field surveys of native fish populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat scope**: Freshwater river systems within the [[Gila River Basin]], a major tributary system of the [[Colorado River]] drainage in the southwestern United States (Arizona/New Mexico region).
- **Data classification framework** (a proxy for habitat/occurrence documentation reliability):
  - **First-order locality data**: Sites supported by preserved voucher specimens.
  - **Second-order locality data**: Sites recorded in peer-reviewed literature.
  - **Third-order locality data**: Sites recorded only in "gray literature" (unpublished reports, agency documents), lacking specimen vouchers or peer-reviewed support.
- This tiered system implies an ecological monitoring approach prioritizing verifiable, specimen-based occurrence records over anecdotal or unverified sightings — a standard practice in [[Conservation Biogeography]] and range-mapping for imperiled native fish assemblages.
- The overarching goal is to identify survey gaps ("preliminary assessment of needs for additional surveys") within the basin, implying concern for incomplete knowledge of native fish distribution — a common issue for threatened and endangered fishes of the arid Southwest, such as those historically found in the [[Gila River]] and its tributaries.

## Key Findings
- The project proposes a **basemap of first- and second-order locality data** for native freshwater fishes of the Gila Basin as a primary first-year deliverable, to be completed by end of the funding period (originally projected end of February 1995).
- A **second basemap incorporating third-order (gray literature) locality data** would be produced insofar as time and resources allow within the same period.
- A **preliminary needs assessment for additional field surveys** would be generated based on gaps identified in the mapped locality data.
- The letter notes logistical readiness at [[Arizona State University]] (space and infrastructure already available) and offers an "in-kind" contribution statement contingent on funding notification.
- Project timeline is explicitly contingent on securing funding, with the author suggesting the nominal March 1994 start date may need revision pending fiscal support from the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]].
