# Biology Committee Meeting Summary — May 29, 1997

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species #cyprinidae #catostomidae #colorado-river-basin

## Executive Summary

This document is a **programmatic/administrative meeting summary** from the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] Biology Committee, convened in Grand Junction, CO. Rather than presenting novel primary data, it synthesizes ongoing recovery efforts, population monitoring conclusions, and management debates for four federally endangered big-river fishes: [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically "Colorado Squawfish," *Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and the [[Gila Chub]]/roundtail-chub species complex. Central topics include a review of Doug Osmundson's trilogy report on Colorado Pikeminnow population trends in the Upper Colorado River, razorback sucker broodstock management (including water-quality risks at the Ouray hatchery facility), nonnative fish control policy, floodplain/levee restoration strategy for endangered fish habitat, and genetics/taxonomy reporting delays for *Gila* species.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological data is minimal in this administrative text, but the following taxon-specific notes are extractable:

- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Large-bodied piscivorous cyprinid; population parameters discussed include an estimated **survival rate of 0.36** and recruitment-limited population dynamics.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Distinguished by the characteristic dorsal "razorback" keel typical of *Catostomidae*; broodstock individuals referenced were **wild-caught Green River fish held at the Ouray National Fish Hatchery**, with staged reproductive ripening noted (only 1 male, 2 females of 14 held fish ripened in the reporting season).
- **[[Gila Chub|Gila spp.]]**: A "Gila Taxonomy Project – Morphology" report (Douglas, pending revision) indicates active taxonomic/morphological differentiation work within the *Gila* complex, including concerns over **hybridization** among Gila taxa.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*): Referenced regarding broodstock development processes (Czapla/Thompson), implying constrained founder stock and genetic bottleneck concerns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range**: The affected species occupy the mainstem and tributary reaches of the [[Upper Colorado River]] system, including the [[Green River]], [[Gunnison River]], [[Duchesne River]], [[Little Snake River]], and [[White River]], spanning multiple biological "strata" used for stratified population sampling (Strata 1–7 discussed; Stratum 4, [[Cataract Canyon|CAHN Canyon]], noted as unsampled).
- **Floodplain/backwater dependency**: Endangered fish recovery strategy emphasizes **floodplain and levee-removal restoration** (e.g., "Old Charlie Wash") as critical rearing habitat. Two management approaches are contrasted:
  - *Highly structured/controlled* floodplain sites (short-term benefit, active management of grow-out areas).
  - *Naturalized* levee-removal approaches (long-term, self-sustaining population benefit) — identified as requiring further study via a **levee removal study**.
- **Nonnative species interaction**: A major ecological stressor identified is **nonnative fish predation and competition**. Management debate centers on whether to prioritize (a) restoring endangered-fish-only response habitat vs. (b) directly addressing the "nonnative question" through isolation of early life stages in predator-free grow-out ponds.
- **Carrying capacity**: Explicitly flagged as an unresolved ecological unknown — recommendation added to Osmundson's report to formally **assess carrying capacity** of the Colorado Pikeminnow population, since removing recruitment bottlenecks alone will not guarantee population growth if carrying capacity is limiting.
- **Hatchery/genetic refugia**: [[Horsethief State Wildlife Area]] and [[Black Rocks]] serve as genetic refugia for Colorado Pikeminnow, treated as **genetically equivalent populations** consolidated into a single broodstock lot.

## Key Findings

- **Population trend**: Over the last 100 years, the Colorado Pikeminnow population trend is **downward**; however, over the most recent **20 years, the trend has been stable**, based on Osmundson's baseline analysis.
- **Survival/recruitment modeling**: At a survival rate of **0.36** with unchanged recruitment, the population **will not grow** — underscoring recruitment as the primary limiting bottleneck, with carrying capacity as a probable secondary constraint.
- **Recommended mortality-reduction actions**: The report identifies **four documented mortality sources**, primarily supported by literature rather than direct field observation.
- **Broodstock risk management**: 14 wild-caught Green River [[Razorback Sucker]] broodfish at Ouray were identified as **at-risk due to water quality problems**; the Biology Committee approved relocating them to [[Horsethief]] pending hatchery construction completion, with future consideration of using them (or "nearest neighbor" fish) to supplement limited Colorado River basin broodstock.
- **Genetics reporting failure**: The **Colorado Squawfish (Pikeminnow) Genetics Report** was significantly delayed due to lack of author cooperation; the Committee escalated the issue via formal letter from the FWS Regional Director.
- **Nonnative species policy (Question 3)**: Majority opinion held that the **existing 1997 management balance** between endangered-species propagation and nonnative control was appropriate, to be adjusted annually via the work-planning process rather than through a fixed policy change. Minority dissenting opinions were filed by Mike Carnevale and Tom Pitts, citing the need to reevaluate razorback stocking given population status.
- **Floodplain management policy (Question 4)**: Majority consensus favored a dual approach — **short-term structured floodplain sites** (e.g., Old Charlie Wash) for immediate benefit, and **long-term levee removal** for self-sustaining recovery, pending outcomes of a dedicated levee-removal study.
- **Programmatic delays**: Multiple recovery deliverables were reported behind schedule, including the **Gila Workshop** (delayed pending data analysis, ~September 1997), the **Habitat Objectives (HO) development** for New Mexico/Utah (deadline slipped from June 1 to September 11), and the **Gila Taxonomy/Morphology** and **Scale Analysis** reports.
- **Interagency coordination**: A joint researchers meeting with the [[American Fisheries Society]] was deemed infeasible for the year due to scheduling conflicts with the Program's January information-gathering timeline for guidance development.
