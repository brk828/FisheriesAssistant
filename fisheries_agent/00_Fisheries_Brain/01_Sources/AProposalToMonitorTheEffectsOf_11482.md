# A Proposal to Monitor the Effects of Interim Flow from Glen Canyon Dam on the Fishery Resources of the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #riverine-ecology #cyprinidae #[[Glen Canyon Dam]]

## Executive Summary

This 1992 proposal by the **Hualapai Tribe** outlines a monitoring program to assess the ecological effects of **Interim Flows** from **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]** on the fishery and aquatic resources of the **Lower Colorado River**, specifically the reach from **Diamond Creek to Lake Mead (River Mile 226–286)**. This segment had previously been unstudied under the **[[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES)** Phase I program, which focused primarily on the reach from the dam to Diamond Creek. The proposal establishes five core objectives: monitoring adult fish populations, larval/juvenile native fish, non-native piscivore predation dynamics, tributary/shoreline habitat conditions, and the aquatic food base (algae, invertebrates). The study was designed to integrate with ongoing GCES research (Arizona Game & Fish Department, Bio/West) and feed into a shared **Geographic Information System (GIS)** database, ultimately supporting long-term flow-management decisions affecting native and non-native fish populations in the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem.

## Background & Description

- **Study Area**: Lower Colorado River from **Diamond Creek to Lake Mead**, extending into the upper reaches of Lake Mead itself, encompassing River Miles 226–286 within the [[Lower Grand Canyon]].
- **Administrative Context**: Managed jointly by the **Hualapai Tribe** and the **National Park Service**. This reach had not been subject to consistent scientific monitoring, unlike the upstream Glen Canyon Dam-to-Diamond Creek segment studied by **Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGF)** and **Bio/West**.
- **Driving Factors**:
  - Initiation of the **Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement**.
  - Formal inclusion of the Hualapai Tribe in dam impact assessment.
  - Continued **declining surface elevation of Lake Mead**, which increased the riverine habitat area exposed to dam operational effects.
- **Prior Data Sources**: Limited baseline data existed from:
  - [[Carothers & Minckley 1981]] — fish, invertebrate, and aquatic plant survey (Lee's Ferry to Separation Rapid).
  - [[Maddux et al. 1987]] — effects of varied flow regimes on Glen and Grand Canyon aquatic resources.
  - [[Minckley et al. 1981]] — observations on **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) in the Colorado and Little Colorado Rivers.
  - U.S. Department of the Interior GCES Final Report (1988) and Phase II Integrated Research Plan (1990).
- **Key Ecological Concern**: Interaction between native fish populations (of cultural and resource importance to the Hualapai Tribe) and the **expansion of non-native fish species** moving upstream into the lower river as Lake Mead levels dropped.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Interim Flow Parameters of Concern**:
  - Monthly discharge volume
  - Maximum and minimum flow levels
  - Daily stage change (ramping rates)
- **Three Levels of Ecological Concern**:
  1. Short- and long-term impacts to native fish populations, compounded by non-native fish expansion.
  2. Short-term impacts of flow alteration on available habitat for native and non-native fish.
  3. Short-term impacts of flow alteration on the aquatic **food base**.
- **Habitat Types Targeted for Study**:
  - Shallow main-channel habitats
  - Tributary mouths (notably **Spencer Creek** and **Diamond Creek**)
  - Shoreline backwaters
  - Mud flats and isolated backwaters in the upper Lake Mead transition zone
- **Food Base Components of Interest**:
  - **[[Cladophora]]** (filamentous green algae)
  - Aquatic **Diptera**
  - **Diatoms**
  - Aquatic macroinvertebrates and invertebrates generally
  - **[[Oscillatoria]]** (cyanobacteria)
- **Water Quality Parameters Monitored**:
  - Dissolved organic carbon
  - Phosphorus (all forms)
  - Nitrogen (nitrate, nitrite, ammonia)
  - Water hardness and alkalinity
  - Chlorophyll *a* (proxy for algal standing crop/productivity)
- **Habitat Degradation Concerns**: Sediment aggradation/degradation at tributary mouths and shallow shoreline zones tied directly to flow regime changes.

## Key Findings

*(Note: This document is a research proposal, not a results paper — "findings" below represent proposed objectives, methodological framework, and anticipated impact pathways rather than completed empirical results.)*

### Objective 1 — Adult Fish Distribution & Behavior
- Focus on native and non-native adult fish response to monthly volumes, max/min flows, and ramping rates.
- Capture methods: **electrofishing** (Coffelt CPS technology), **trammel nets**, **hoop nets** (baited for catfish).
- Nets checked at minimum 12-hour intervals.
- All captured fish measured; subsample aged via **otoliths** or **pectoral spines**.
- Non-native fish stomachs collected seasonally to assess food preferences/predation.

### Objective 2 — Larval & Juvenile Native Fish
- Life stages considered **most vulnerable** to short-term flow fluctuations and habitat availability changes.
- Tributary sampling (Spencer Creek, Diamond Creek) conducted **minimum 3x/year**.
- **Larval drift nets** deployed during active reproduction periods to study drift rate and timing.
- Fish locations correlated with flow measurements and depth via microhabitat mapping.

### Objective 3 — Non-Native Piscivore Predation Dynamics
- Investigates reproduction, food habits, and habitat use of **piscivorous non-native fish** predating on native species.
- Shares capture methodology with Objective 1 (electrofishing, trammel/hoop nets).
- Seasonal stomach content analysis to quantify predation pressure on native fish.

### Objective 4 — Tributary & Shoreline Habitat Conditions
- Two-tier sampling intensity:
  - **Level A**: Fish sampling (nets, seines, electrofishing) + substrate, temperature, depth, current velocity measurements + site mapping.
  - **Level B**: All Level A elements + long-term change tracking via **remote sensing water chemistry recorders** and **remote cameras** to monitor usable habitat surface area over time.

### Objective 5 — Aquatic Food Base & Productivity
- Measures **photosynthesis/productivity** and **algal standing crops** (via chlorophyll *a*) across mainstem, tributary mouth, and backwater sites, seasonally.
- **Benthic metabolism chambers** used to partition planktonic vs. benthic contributions to system production.
- Filtered samples preserved via liquid nitrogen chamber prior to lab analysis.

### Program Integration & Deliverables
- Data integrated into the **GCES Geographic Information System (GIS)** as a shared long-term baseline database.
- Coordinated field logistics with AGF to reduce redundant sampling trips.
- Deliverables: quarterly reports, annual draft/final reports (1992–1994), and peer-reviewed journal submissions.
- Total two-year project budget: **$355,052** (Year 1: $189,076; Year 2: $165,976).
- Key equipment: Coffelt CPS electrofishing pulsator, benthic metabolism chambers (2), Passive Integrated Transponder (PIT) tag reader system, trammel nets, current meter.

### Personnel & Institutional Roles
- **Arizona Game & Fish Department**: primary fishery science expertise/direction.
- **Travis Magenta** and **Donald Bay**: primary project leadership.
- **GCES Office**: technical support, GIS surveying equipment, remote sensing/image processing support.
- **GCES**: river logistics and permitting coordination with **U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service**, AGF, and **National Park Service**.
