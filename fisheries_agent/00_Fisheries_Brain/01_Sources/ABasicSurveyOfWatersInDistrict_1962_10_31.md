# A Basic Survey of Waters in District IV

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #Clupeidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This completion report from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] documents basic limnological survey work conducted in **District IV** during the 1961–1962 period, focused on backwater lakes associated with the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] along the lower [[Colorado River]]. Due to competing project priorities, survey effort was limited, but preliminary physical, chemical, and biological data collection was initiated on three previously unstudied lakes: [[Island Lake]], [[Cable Lake]], and [[Federal Lake]]. Additional work continued on [[No Name Lake #1]], including a preliminary investigation into the reproductive cycle of the [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*, noted in text as *petenensis*). The report also references fish kill investigations at [[Painted Rock Dam]] and in the [[Colorado River]] main channel between [[Laguna Dam]] and Yuma, though detailed findings from those kills are not presented in this excerpt. The core objective of the broader survey program is to determine whether physical, chemical, and biological water properties exert a predominant influence on fish populations in District IV waters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Backwater lakes of the lower [[Colorado River]] within the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]], representing a complex transitional riverine-lacustrine ecosystem.
- **Comparative limnology**: Hydrographic data comparison indicates [[Island Lake]] is limnologically similar to [[No Name Lake #1]] (per prior Table VIII, Project F-7-R-4), whereas [[Cable Lake]] and [[Federal Lake]] are described as limnologically distinct from all other study lakes in the program.
- **Predator-forage relationship**: The [[Threadfin Shad]] is highlighted as a species of interest due to its potential role in the predator-forage dynamic and its broader effect on the total fisheries potential of the region — implying its function as a key forage fish within this ecosystem.
- **Anthropogenic stressors**: Fish kill events were documented at [[Painted Rock Dam]] and in the [[Colorado River]] channel above Yuma/below [[Laguna Dam]], suggesting water quality or hydrological disturbances impacting fish populations in the district.

## Key Findings
- Survey activity in District IV was limited during this reporting period due to higher-priority competing project work.
- Preliminary limnological data (Table I) were collected monthly, covering parameters including **turbidity, alkalinity, hardness, chlorides, total solids, sulfates, total nitrogen, nitrate, phosphates, and plankton density** (measured per cubic unit).
- Total plankton counts across sampled months ranged widely, from as low as **1.86** to as high as **16.78**, with several months recorded as "unmeasured."
- [[Island Lake]] shows limnological comparability to [[No Name Lake #1]], while [[Cable Lake]] and [[Federal Lake]] represent ecologically distinct backwater lake types.
- A preliminary study on the **reproductive cycle of the threadfin shad** was initiated at [[No Name Lake #1]].
- Fish kill investigations were formally reported for the [[Painted Rock Dam]] and the [[Colorado River]] main channel (above Yuma, below [[Laguna Dam]]), though outcomes/causes are not detailed in this excerpt.
- **Recommendation**: Continued periodic limnological observation by wildlife managers is advised to build baseline data for future fisheries investigations.
- **Recommendation**: Further study of threadfin shad life history and its role in predator-forage relationships is recommended to better assess its impact on overall fisheries potential in the area.
