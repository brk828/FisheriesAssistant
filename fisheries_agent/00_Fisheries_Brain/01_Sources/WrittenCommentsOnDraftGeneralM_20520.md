# Defenders of Wildlife Comments on the Draft General Management Plan for Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This is a written comment letter, dated July 10, 1995, from [[Defenders of Wildlife]] (represented by Wildlife Counsel Gregory J. Sater) to the Superintendent of [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]]. It responds to the Draft General Management Plan, Development Concept Plan, and Environmental Impact Statement. Defenders, which describes itself as representing over 100,000 members, welcomes several elements of the plan, including marked trails at Quitobaquito Springs and proposed wilderness designations. It concludes, however, that the plan does not meet the requirements of the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) or the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The central objection is that the plan inadequately addresses the protection, survival, and recovery of threatened and endangered species, especially where increased visitation, road expansion, and groundwater loss are concerned.

## Background & Description

The letter reviews the draft plan species by species and threat by threat. Its main points are:

- **Positive elements:** Defenders supports a marked trail network at [[Quitobaquito Springs]], which could reduce trampling, and the conversion of "potential" wilderness areas to designated wilderness. It also supports naming the Monument a National Park.
- **Legal standard:** NEPA requires full analysis of all likely impacts, including impacts to endangered species. The ESA requires federal agencies to avoid jeopardy to listed species and to take positive recovery steps.
- **Scope:** The letter addresses individual species, the Monument-wide species list, ESA consultation documents, cumulative impacts, the National Park Service's (NPS) top stated concerns, and the regional groundwater problem.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sonoran Pronghorn.** The Monument contains some of the last known habitat of this listed species. Aerial survey data from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], based at [[Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge]], indicate that the dwindling population is being pushed out of the eastern section of the Monument. The likely cause is the barrier effect of [[State Route 85]], which "effectively fragment[s] the monument into two units." Low-level flights from the [[Barry M. Goldwater Air Force Range]] and human visitation also affect the species.

**Lesser Long-Nosed Bat.** The Monument holds the largest known maternity colony of this endangered bat in the United States. The draft plan proposes expanding [[Alamo Canyon Campground]], which lies near the mine adit where the colony roosts. The plan admits that hikers attracted to the adit could cause "potentially adverse effects on the species' survival," but it offers no management strategy.

**Mexican Rosy Boa.** A candidate for listing, this species has been nearly extirpated from the Monument by road mortality along State Route 85. The rocky outcrops beside the road are described as one of the few areas of potential habitat, yet the plan proposes new road pullouts and a trail through them.

**Quitobaquito Springs ecosystem.** The springs are designated critical habitat for the Desert Pupfish. Several other species share this riparian area: the Quitobaquito Snail and Lesser Long-Nosed Bat (candidates), [[Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy Owl]] (proposed for listing), and [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (nearly extirpated from the Monument). Visitor trampling and disturbance of the springs are ongoing.

**Groundwater.** The springs depend on the same aquifer as the [[Sonoyta Valley]] in Mexico. The plan reports that annual pumping in Mexico is two and a half times the recharge rate, and that the water table below Quitobaquito has dropped ten feet in two decades. The decline is accelerating.

**Other listed and candidate species.** The Monument hosts 27 threatened, endangered, and candidate species, along with a list of "species of special concern." Examples include the Acuña Cactus and Trelease Agave (both candidates), transient birds such as the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher and Brown Pelican, and resident species such as the [[Desert Tortoise]] and [[Chuckwalla]].

## Key Findings

1. **Sonoran Pronghorn (listed):** The plan does not analyze impacts from campground and trail expansion, State Route 85, low-level military flights, or visitation. It also has no monitoring plan. Defenders asks for an analysis of reduced visitation in prime habitat and for underpasses across State Route 85. The plan's failure to consider these alternatives violates NEPA and the ESA.

2. **Lesser Long-Nosed Bat (endangered):** The plan proposes campground expansion near a major maternity colony without a protective strategy. Defenders argues that closing existing campsites and analyzing reduced visitation are reasonable alternatives that were ignored, violating both NEPA and the ESA.

3. **Mexican Rosy Boa (candidate):** The plan proposes pullouts and a trail through the species' last stronghold. Its justification, that traffic mortality will "eventually" eliminate the boa, is called capricious, since the species is still present. Defenders says no alternatives were considered, which violates NEPA and the ESA.

4. **Desert Pupfish and Quitobaquito Springs:** The plan itself warns that habitat degradation "could potentially jeopardize" the species. Defenders asks for specific measures, including population monitoring, visitor oversight, removal of nonnative fish (a black bullhead was found in 1993), and water quality monitoring. It objects to the plan's ambiguous statement on carrying capacity (Draft Plan at 20), its lack of impact quantification, and its proposal to move the parking area and trailhead.

5. **Shared riparian species:** Defenders says the Pupfish concerns apply equally to the Quitobaquito Snail, Sonoran Mud Turtle, Lesser Long-Nosed Bat, and Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy Owl. It calls for detailed impact analysis, mitigation to insignificance, and consideration of alternatives.

6. **Monument-wide species:** The plan mentions 27 threatened, endangered, and candidate species, plus species of special concern, but has no strategy to protect or recover them. Defenders calls for a comprehensive species management plan, which the draft plan (at 74) admits is missing.

7. **ESA Section 7 consultation:** The record contains only a two-page FWS letter listing species that may occur. It does not include a biological assessment, a biological opinion, or reasonable and prudent alternatives. The "tables" referenced in that letter were also not provided. Defenders asks that all consultation documents be attached to the EIS.

8. **Cumulative impacts:** NEPA requires analysis of off-site effects, including grazing to the north and east (on [[Bureau of Land Management]] and Indian lands), low-altitude Air Force flying to the west, and agricultural expansion and aquifer depletion in Mexico to the south.

9. **NPS top concerns:** The plan names three priority threats: herbicide and pesticide drift, nonnative species invasion, and groundwater depletion in the Sonoyta Valley. It provides no analysis or management strategy for any of them.

10. **Groundwater loss:** Defenders asks for a management strategy and water monitoring at Quitobaquito. Options it lists include diplomatic appeals to Mexico, a [[North American Free Trade Agreement]] petition, and possible recharge using water from the [[Central Arizona Project]] or the Lower Colorado River.

11. **State Route 85:** The plan acknowledges that the road is "annihilating" species, including the Rosy Boa (cited at Draft Plan xii and 18). It nonetheless defers analysis of rerouting to a future "separate planning effort," citing controversy. Defenders argues this deferral is not justified under NEPA or the ESA.

12. **Required action:** Defenders calls on the NPS to analyze all reasonable, practicable alternatives, to take affirmative steps to remove jeopardy to the listed Sonoran Pronghorn and candidate Rosy Boa, and to propose solutions within its means. It states that the plan must be revised before it complies with NEPA and the ESA.
