# Netting of High Levee Pond and Growout Site Assessment for Big River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker field office (dated 6 January 1994) reports a overnight netting survey of the [[High Levee Pond]] at [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] and a follow-up site visit to [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] to evaluate ponds for razorback sucker growout. The survey captured 13 [[Razorback Sucker]] and one [[Bonytail Chub]], with no introduced fish taken. Both species were released back into the pond. The memo concludes that several Imperial sites have good potential for development as razorback sucker growout facilities, and that the Cibola refuge manager would be informed that development of an on-refuge facility would be pursued in 1994.

## Background & Description

The memo is addressed from the Project Coordinator to the AZFRO project leader. Its purpose is twofold:

- To determine whether the High Levee Pond, a water body on Cibola refuge, held a recruiting population of large-river native fishes.
- To assess whether ponds at Imperial refuge were suitable for raising razorback suckers and bonytail chub in captivity before release.

The two species are federally listed big-river fishes of the Colorado River system. The memo treats them as the target species for both the netting survey and the growout planning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The High Levee Pond was netted overnight, from about 1700 hrs on 28 December 1993 to 0800 hrs on 29 December 1993. Refuge staff assisted with the work. The memo describes the pond as a potential recruitment stock site, meaning a place where young fish from wild or stocked parents could be surviving and maturing. Because the captured fish were a mix of sizes, the authors treated the pond as a likely source of recruitment rather than a one-time stocking.

The Imperial refuge visit assessed several ponds and embankment areas. One pond, identified as Cibola-style “Cibola Pond” in the memo’s wording, had previously been stocked with razorback suckers several years earlier. The memo notes that several areas visited had “great potential” for development.

## Key Findings

**High Levee Pond netting survey (28–29 December 1993)**

- Razorback Sucker: 13 individuals captured.
  - Mean total length: 201 mm (n = 13).
  - Weight data were reported, but the OCR text for the range is partly illegible. Weight-range values should be checked against the original.
- Bonytail Chub: 1 individual captured.
  - Total length and weight were recorded; the bonytail weighed 220 g.
  - The fish was tagged with an individual identification tag.
- No introduced (non-native) fish were captured.
- All fish were released at the capture site.

**Release and follow-up measurements**

- Razorback suckers were released on one date and bonytail chub on a second date. The release dates are partly illegible in the OCR text; the memo gives "October" for both.
- At the time of release, the mean total length of razorback suckers was reported as a value that is partly illegible in the OCR text (n = 100).
- The mean total length of the bonytail chub at release was 62 mm.
- The memo states that the bonytails had not grown substantially over about two months, with the tagged individual still close to 220 mm.
- The authors describe the captured fish as representative of the pond’s recruitment stock.

**Imperial National Wildlife Refuge site visit**

- Refuge manager [[Andy Loranger]] accompanied the visit to several ponds and embankments to judge their suitability for growout facilities.
- A pond there had been stocked with razorback suckers several years before the visit.
- Several areas were judged to have “great potential” for development.

**Planned actions and conclusions**

- The Project Coordinator committed to pursue developing an on-refuge growout facility at Imperial refuge in 1994 for razorback suckers and bonytail chub.
- The memo’s overall conclusion is that the High Levee Pond supports both target species and that Imperial refuge is a promising location for growout work.

**Data limitations**

- Several numeric values (razorback length range, weight range, release dates, and the mean razorback length at release) are illegible in the scanned text. They should be verified against the source before citation.
