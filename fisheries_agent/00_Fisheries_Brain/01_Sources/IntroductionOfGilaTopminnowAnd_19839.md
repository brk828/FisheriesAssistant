# Introduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish into Selected Waters in the Phoenix District

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

A June 4, 1984 memorandum from the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Phoenix District Office to the State Director transmits a Briefing Summary, Supplemental Environmental Assessment, and Operational Plan for introducing [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] to eleven sites. The effort is a cooperative project of BLM, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). It aims to recover two federally or state-listed nongame desert fishes and to mitigate the loss of the [[Cocio Wash Topminnow]] population, which was extirpated by copper mine spills in 1981. Three sites receive full [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) protection, and the remaining eight are designated experimental under Section 10(j). The target introduction date is July 16–20, 1984.

## Background & Description

**Species.** Both fishes historically co-occurred throughout the Lower Colorado River basin below 5,000 feet. The Gila topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) was described in 1853 from the Santa Cruz River near Tucson. The desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) was first described from the San Pedro River. Both are Gila River tributaries. Hubbs and Miller (1941) described the topminnow as "one of the commonest fishes in the southern part of the Colorado River drainage basin."

**Status.** The topminnow is federally endangered and survives in nine naturally occurring populations. Two natural populations, Mammoth and Cocio Wash, have been eliminated by habitat alteration. The desert pupfish is a Group 1 species under AGFD, meaning it is considered totally eliminated from Arizona, though not globally extinct. Both species declined through habitat loss or degradation and through introduced non-native fishes. Populations diminished after [[Mosquitofish]] were introduced for mosquito control in 1926.

**Agency context.** In 1981, AGFD and USFWS asked BLM to help reestablish self-sustaining populations on Phoenix District lands. Recurrent copper spills from the ASARCO Silver Bell Mine in August and September 1981 eliminated the Cocio Wash population. More than 30 candidate sites were jointly evaluated by BLM and AGFD, and 11 were selected. A [[Recovery Plan]] for the topminnow, signed by USFWS Region 2 in March 1984, names reintroduction on public lands as a major recovery criterion and includes an implementation schedule and down- or delisting criteria.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Prior introductions and refugia.** Desert pupfish were introduced to Peoples Canyon and Tres Alamos Falls in August 1982 under a cooperative MOU. In December 1983, pupfish were salvaged from [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]], an AGFD refugium for both species, after renovation and moved to three public-land sites: Howard Well (Safford District), Peoples Canyon, and Mesquite Spring. All transplants except Tres Alamos Falls are surviving and reproducing. These are the only known locations of the Gila River subspecies of desert pupfish in Arizona.

**Site network.** The eleven sites are all in the Phoenix District and fall into two BLM resource areas: the Lower Gila Resource Area (LGRA) and the Phoenix Resource Area (PRA). They lie in AGFD Regions II through VI. Representative sites include:
- Peoples Canyon (LGRA, AGFD Region III), a pupfish site and a protected topminnow site
- Tres Alamos Falls (LGRA, Region IV), protected topminnow site
- Grapevine Spring (Yerba Mansa) (LGRA, Region IV), protected topminnow site
- Mesquite Spring (PRA, Region V), pupfish transplant site
- Dandrea Spring (PRA, Region II), Doe Peak Spring (PRA, Region VI), Chalky Spring (PRA, Region VI), "Sooz" Spring (PRA, Region IV), Hidden Spring and Hackberry Spring (LGRA, Region IV), and an unnamed tributary to Cottonwood Gulch (PRA, Region IV)

**Regulatory framework.** The USFWS designates the eight non-protected sites as experimental under ESA Section 10(j), as amended October 13, 1982. No critical habitat will be designated for any protected or experimental site. Introduction depends on approval of a cooperative Operational Plan that defines the roles of each agency.

## Key Findings

- **Cooperative recovery:** BLM, USFWS, and AGFD are jointly introducing Gila topminnow and desert pupfish to 11 Phoenix District sites.
- **Mitigation:** The project offsets the local extirpation of the Cocio Wash topminnow population caused by copper mine spills in 1981.
- **Protection tiers:** Three sites (Peoples Canyon, Tres Alamos Falls, Grapevine Spring) receive full ESA protection; eight are experimental under Section 10(j).
- **No critical habitat:** None will be designated for any site.
- **Stakeholder support:** All permittees and interested parties, including the Arizona congressional delegation, were contacted. A public meeting on November 21, 1983 drew no adverse comment or opposition.
- **Target schedule:** Introduction is planned for July 16–20, 1984, pending approval of the Operational Plan.
- **Recovery outcome:** The goal is self-sustaining populations of two nongame desert fishes, described as the most endangered and rapidly disappearing wildlife group in the continental United States.
- **Prior success:** Pupfish transplants (except Tres Alamos Falls) are surviving and reproducing, and the sites are the only known Arizona locations of the Gila River pupfish subspecies.
