# Colorado River Recovery Program: Aspinall Unit Larval Drift Study FY93

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #larval-ecology #flow-management

## Executive Summary

This FY 1993 annual report documents ongoing drift-net sampling to evaluate [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) larval production in the [[Colorado River]] in response to flow manipulations from the [[Aspinall Unit]]. Principal investigators Leo Lentsch and Melissa Trammell from the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] conducted daily dawn sampling at two sites (Westwater Canyon and Moab) from June 20 to August 20, 1993. The study aims to document spawning timing, determine peaks in larval abundance, and assess larval transport into nursery habitats. Delayed spawning in 1993 relative to 1992 was attributed to late high flows and colder temperatures. Complete 1992 data showed larval collection peaks between July 15-20, with recurring patterns of squawfish numbers peaking 6-7 days following hydrograph peaks early in the season, decreasing to 1-3 days later in the season.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] initiated this study (Project 19-B) in 1992 as an ongoing effort to evaluate biological responses to flow recommendations associated with the [[Aspinall Unit]]. The study targets [[Colorado Squawfish]], an endangered cyprinid species endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. 

Sampling methodology employs three conical plankton drift nets at each site, each 4m long with 0.5m mouth diameter tapering to 10cm, ending in removable collection buckets. Nets are deployed in shallow (<0.5m) areas with fairly laminar flow over cobble or gravel/sand substrate, positioned perpendicular to flow. Mechanical flow meters secured in each net mouth quantify water volume sampled. Daily sampling occurs at dawn (0600-0800 hours) for 1-2 hours during the June-August spawning period. Collected larvae are preserved in ethanol for subsequent aging using otoliths to determine precise spawning times.

Two sampling sites were relocated in 1993: the [[Westwater Canyon]] site moved from RM 132 to RM 127.5 at the Ranger Station (due to road access issues), and the [[Moab]] site moved from RM 64.7 to RM 65.4 (due to high water making the original site impassible). Site selection was based on previous collection locations of young-of-year (YOY) squawfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] system experienced notably different hydrological conditions between 1992 and 1993. In 1993, relatively high water and late high flows combined with colder temperatures resulted in delayed spawning compared to 1992. In 1992, the first squawfish was collected at [[Westwater Canyon]] on June 26, whereas no squawfish were identified in samples through July 8, 1993 at either site. This delayed spawning was corroborated by low YOY squawfish collections during concurrent nursery habitat studies.

The [[Moab]] site is characterized by relatively low current velocity, sand substrate, and a shallow shelf extending across the river. Water levels fluctuated daily throughout the sampling period, particularly early on, though decreasing steadily overall. Scattered tamarisk vegetation around the site occasionally influenced net placement and potentially affected catch rates.

The [[Westwater Canyon]] site features swift current over large cobble substrate (3-8 inch diameter) that is fairly well cemented. The river slope is steeper than at Moab, requiring nearly daily net repositioning as water receded.

The study provides critical information on relationships between relative reproductive success and annual flow regimes, offering a relative measure of larval input into downstream nursery habitats. This data directly supports flow management decisions for the [[Aspinall Unit]] and contributes to recovery efforts for this endangered species.

## Key Findings

**1993 Field Season Results:**
- Sampling conducted June 20 - August 20, 1993 at both sites
- No sample days missed at either location
- No [[Colorado Squawfish]] larvae identified in samples examined through July 8, 1993
- Delayed spawning attributed to late high flows and colder temperatures in 1993 relative to 1992
- Remaining 1993 samples sent to [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at [[Colorado State University]] for identification and enumeration in November

**1992 Completed Data Analysis:**
- Collections at both [[Moab]] and [[Westwater Canyon]] sites peaked between July 15-20
- Six peaks in squawfish collection at Moab site: June 29, July 3, 17, 19, 29, and August 11
- Five peaks at Westwater site (lacking the final August peak observed at Moab)
- Recurring temporal pattern: squawfish numbers peaked 6-7 days following hydrograph peaks for first four peaks, then decreased to 3 days and 1 day for subsequent peaks
- On three occasions, collections peaked on the same day at both sites
- Peak date collections showed 3-5 times more squawfish collected at [[Moab]] compared to [[Westwater Canyon]], despite majority of total fish being collected at Westwater
- Zero collections on July 20-22 were actual zeros (not missed sampling days), while July 25-27 were missed days

**Methodological Notes:**
- Early sampling period at Moab may lack data consistency compared to later period due to fluctuating water levels
- Occasional net placement behind tamarisk bushes may have affected catch rates
- Site relocations in 1993 did not affect fundamental site selection criteria based on YOY squawfish collection history
