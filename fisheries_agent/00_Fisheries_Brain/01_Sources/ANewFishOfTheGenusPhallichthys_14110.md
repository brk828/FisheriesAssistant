# A New Fish of the Genus Phallichthys (Family Poeciliidae) from Costa Rica

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

William A. Bussing describes a new poeciliid fish, [[Phallichthys Quadripunctatus]], from the Baja Talamanca lowlands of eastern [[Costa Rica]], based on 48 specimens. The species is closely allied to [[Phallichthys Tico]] of northern Costa Rica and southern Nicaragua. Both share a sinistral gonopodium and a membranous swelling on the terminal element of gonopodial ray 4a. The new species is diagnosed by four conspicuous body spots, and it was collected only from shallow streams and standing waters of the [[Rio Sixaola Basin]]. The paper appeared as Number 301 of [[Contributions in Science]], published by the [[Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County]] on March 16, 1979 (accepted April 21, 1977).

## Background & Description

The genus *Phallichthys* comprises four Central American species and was placed, with four other genera, in the tribe Heterandriini by [[Rosen and Bailey]] (1963). Most members of the tribe occur in Middle America. The genus is largely restricted to the Atlantic versant, though *P. amates* was apparently introduced to the Valle Central and has been recorded from the Pacific slope of northwestern Costa Rica. *P. fairweatheri* inhabits northern Guatemala and part of Belize. With the new species, Costa Rica holds three *Phallichthys* species.

**Diagnosis:** The new species has typically four conspicuous spots on each side of the body and 11–17 gill rakers on the first arch (usually 13–16 in large adults). Males of both *P. quadripunctatus* and *P. tico* have a very long terminal segment of gonopodial ray 4a bearing a fleshy dorsal pad and a membranous hook.

**Morphology:**
- Standard length of the holotype: 14.6 mm (mature male). Maximum size: 32.7 mm SL.
- Greatest body depth 3.2–3.6 times in SL in males and 2.8–3.4 in females.
- Head length 3.1–3.7 times in SL; eyes slightly below the dorsal head profile.
- Two rows of incurved teeth on each jaw; outer-row teeth spatulate, likely for scraping.
- Dorsal-fin rays usually 9; anal-fin rays usually 10; pectoral rays usually 11; pelvic rays usually 6; caudal rays usually 13; scales in lateral series usually 26.
- Vertebrae 29 on two cleared and stained paratypes.
- Gonopodium sinistral, with ray 4a bearing a terminal segment 4–5 times longer than wide.

**Coloration:** Pale yellow-gray ground colour with black-edged scale pockets. Four subcircular spots along the body axis, with variants in 21% of specimens showing three or five spots. Holotype normal on left side, five spots on right.

**Type material:** Holotype LACM 36018-1 (mature male, 14.6 mm SL), collected 4 October 1975 near Chase, Limon Province. Paratypes are held in LACM, UCR and USNM, totalling roughly 48 specimens across four lots.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitats:** Collected only in two shallow rivulets and a roadside ditch.
  - UCR-897: clear, shaded brook (0.5 m wide, 0.1 m deep, 26 °C, sand and gravel), plus an adjacent stagnant, turbid, strongly insolated ditch (temperatures 26–40 °C; fish occurred where bottom temperatures reached 34 °C).
  - UCR-1017: small stream (2.0 m wide, 0.2–0.5 m deep, 20 °C, sand and rock); the species was uncommon here.
- **Associated species:** At the ditch site, only one other fish, a *Rivulus* species, was taken. At the stream site, *Phallichthys amates*, *Alfaro cultratus* and a *Rivulus* species were found.
- **Distribution:** Known only from two tributaries of the Rio Sixaola in extreme eastern Costa Rica. It probably also occurs in Panamanian tributaries of the Sixaola and has not been found in apparently suitable habitats in adjoining northern drainages.
- **Sympatry:** Both *P. quadripunctatus* and *P. tico* are sympatric and often syntopic with *P. amates*.
- **Aquarium behaviour:** Shy unless kept with smaller, less timid species in a heavily planted tank; accepts small entomostracans (*Ceriodaphnia* spp.) and prepared dry foods.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Phallichthys quadripunctatus* is distinguished by four body spots and a moderate gill-raker count, and is most closely related to *P. tico*.
- **Restricted range:** The species is endemic in practical terms to two Sixaola tributaries, which makes it vulnerable to local habitat change.
- **Size and ontogeny:** Gill-raker counts increase with growth. Specimens of 12–16 mm SL (including all males examined) had 11–14 rakers, while larger females (17.0–32.7 mm SL) had 15–17.
- **Spot variation:** 21% of specimens showed three or five spots, indicating intraspecific pattern variability.
- **Phylogenetic hypothesis:** Following [[Donn E. Rosen]], the shared pad on the gonopodium may indicate that *P. quadripunctatus* and *P. tico* are sister to *P. fairweatheri*, and these three form a sister group to *P. amates*. Bussing notes that the dextral asymmetry of *P. fairweatheri* may reflect a simple genetic change and need not indicate deep divergence.
- **Taxonomic context:** The paper increases the number of Costa Rican *Phallichthys* species to three and supports the placement of the genus in Heterandriini.
- **Publication note:** The paper includes a Spanish-language summary (Resumen), and the serial merged the former Science Bulletin and Contributions in Science into a single numbered series starting with No. 301.
