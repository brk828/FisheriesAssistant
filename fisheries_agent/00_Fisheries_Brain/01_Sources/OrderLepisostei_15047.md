# Gars of the Western North Atlantic: Order Lepisostei (Lepisosteidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepisosteidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 taxonomic monograph, reprinted from the [[Sears Foundation for Marine Research]] *Memoir*, revises the Garfishes (Order [[Lepisostei]], family [[Lepisosteidae]]) of the Western North Atlantic. It covers the single living genus [[Lepisosteus]] and four species: [[Spotted Gar]] (*L. oculatus*), [[Longnose Gar]] (*L. osseus*), [[Florida Gar]] (*L. platyrhincus*), and [[Alligator Gar]] (*L. spatula*). The work provides detailed morphological descriptions, measurement and meristic statistics, a dichotomous [[Key to Species]], notes on spawning, feeding, range, and human use, and a review of the group's taxonomic history and relationships. Gars are described as largely freshwater fishes that tolerate brackish and, to varying degrees, marine water, with Alligator Gar the most saline-tolerant of the four.

## Background & Description

**Scope and methods.** The account is based on examination of many museum specimens from institutions across North America, supplemented by the literature and field observations. Measurements are reported as percentages of total length (or head length) and meristic counts are given as ranges and means. A specimen of *L. spatula* (49.5 mm length, 57.3 mm TL) arrived after submission and was incorporated in brackets.

**Diagnostic characters.** Gars are slender, ray-finned fishes with a fully ossified skeleton but retain archaic [[Ganoid Scales]], a trait otherwise shared only with polypteroids among living fishes. Notable features include:

- **[[Arterial Cone]]** of the heart with eight transverse rows of valves, more than any other living ray-finned fish.
- **Caudal fin** of the abbreviate heterocercal type, with hypural bones supported by the upturned vertebral column.
- **[[Opisthocoelous Vertebrae]]**, with posterior faces concave and anterior faces convex, a condition unique among living fishes.
- An elongated snout, a lower jaw articulating in front of the eye, and a dorsal fin set posteriorly above the anal fin, with no adipose fin.
- A [[Swim Bladder]] that is cellular, highly vascularized, and functions as a supplementary respiratory organ.
- Staggered gonads, with ovaries closed and a single urinogenital sinus.

**Taxonomic history.** The group has been placed variously within Holostei (including with amiids and polypteroids) or as a separate order (Ginglymodi, Lepisosteoidei, Lepisosteiformes, and others). The authors retain *Lepisostei* because of the name's established connotation. Genus-level treatment has also varied: Rafinesque proposed several genera and subgenera, some based on a juvenile or a fictitious description, and later authors recognized one, two, or three genera. This study recognizes one genus with two subgenera, [[Atractosteus]] and *Lepisosteus*.

**Relationships.** Gars are commonly placed next to the [[Amiidae]] because they share a heterocercal caudal skeleton, flattened overlapping pelvic bones, a chambered swim bladder used for air breathing, and a rudimentary spiral valve. They differ in scale structure, snout length, branchiostegal number, jaw articulation, vertebral form, and conus arteriosus valve rows (eight in gars, two in amiids).

**Life history traits.**

- Eggs are adhesive and attach to the substratum. There is no parental care.
- Larvae bear ventral adhesive organs at the snout tip and are relatively inactive until the yolk is absorbed, then become active predators.
- A fleshy caudal filament persists in larvae and atrophies at different sizes among species.
- Differential growth of body parts, especially orbit diameter, continues after maturity and has complicated gar systematics.
- Males mature and grow to smaller sizes than females, with sexual dimorphism in snout length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spawning.** Per earlier observations in the latitude of New York, spawning occurs entirely in fresh water from about mid-May to mid-June. Each female is accompanied by one to four males, and fertilization is external. Large aggregations disperse quickly afterward.

**Habits and feeding.** Gars are sluggish for most of the time, often lying motionless near the surface in summer. They surface to gulp air, which the authors interpret as a supplementary respiratory function. In winter they occupy deeper waters, and trawlers in Louisiana regularly capture Alligator and Longnose Gars from estuarine holes. Gars primarily eat other fishes, mostly forage species. In Lake Pontchartrain, Spotted and Longnose Gars consume the [[Blue Crab]] (*Callinectes sapidus*), and Alligator Gar also takes refuse and mullet. Alligator Gar is considered a scavenger.

**Salinity tolerance.** The four species differ in their use of brackish and marine habitats:

- *L. spatula* is a common brackish-water inhabitant in Louisiana and the most saline-tolerant of the four. It has been recorded in the Gulf of Mexico, including off Breton Island, Grand Isle, and Destin, Florida.
- *L. osseus* is less tolerant of marine water and is seldom captured outside brackish estuaries.
- *L. oculatus* is a common summer inhabitant of fresher parts of Louisiana estuaries and seldom ranges far into open lakes. Records suggest it and *L. platyrhincus* are the least tolerant of saline conditions.
- *L. platyrhincus* has few salt-water records, though the authors note collecting gaps may explain this.

**Range.** The genus ranges from Quebec west to the upper [[Mississippi River Basin]] and Great Lakes region, and south to Costa Rica and southwestern Cuba. The *L. platyrhincus* range extends from the southern tip of peninsular Florida into Georgia lowlands, and *L. spatula* extends from western Florida along the Gulf coast to Mexico. Another species, *L. sinensis*, is reported from China on the basis of a single literature source, and most references do not confirm an extant Asiatic form.

**Geological history.** Garfishes are known from the Upper Cretaceous to Lower Miocene, with Eocene records from India and from North America from the Middle Eocene to Recent. Fossil nomenclature is considered confused. The authors suggest additional subgenera may be needed for some fossil forms.

**Human interactions.** Gars are generally regarded as nuisances and predators of game fish, and they damage gill and trawl nets. Sport fishing for Alligator Gar has grown in Louisiana, Arkansas, Mississippi, and other Mississippi Valley states, and gar fishing rodeos are held annually in Louisiana. Alligator Gar is sold in the French Market in New Orleans. In Central America, *L. tropicus* is an important food item on the Pacific side of southern Mexico and Guatemala and is eaten in the Lago de Nicaragua area. Indigenous peoples used gar scales and bones for arrow points, ritual objects, and ornaments. The authors state that Longnose Gar roe is poisonous and not used in caviar production. No authenticated attacks on humans by Alligator Gar were found in the records reviewed.

## Key Findings

**1. Four species are treated in detail.** Of the seven species currently recognized in North American waters, this account covers *L. oculatus*, *L. osseus*, *L. platyrhincus*, and *L. spatula*. The authors note that some forms may prove to be only subspecifically distinct and that hybridization has been indicated but not verified.

**2. Study material and key counts.** The main specimen bases are:

| Species | Specimens examined (description) | Length range |
|---|---|---|
| *L. oculatus* | 177 (total study material) | 32–819 mm TL |
| *L. osseus* | 42 (description) | 139–779 mm TL |
| *L. platyrhincus* | 76 (total study material) | 187–516 mm TL |
| *L. spatula* | 29 (total study material) | 187–2,159 mm TL (plus one 49.5 mm specimen) |

**3. Diagnostic separation.**

- *L. osseus* is distinguished at a glance by its long, narrow snout (least snout width 13–25.5 times in snout length in the key).
- *L. spatula* is separated by its large size and broad, short snout. Its gill rakers number 59–66 on the left outside arch, the lowest counts in the adult Subgenus [[Atractosteus]] group.
- *L. oculatus* has numerous dark spots on the body, head, and fins, and bony plates on the ventral surface of the isthmus. It has a longer, narrower snout than *L. platyrhincus*.
- *L. platyrhincus* shares dark spots with *L. oculatus* but lacks isthmus plates, has a wider snout, and wider lower jaws.

**4. Growth and maturity data.**

- *L. oculatus* young develop mid-dorsal spots when between 100 and 150 mm, and lateral spots at about 170 mm.
- In *L. oculatus*, body scalation is complete at 140–150 mm, and isthmus plates begin forming near 200 mm.
- In *L. osseus*, the caudal appendage was observed at lengths up to 271 mm, and one 300 mm specimen had it nearly atrophied.

**5. Size records.**

- The Lake Pontchartrain sample included 26 *L. oculatus* (297–690 mm TL, July 1953–February 1955), 28 *L. osseus* (541–1,180 mm TL), and 21 *L. spatula* (410–1,472 mm TL).
- Maximum reported sizes include about 1,500 mm TL for *L. osseus*, 1,330 mm TL for *L. platyrhincus* (from a sample of 225), and a female *L. spatula* of 9 ft 8.5 in weighing 302 lb from Belle Isle Lake, Louisiana.

**6. Spawning observations.** Spawning of *L. oculatus* was observed in a group of four fish seined from a borrow pit in the Bonne Carre Spillway on March 25, 1959. The largest was a 757 mm female, though no spawning activity was seen before capture. Earlier notes on *L. spatula* spawning in December–January were found to be incorrect; recent observations place spawning in April–June in Louisiana.

**7. Taxonomic conclusions.**

- The author retains *Lepisostei* as the order name and recognizes one genus with two subgenera, *Lepisosteus* and *Atractosteus*.
- Rafinesque's genus *Litholepis* and his *Sarchirus* (based on a young *L. osseus*) are treated as invalid or synonymous.
- Many nineteenth-century species names are synonymized, with the cited synonymy showing numerous historical names for *L. osseus* and *L. spatula*.

**8. Relation to other fishes.** Gars share several characters with amiids, but their scales, jaws, branchiostegals, and conus arteriosus differ significantly, supporting their treatment as a separate order.
