## Executive Summary
The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), under Regional Director Ralph Morgenweck, announced on May 28, 1994, a proposal to list the [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) as a **threatened species** under the Endangered Species Act. This minnow is endemic to the [[Virgin River]] drainage and its tributaries. A public comment period was opened, with comments due by July 18, 1994, addressed to the USFWS Field Supervisor in Salt Lake City, Utah. The proposal stems from a documented ~50-year decline in distribution, driven primarily by habitat loss and non-native species competition.

## Background & Description
- **Common Name Origin**: Named for its spine-like fin rays ("spinedace").
- **Coloration**: Described as a "bright silvery minnow."
- **Taxonomic Classification**: Subspecies-level designation (*L. m. mollispinis*), implying morphological or genetic differentiation from other [[Lepidomeda]] subspecies within the genus.
- **Family**: Member of the [[Cyprinidae]] (minnow family), consistent with small-bodied freshwater cyprinids of the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Endemism**: Restricted entirely to the [[Virgin River]] and its tributary system — a defining feature of desert-adapted, range-restricted cyprinids.
- **Historic Range**: Southwestern Utah, northwestern Arizona, and southwestern Nevada.
- **Current Range**: Significantly contracted — the species **no longer occurs in Nevada** as of 1993 survey data, and Arizona populations show greatly reduced distribution.
- **Habitat Loss**: Approximately **40% of habitat in southwestern Utah** has been lost or degraded due to:
  - Reservoir construction and water resource development
  - Non-native fish introductions (interspecific competition)
  - Mining activity
  - Stream erosion
  - Livestock grazing impacts (riparian/instream degradation)
- **Interspecific Interactions**: Non-native fish species are identified as competitors, implicating [[Invasive Species Impacts]] on native cyprinid persistence in the [[Colorado River Basin]] tributary systems.

## Key Findings
1. **Regulatory Action**: Formal proposal for listing as **Threatened** under the Endangered Species Act, published for public comment (comment period closing July 18, 1994).
2. **Range Contraction**: Documented extirpation from Nevada; substantial distributional decline in Arizona based on 1993 catch data.
3. **Timeline of Decline**: Population and range declines have been reported for approximately **50 years** prior to the listing proposal.
4. **Primary Threats Identified**:
   - Water resource development (reservoirs, diversions)
   - Non-native fish introductions
   - Mining
   - Stream erosion
   - Livestock grazing impacts
5. **Recovery Requirements** (as stated by USFWS):
   - Habitat restoration, including maintenance of **adequate instream flows**
   - Eradication of select non-native fish competitors
   - Protection from further water resource development
6. **Administrative Process**: Public hearing details to be announced later via Federal Register and local newspapers, indicating this news release represents an early procedural stage (proposed rule, not final listing).
