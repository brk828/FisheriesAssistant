## Executive Summary

This document is a **book review** published in *The Canadian Field-Naturalist* (1971), authored by ichthyologist W. B. Scott of the [[Royal Ontario Museum]]. It evaluates *Biology of Coregonid Fishes*, an edited volume (Lindsey & Woods, 1970) compiling 29 papers from the **International Symposium on Biology of Coregonid Fishes** held in Winnipeg, Manitoba in August 1969. The [[Coregonidae|Coregonids]] (whitefishes) are noted as the most widely distributed and species-rich group of freshwater fishes in Canada, making this the first major international gathering focused specifically on the taxon. The review praises the volume's production quality and value, while noting its heavy bias toward systematics and evolutionary biology at the expense of life history, behavior, and commercial fisheries topics.

## Background & Description

The source text is a bibliographic review and does not provide primary morphological data (e.g., meristic counts, body plan descriptions) for coregonid species. However, it references foundational comparative work:

- **Cavender's contribution** compares [[Coregoninae|coregonines]] and other [[Salmonidae|salmonids]] against the earliest known teleost fishes, implying a phylogenetic/morphological framework for understanding coregonid body plans and relationships within Salmonidae.
- The genus **[[Prosopium]]** is highlighted (Norden's paper) as a distinct evolutionary and distributional lineage within the family, though no specific morphological diagnostic traits are detailed in this review.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Coregonids are exclusively freshwater fishes (lakes and rivers), in contrast to many other salmonid relatives.
- **Distribution**: The family exhibits a Holarctic distribution, with significant representation in both **North America** (13 papers) and **Eurasia** (12 papers).
- Notable zoogeographic contributions covered in the volume:
  - Maitland — origin and present distribution of *[[Coregonus]]* in the **British Isles**.
  - Himberg — systematic and zoogeographic study of North European coregonids.
  - Norden — evolution and distribution of the genus *[[Prosopium]]* (reviewer notes an inaccuracy in the distribution map for northern Ontario, p. 70).
  - Nikolsky and Reshetnikov — systematics of coregonid fishes in the **USSR**.
- **Introgression** is identified (Svärdson's paper) as ecologically and evolutionarily significant in shaping coregonid diversity, suggesting hybridization between sympatric forms plays a key role in niche differentiation.

  - Zoogeography and distribution: 4 papers
  - General biology: 14 papers
  - Populations: 3 papers

  - Commercial fisheries: 1 paper (Leopold et al., on Polish [[Coregonid Fisheries|coregonid fisheries]])
  - *(Note: category totals exceed 29 as some papers span multiple categories)*
- Only two invited authors withdrew their papers from the published proceedings.
- The reviewer identifies six papers of particular significance for their comprehensiveness:
  1. **Cavender** — Comparative anatomy of coregonines vs. early teleosts.
  2. **Svärdson** — Significance of introgression in coregonid evolution.
  3. **Norden** — Evolution and distribution of *Prosopium* (map error noted for northern Ontario).
  4. **Maitland** — Origin and distribution of *Coregonus* in the British Isles.
  5. **Himberg** — Systematic/zoogeographic study of North European coregonids.
  6. **Nikolsky and Reshetnikov** — Systematics of coregonid fishes in the USSR.
- A major secondary output of the 1969 symposium was a comprehensive **bibliography of coregonid fishes**, published separately as **Technical Report No. 151** by the [[Fisheries Research Board of Canada]].
- **Critical gap identified**: The volume underrepresents life history, behavioral ecology, and commercial fishery aspects of coregonids, focusing overwhelmingly on systematics, evolution, genetics, and distribution.
- The reviewer commends the book's production quality, minimal typographic errors, and overall value relative to its price ($8.00 for 560 pages).
