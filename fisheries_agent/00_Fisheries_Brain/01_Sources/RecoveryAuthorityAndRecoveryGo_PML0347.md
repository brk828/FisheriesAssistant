# Recovery Authority and Recovery Goals for Endangered Colorado River Fishes (1989 Letter and 1988 ESA Amendments)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 letter from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] Regional Director to Tom Pitts, a consultant, explaining why the Service, not the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]], holds ultimate authority for setting recovery goals for three endangered Colorado River fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], the [[Humpback Chub]], and the [[Bonytail Chub]]. The letter argues that the [[Endangered Species Act]] assigns recovery plan responsibility to the [[Secretary Of The Interior]], that this duty cannot be delegated, and that detailed, measurable recovery goals are required under the 1988 amendments. The document also reproduces excerpts of H.R. 1467, the 1988 Endangered Species Act Amendments, which set out revised recovery plan requirements, public comment rules and post-recovery monitoring.

No original field data, population counts or habitat measurements are reported. The content is administrative and legal.

## Background & Description

The letter follows an April 4, 1989 [[Management Group]] meeting, at which the Service agreed to explain its position in writing. It responds to a February 1, 1989 letter from Pitts to Deputy Regional Director Spinks, which argued that the Implementation Committee had responsibility to establish upper basin recovery goals for the listed fishes.

The three species are the endangered fishes covered by the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The letter is copied to the Implementation Committee, the Management Group, the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] leader, and the Recovery Team.

H.R. 1467 is the bill authorizing appropriations for the Endangered Species Act for fiscal years 1988 through 1992. It was passed in the Second Session of the 100th Congress, and its Title I contains the 1988 amendments. The excerpt is partly degraded by OCR, so some wording below is paraphrased.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document provides no species biology, abundance, distribution or habitat data. Its environmental relevance lies in the recovery framework. The Recovery Program is intended to generate information on population trends and on the parameters that define "self-sustaining, viable populations" of the three fishes. The letter states that the Recovery Team's basinwide perspective will support unbiased, peer-reviewed assessment of the biological basis for upper basin activities.

Post-recovery monitoring is also addressed. The amended Act requires the Secretary to run a monitoring system, in cooperation with the states, for not less than five years after a species has recovered and been removed from the lists. The system is meant to catch recovered species whose status is slipping before they require relisting.

## Key Findings

**1. The Service holds ultimate recovery authority.**
- The Secretary of the Interior is responsible for developing and implementing recovery plans under Section 4(f).
- The Secretary may procure services from public and private agencies, institutions and qualified persons, including appointing recovery teams. The Service has delegated these duties to itself and retains final approval over plan contents.
- None of the Secretary's statutory responsibilities were transferred to the Implementation Committee.

**2. The Cooperative Agreement cannot delegate statutory duties.**
- Paragraph 5 of the Cooperative Agreement confirms that the Secretary is responsible for developing and implementing recovery plans.
- Paragraph 6 states that the parties hold statutory responsibilities that cannot be delegated and that the agreement does not abrogate them.
- The Recovery Program document (page 1-7) says the program "cannot, and does not in any way, diminish or detract from or add to the Secretary's ultimate responsibility" under the Act.

**3. The Service's proposed process for setting upper basin recovery goals has three steps.**
- (a) Recovery activities, including monitoring, generate data on population trends and on viability parameters.
- (b) The Implementation Committee uses these data to develop more specific upper basin recovery goals and recommends them to the Service, which may request review by the Recovery Team.
- (c) The Service keeps final approval authority over goals recommended by the Implementation Committee or the Recovery Team.
- The Service says recommendations with a strong technical basis will receive serious consideration.

**4. The Recovery Team will advise the Service on biological issues.**
- The Service will ask the Recovery Team to review and advise on biological questions for the Colorado River fishes.
- The letter cites the team's basinwide perspective, scientific credibility and political autonomy as grounds for high-quality, unbiased peer review.

**5. Detailed, measurable recovery goals are required.**
- The 1988 amendments require each recovery plan to include, to the maximum extent practicable, "objective, measurable criteria" that, when met, would support removing the species from the list.
- The Service states that the goals for the Colorado River fishes should be as specific as possible within the bounds of good science, and that the revised plans meet the intent of the Act and are scientifically supportable.

**6. The 1988 amendments set requirements for all recovery plans.**
- Plans must give priority to species most likely to benefit, without regard to taxonomic classification, especially those in conflict with construction or other development projects.
- Each plan must describe site-specific management actions, measurable delisting criteria, and estimates of the time and cost needed to reach the goal and intermediate steps.
- Recovery teams are exempt from the Federal Advisory Committee Act.
- The Secretary must report to the Senate Committee on Environment and Public Works and the House Committee on Merchant Marine and Fisheries every two years on recovery planning status.
- The Secretary must provide public comment before approving a new or revised plan, and each federal agency must consider public comments before implementing it.

**7. Post-recovery monitoring and state cooperation.**
- The Secretary must monitor recovered species for not less than five years after delisting, in cooperation with the states, and must act promptly to prevent significant risk to recovered species.
- The amendments also add provisions on state cooperation, including financial assistance to states under Section 6.

**8. Next steps.**
- The letter closes by stating the Service's view that the Recovery Team and the Implementation Committee will keep advisory roles. The Implementation Committee's upper basin data will support refinement of the goals in the [[Recovery Plan]].
