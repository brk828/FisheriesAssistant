## Executive Summary

This study, conducted as part of the Upper Mississippi River Conservation Committee's fishery investigations, examines the age and growth of the [[Freshwater Sheepshead]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) across four navigation pools (4A, 8, 10, 17) spanning Lake Pepin to Muscatine, Iowa. Using scale-based age determination validated through three independent criteria, researchers analyzed 902 specimens collected via diverse gear types (trot line, trap net, hoop net, trammel net, gill net, minnow seine, trawl, shore seine) during 1946-1948. Key findings demonstrate that growth rates were statistically uniform across pools and stations despite latitudinal separation, supporting the case for uniform fishery regulations across the study area. Mississippi River sheepshead grow significantly faster than their [[Lake Erie]] counterparts, reaching 18.1 inches by age VII versus 15.8 inches for Lake Erie fish. Sexual maturity patterns reveal males mature earlier (age III) than females (age V), with management implications centered on protecting the 16-inch female size class where 50% attain maturity.

## Background & Description

### Scale Development & Formation
- **Scale formation onset**: Assumed at 20mm total length (midpoint between scale-absent specimens at 13.3-15.6mm and scale-present specimens at 24mm)
- **Scalation pattern**: Progressive development beginning at the caudal peduncle, proceeding anteriorly along the lateral line, then extending dorsally and ventrally
- Comparison with related Sciaenidae: *Sciaenops ocellatus* (redfish) shows scales at 25mm; *Micropogon undulatus* (croaker) at 32mm

### Body-Scale Relationship
- Derived from 608 specimens (1.6–23.2 inches total length)
- **Combined sexes formula**: Y = 1.20x − 11.97
- **Female-specific formula**: Y = 1.23x − 18.17
- **Male-specific formula**: Y = 1.25x − 15.93
- Where Y = magnified anterior radius of key scale (mm, at 25.5x), x = total length (inches)
- Sex-based differences were not significant enough to warrant separate growth calculations

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat & Distribution
- Inhabits impounded and non-impounded river sections of the [[Upper Mississippi River]] navigation system
- Study area spans 18 impoundments (locks and dams) completed 1930-1938 between Minneapolis, MN and Muscatine, IA
- Historical range noted to extend farther north (Michigan) in postglacial times than present distribution ([[Hubbs 1940]])
- Growth was compared across impounded areas (e.g., Stoddard, WI; Guttenberg) vs. open-river/riverine sections (e.g., LaCrosse, WI; Harpers Ferry, McGregor)

### Commercial Importance
- Ranks **3rd in abundance and value** among Mississippi River commercial fisheries, behind carp and buffalo
- 1948 catch: 617,600 lbs valued at $74,112 (6.8% of total catch by weight, 10.6% by value)
- Primary producing states: Wisconsin, Iowa, Illinois
- Regulated only via gear-type restrictions (no size/season limits at time of study)
- Market utilization occurs at 11-16 inches (¾–2 lbs), a sub-optimal size relative to growth potential, due to declining flesh quality in larger individuals

### Spawning Biology
- **Spawning season**: Early May through late June (peak "ripe+" condition observed in late May 1946 and early June 1948)
- Spawning determined via gonadal condition categories: "ripe," "ripe+," "spent," and immature (translucent gonad)
- Some individuals retained gametes 2 weeks post-peak spawning, suggesting unspawned "hold-over" individuals

### Related Taxa Context
- Comparative early life history references drawn from other Sciaenidae: [[Sciaenops Ocellatus]] (redfish) and [[Micropogon Undulatus]] (croaker)

## Key Findings

### Age Validation
Three independent lines of evidence confirmed scale-based age assessment reliability:
1. **Monotonic length increase** with assessed age (4.9 in at age I → 21.3 in at age IX)
2. **Consistent percentage deviation trends** in growth between same year-classes sampled in different collection years (1946 vs. 1948)
3. **Cross-cohort agreement** on relative growth quality/poorness across different calendar years

### Latitude & Geographic Growth Comparison
- **No significant latitudinal effect** on growth rate was detected among Pools 4A, 8, 10, and 17
- Pool 8 showed superior growth; Pool 17 (southernmost, Muscatine, IA) showed slightly slower growth in 2- and 3-year-olds
- Within-pool station comparisons (impounded vs. riverine) showed inconsistent patterns — impoundment areas did NOT consistently produce larger fish, contradicting some commercial fishermen's beliefs

- **Poorest growth year**: 1945
- **No consistent correlation** found between annual water levels (Lake Pepin/Pool 4A) and growth rate

### Sexual Maturity Schedule
| Age | Grand Avg. Length (in) | Males % Mature | Females % Mature |
|-----|------------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| II | 9.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| III | 11.7 | 23.2 | 0.0 |

| V | 14.8 | 97.2 | 46.1 |
| VI | 16.5 | 100 | 96.6 |

- **Males**: First mature at age III (mean length 12.2 in, 3.2 in above age-class average); fully mature by age VII
- **Females**: First mature at age V (mean length 15.2 in, 1.8 in above average); fully mature beyond age VI
- Fish maturing earliest showed growth rates **far above the population average**, indicating a strong correlation between individual growth superiority and early maturation
- Males mature approximately **2 years earlier** than females on average

### Management Implications
1. **Uniform regulation justified**: Growth pattern consistency from Lake Pepin to Muscatine, IA supports single regulatory framework across the study pools
2. **Spawning stock protection**: Survival of females ≥16 inches (the length at which >50% reach maturity) is critical for sustaining reproductive capacity
3. Current commercial harvest size (11-16 in) largely precedes female sexual maturity, creating potential recruitment bottleneck if increased spawning stock becomes necessary
