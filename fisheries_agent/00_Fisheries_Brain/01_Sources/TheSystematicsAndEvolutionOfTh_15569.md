# Systematics and Evolution of the Genus Chirostoma (Pisces, Atherinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This revision by [[Clyde D. Barbour]] (Department of Biology, University of Utah) reassesses the freshwater atherinid genus [[Chirostoma]]. The genus is treated as comprising 18 species and six subspecies, excluding *C. compressum*, which is thought to be extinct. Apart from a few populations of *C. jordani* near Durango, the genus is restricted to the [[Mesa Central]] of Mexico, where it makes up about 20 percent of the fish fauna. The paper synonymizes several names, recognizes *C. reseratum* as a subspecies of *C. consocium*, describes *C. aculeatum* as new, and proposes that the genus is diphyletic, with a [[Menidia]]-like ancestor giving rise to the jordani group and a [[Melaniris]]-like ancestor giving rise to the arge group.

## Background & Description

The Mexican fish fauna is shaped by coastal lowland migration routes and by high endemism on the uplifted central plateau. The southern third of the plateau, the westward-flowing [[Lerma-Santiago River System]] and contiguous basins, shows endemism at generic and familial levels. Chirostoma is one of the unique groups in this system.

Taxonomic history is confused. Earlier authors (Jordan and Evermann, Meek, Regan, Jordan and Hubbs, De Buen, Alvarez) recognized between one and three genera and varying numbers of subgenera. Barbour does not diagnose the genus against other atherines, because comparative data for most related genera are scarce.

**Methods.** Nineteen morphometric and nine meristic characters were examined across the species, plus [[Menidia Berylina]] (Grand Isle, Louisiana) and [[Melaniris Crystallina]] (Rio Grande de Santiago, Nayarit) as outgroups. Body proportions were measured on up to 20 adults and meristics on 50 or more specimens where available. Statistics (ranges, means, 95% confidence intervals, standard deviations) were computed for samples of more than six specimens. Key diagnostic characters were scale margin type (smooth versus laciniate) and lateral line opening (pores versus canals). Specimens are housed mainly at [[Tulane University]] (TU) and the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ).

**Diagnostic characters.** Single diagnostic characters are rare; most identifications rely on character combinations. Dentition consists of conical teeth in all species, but tooth size and placement differ. Pigmentation is often environmentally variable, though some concentrations are reliable.

**Species groups.** The genus is divided into two groups:
- **Jordani group** (high meristic values, laciniate scales, canals): *C. jordani*, *C. patzcuaro*, *C. chapalae*, *C. consocium*, *C. humboldtianum*, *C. estor*, *C. grandocule*, *C. lucius*, *C. sphyraena*, *C. promelas*.
- **Arge group** (low meristic values, smooth scales, pores): *C. arge*, *C. melanoccus*, *C. charari*, *C. riojai*, *C. bartoni*, *C. attenuatum*, *C. labarcae*, *C. aculeatum*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Species were found in small ponds and streams, the main channel of the Rio Santiago, and large lakes such as [[Lake Chapala]], [[Lake Patzcuaro]], [[Lake Cuitzeo]] and [[Lake Zirahuen]]. *C. jordani* was often associated with vegetation, including drowned terrestrials, *Scirpus* stands and water hyacinth (*Eichornia* sp.). Spawning *C. jordani* were recorded among hyacinth roots on the Rio Santiago at Poncitlan on July 10, 1963.
- **Breeding:** On April 1, 1964, *C. consocium* was apparently spawning in very shallow water on the north shore of Lake Chapala. Eggs were attached to pebbles by threads. Ripe *C. attenuatum zirahuen* were collected on March 30, 1964.
- **Lake drying:** The drying of Lake Cuitzeo in winter 1941 probably contributed to the presumed extinction of *C. compressum*. The author suggests that a larger species would have more difficulty surviving in small springs and tributaries than *C. jordani*.
- **Introductions and decline:** [[Largemouth Bass]] were introduced into the lake north of Santa Maria in 1961 and into the lake at San Pedro Lagunillas in 1967. Poeciliids and goodeids in those lakes disappeared, and atherinids declined sharply or vanished. The western *C. humboldtianum* populations are described as in danger of extinction. Lake Juanacatlan was free of introductions in 1963.
- **Introductions elsewhere:** According to [[Minckley]] (1965), *C. sphyraena* and another unidentified *Chirostoma* were introduced into the Rio Conchos, Chihuahua.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic changes**
- Synonymies: *Otalia* = *Chirostoma*; *C. ocampoi* and *C. regani* = *C. humboldtianum*; *C. diazi* = *C. sphyraena*; *C. ocotlanae* = *C. lucius*.
- No subspecies of *C. jordani* are recognized.
- *C. reseratum* is recognized as a subspecies of *C. consocium*.
- The name *C. attenuatum* is reapplied to populations in Lakes Patzcuaro and Zirahuen previously called *C. bartoni*; *C. bartoni* is retained as a species.
- Five specimens from Balneario Cointzio, Michoacan, are referred to *C. charari*.
- *C. aculeatum* is described as new, from Lake Yuriria, Guanajuato (holotype TU 40889, 85.0 mm S.L.). It is distinguished from *C. labarcae* by a longer head, snout and mandible, and by anal fin rays of 18–23.

**Diagnostic examples (as reported)**
- *C. jordani*: median lateral scales 36–48; gill rakers 14–22; largest specimen from Lake Chapala 91 mm S.L.; typical maximum about 70 mm S.L.
- *C. humboldtianum*: median lateral scales 43–73; maximum length about 200–250 mm S.L.
- *C. estor estor*: predorsal scales 56–108; gill rakers 23–28; largest specimen 273 mm S.L.
- *C. lucius* and *C. sphyraena*: strongly projecting or pointed jaws, with *C. sphyraena* having canine-like teeth and a barracuda-like body.
- *C. aculeatum*: 17 paratypes plus holotype, 52.0–109.4 mm S.L.

**Phylogenetic conclusions**
- Smooth scale margins, pore-type lateral lines, small teeth within the gape and low meristic values are interpreted as primitive. Laciniate scales, canals, enlarged teeth and extreme body or morphometric values are interpreted as derived.
- The genus is most likely diphyletic. The jordani group diverged early from a Menidia-like ancestor that invaded the Lerma-Santiago system. The arge group came from a Melaniris-like stock that invaded later.
- *C. arge* shares pigmentation and dentition with *M. crystallina*, which the author interprets as evidence of close relationship and as support for placing *C. arge* nearest the arge-group ancestral stock.
- The most important step in the jordani group was the appearance of a large, generalized species similar to eastern *C. humboldtianum*, which likely gave rise to most other members.
- The arge group evolved parallel to but later than the jordani group and shows poorly defined trends, reversals and abrupt meristic increases.

**Limitations**
- The author notes that a full revision of the Atherinidae, including osteology, has not been done, so generic and specific relationships are tentative.
- Identification of subadults is unreliable because morphometric characters show allometric growth.
- *C. compressum* was known only from De Buen's description; the holotype is lost and it has not been seen since collection.
