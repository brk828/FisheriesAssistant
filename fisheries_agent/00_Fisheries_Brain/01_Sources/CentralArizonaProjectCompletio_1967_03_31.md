# Central Arizona Project — Completion Report (FW-16-R-6)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #water-resource-planning #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This completion report documents the Arizona Game and Fish Department's involvement in monitoring and influencing the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP), a large-scale water transportation and storage initiative designed to deliver 1.2 million acre-feet of Colorado River water annually to central Arizona. The report is not a species-level biological study, but rather a fisheries and wildlife impact assessment tied to legislative and infrastructural developments. Key activities included tracking congressional legislation (89th and 90th Congress), reviewing Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife feasibility reports on proposed reservoir units, and conducting recreational visitation surveys on seven Arizona lakes to quantify fishing use and inform future reservoir site selection. The report underscores tension between water development, hydroelectric financing schemes, wildlife habitat preservation, and recreational fishing demand.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although no specific fish species are named, the report characterizes several aquatic habitat types relevant to fisheries management in Arizona:

- **[[Riggs Lake]]**: A high-elevation put-and-take trout fishery in the Ponderosa pine zone of the Graham Mountains (southeastern Arizona). Managed by the Department specifically for stocked trout; noted as having no comparable coldwater lake fishery of similar size in the region, driving exceptionally high per-acre recreational value.
- **[[Bartlett Lake]]**: A warm water reservoir with minimal developed facilities and limited vehicle access, yet the highest overall percentage of fisherman use among the seven surveyed lakes — suggesting fish-driven visitation independent of amenity development.
- **[[Lake Havasu]]**: Proposed source reservoir for CAP water withdrawal and site of the proposed Havasu Pumped Storage Power Project, involving nightly pump-back cycling of water for hydroelectric peaking.
- **[[Orme Reservoir]]**: Proposed impoundment at the confluence of the Verde and Salt Rivers, intended to store both Colorado River water and native Verde/Salt River flows — with direct implications for downstream fish habitat and flow regimes.
- **[[Picacho Lake]]**: Existing lake proposed for repurposing as a storage node in the Salt-Gila Aqueduct system.
- **[[Charleston Reservoir]]** (San Pedro River, near Tombstone): Proposed for flood control, municipal water supply, and explicit fish and wildlife/recreation value.
- **[[Hooker Reservoir]]** (upper Gila River, New Mexico), **[[Buttes Reservoir]]** (Gila River east of Florence), and the **[[Florence Dam]]** site: All identified as having potential fish and wildlife/recreational value due to proximity to population centers and lack of comparable water-oriented recreation in their respective regions.
- **[[Hualapai Dam]]** and **[[Marble Canyon Dam]]** sites on the Colorado River: Controversial proposed impoundments near Grand Canyon National Park, valued for fish and wildlife habitat creation and hydroelectric revenue but opposed by conservation groups (e.g., Sierra Club) due to National Park boundary conflicts.

The report frames these water bodies within a broader niche framework of **coldwater vs. warmwater fisheries management**, noting a geographic scarcity of coldwater lake habitat in southeastern Arizona (driving Riggs Lake's disproportionate use value) and the influence of habitat accessibility, facility development, and scenic surroundings on angler visitation patterns.

## Key Findings

- The CAP was designed to deliver **1.2 million acre-feet** of the **2.8 million acre-feet** of Colorado River water allocated to Arizona (per the 1964 Supreme Court decree, *Arizona v. California*) to the central part of the state; the remaining 1.6 million acre-feet would be used along the mainstem river.
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department has requested **84,000 acre-feet** of mainstream Colorado River water annually be reserved for fish and wildlife purposes under CAP or the Pacific Southwest Water Plan, with 40,000 acre-feet earmarked for water-rights exchange to enable new lake construction ([[Kufeld 1966]]).
- Visitation surveys (June 1 – September 15, 1966) across seven Arizona lakes found:
  - **[[Bartlett Lake]]** had the highest percentage of fisherman use despite minimal facilities.
  - **[[Riggs Lake]]** had the highest recreational value per surface acre, both for fishing and for all recreation combined, attributed to its coldwater trout fishery, scenic isolation, and lack of regional competition.
  - **[[Lyman Lake]]** and **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]**, with well-developed facilities, showed correspondingly higher camper use percentages relative to fishing use.
- Two CAP feasibility-grade units (**[[Orme Reservoir]]** and **Salt-Gila Aqueduct**) were reviewed for fish and wildlife impacts by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, with Department recommendations submitted under **P.L. 89-72** (Federal Projects Recreation Act) to incorporate fish and wildlife enhancement features.
- Proposed hydroelectric "pumped storage" schemes (**Havasu Pumped Storage Power Project**, **Montezuma Pumped Storage Power Project**) were assessed for indirect ecological impacts, notably the concern that new paved road access into the previously remote Estrella Mountains would negatively affect resident **bighorn sheep** populations.
- Legislative controversy (HR 4671, 89th Congress; Saylor Bill HR 14176; multiple 90th Congress bills) centered on proposed dams at **Hualapai (Bridge) Canyon** and **Marble Canyon**, with conservation groups seeking to expand Grand Canyon National Park boundaries to preclude dam construction — a move the Department opposed due to resulting loss of public hunting access and wildlife management capacity.
- The Arizona State Legislature's **State Water and Power Plan (S 204)** incorporated all major in-state CAP features plus additional hydroelectric infrastructure to help finance the project.
- Recommendations for future work include expanding visitation surveys to cover **coldwater and warmwater streams** and **winter use of warmwater lakes**, to better inform fish and wildlife benefit planning for future reservoir siting decisions.
