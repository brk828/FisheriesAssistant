## Executive Summary

This document is a taxonomic checklist compiled by Carl L. Hubbs (corrected to February 8, 1939), enumerating the fish fauna of the Tennessee River System. It spans 19 families, from primitive jawless fishes ([[Petromyzonidae|Lampreys]]) to advanced [[Percidae|Perch and Darters]], and represents an early-to-mid 20th century snapshot of regional freshwater ichthyological diversity. The list is notable for its extensive use of trinomial subspecies designations and its inclusion of numerous undescribed "new species" and "new subspecies," indicating this was likely a working document supporting active taxonomic revision of the Cyprinidae (minnows), Catostomidae (suckers), and Percidae (darters) of the region.

## Background & Description

The list is organized taxonomically by family, genus, and species, largely following the nomenclature conventions of the early 20th century (using genera such as *Hybopsis*, *Notropis*, *Hadropterus*, *Boleosoma*, and *Schilbeodes*, many of which have since been synonymized or reclassified under modern taxonomy, e.g. into [[Noturus]] or [[Percina]]).

Key taxonomic groups documented include:
- **[[Petromyzonidae]]**: Six lamprey taxa, including the [[Western Lamprey]] (*Ichthyomyzon castaneus*), [[Southern Brook Lamprey]] (*I. gagei*), and an unnamed "Mountain brook lamprey."
- **[[Acipenseridae]]** and **[[Polyodontidae]]**: The [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*), [[Rock Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*), and [[Shovelhead Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*).
- **[[Lepisosteidae]]**: Four gar species including the [[Alligator Gar]] (*Lepisosteus spatula*) and [[Longnose Gar]].
- **[[Catostomidae]]** (Suckers): A large radiation including [[Bigmouth Buffalo]] (*Megastomatobus cyprinella*), [[Blue Sucker]] (*Cycleptus elongatus*), [[Harelip Sucker]] (*Lagochila lacera*) — now extinct — and multiple redhorse (*Moxostoma*) taxa.
- **[[Cyprinidae]]** (Minnows/Shiners): By far the most speciose family in the list, dominated by *Notropis* (shiners), *Hybopsis*/*Erimystax* (chubs), and *Hybognathus* (silvery minnows), with dozens of taxa including several described as new (e.g. "Cumberland chub," "Striking chub," "Blotched chub," "Slender chub").
- **[[Ameiuridae]]** (Bullhead Catfishes, = modern Ictaluridae): Includes [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus lacustris*), [[Blue Catfish]] (*I. furcatus*), [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pilodictis olivaris*), and madtoms (*Noturus*/*Schilbeodes*).
- **[[Esocidae]]**: Pickerels and the [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy ohioensis*, "Ohio muskellunge").
- **[[Amblyopsidae]]**: The cave-dwelling [[Southern Cavefish|Blindfish]] (*Typhlichthys subterraneus*).
- **[[Percidae]]** (Perches & Darters): A highly diverse assemblage including *Stizostedion* (sauger/walleye), and numerous darter genera now largely merged into *Etheostoma* and *Percina* — e.g. *Hadropterus*, *Cottogaster*, *Ammocrypta*, *Doration*, *Ulocentra*, *Boleosoma*, *Psychromaster* — several represented by multiple undescribed new species/subspecies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist reflects a river system draining into the [[Mississippi River]] basin, encompassing diverse freshwater habitats:
- **Main channel/large river habitat**: supporting large-bodied species like [[Paddlefish]], sturgeons, buffalofish, and catfish.
- **Riffle and gravel-bed habitats**: home to many darter species (Percidae) and suckermouth minnows (*Phenacobius*), which show morphological specialization (e.g., heavily scaled napes, upturned mouths) tied to benthic riffle-dwelling ecology.
- **Spring and cave habitats**: exemplified by *Typhlichthys subterraneus* (Amblyopsidae), an obligate cave-dwelling, presumably blind, subterranean species — indicating the karst topography of the Tennessee River System (Appalachian/Cumberland Plateau region).
- **Headwater/mountain streams**: several taxa are described with "mountain" epithets (e.g., "Mountain brook lamprey," "Mountain blacknose dace," "Mountain stoneroller," "Mountain longnose dace"), suggesting distinct upland lineages diverging from lowland relatives — an early recognition of intraspecific geographic variation within the drainage.
- The prevalence of paired "northern" vs. "southern" subspecies (e.g., golden shiner, black bullhead, brown bullhead) reflects the Tennessee River System's biogeographic position as a zone of introgression/intergradation between northern and southern faunal lineages, as explicitly noted for *Notemigonus crysoleucas* ("Intergrades between northern and southern golden shiners").

## Key Findings

- **High undescribed diversity**: The list contains numerous entries marked "new species" or "new subspecies" without formal names yet assigned, particularly concentrated in *Erimystax* (chubs: "Striking chub," "Blotched chub," "Slender chub"), *Notropis* (shiners), and Percidae genera (*Hadropterus*, *Doration*, *Percina*, *Cottogaster*) — indicating this document served as a preparatory taxonomic inventory for subsequent species descriptions by Hubbs and collaborators.
- **Extinction note**: The [[Harelip Sucker]] (*Lagochila lacera*) is listed here as an extant component of the fauna; this species is now understood to be extinct (last collected early 1900s), making this list a historical baseline record.
- **Subspecies-level biogeography**: Extensive trinomial nomenclature is used to document north–south subspecific divergence across many taxa (e.g., *Ameiurus melas* vs. *A. m. catulus*; *Esox americanus* vs. *E. niger*), reflecting Hubbs' broader interest in geographic variation within Tennessee Valley fish populations.
- **Taxonomic instability/synonymy flagged**: Several entries explicitly note taxonomic uncertainty, e.g., "*I. anguilla* is probably based on mature males of *punctatus*," "*Hiodon selenops*... perhaps same as *H. tergisus*," and "*lacertosus* (Cope)—of very doubtful validity," reflecting active debate over species validity at the time.
- **Distinct genera later synonymized**: Many genera used here (*Hybopsis*, *Erimystax*, *Extrarius*, *Ceratichys*, *Hyborhynchus*, *Hadropterus*, *Boleosoma*, *Cottogaster*, *Ammocrypta*, *Doration*, *Ulocentra*, *Psychromaster*, *Schilbeodes*, *Amphiodon*, *Pomolobus*, *Megastomatobus*, *Lepibema*) have since been absorbed into broader modern genera (e.g., *Cyprinella*, *Notropis*, *Percina*, *Etheostoma*, *Noturus*, *Ictiobus*, *Morone*), making this list a valuable snapshot of pre-revision nomenclature for the [[Tennessee River System]].
- **Habitat specialists documented**: Notable ecological specialists include the cave-adapted [[Blindfish]] (*Typhlichthys subterraneus*), the springs-associated [[Spring Darter]] (*Psychromaster tuscumbia*), and the swamp-dwelling [[Pirate Perch]] (*Aphredoderus sayanus*).
