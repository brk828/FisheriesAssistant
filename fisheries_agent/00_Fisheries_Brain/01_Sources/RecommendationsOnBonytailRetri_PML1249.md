# Bonytail Retrieval and Transport Recommendations (1987 Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail

## Executive Summary

This September 1987 memorandum from the [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] in Grand Junction, Colorado, responds to a draft protocol for handling and transporting wild [[Bonytail]] (BT). The draft, dated 24 June 1987 and prepared by a project staff member named Burdick, was reviewed by upper-basin fishery workers and discussed at a [[Biological Subcommittee]] meeting. The memo endorses the draft as practical guidance and sets out recommendations on researcher discretion, collecting-permit changes, identification criteria, equipment, and temporary holding. It urges that the guidelines be issued to permit holders immediately, because bringing in the few remaining wild bonytail outweighs the risks of transport and holding.

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** [[Bonytail]], referred to throughout as "BT." The document does not give a scientific name, family, or morphological description.
- **Issuing body:** [[Colorado River Fishery Project]], part of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Department of the Interior), with a Project Leader and a Fish & Wildlife Coordinator as addressees.
- **Document type:** Interoffice recommendation memo with an enclosure (the draft protocol) and a copy to a Lee Mills (FR).
- **Context:** The memo addresses the accidental or deliberate capture of wild bonytail by field researchers, who may need to move and hold the fish until agency personnel take possession.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses only the handling of wild fish. It gives no population counts, habitat measurements, water chemistry, or ecological data. Its sole environmental reference is to "appropriate river locations" from which the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] can be contacted to retrieve a fish. The memo states that bonytail are rare: it refers to "whatever few" individuals may be encountered during the next few years.

## Key Findings

1. **Draft protocol endorsed in general.** The memo finds the draft "useful, practical guidance" for workers who may encounter BT, provided the recommendations below are incorporated.

2. **Researcher discretion on transport.** Field researchers should have full discretion to judge whether a probable BT can be transported successfully to a river location where the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] can retrieve it. If the risk is judged too high, the researcher should release the fish back into the river.

3. **Collecting-permit modification.** Collecting permits should be amended to allow researchers to remove BT from the wild. If a fish dies during transport and no negligence is involved, the researcher should not be held responsible. The memo acknowledges that risk is unavoidable when wild fish are transported and held in captivity, even with precautions.

4. **Identification criteria.** The minimum criteria for identifying a fish as a bonytail, as outlined in the draft, should be strictly followed.

5. **Equipment supplied by the agency.** The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] will provide appropriate transport and holding equipment, including a cooler of adequate size, oxygen cylinders, a fish key, and similar items.

6. **Possible extended holding.** Researchers who encounter a BT must be prepared to hold the fish under appropriate conditions for what may be two or more days, until agency personnel can take possession.

7. **Immediate issuance.** The memo recommends that the guidelines and the draft protocol be adopted and sent immediately to [[Collecting Permits]] holders and other cooperators.

8. **Bottom-line conclusion.** No single protocol can guarantee cost-effective and safe transport and handling of wild bonytail. The authors judge that securing whatever few bonytail are encountered in the coming years outweighs the risks of transport and holding.
