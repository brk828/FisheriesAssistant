## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery**, located near Whiteriver, Arizona, and operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region II) since 1934 under authority of the Coordination Act (48 Stat. 401, March 10, 1934). The facility functions as one half of the **Alchesay-Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery Complex** and is not managed as an independent station -- most operational and distribution data are reported as cumulative complex totals rather than station-specific figures. The hatchery's federal mission is to produce and stock trout species, most notably the [[Apache Trout]], to fulfill federal trust responsibilities for Indian Reservation waters and other federally managed waters across Arizona, New Mexico, and southern Colorado. The station also serves an active public education role, hosting approximately 3,500 visitors in FY1991 and maintaining a "Living Stream" exhibit used to showcase Apache trout at regional public events.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water Source:** Production water is supplied entirely by gravity flow from **three natural springs**, delivering a combined rate of **1,800–2,250 gpm**, indicating a coldwater spring-fed hatchery system suited to salmonid physiology.
- **Habitat Infrastructure:** The facility replicates coldwater stream/raceway conditions via:
  - A tankhouse with 24 concrete tanks
  - A second tankhouse with 20 fiberglass tanks
  - Three banks of three concrete raceways
  - Two banks of six concrete raceways
  - Five rearing ponds
- **Stocking Range:** Distributed fish are released into **Federal (FWS and other federal) waters, state waters, and private waters** across **Arizona, Colorado, and New Mexico**, supporting native and recreational trout fisheries on Indian Reservation lands and other federally managed watersheds.
- **Ecological Role:** The hatchery functions as a critical link in [[Salmonid Stock Enhancement]] and conservation aquaculture, particularly supporting the recovery and management of the native, ecologically restricted [[Apache Trout]] within its historic White Mountains range, alongside stocking of non-native game trout species for recreational fisheries.
