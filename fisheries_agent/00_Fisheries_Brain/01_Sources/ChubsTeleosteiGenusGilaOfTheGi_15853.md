## Executive Summary

This ichthyological note by W. L. Minckley addresses the long-disputed taxonomy of chub fishes (genus *[[Gila]]*) inhabiting the Gila River Basin of Arizona, New Mexico (USA), and Sonora (México). Historically lumped as a polytypic *[[Gila Robusta]]* or treated as a "*Gila robusta* complex," these fishes have alternated between recognition as full species, subspecies, or ecological variants. The paper synthesizes decades of morphological and genetic (allozyme, mtDNA) data to argue that three morphologically distinct, largely parapatric forms — *[[Gila Robusta]]* (roundtail chub), *[[Gila Intermedia]]* (Gila chub), and the newly resurrected *[[Gila Nigra]]* (headwater chub, replacing the informal "grahami" designation and the invalid name *Gila grahami*) — warrant separate species-level recognition despite the absence of fixed genetic differences among them. The author proposes that "grahami" (=*G. nigra*) likely arose through ancient introgressive hybridization between *G. robusta* and *G. intermedia*, yet has since stabilized as an independently evolving lineage. The note closes with a dichotomous identification key and strong conservation recommendations, given the pending or existing Endangered Species Act status of nearly all taxa in the complex.

## Background & Description

Diagnostic meristic and morphometric characters distinguishing the four taxa treated in the key (see [[Table 1]] for population-level data):

- **[[Gila Elegans]] (bonytail)**
  - Lateral line scales: >90 (up to >110), often thin/embedded or apparently absent
  - Dorsal-fin rays ≥10; anal-fin rays 10; pelvic-fin rays 9(–10)
  - Body strongly elongate/attenuate; caudal peduncle "pencil-like," longer than head
  - Head length / caudal peduncle depth usually ≥5
  - Fins large, margins often strongly falcate (deeply concave)
  - Coloration variable: silvery in turbid water; dark above/silvery-white below in clear water

- **[[Gila Intermedia]] (Gila chub)**
  - Lateral line scales: 63–80 (upper limit ~83), thick, broadly overlapping; basal radii present
  - Dorsal-fin rays ≤8 (rarely 9); anal-fin rays 8 (rarely 7 or 9); pelvic-fin rays 7–8 (rarely 9)
  - Head length / caudal peduncle depth ≤3.0
  - Body thick, chunky; fins small, often darkly pigmented; margins mostly rounded
  - Coloration dark overall, sometimes blackened; scales often darkly outlined with lighter centers

- **[[Gila Nigra]] (headwater chub, = "grahami")**
  - Lateral line scales: typically 78–82 (range 73–90)
  - Dorsal- and anal-fin rays 8–9
  - Caudal peduncle length/depth <2.4; Head length/caudal peduncle depth <3.2 (population means)
  - Coloration typically dark but grayish rather than black; sometimes dark dorsal/lateral blotches
  - Morphologically intermediate between *G. intermedia* and *G. robusta*, but nearer *G. intermedia*

- **[[Gila Robusta]] (roundtail chub)**
  - Lateral line scales: typically >82 (range 76–99), thin, narrowly overlapping; basal radii absent to weak
  - Dorsal- and anal-fin rays typically 9 (rarely 8)
  - Caudal peduncle length/depth >2.3; Head length/caudal peduncle depth >3.25
  - Coloration typically silvery, especially on sides; dark blotches rare (most common in Bill Williams drainage, AZ, and in *G. jordani* of NV)

Nomenclatural note: *Gila grahami* Baird and Girard 1854b is a synonym of *G. robusta* (based on syntype re-identification; Fowler, 1925) and thus unavailable. *[[Gila Nigra]]* Cope 1875 is the valid applicable name for the "grahami" form, based on syntypes from Ash Creek and San Carlos, AZ.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Each taxon occupies a distinct habitat position along a headwater-to-mainstem gradient within the same drainage systems, exhibiting **parapatry** rather than sympatry:

- **[[Gila Elegans]]**: Historically occupied open, strongly flowing channels of large rivers throughout the Colorado River system (CO, WY to México); now extirpated from the Gila River Basin and restricted mainly to lower Colorado Basin mainstem reservoirs; reintroduced in the Gila River Basin.

- **[[Gila Intermedia]]**: Restricted to the Gila River Basin (AZ, NM, northern Sonora). Typical of headwaters of small-to-middle-sized streams, marshes (e.g., beaver ponds), cutoff pools, and undercut pools of eroded reaches. Strongly associated with cover — undercut banks, woody debris, scoured areas near obstructions.

- **[[Gila Nigra]]**: Restricted to the Gila River Basin (AZ, NM). Found in headwater-to-middle reaches of mid-sized streams; strongly associated with cover such as undercut banks and deep pools near obstructions.

- **[[Gila Robusta]]**: Distributed throughout the Colorado River Basin (CO, WY to northern Sonora–Baja California) and as poorly studied forms in coastal rivers south to Sinaloa, México. Typical of deepest pools and eddies of mid-sized to large streams/rivers; less associated with cover than *intermedia* or *nigra*, favoring eddies in the deepest pools.

**Key ecological pattern**: *G. intermedia* or *G. nigra* commonly occupies upstream/tributary reaches while *G. robusta* occupies downstream/mainstem reaches of the same river system — documented repeatedly since the 1920s (Hubbs, Miller, Minckley) with no confirmed case of true syntopic co-occurrence among any two forms.

## Key Findings

- **No fixed genetic differentiation** exists among *G. robusta*, *G. intermedia*, and "grahami" across 16 allozyme loci (DeMarais, 1992); a shared mitochondrial DNA restriction-endonuclease haplotype was found across all 54 individuals sampled from the Gila River Basin, spanning all three forms.
- **Genetic variation is geographically independent** of taxonomic designation when taxonomy is ignored, indicating uncoupling of morphology and genetics — a pattern increasingly recognized in Western American fishes (Dowling and Secor, 1997).
- **Parapatric, not sympatric, distributions**: In no confirmed instance have two of the three forms been collected at the same locale; a single historic claim of co-occurrence (Evermann and Rutter, 1895) is considered dubious due to specimen-purchasing practices of the era.
- **Hybrid origin hypothesis**: "grahami" (=*[[Gila Nigra]]*) is proposed to have originated through ancient introgressive hybridization between *G. robusta* and *G. intermedia*, subsequently stabilizing as a distinct, independently evolving lineage — fitting the Dowling and Secor (1997) definition of a hybrid-derived species.
- **Taxonomic recommendation**: The author advocates species-level recognition for all three forms (*[[Gila Robusta]]*, *[[Gila Intermedia]]*, *[[Gila Nigra]]*) rather than treating them as subspecies or a single polymorphic species, arguing this best serves both scientific clarity and conservation prioritization.
- **Nomenclatural resolution**: *Gila grahami* is invalid (synonym of *G. robusta*); *Gila nigra* Cope 1875 is resurrected as the valid name for the "grahami" morphotype/headwater chub.
- **Conservation urgency**: All members of the *Gila* species complex in the Colorado River Basin face significant conservation concern — *G. elegans*, *[[Gila Seminuda]]*, and *[[Gila Jordani]]* are already federally Endangered (USFWS 1996); *G. intermedia* (including "grahami"/*G. nigra*) is a listing candidate (USFWS 1997); *G. robusta* status surveys are ongoing. All Mexican-range taxa (*elegans*, *intermedia*, *robusta*) are listed under Mexico's NOM-059-ECOL-1994 as species of special concern. The author recommends conserving the entire complex as a unit given irreplaceable evolutionary and biogeographic information encoded within these enigmatic lineages.
