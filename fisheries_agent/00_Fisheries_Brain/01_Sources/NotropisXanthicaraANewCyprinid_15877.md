# Notropis xanthicara, a New Cyprinid Fish from the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, North-Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 taxonomic paper by [[W. L. Minckley]] and Gerald L. Lytle (Arizona State University) formally describes *Notropis xanthicara*, the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Shiner]], a cyprinid minnow endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The species is most closely related to, and probably derived from, [[Notropis Rutilus]] of the [[Cyprinella Subgenus]]. The authors examined about 1,000 specimens and separate it from its relative by a combination of pigmentation, meristic counts, and body proportions. The paper also places the species within the broader biogeography and endemism of the basin.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The *lutrensis-ornatus* group of the subgenus Cyprinella of *Notropis* contains several nominal and undescribed forms related to [[Notropis Proserpinus]] (Girard). The assemblage includes:

- *N. proserpinus*: clear waters of the lower Pecos River system, southern New Mexico to Devil's River and San Felipe Spring, Texas (the authors could not verify a record from the lower Rio Grande Valley).
- *N. lepidus*: spring-fed waters of the Nueces, Frio, Medina, and Guadalupe systems, Texas.
- *N. rutilus*: similar habitat in the Ríos Salado and San Juan, northern Mexico.
- Two undescribed species, one from the Río Conchos basin, Chihuahua, and *N. xanthicara* from the Cuatro Ciénegas basin.

**Material.** About 1,000 specimens of *N. xanthicara* were examined. The holotype (UMMZ 188782) is a mature, tuberculate male, 45 mm long, collected 6 April 1961 in the Río Puente Colorado. Paratypes are held in several collections, including ASU, KU, UMMZ, and UNL (Universidad de Nuevo León). Localities are given as kilometres from the centre of Cuatro Ciénegas.

**Diagnosis and morphology.** Key diagnostic traits include:

- Terete body, not slab-sided; slender, delicate structure with fragile fin-rays.
- Lateral-line scales usually 34 (about 66% of 149 fish), often 33 (21%).
- Anal fin-rays usually eight (264 of 283 fish).
- Pharyngeal teeth 4-4, slender and hooked, with serrated grinding edges.
- Standard length 30.2–56.0 mm (149 fish).
- Predorsal length averages 54.1% of standard length; head 26.6%; snout 8.0%; body depth 21.7%.
- Discrete, black lateral band about one scale-row wide, extending to the eye and continuing as a preocular bar.
- Belly immaculate, with black peritoneum showing at the midline; predorsal streak broad and diffuse; postdorsal streak faint or absent.
- Breeding males predominantly yellow (especially head and fins), with brassy-gold dorsum and intensely yellow fins. Nuptial tubercles on the snout are separated from those on the dorsum by a pre-narial hiatus, and the nape lacks tubercles.

**Comparison with *N. rutilus*.** The two species are similar in fin shape and overall structure, but *N. rutilus* has a deeper body (23.1% vs 21.7%), a more decurved lateral line with melanophores at scale margins, a blunter head, a darker and less discrete lateral band, a white belly with a speckled peritoneum, and generally darker fin pigmentation. Breeding male *N. rutilus* are greenish-yellow and often have small nape tubercles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** *N. xanthicara* occurs widely throughout the Cuatro Ciénegas basin but has never been taken in shallow marshes or large terminal lakes. It is most abundant in the upper reaches of streams, just below spring sources (limnocrenes). Localities span the Río Churince, Posos de la Becerra, Río Garabatal, Río Mesquites, Río Puente Chiquito, and Santa Tecla systems, among others. Canals on the north and northeast sides of the basin yielded no shiners in recent collections, apart from a single specimen from Canal de La Angostura (more than 50 seining collections there since 1960).

**Behaviour.**
- In streams and canals, groups of 3–25 individuals occupy shear zones between current and backwater, or move near the bottom in current.
- By day, they forage at the surface and inspect nearly all floating objects.
- At night, groups of 4 to more than 30 rest on the bottom in eddies and are lethargic when illuminated.
- Breeding behaviour has not been observed.
- *N. rutilus* concentrates above and below riffles, with nuptial males on the swiftest riffles.

**Associated fauna.** The most common companions are [[Astyanax Fasciatus Mexicanus]] (Mexican tetra) and [[Dionda Episcopa]] (roundnose minnow), both in springs and streams. *N. xanthicara* has been caught with most other basin fishes but not with those of marshes or saline lakes (*Cyprinodon atrorus*, *Lucania interioris*, *Gambusia longispinis*).

**Biogeography and endemism.** The Cuatro Ciénegas basin has been isolated sporadically for millennia. Aquatic organisms there show a range of differentiation, from endemic subfamilies and genera in molluscs (Taylor, 1966) to vertebrates and invertebrates indistinguishable from outside populations. The authors place *N. xanthicara* at an intermediate level, probably dating to the later [[Pleistocene]]. Of 18 known fish species in the basin, 10 are endemic (more than 50%), with three cichlids and a darter still to be described. Other species (a catfish, [[Largemouth Bass]], and a sunfish) differ from outside forms at most at the subspecies level.

**Hybridization and variation.**
- Some *N. rutilus* from the Río Salado de los Nadadores have pigmentation approaching that of *N. xanthicara*, especially in non-breeding fish. The authors attribute this more likely to water clarity, but introgression of pigment characters is possible and under study.
- Five specimens from Laguna de los Fresnos (Santa Tecla system) have longer heads, smaller snouts, larger eyes, and deeper bodies, tending toward *N. rutilus*, but their pigmentation resembles *N. xanthicara*. The authors tentatively treat them as a form of *N. xanthicara*.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Notropis xanthicara* is a valid new species, closely related to and probably derived from *N. rutilus*, and endemic to the Cuatro Ciénegas basin.
- **Diagnostic characters:** A discrete black lateral band, immaculate belly with black peritoneum, slender body and head, fragile fins, and yellow breeding coloration separate it from *N. rutilus*.
- **Meristic and morphometric differences:** Differences are slight but consistent. For example, predorsal length averages 54.1% vs 53.3% in *N. rutilus*, and the snout is longer (8.0% vs 7.1%). The dorsal-to-anal distance is shorter (25.3% vs 27.0%).
- **Sample base:** About 1,000 specimens were examined, with 149 counted for most *N. xanthicara* characters and 447 *N. rutilus* for comparison.
- **Distribution:** The species is widespread in springs and upper streams of the basin, absent from marshes and terminal lakes, and rare in parts of the basin where *N. rutilus* might be expected.
- **Origin:** Isolation in the large springs and spring-fed streams of the basin probably drove divergence, likely during the later Pleistocene.
- **Endemism:** The basin supports a high proportion of endemic fishes (10 of 18 species), reinforcing its status as a centre of freshwater endemism.
- **Open questions:** Possible introgression with *N. rutilus* in the Río Salado, the status of the Los Fresnos specimens, and breeding biology remained unresolved, pending further study.
