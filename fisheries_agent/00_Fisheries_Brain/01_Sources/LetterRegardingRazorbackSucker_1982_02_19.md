## Executive Summary

This document is a 1982 interagency letter from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (James Brooks, Fisheries Biologist) to the Nevada Department of Wildlife (Cliff Jett), clarifying field activities conducted January 16–20, 1982, at Carp Cove and Arizona Cove, Lake Mohave. The stated purpose of the operation was to collect [[Razorback Sucker]] broodstock for transfer to the Dexter National Fish Hatchery. The letter addresses a "misunderstanding" arising after anglers observed piles of dead non-target fish (bycatch) during netting operations, and explains standard handling/disposal protocols for incidental catch. The document is valuable primarily as a historical record of broodstock collection methodology and bycatch composition during early conservation efforts for this now-endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] endemic.

## Background & Description

The source text contains no direct morphological descriptions (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) of the [[Razorback Sucker]] or bycatch species. Species are identified only by common name and count. Species encountered during the operation include:

- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — target species, family Catostomidae
- **Common Carp** (*Cyprinus carpio*) — bycatch, sacrificed for pituitary gland extraction
- **Channel Catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — bycatch
- **Rainbow Trout** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) — bycatch, high incidental mortality noted
- **Cutthroat Trout** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii*) — bycatch
- **Largemouth Bass** (*Micropterus salmoides*) — bycatch, mortality attributed to capture method

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context:** [[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the Colorado River, straddling the Arizona–Nevada border. Collection sites (Carp Cove, Arizona Cove) represent nearshore/cove littoral habitat likely used by spawning-aggregated Razorback Suckers in winter.
- **Seasonal timing:** Collections occurred in mid-to-late January, consistent with known winter/early-spring spawning aggregation behavior of [[Razorback Sucker]] in Colorado River reservoirs.
- **Species assemblage:** The bycatch composition (carp, channel catfish, rainbow trout, cutthroat trout, largemouth bass) reflects a mixed native/non-native fish community typical of altered Colorado River reservoir systems, with introduced salmonids and centrarchids co-occurring with the native catostomid.
- **Gear-habitat interaction:** Trammel nets were deployed across a ~1 mile stretch of open lake near the Arizona shoreline, while seining was restricted to shallow cove habitat (Arizona Cove), suggesting differential habitat use/capture efficiency between gear types for [[Razorback Sucker]].

## Key Findings

- **Collection methods:** Two gear types were used — trammel netting (open water/nearshore) and seining (shallow cove) — explicitly targeting Razorback Sucker broodstock.
- **Catch totals by date/method:**
  - Jan 16 (trammel nets, 7 nets, run at 2200 hrs): 8 Razorback Sucker, 1 Channel Catfish, 2 Carp, 6 Rainbow Trout
  - Jan 17 (trammel nets, run 0700 & 1900 hrs): 9 Razorback Sucker, 23 Carp, 6 Channel Catfish, 9 Largemouth Bass, 33 Rainbow Trout, 1 Cutthroat Trout
  - Jan 17 (seining, Arizona Cove, 1400–1600 hrs): 90 Razorback Sucker (no bycatch reported)
  - Jan 18 (seining, Arizona Cove, 0900 hrs): 126 Razorback Sucker
  - Jan 18–19 (trammel nets, delayed retrieval due to weather; all fish deceased upon final "run"): 44 Razorback Sucker, 38 Carp, 23 Channel Catfish, 13 Largemouth Bass, 26 Rainbow Trout, 6 Cutthroat Trout
- **Total Razorback Sucker collected across the operation:** approximately 277 individuals (8+9+90+126+44), later transported live via USFWS hatchery truck to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] on January 20, 1982.
- **Bycatch mortality:** Adverse weather (high winds, rough lake surface) on January 18 forced rapid net retrieval without immediate fish removal, resulting in total mortality of all non-target fish left in nets by the time nets were "run" for species counts.
- **Bycatch disposal protocol:** Standard practice described — carp retained for pituitary gland harvest (relevant to induced spawning/hormone research), dead/dying trout and other sport fish (e.g., Largemouth Bass) disposed of by puncturing the abdominal cavity or air bladder and sinking them in the lake to prevent surface nuisance accumulation.
- **Public relations context:** The letter explicitly addresses concern raised by observing anglers who witnessed piles of dead fish and counting procedures, indicating the operation's optics required formal clarification between AZGFD and NDOW.
- **Institutional coordination:** Distribution list (L. Paulson–UNLV, T. Liles–AGFD, B. Rinne–BR, J. Burton–USFWS, Parsons–NDOW) indicates multi-agency involvement in early [[Razorback Sucker]] conservation and broodstock development efforts, foundational to later captive propagation programs.
