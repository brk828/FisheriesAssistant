# FY1989 Annual Report: Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fishes, Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is the final Fiscal Year 1989 Annual Report of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin, issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6, Denver) in March 1990. It summarizes the status and accounting of all FY-1989 projects funded under the Work Plan. Projects focus on four endangered fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. Work covers instream flow determination, habitat and hydrology studies, propagation and stocking, nonnative fish and sportfishing control, monitoring, and data management. The total FY-89 budget was $2,326.5 thousand allocated and $2,288.6 thousand expended.

## Background & Description

The Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee required that every project funded under the Recovery Program submit an annual report. Each report summarizes accomplishments against objectives, presents a preliminary data analysis, and recommends actions for the coming year. The Technical Group reviews these reports and advises the Implementation Committee. The report has three main sections: status reports for 36 numbered projects (Section 2.0), an accounting summary of projects more than 10 percent over or under budget (Section 3.0), and individual project reports, which are available only on request.

Projects are grouped into categories: Program Management, Habitat Management (defining and acquiring flows), Habitat Development (fish passage), Stocking of native fishes, Nonnative Fish and Sportfishing Control, Research/Monitoring/Data Management, and Other. Each project carries a Technical Group priority rank (High, Medium or Low). Lead agencies include the Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR), the Colorado Division of Wildlife (CDOW) and the Wyoming Attorney General's Office. The project contact list names about 15 individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document centers on the Upper Colorado River system and its regulated flows. Several findings link fish ecology to flow management:

- **Flow regulation and reservoirs:** [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations, Aspinall Unit operations, and the proposed Muddy Creek Reservoir all affect river flows and temperature. Biological Opinions for Flaming Gorge rely on backwater, sediment and trophic studies of the [[Green River]].
- **Habitat modelling limits:** The [[PHABSIM]] instream flow model proved problematic on large, turbid rivers and needs years of field validation before it can guide Green River flow recommendations.
- **Flow needs in the [[15-Mile Reach]]:** Summer releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] supplement flows. The lack of stream gauges is noted as an inherent weakness for studying the reach.
- **Predation and competition:** Northern pike, channel catfish and smallmouth bass were sampled in the [[Yampa River]] for stomach contents. No identifiable Colorado squawfish were recorded in these samples, though processing was incomplete.
- **Nonnative fish:** Age-0 Colorado squawfish use habitat similar to several nonnative fishes but different from some native fishes.

## Key Findings

### Species Status and Research Results

- **Humpback chub:** Hypothesized that adults are limited by availability of their unique preferred habitat.
- **Colorado squawfish reproduction:** Hypothesized that reproductive success of Colorado squawfish and sympatric species is related to peak river discharge.
- **Overwinter survival:** A laboratory study of hatchery-reared age-0 Colorado squawfish examined the effects of size, condition factor and lipid content. Results were used to build curves for the range of length-overwinter survival scenarios possible in wild populations.
- **Low-flow effects:** The evaluation of low-flow releases on young Colorado squawfish in [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] studies was completed.
- **Predator interactions:** Yampa Canyon sampling of gamefish predation on drifting squawfish larvae failed because no drifting larvae of any species were collected. Northern pike were radio-tracked to study habitat use and competition.
- **Nursery habitat:** Backwater mapping reported on squawfish nursery habitat under varying Green River flows.
- **Channel dynamics:** Sediment studies recommended determining which spring flows would produce overbank flooding to provide habitat for adult Colorado squawfish and razorback suckers, with video monitoring if dam operations change.

### Flow Recommendations

- Biologically defensible flow recommendations were produced for the 15-Mile Reach for July, August and September.
- Yampa River biological report (Phase I) was completed and interim flow recommendations (Phase II) were issued in November 1989.
- The [[Little Snake River]] sediment dynamics study completed Phases I and II, with a draft report submitted in January 1990.
- The 40-year lease to deliver 10,000 acre-feet of water per year from Ruedi Reservoir to the 15-Mile Reach was finalized in February 1990.

### Propagation, Stocking and Introduction

- **Bonytail introduction:** Field work was completed, but few stocked hatchery-reared bonytails survived. Multiple handling and treatment techniques failed to improve survival. Adult hatchery bonytails are therefore not feasible for habitat-use studies. Further work on introduction protocols is recommended.
- **Bonytail retrieval:** Cancelled due to procedural concerns and reprogramming for cost overruns.
- **Razorback sucker (Green River):** Wild fish were captured, spawned artificially, and larvae reared for release. A draft report was due in May 1990.
- **Squawfish augmentation:** A PIT tag study was limited by a lack of target fish and suitable holding facilities. Survival of test fish in riverside gravel-pit ponds was low, so the tag's effect on growth and survival was not evaluated. Recommendations include controlled-environment PIT tag studies and developing refugia/broodstock populations from wild adult razorback suckers and adult and age-1 squawfish captured in spring 1990.
- **Genetics:** Accomplishments included an ad hoc work group, hiring a propagation coordinator, and an updated propagation/genetics plan.

### Kenney Reservoir Sportfishery

- 32,000 Colorado squawfish were stocked in late April 1989.
- 246 squawfish were collected by seining, gillnetting and electrofishing.
- No squawfish were collected within 10 miles of the White River upstream of the reservoir. 213 were collected up to 28.5 river miles below Taylor Draw Dam.
- CDOW recommended stocking at a minimum length of 4 inches, preferably in late May 1990, and implanting ultrasonic transmitters in six adults.

### Angler Education and Creel Survey

- "Swimming Upstream" brochures were distributed during a Utah creel census. Temporary signs appeared effective, and a Recovery Program newsletter was published.
- An Information and Education Plan was finalized in January 1990. Five of the 14 planned permanent signs were installed.
- The creel survey was implemented in Utah only. No incidental take was observed, although some anglers reported catch and release. Most anglers were local, were well aware of the Recovery Program, and made up most of the fishing pressure.

### Monitoring, Data and Taxonomy

- **Standardized monitoring:** The program for adult and young-of-the-year Colorado squawfish was completed. Data analysis is ongoing, and its efficacy will be evaluated in FY-90.
- **Database:** The database held 158 data files from four organizations. Late submissions (due March 1 after collection) are a recurring problem.
- **Cataract Canyon chub study:** Four trips monitored the [[Humpback Chub]] population in 1989. The final report was available in January 1990.
- **Gila taxonomy:** A Smithsonian project manager was hired to clarify confusion over the taxonomy of Gila species. A draft review of existing information was sent out for review in mid-December.

### Schedule Slippage and Pending Items

- The Razorback Sucker Management Plan draft was distributed, with few comments so far, and will be finalized in October 1990.
- The Compendium of Existing Information is due April 30, 1990.
- Establishment of Recovery Goals was deferred to FY-90.
- The 15-Mile Reach Flow Options study is about 2–3 months behind schedule. Recommendations include a river gauge near the upstream end of the reach, a downstream gauge to measure flow gains, and measuring discharge from the Orchard Mesa Pumping/Powerplant radial gates.
