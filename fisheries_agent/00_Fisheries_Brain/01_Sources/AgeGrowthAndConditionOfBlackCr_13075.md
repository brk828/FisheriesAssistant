# Age, Growth and Condition of Black Crappie in Lewis and Clark Lake, South Dakota (1954–1967)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This study analyzed age, growth, and length-weight (condition) relationships of [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*) in [[Lewis and Clark Lake]], South Dakota, a Missouri River mainstem reservoir formed in 1955 by the closure of Gavin's Point Dam. Based on 1,302 fish sampled from 1954–1964 and 1967 (808 scales aged), the study found an unusually abundant 1956 year class (the year following impoundment), but overall growth rates were slow relative to other black crappie populations nationally, and notably slower during the first several years post-impoundment than in later years (8–10 years post-impoundment). No sex-based differences were found in growth rate or length-weight relationships. Condition (length-weight slope, K factor) varied seasonally, typically dropping in June (post-spawn weight loss) and recovering by July–August, except in 1960, 1961, and 1967. This work stands as a key early record of [[Reservoir Fish Population Dynamics]] following impoundment of a large Missouri River reservoir.

## Background & Description
- **Body-scale relationship**: Slight curvilinearity detected; regression intercept at 11.5 mm total length was adopted as an approximation of a straight-line relationship (Fig. 1).
- **Scale reading challenges**: Annuli often poorly defined; growth sometimes continuous across year boundaries, making the first annulus difficult to detect in some individuals. Criteria for annulus identification included:
  1. Erosion of posterior circuli before formation of a complete new series
  2. Crowding of circuli
  3. Anastomosis of circuli in lateral fields
  4. Clear, narrow gaps encircling the focus
  - Erosion of outer circuli was particularly useful for aging older fish, though excessive erosion could obscure boundaries.
- **Reading consistency**: First and second scale readings agreed in 76.7% of cases; third readings agreed with a previous reading in 18.7%; fourth/fifth readings contributed 3.8% and 0.9% respectively (Table 1). Comparable to but better than the 67% first/second reading agreement reported by Neal (1963) for [[Clear Lake]] black crappie.
- **Maximum size/age**: Only one specimen exceeded 330 mm total length across the entire 1954–1967 dataset — a 9-year-old fish measuring 380 mm. No black crappies over 6 years old were collected before 1960.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Lewis and Clark Lake — smallest of six mainstem Missouri River reservoirs, area 113.3 km², maximum depth 17.7 m, mean depth 4.9 m. Water level maintained within ~1 m of 368.2 m mean sea level. Flushing time 8–10 days (March–November), ~30 days in winter.
- **Population status**: Black crappie were relatively abundant in 1957–1958 but assumed a minor role in the fishery thereafter (per Walburg 1964), unlike some reservoir systems where crappie become dominant.
- **Reproductive continuity**: Every year class from 1951 through 1966 was represented in collections, indicating consistent annual reproductive success rather than domination by one or two year classes — contrasting with patterns noted by [[Thompson 1941]] in some crappie populations.
- **Post-impoundment dynamics**: The 1956 year class (first full year after impoundment) was the most abundant recorded, though it exhibited comparatively poor growth — contrary to the typical expectation of rapid growth in newly expanding reservoir environments. Growth rates in 1956–1959 were notably slow, later improving from 1964–1966, suggesting increasing habitat/food stabilization as the reservoir matured. This mirrors findings by [[Shields 1958]] and [[Nelson 1961]].
- **Comparative growth**: Growth was markedly slower than in [[Norris Reservoir]], Tennessee (Eschmeyer and Jones 1941) — crappies there reached 340 mm within 3–4 years post-impoundment, whereas Lewis and Clark Lake crappies took ~8 years to reach only 290 mm.
- **Sampling gear**: Collected via frame nets (primary), experimental gill nets, bag seines, trap nets, semiballoon trawl, and electric shocker.

## Key Findings

### Age and Growth
- Sample: 1,302 total fish (526 males, 618 females); sex ratio not significantly different from 1:1 (χ² = 0.64).
- No significant growth rate differences detected between sexes.
- Growth was most rapid in year 1 of life, with annual increments decreasing in each successive year (standard crappie growth pattern).
- [[Lee's Phenomenon]] (apparent decline in back-calculated growth with fish age due to selective mortality/gear bias) was **not observed**, supporting non-selective sampling methodology.
- Hile growth indices (Fig. 2) showed 1956–1959 as years of below-average growth; growth improved 1964–1966.
- Mean calculated total lengths at annulus (all year classes combined, Table 2): Annulus 1 ≈ 68 mm; Annulus 5 ≈ 220 mm; Annulus 9 ≈ 314 mm.
- Equivalent weight-at-age (grams): Annulus 1 ≈ 4 g; Annulus 5 ≈ 153 g; Annulus 9 ≈ 456 g.

### Length-Weight Relationships & Condition
- Overall pooled equation (n=1,229): **Log W = -5.0186 + 3.075 Log TL** (W in grams, TL in mm) — used to approximate weight-at-age in Table 2.
- Slopes (b) of log length-log weight regressions across 98 samples ranged from 2.313 to 3.499 (extreme values from very small/skewed samples); ~49% of slopes fell between 2.75–3.25; 56% for samples with >10 fish.
- No significant differences in length-weight relationships attributable to **area of collection** or **sex** (tested via analysis of covariance; closest case was May 1964, pre-spawning: males b=3.19, females b=2.99; F=1.78 slopes, F=3.58 adjusted means, both below 95% critical value of 4.00).
- **Seasonal pattern in slope**: Slope consistently <3.0 in June samples — attributed to weight loss in larger/spawning individuals. Slope typically increased in July–August, **except in 1960, 1961, and 1967**.
  - Analysis of covariance for these anomalous years: no significant difference in slopes (F=0.43) but significant difference in adjusted mean weights (F=7.58); composite slope = 2.80; calculated weight at 160 mm was 53 g (1960), 64 g (1961), 60 g (1967).
- **Condition factor K(TL)**: Average values fell within the range designated "average" for Minnesota black crappie (1.21–1.50; Carlander 1953).
  - Condition was notably **poorer in 1957, 1959, and 1960** than other years.
  - Condition factors showed little correlation with Hile growth indices — e.g., 1956 had high condition factor despite low growth increment.
- Calculated weight at 178 mm (7 inches) total length varied by year from ~66 g (1959) to ~92 g (1963) (Table 6).

### Data/Methodology Notes
- Stratified subsampling (Ketchen 1949) used: max 10 fish per inch class per sex read for scales; all fish read if fewer than 10 per class.
- Data sourced from two agencies: South Dakota Dept. of Game, Fish and Parks (through 1961) and North Central Reservoir Investigations, Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife (from 1961 onward).
- Related unpublished reports by [[Shields 1957]], [[Shields 1958]], [[Sprague 1959]], and [[Walburg 1964]] examined overlapping scale data with generally consistent but not identical age-class compositions, attributed to differing subsampling.
