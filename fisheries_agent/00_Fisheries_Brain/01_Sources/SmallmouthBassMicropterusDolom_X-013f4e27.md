# Smallmouth Bass (Micropterus dolomieui dolomieui) - Arizona Distribution and Ecology

#ichthyology #centrarchidae #invasive_species

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui dolomieui*) is a member of family [[Centrarchidae]]. Body thick and chunky with moderate-sized mouth; upper jaw usually not reaching posteriad past front of eye. Spinous dorsal fin low, soft dorsal fin with 13-15 rays. Anal fin with 10-12 soft-rays. Lateral line with 66-78 scales. Coloration dark olive- or yellow-green to pale olive-brown, often mottled with vermiculations. Sides often with darkened vertical bars. Three dark streams radiate backward from eye and snout. Juveniles barred or spotted but never with dark lateral band; caudal fin with yellow or orange base, dusky to black center, and whitened tip.

**Fecundity:** Female smallmouth bass produce between 4,400 and 15,400 eggs per kilogram of body weight. Individual nests usually contain about 2,000 fertilized ova.

**Growth Parameters:** In eastern streams, smallmouth rarely achieve more than 100 mm at beginning of first autumn. However, in [[Black River]], Arizona, they attained lengths as great as 225 mm in their first summer, reflecting rich food supply and lack of competition. Such exceptional growth rates expected to decline as population increases and food becomes depleted.

**Maturity:** Spawning occurs from March through May in Arizona when water temperatures reach 15° to 18° C. Females do not mature all eggs simultaneously and may spawn again in a few days with same or another male.

**Habitat Associations:** Within natural range ([[Mississippi River]] basin east of [[Missouri River]] drainage and [[Great Lakes]] system), smallmouth bass most abundant in pools of streams with substantial proportion of riffle habitat, clean rocky hard bottoms, and gradients of 0.5 to about 5.0 m per km. In large rivers and lakes, smallmouth congregate over hard stony bottoms where currents are present. In Arizona, occurs in mainstream of lower [[Colorado River]], [[Verde River]] system, and throughout [[Salt River]] basin below about 2,200 meters elevation.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** In smaller streams with well-established permanent pools, smallmouth bass relatively sedentary and select "home pools" where individuals may remain for long periods. Remarkably capable of returning to home pool when displaced, sometimes from distance of many kilometers. This behavior compensates for downstream displacement during flooding, maintaining ordered spacing in habitat. Large permanent habitat exists in rocky streams of upper [[Salt River]] basin where bass are doing well. In streams where pools are transitory (changing with flood and sediment movement), bass are far more mobile and seem less abundant, possibly explaining lesser success in [[Verde River]] basin.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** Foods of young smallmouth bass consist mostly of tiny crustaceans. As they grow, shift to insects, then to other fishes, and finally to mixed diet of crayfish and other fishes. In upper [[Salt River]] system, rich food supply consists of native minnows and suckers ([[Gila]], [[Pantosteus]], [[Catostomus]]). Smallmouth appear to have totally suppressed reproductive success by local native fish species, presumably through direct predation on newly-hatched young. In 1969, schools of young *Gila*, *Pantosteus*, and *Catostomus* were closely attended by juvenile smallmouth bass, which repeatedly attacked feeding aggregations of young cyprinoids. Stomachs of small bass were distended with tiny fish larvae, yet they continued to feed. In [[Black River]], smallmouth bass occupied virtually all available habitats, and no young native fishes were found despite intensive examination of more than two kilometers of stream.

**Environmental Drivers:** Spawning triggered by water temperatures of 15° to 18° C. Habitat preference strongly influenced by substrate type (hard, rocky, clean bottoms) and stream gradient (0.5 to 5.0 m per km). Population success related to pool permanence and stability.

**Reproductive Behavior:** Males fan oval depression in sand or gravel (35 to 75 cm diameter) through violent lateral movements of body and caudal fin. Nests often concealed beneath cut banks or near debris. Male seeks sexually-mature female and actively drives her by nudging and biting to completed nest. Female rarely spawns immediately but usually flees, only to be pursued and driven back. Actual reproduction occurs with two fish side by side, male slightly behind and above, female against bottom tipped slightly on side. Female becomes rigid before egg extrusion, then rapidly vibrates during gamete passage; male simultaneously releases sperm. Process repeated several times until female depleted of eggs and moves or is driven to deeper waters. Female always driven away from nest; male assumes all responsibilities for caring for eggs and young. Eggs hatch in 3 to 5 days at typical water temperatures. Young remain in gravel interstices for about a week, then rise as school. Male herds and vigorously defends fry until 30 to 40 mm total length, when they become relatively solitary or travel in small groups and male ceases parental care.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** Smallmouth bass have been in Arizona at least since early 1940s. At present occur in mainstream of lower [[Colorado River]], [[Verde River]] system, and throughout [[Salt River]] basin below about 2,200 meters elevation. In larger reservoirs and lower Colorado River, seem relatively rare. Abundant in [[Verde River]] but rarely attain large sizes and unusually susceptible to external parasitism by *[[Learnea cyprinacea]]*, a parasitic crustacean common in that area. Recently stocked in upper [[Salt River]] system where enjoyed immediate "population explosion" and spread throughout basin in period of only 2 or 3 years. This phenomenal population growth presumably occurred because of general lack of competitors and rich food supply.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** Not reported for smallmouth bass itself. However, smallmouth bass represents significant threat to native fish species in Arizona through predation on newly-hatched young, resulting in total suppression of reproductive success by local native species.
