# Lake Mohave Fishery Survey Planning and Data Summary 1986-1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #Moronidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This inter-office memorandum from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] outlines data collection protocols for a planned March 1988 sampling effort on [[Lake Mohave]], part of the [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]]. The document includes historical fishery survey data from 1986-1987 showing distribution of multiple fish species across numbered sampling sites, with particular focus on [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]], and endangered native species including [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. The memo emphasizes standardized site identification and specific biological data requirements for the upcoming survey.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] system managed jointly by the [[National Park Service]] and state fish and wildlife agencies. The fishery contains both sport fish species and endangered native species. Survey work during 1986-1987 utilized multiple collection methods including [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Netting]], and [[Shore Seining]]. The [[National Park Service]] maintains a standardized cove naming and numbering system for the lake to facilitate consistent site identification across agencies.

Species documented in surveys include:
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
- [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*)
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
- [[Trout]] (species unspecified)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) - endangered native
- [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) - endangered native
- [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*)
- [[Carp]] and sunfish (present but omitted from summary data)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mohave]] represents critical habitat for endangered [[Colorado River]] native fishes, particularly [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. The 1986-1987 survey data shows extremely limited distribution of these native species: only 8 [[Razorback Sucker]] captured in 1986 and 12 in 1987 across all sites, with zero [[Bonytail]] captured either year. In contrast, introduced sport fish species were abundant, with 1,183 total fish captured in 1986 and 450 in 1987 across surveyed sites.

[[Threadfin Shad]] showed the highest abundance among documented species with 224 individuals captured in 1987. The presence of [[Striped Bass]] (68 in 1986, 5 in 1987) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (127 in 1986, 136 in 1987) indicates established predator populations that may impact native species recovery.

Shore seining efforts in 1987 covered 24 sites with varying effort levels (76-2745 square meters), documenting species composition in nearshore habitats. Multiple sites yielded no fish (effort = 0), suggesting patchy distribution or habitat-specific preferences.

## Key Findings

**Data Collection Requirements for March 1988 Survey:**

Minimum required data:
1. Total number by species of all fish collected by site
2. Total length and weight of all [[Largemouth Bass]]
3. Total length, weight, and gross stomach analysis of all [[Striped Bass]] (requiring sacrifice)

Additional data if time permits:
1. Scale samples and sex determination of [[Striped Bass]]
2. Scale samples of [[Largemouth Bass]]

**1986-1987 Survey Results:**

Total fish captured: 1,183 (1986), 450 (1987) across all surveyed sites

Species-specific totals:
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 127 (1986), 136 (1987)
- [[Channel Catfish]]: 103 (1986), 27 (1987)
- [[Trout]]: 68 (1986), 5 (1987)
- [[Striped Bass]]: 8 (1986), 12 (1987)
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: 8 (1986), 12 (1987)
- [[Bonytail]]: 0 (1986), 0 (1987)
- [[Threadfin Shad]]: 141 (1986), 99 (1987) [partial year]; 224 (1987 complete)

**Site-Specific Observations:**

Highest fish abundance at sites: 128 (107 fish in 1987), 136 (67 fish in 1986), 167 (36 in 1986, 42 in 1987), and 59 (64 in 1986, 15 in 1987).

[[Razorback Sucker]] documented at only 6 sites total across both years, with maximum of 2 individuals per site.

Shore seining in 1987 documented [[Largemouth Bass]] presence at 17 of 24 sites, with additional species including bluegill (BG), golden shiner (GS), black crappie (BC), mosquitofish (MoF), red shiner (RSh), and carp (C).
