# Lake Mohave Sampling Report (21–25 May 1990)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Moronidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This field report documents a targeted trammel-net survey conducted by [[Arizona State University]] (with assistance from the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]) in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada, during 21–25 May 1990. The primary objective was capture and removal of two endangered native Colorado River fishes, the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), for translocation to hatchery broodstock programs. Nets were fished along points and shorelines at the northern end of [[Cottonwood Bay]] at depths of 1–5 m, run continuously day and night, with non-target species cleared during daylight hours. Total sampling effort was 13.36 net-units (1 unit = 100 m² fished for 24 hours), yielding 666 fish across six species, overwhelmingly dominated by non-native [[Common Carp]] (89.5% of catch).

## Background & Description

### Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)
- 9 individuals captured, all during darkness in shallow water (1–2 m) over cobble substrate.
- Routine morphometric measurements (TL, weight) were **not taken** to minimize handling stress on this critically endangered species.
- Catch composition: 6 adults (21 May), 2 adults + 1 unmarked juvenile (23–24 May).
- Two adults died in overnight holding; carcasses frozen for later study.

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- 8 individuals captured; 7 measured, weighed, examined, and tagged with plain yellow Floy tags (nos. 05258–05262, 05264 scratched).
- **Males (n=2):** 51.0 cm TL / 1700 g; 59.0 cm TL / 2300 g.
- **Females (n=5):** mean 61.8 ± 3.6 cm TL (range 55.5–64.6 cm); mean weight 2820 ± 340 g (range 2300–3100 g).
- One specimen not examined.
- Recapture: one female (64.6 cm, 3050 g) originally tagged with red Floy tag ("AGFD 01056") in the same location in November 1989 — indicating **6-month site fidelity** within Cottonwood Bay.

### Striped Bass (*Morone saxatilis*)
- 29 individuals collected (non-target, cleared during daytime effort).
- 26 measured fish: mean 32.5 ± 10.5 cm TL (range 20.5–49.0 cm).

### Other Species (Non-target bycatch)
- **Common Carp** (*Cyprinus carpio*): 596 individuals (dominant catch).
- **Channel Catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus*): 23 individuals.
- **Bluegill** (*Lepomis macrochirus*): 1 individual.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat targeted:** Shallow (1–5 m), cobble-substrate shoreline and point habitat at the northern end of [[Cottonwood Bay]] within [[Lake Mohave]], a mainstem Colorado River reservoir.
- **Bonytail behavior:** Captured exclusively at night in shallow cobble habitat, suggesting nocturnal shoreline activity/foraging or refuge-seeking behavior in this age class/population remnant.
- **Razorback Sucker site fidelity:** Recapture of a tagged female in the same bay after 6 months suggests localized habitat use or seasonal return to specific reservoir zones, relevant to broodstock and reintroduction planning.
- **Community structure:** The overwhelming numerical dominance of introduced [[Common Carp]] (89.5% of total catch, CPE 44.611) relative to native [[Bonytail]] (1.4%) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (1.2%) illustrates the degree to which non-native species have come to dominate the reservoir fish community, a well-documented conservation concern for endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **Predatory bycatch:** Presence of [[Striped Bass]], a non-native piscivore, in substantial numbers (4.5% of catch, CPE 2.171) is ecologically relevant as a potential predation pressure on juvenile native fishes, including bonytail juveniles.

## Key Findings
- **Total sampling effort:** 13.36 trammel-net units (1 unit = 100 m² fished/24 hr); total catch = 666 fish across 6 species.
- **Catch Per Effort (CPE) by species:**
  | Species | Number | Percent | CPE |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | [[Common Carp]] | 596 | 89.5% | 44.611 |
  | [[Bonytail]] | 9 | 1.4% | 0.674 |
  | [[Razorback Sucker]] | 8 | 1.2% | 0.599 |
  | [[Channel Catfish]] | 23 | 3.5% | 1.722 |
  | [[Striped Bass]] | 29 | 4.5% | 2.171 |
  | [[Bluegill]] | 1 | 0.2% | 0.075 |
  | **Total** | **666** | **100%** | **49.850** |

- **Conservation actions:**
  - 4 healthy adult bonytail transported to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] on 22 May.
  - 2 bonytail that died in holding were frozen for later scientific use.
  - 2 additional adult bonytail (23–24 May) transported immediately to Willow Beach NFH; 1 unmarked juvenile bonytail sacrificed and frozen for examination.
  - Surviving wild-caught bonytail slated for transfer to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], joining a broodstock of only 11 other wild-caught Mohave bonytail — highlighting the **extreme rarity** of wild bonytail genetic stock.
  - 7 of 8 razorback suckers tagged (plain yellow Floy tags) and released back into Lake Mohave for continued monitoring.
- **Notable recapture:** A tagged female razorback sucker (red Floy tag "AGFD 01056") was recaptured in the same location after 6 months, providing direct evidence of short-term site fidelity relevant to population monitoring and future capture efforts.
- **Population rarity context:** The addition of even a handful of wild bonytail (9 captured, 6 ultimately viable for hatchery transfer) represents a significant proportional increase to the known wild-caught broodstock population, underscoring the critically endangered status of this species in [[Lake Mohave]].
