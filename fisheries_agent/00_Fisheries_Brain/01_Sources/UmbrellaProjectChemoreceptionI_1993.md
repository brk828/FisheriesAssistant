# Umbrella Project: Chemoreception, Imprinting, and Propagation of Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #conservation-biology #chemoreception #olfactory-imprinting

## Executive Summary

This FY93 annual report details a multi-year investigation into **olfactory imprinting** and **thyroid hormone (thyroxine, T4) dynamics** in two endangered Colorado River Basin fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The central hypothesis, modeled on salmonid natal homing research, is that these species imprint on chemical cues at their spawning sites during egg/swim-up larval stages via a thyroid-hormone-mediated mechanism, and later use these imprinted cues to home back to spawning grounds as migratory adults. The study aims to develop synthetic chemical imprinting (using morpholine and phenethyl alcohol) and natural stream-odor imprinting techniques that could be applied to recovery efforts, including reintroduction to extirpated stream reaches in the Upper Colorado River Basin. Key FY93 activities included T4 hormone assays across early development, synthetic chemical exposure trials at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], growout of multiple year-classes at [[Ouray Hatchery]], gonadotropin-induced maturation attempts, and natural stream-water imprinting trials using Green, Gunnison, and Uncompahgre river water. Significant shortcomings included failure to induce spawning maturity in 2-year-old fish and high fungal mortality in natural-odor imprinting egg batches.

## Background & Description

- **Life stages studied**: Eggs, swim-up larvae, juveniles, and 2-year-old subadults.
- **Thyroxine (T4) profile in Colorado Squawfish** (measured across 1,350 eggs/larvae from fertilization to 68 days post-fertilization):
  - Peak 1: **15.8 ± 6.9 ng/g body weight** immediately post-fertilization (egg stage).
  - Decline to **7.5 ± 2.0 ng/g** by day 3.
  - Peak 2: **14.5 ± 3.2 ng/g** just prior to hatching (day 4 post-fertilization).
  - Trough: **2.7 ± 0.6 ng/g** on day 11 post-fertilization.
  - Peak 3: **12.5 ± 5.8 ng/g** at swim-up (day 14 post-fertilization) — coincides with the presumed critical imprinting window.
  - Decline to **<1.0 ng/g** by day 41, remaining low through day 68.
- **Secondary sex characteristics (tubercle development)** were tracked in 2-year-old razorback suckers as a proxy for approaching sexual maturity/migratory readiness (see Ecological Niche/Key Findings for data table).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: [[Colorado River Basin]] mainstem rivers, including the Green, Gunnison, Uncompahgre, and Colorado River (Lake Powell arm). Both target species are large-river endemics adapted to turbid, warm, historically free-flowing river conditions.
- **Reproductive migration & natal homing**: Both [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] display documented repeat homing to specific spawning sites, analogous to salmonid natal homing behavior, implicating olfactory imprinting as the underlying mechanism.
- **Larval drift ecology**: Larvae are known to drift up to **200 km downstream** from spawning sites within days of swim-up (~day 10–14 post-fertilization), meaning imprinting to spawning-site cues must occur at or before this stage if larvae/juveniles are to later relocate the natal site.
- **Conservation status driver**: Both species are federally endangered; the study is explicitly tied to the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] and addresses extirpation of populations from portions of the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **Interspecific comparison**: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) was studied in a companion technical report as a comparative species for thyroxine developmental profiling.
- **Threats/challenges to recovery**: Fungal infection of eggs during natural-odor imprinting trials (Bellevue and Ouray hatcheries) caused near-total mortality, suggesting water quality/recirculation and fungal control are major bottlenecks for natural imprinting-based propagation strategies.

## Key Findings

1. **Thyroid hormone (T4) surges align with hatching and swim-up stages**, supporting the hypothesis that these are the critical periods for olfactory imprinting in Colorado squawfish, paralleling thyroid-hormone-induced imprinting mechanisms documented in [[Salmonidae]].

2. **Synthetic chemical imprinting protocol established**: Colorado squawfish eggs (from 8 males × 7 females spawned 2 June 1993) were successfully split into three treatment lots (morpholine at 5×10⁻⁵ mg/L, phenethyl alcohol at 5×10⁻⁷ mg/L, and untreated controls) and exposed continuously from fertilization through two days post-swim-up.

3. **Gonadotropin (HCG) injection failed to induce full sexual maturation** in 2-year-old razorback suckers despite doses escalating from 10 IU/kg to 200 IU/kg over four months (Jan–May 1993). Tubercle development increased initially then subsided at higher doses, suggesting a **negative feedback inhibition of pituitary hormone production** at high HCG dosages — a pattern also observed in kokanee salmon, where reduced dosing successfully triggered maturation.

4. **Natural stream-odor imprinting trials suffered severe fungal mortality**: 
   - Uncompahgre/Gunnison River water incubation (Bellevue hatchery) resulted in near-complete loss; only 7 fish survived by end of study.
   - Green River water incubation (razorback bar) from ~5,100 eggs yielded only ~100 hatched, with 32 fish surviving as of November 1993.
   - Proposed remediation: upwelling jar incubation, dilution of river water with fungus-free well water, and formalin/fungicide treatment prior to the imprinting window (~day 4).

5. **Imprinting-response testing was delayed** due to inability to bring fish into migratory physiological condition and administrative delays in preparing the Leota Pond 4 semi-natural test channel at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]].

6. **A backup Y-maze/radial-arm testing apparatus was proposed** (~30 ft diameter center tank with four 50 ft arms), modeled on salmon imprinting test designs, to supplement the semi-natural pond trial and control for confounding variables (water velocity, channel shape, pump failure). Estimated cost: **$17,000**, funded via existing Bureau of Reclamation contract funds.

7. **Recovery strategy implications**: Four alternative hatchery/recovery strategies were proposed leveraging imprinting biology — (1) in-situ Heath Tray incubation at natural spawning sites, (2) egg transfer to "adopted" imprinting sites, (3) synthetic chemical imprinting with later river-metering of the same chemical to guide hatchery-reared fish to new spawning/nursery habitat, and (4) full hatchery-broodstock imprinting to establish new spawning runs at remote sites to avoid mixing with wild populations.

8. **Budgetary note**: EWU underspent its FY93 allocation by $8,000 due to the inability to test the 1991 razorback sucker year-class; funds were redirected toward construction of the Y-maze apparatus.
