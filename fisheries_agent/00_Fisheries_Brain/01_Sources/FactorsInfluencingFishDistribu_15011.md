# Factors Influencing Fish Distribution in Two Desert Reservoirs, Central Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #Centrarchidae #Clupeidae #Cyprinidae #desert-reservoirs

## Executive Summary

This study (Rinne, Minckley & Bersell, 1981) examined vertical and horizontal distribution of introduced sport and forage fishes—[[Threadfin Shad]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], and [[Carp]]—in two central Arizona desert reservoirs, [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[Apache Lake]], on the Salt River. Sampling from 1970–1971 using vertical gillnets, water quality profiling, and plankton/benthos collection revealed that approximately 90% of fish were confined to the upper 10 m of the water column. This depth restriction was attributed to a combination of thermocline depth, dissolved oxygen concentration, and depth of the euphotic zone, with euphotic zone depth (via its control of phytoplankton and zooplankton food supply) emerging as the most consistent limiting factor. The authors conclude that fishes introduced into western reservoirs remain intrinsically shallow-water, littoral inhabitants due to fundamental limnological constraints common to mid-latitude lentic systems, a pattern consistent with findings from southeastern U.S. reservoirs.

## Background & Description

- **Study system**: Two warm, monomictic impoundments on the Salt River, central Arizona — [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[Apache Lake]] — built originally under the Reclamation Act of 1902, now supporting multi-purpose use including a substantial [[Sport Fishery]] and formerly a limited commercial fishery.
- **Study period**: Summer 1970 through autumn 1971, sampled during three limnologically distinct periods: summer stagnation (Jul-Aug), winter homeothermy (Nov-Dec), and spring stratification onset (Mar-Apr).
- **Target species** (from 40 recorded, 24 common, only 4 abundant enough for analysis):
  - [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) — introduced forage fish, comprised 76% of catch by number
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) — principal predator on shad, 12% of catch
  - [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*)
  - [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
  - (combined Black Crappie + Carp made up remaining 12%)
- **Methods**:
  - Vertical gillnets (13, 38, 76 mm bar mesh panels) set at fixed transects, surface to bottom
  - Multi-sensor probe for temperature/dissolved oxygen profiles; submarine photometer for light penetration
  - Chlorophyll *a* measured as proxy for phytoplankton abundance (method after Creitz & Richards; Richards & Thompson)
  - Net plankton (zooplankton proxy) collected via Clarke-Bumpus sampler (#10 net, 0.158 mm aperture) at 3.5, 7.0, 14.0 m
  - Benthos (chironomid dipterans, oligochaete worms) sampled via Ekman dredge (225 cm²)
  - Multiple Linear Regression Program (MLRP) used to correlate fish distribution with physico-chemical and biological variables

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Reservoir Morphology
| Parameter | Roosevelt Lake | Apache Lake |
|---|---|---|
| Max depth (m) | 62.5 | 76.2 |
| Mean depth (m) | 24.3 | 28.7 |
| Volume (ha-m ×10³) | 170.4 | 30.2 |
| Shoreline length (km) | 158.8 | 88.5 |
| Mean slope of basin | 7.0 | 32.0 |

- Roosevelt Lake is far larger (6× the volume, 2× the shoreline, 6× the mean breadth/surface area of Apache) but morphologically distinct — broader, more wind-exposed, receiving warm surface inflow from the [[Salt River]] and [[Tonto Creek]].
- Apache Lake is fjord-like, receives cold hypolimnic inflow, and is far more transparent (euphotic zone ~9.2 m vs. 5.4 m in Roosevelt).
- Both lakes are strongly stratified April–November and isothermal December–March, typical of [[Warm Monomictic Lakes]].
- **Metalimnion depth**: 10–15 m in Roosevelt vs. 5–7 m in Apache — attributed to differential wind exposure and inflow characteristics.
- **Dissolved oxygen**: High (≥7.0 mg/L) surface-to-bottom in winter/spring; hypolimnic deficiencies (<1.0 mg/L) in summer. Apache Lake maintained a slightly thicker oxygenated zone (>2.0 mg/L) than Roosevelt.
- **Turbidity**: Highest near inflow transects in Roosevelt Lake (R-1, R-4), consistent with its role as a sediment settling basin.

### Trophic Context
- [[Chlorophyll A]] concentration (phytoplankton proxy) showed strong positive correlation with fish abundance (r = 0.85).
- [[Net Plankton]] (zooplankton proxy) showed weak correlation with fish abundance (r = 0.23), likely due to being influenced by both predation (shad) and food supply (phytoplankton) simultaneously.
- Benthic invertebrates ([[Chironomidae]], [[Tubificidae]]) showed no consistent correlation pattern with gillnet catches.
- Threadfin Shad diet dominated by diatoms and unicellular chlorophytes (pelagic feeding), not benthic invertebrates, despite chironomid pupae found in stomachs (ingested during pre-emergence surface migration).

## Key Findings

### Vertical Distribution
- **~90% of all fish captured in upper 10 m** of the water column across both reservoirs.
- **Threadfin Shad**: Concentrated above 10 m; deeper penetration in autumn/spring; average capture depth 4.2–5.2 m.
- **Largemouth Bass**: Mostly above 10 m; average capture depth 5.8–6.7 m; moved deeper in spring/autumn.
- **Black Crappie**: Essentially restricted to upper 10 m, concentrated above 5 m; average capture depth 3.4 m — most restricted species.
- **Carp**: Least restricted by physico-chemical factors; distributed more uniformly through depth (average depth 7.7–11.6 m); considered a habitat generalist relative to other species.

### Controlling Physico-Chemical Factors
| Fish species | Above thermocline (%) | >2.0 mg/L DO (%) | Within euphotic zone (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threadfin Shad | 100 – 30 | 99 – 84 | 71 – 63 |
| Largemouth Bass | 98 – 50 | 90 – 80 | 58 – 54 |
| Black Crappie | 100 | 94 | 77 |
| Carp | 100 – 20 | 80 – 70 | 27 – 41 |

(values = Roosevelt – Apache, respectively)

- Nearly all fish in Roosevelt Lake were captured above the thermocline, vs. <40% in Apache Lake — indicating fish penetrated deeper in Apache despite its shallower metalimnion.
- [[Dissolved Oxygen]] and [[Thermocline]] depth were less limiting in Apache than in Roosevelt.
- **Euphotic zone depth** was identified as the most ecologically important limiting factor overall, acting indirectly by controlling primary (phytoplankton) and secondary (zooplankton) productivity, which in turn structures shad distribution and, cascading upward, predator (bass, crappie) distribution.
- Multiple regression showed temperature (and secondarily dissolved oxygen) most consistently correlated with Shad/Bass distribution, but explained <20% of variance — a general rather than deterministic relationship.

### Horizontal Distribution
- Greater fish abundance consistently found at shallow-water, up-lake/inflow transects (R-1, R-4 in Roosevelt; A-1–A-3 in Apache) compared to deep-water transects.
- Seasonal shift: Shad moved from shallow to deep-water transects in autumn-winter; Largemouth Bass and Black Crappie were comparatively sedentary.
- Statistically significant differences (P<0.05) in catch between shallow and deep transects in Roosevelt Lake during all but autumn.
- Extreme drawdown events (e.g., Apache Lake, summer 1971) altered normal shallow-water concentration patterns temporarily; pre-drawdown patterns re-established after refilling.
- Declining catch-per-unit-effort over the study period linked to drought-driven lake level reductions and reduced runoff.

### Comparative Context
- Findings align with studies in southeastern U.S. reservoirs (Bull Shoals, Beaver, Keystone, Norris) showing similar shallow-water (<10 m), littoral distribution patterns for [[Threadfin Shad]] and [[Largemouth Bass]], and broader/deeper distribution for [[Carp]].
- Supports overall conclusion: fish introduced into western U.S. reservoirs remain fundamentally shallow-water, littoral organisms regardless of desert-specific limnological conditions — a pattern driven by universal mid-latitude lentic limnological constraints rather than region-specific desert reservoir dynamics.

## Conclusions
- No single environmental factor fully explains fish distribution; a combination of thermocline depth, dissolved oxygen, and euphotic zone depth jointly constrains fish to the upper water column.
- Euphotic zone depth is proposed as the most mechanistically important factor via its control over the food chain ([[Phytoplankton]] → [[Zooplankton]]/Shad → predatory fishes).
- Food supply (chlorophyll *a*, net plankton, benthos) explained seasonal but not spatial variation in fish catches — suggesting temporal synchrony between fish and food resources outweighs fine-scale spatial matching.
