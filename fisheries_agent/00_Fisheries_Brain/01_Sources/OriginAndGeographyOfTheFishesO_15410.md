# Origin and Geography of the Fishes of Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This chapter, authored by [[Robert Rush Miller]] and [[Michael Leonard Smith]], analyzes the zoogeography of the native freshwater fish fauna of central Mexico. The region's 202 native species in 31 families are dominated by endemic lineages, especially the [[Goodeidae]] and the silverside genera [[Chirostoma]] and [[Poblana]], whose distributions correspond more closely to ancient geological features than to modern drainage patterns. The authors identify six distributional patterns, or "tracks," reflecting Nearctic, Neotropical, western mountain, plateau, Pacific coastal and marine-origin components. Tectonic uplift, volcanism and stream capture fragmented drainages during the Late Cenozoic, producing lake systems that drove speciation and, later, extinction.

## Background & Description

The fauna sits at the transition between Nearctic and Neotropical faunas. North American groups thin out southward from the [[Rio Grande]], while coastal lowlands on the Atlantic side served as dispersal corridors. The study area includes the Atlantic Slope from the Rio Panuco to the Rio Papaloapan, the Pacific Slope from the Lerma-Santiago to the Verde-Atoyac drainage, and the Mexican Plateau from the Lerma-Santiago to the Puebla Basin, spanning roughly 16°N to 23°N.

The [[Lerma-Santiago River System]] is the hydrographic core of the region. The Rio Lerma historically drained most of the [[Mesa Central]] before capture by the Rio Grande de Santiago, which now outlets [[Lake Chapala]]. The Lerma Basin contains about 40 goodeid species and 19 *Chirostoma* plus 3 *Poblana* species, and relatively few primary freshwater fishes.

Study history is traced from early workers (Jordan and Evermann, Meek, Regan) through recent systematic work on lampreys, goodeids, atherinids and cyprinids. Fossil fish research began with Downs (1958) and expanded through the Late Cenozoic record of the western Mesa Central.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geology and climate.** The Mesa Central, one of the world's great tropical highlands, is dominated by volcanic features. Plate reorganization from the Eocene through the Oligocene produced a fracture-zone system beneath the region, with the Clarion fracture zone aligned with the Neovolcanic Axis. The [[Sierra Madre Oriental]] rose in the early Eocene (Hidalgoan Orogeny), while the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] developed from ignimbrite extrusion beginning 22-30 million years ago. Most relief is Miocene to Pleistocene in age.

**Paleohydrography.** Endorheic basins formed along the Neovolcanic Axis at about 20°N, through volcanic damming (e.g., Lake Zirahuén) and stream piracy. Lacustrine sediments range from Miocene to Late Pleistocene. A sequence of lakes from about 2 million years ago to the present is recorded at Lake Chapala, where the "Chapala Beds" of Palmer (1926) and Chapala Formation (Downs 1958) are inferred to represent a single lake larger than the modern one. Clements (1963) proposed shoreline terraces at 200, 150 and 100 m above Lake Chapala, but later mapping found no such terraces, so a lake at those levels could not have existed.

**Drainage capture and desiccation.** Headward erosion by coastal streams, including the Rio Atoyac Poblano (Puebla Basin), the Rio Grande de Santiago (Lake Chapala outlet) and the Rio Moctezuma (through the Sierra Madre Oriental), progressively reduced lacustrine habitat. Loss of a western outlet near Jocotepec likely caused desiccation of the San Marcos Basin lakes.

## Key Findings

- **Fauna composition:** 202 native species in 31 families. Of these, 53 species in 21 families are euryhaline or diadromous, and 149 are freshwater species or freshwater representatives of peripheral groups.
- **Dominant groups:** [[Goodeidae]] (15 genera, 39 species) and Atherinidae (three genera, 23 species) together make up 63 species, or 44% of the freshwater fauna. Goodeids alone account for nearly 20% of the total fauna.
- **Marine-origin residents:** About 30% of Mexican freshwater species are of marine origin, including ten permanent freshwater residents in eight families (for example, *Gobiesox fluviatilis*, *Strongylura hubbsi*, *Pseudophallus starksi*).
- **Six distributional patterns:**
  1. Endemic adaptive radiations on the Mesa Central (goodeids, silversides).
  2. Neotropical groups in the coastal lowlands (*Astyanax*, *Cichlasoma*, poeciliids).
  3. Plateau track, with sister groups in the Rio Grande Basin or eastern North America (*Moxostoma*, *Ictalurus*, *Micropterus*).
  4. Western mountain track, linking *Algansea* and *Yuriria* to *Gila* of the Sierra Madre Occidental.
  5. Pacific coastal track, with anadromous-derived lampreys and salmonids.
  6. Diverse marine invaders.
- **Plateau track evidence:** Concordant distributions of *Moxostoma*, *Ictalurus* and *Micropterus* suggest hydrographic exchanges across the currently arid Central Plateau, possibly as early as the Pliocene.
- **Goodeid antiquity:** [[Tapatia occidentalis]] was present in the Late Miocene or Early Pliocene, making goodeids the oldest documented freshwater component. Their sister family, the [[Empetrichthyidae]], occurs in western North America, which the authors link to plate movements along the faults of the Gulf of California.
- **Lake-dwelling species:** Silversides (*Poblana* and at least ten *Chirostoma*) dominate the lake fauna. Four goodeids (*Allotoca maculata*, *Chapalichthys encaustus*, *Girardinichthys viviparus*, *Goodea luitpoldi*) are strictly lacustrine.
- **Speciation:** The three *Chirostoma* species of Lake Chapala (*C. sphyraena*, *C. lucius*, *C. promelas*) form a sympatric monophyletic group, the clearest example of a species flock. Five of seven *Algansea* species are allopatric. Lake Chapala silversides are documented in sediments about 2 million years old.
- **Late Cenozoic extinction:** Several large Pleistocene piscivores are now extinct, including *Micropterus relictus*, *Oncorhynchus* sp., *Rhabdofario* sp. and *Salmo australis*. Their loss is attributed to lacustrine habitat reduction and basin capture. *Ictalurus spodius* was replaced by *I. dugesi* near the end of the Pleistocene.
- **Freshwater Pacific salmonids:** Land-locked lampreys (*Lampetra spadicea*, *L. geminis*) lie far south of other petromyzontids. Golden trout (*Salmo chrysogaster*) occur in western Mexico. The southern limit of North American *Oncorhynchus* is near 32°N.
- **Future research:** The authors call for combined biochemical genetics, comparative osteology, ecological fieldwork and karyology. They also stress fossil paleoecology, age and stratigraphy, which are not yet fully understood.
