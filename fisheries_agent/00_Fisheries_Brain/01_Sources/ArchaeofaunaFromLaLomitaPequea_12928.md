# Archaeofauna from La Lomita Pequeña

#zooarchaeology #archaeofauna #Hohokam #subsistence-patterns #bone-artifacts

## Executive Summary

This chapter reports the zooarchaeological analysis of 255 unmodified bone fragments and 28 bone artifacts recovered from excavations at [[La Lomita Pequeña]], a [[Santa Cruz]]/[[Sacaton Phase]] hamlet in the [[Salt River Valley]], Arizona. The assemblage is dominated by lagomorphs (74% of identified specimens), followed by ungulates (19%). Small percentages of rodents, a single carnivore, and nonmammalian vertebrates (fish, reptiles/amphibians, birds) are also present. The majority of remains are unidentifiable mammals (85% of total assemblage). Bone artifacts include awls, pendants/beads, and worked fragments, primarily manufactured from ungulate limb bones. Faunal patterns are remarkably similar to contemporaneous [[Hohokam]] sites at [[Snaketown]] and [[New River]], suggesting consistent subsistence strategies during the Sedentary period.

## Background & Description

La Lomita Pequeña is a [[Santa Cruz]]/[[Sacaton Phase]] hamlet occupied during the [[Hohokam]] Sedentary period (approximately A.D. 990-1050). The site yielded a total of 283 faunal specimens when combining screen-recovered (181 specimens) and flotation-recovered (102 specimens) materials. Bone preservation was generally good, with burned bones better preserved than unburned specimens. Many unburned pieces showed moderate erosion, and calcium carbonate deposits obscured some specimens.

**Identified Taxa:**

*Mammals:*
- **Order [[Lagomorpha]]**: [[Sylvilagus]] sp. (cottontails, 10 specimens), [[Lepus californicus]] (black-tailed jackrabbits, 18 specimens), unidentified leporids (3 specimens)
- **Order [[Rodentia]]**: [[Thomomys bottae]] (Botta's pocket gopher, 1 specimen), [[Sigmodon hispidus]] (hispid cotton rat, 1 specimen)
- **Order [[Carnivora]]**: Unidentified carnivore (1 third phalanx, larger than coyote)
- **Order [[Artiodactyla]]**: [[Odocoileus]] sp. (deer, 4 specimens), [[Antilocapra americana]] (pronghorn, 1 atlas)

*Non-mammalian Vertebrates:*
- **Class [[Osteichthyes]]**: Small fish (5 vertebrae), medium fish (1 tooth fragment)
- **Class [[Reptilia]]/[[Amphibia]]**: Small reptiles and amphibians (12 specimens total)
- **Class [[Aves]]**: Small bird (1 distal tibiotarsus)

The unidentified remains were sorted into five mammalian body-size classes: small (90 specimens, mostly rodents and rabbits), small-medium (2), medium (17), medium-large (12), and large mammals (86, primarily ungulates).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Salt River Valley]] during the [[Hohokam]] Sedentary period supported diverse habitats including riparian corridors, desert scrubland, and grasslands. The identified fauna reflects exploitation of multiple habitat zones:

**Riparian/Aquatic Resources:**
Fish remains, though sparse (6 specimens total), indicate utilization of aquatic resources from the prehistoric canal system near the site. Species historically present in the [[Phoenix]] canal system include [[Gila coarse-scaled sucker]] ([[Catostomus insignis]]), [[Gila mountain sucker]] ([[Pantosteus clarki]]), [[Colorado chub]] ([[Gila robusta]]), [[razorback sucker]] ([[Xyrauchen texanus]]), and [[Colorado River squawfish]] ([[Ptychocheilus lucius]]). The small size of recovered fish vertebrae (<1 mm diameter for one specimen) suggests local procurement.

**Desert and Brushy Habitats:**
[[Desert cottontail]] ([[Sylvilagus audubonii]]) occurs below 1800 m elevation in brushy areas. [[Black-tailed jackrabbits]] are distributed throughout Arizona below 1800 m. [[Botta's pocket gopher]] is common along riparian habitats in desert regions and grasslands.

**Grassland and Forest Margins:**
[[Mule deer]] ([[Odocoileus hemionus]]) prefer brushy areas and forest margins. [[White-tailed deer]] ([[O. virginianus]]) occur in grasslands and riparian environments. [[Pronghorn]] were historically distributed in grasslands throughout Arizona, though their range has been greatly reduced since the historic period.

The single burned [[hispid cotton rat]] mandible (immature individual) was recovered from a hearth flotation sample. This species inhabits riparian and grassland areas between 37 and 1524 m elevation, with breeding potentially occurring June through November (though year-round breeding is also reported).

## Key Findings

**Faunal Composition and Subsistence Patterns:**

1. **Lagomorph Dominance**: Lagomorphs represent 74% of identified mammalian specimens (NISP=31 of 42 total identified mammals), with [[Lepus californicus]] (43%) more abundant than [[Sylvilagus]] sp. (24%). This pattern is consistent with other [[Hohokam]] Sedentary period sites.

2. **Ungulate Utilization**: Ungulates account for 19% of identified mammals (NISP=8), including [[Odocoileus]] sp. (10%), [[Antilocapra americana]] (2%), and unidentified artiodactyls (7%). The pronghorn atlas is "very robust," suggesting a large individual, possibly male.

3. **Minor Taxa**: Rodents (5%, NISP=2) and carnivores (2%, NISP=1) represent small percentages. The burned [[Thomomys bottae]] mandible suggests consumption, though pocket gophers were likely agricultural pests.

**Flotation Recovery Significance:**

Flotation samples yielded 102 specimens (36% of total assemblage), predominantly unidentifiable small mammals (72% of flotation-recovered small mammals) and all nonmammalian vertebrates. Without flotation, significant data on fish, reptiles/amphibians, and birds would have been lost. This demonstrates that 1/4-inch mesh screen misses substantial small vertebrate material.

**Bone Artifacts (N=28):**

- **Awls** (majority of artifacts): Short awls (1 specimen), long awls (3 specimens, 180-220 mm estimated lengths), miscellaneous awl fragments (14 specimens)
- **Pendants/Beads** (3 specimens): One complete artifact (Spec. No. 426) manufactured from large mammal distal metapodial, maximum outside diameter 10.7 mm, inside diameter 4.0 mm; possibly functioned as bead, pendant, ear spool, or cheek plug
- **Antler Tine** (1 specimen): [[Odocoileus]] sp. tip fragment with slight polish, possible lithic flaker
- **Miscellaneous Worked Fragments** (5 specimens): Include cut bone ends (possible manufacturing debris) and one specimen with 10+ longitudinal striations

Most artifacts were manufactured from medium and large mammal limb bones, particularly deer ([[Odocoileus]] sp.) metatarsals. Long awls (also termed hair ornaments, hairpins, wands, or daggers in literature) are often found in [[Hohokam]] burials and cremations. Three long awls from [[La Lomita Pequeña]] show evidence of predepositional breakage and burning. Functional distinctions between short awls (typically associated with basketry or hide sewing) and long awls remain to be objectively demonstrated.

**Intrasite Distribution Patterns:**

- **Pithouses** (22 features): 63% of faunal material (177 elements), 85% unidentifiable mammals
- **Middens and Trash Pits**: 29% of fauna (82 elements), most diverse assemblage with lagomorphs (15%), fish (7%), reptiles/amphibians (15%), birds (1%), unidentified vertebrates (16%)
- **Cremations**: 6% of fauna (18 elements), 100% burned, including [[Sylvilagus]] sp. and all mammal size classes; several awl fragments present
- **Test Units** (no features): 2% (6 elements)

Identified ungulates are notably absent from trash middens and pits. Their presence in pithouses and cremations is primarily as bone artifacts, suggesting differential deposition patterns.

**Burning Patterns:**

High proportions of burned bone characterize the assemblage: [[Lepus californicus]] (61%), [[Thomomys bottae]] (100%), carnivore (100%), [[Odocoileus]] sp. (50%), [[Antilocapra americana]] (100%), and medium to medium-large mammals (71-92%). All 18 cremation specimens are burned, including small and medium-sized mammals, suggesting these are not natural intrusions but may represent food offerings or materials present during cremation. Fish, reptile/amphibian, and bird elements from middens are not burned.

Unidentified small mammals from burned pithouses show only 7% burned bones (3 of 43 elements), suggesting many represent natural introduction (burrowing rodents) rather than cultural deposition.

**Regional Comparisons:**

Faunal assemblages from [[La Lomita Pequeña]], [[Snaketown]] (Santa Cruz-Sacaton and Sacaton phases), and [[New River]] sites (Northern Cluster, Sacaton phase) show remarkable similarity:

| Site/Cluster | Lagomorphs | Rodents | Artiodactyls | Carnivores |
|--------------|------------|---------|--------------|------------|
| La Lomita Pequeña | 74% | 5% | 19% | 2% |
| Snaketown | 75% | 4% | 21% | 0% |
| New River (Northern Cluster) | 83% | 9% | 7% | 1% |

This consistency suggests stable subsistence strategies across the [[Hohokam]] region during the Sedentary period, with lagomorphs providing the primary meat source (75%+ of identified fauna) and ungulates contributing 7-20%. Rodents (4-9%) and carnivores (1-2%) represent minor components, with many rodents likely natural rather than cultural inclusions.

**Methodological Notes:**

Identifications used comparative collections at the [[Museum of Southwest Biology]], [[University of New Mexico]]. Analysis recorded body parts, bone portions, symmetry, epiphyseal fusion, cut marks, burning, and rodent/carnivore gnawing. Both NISP (Number of Identified Specimens) and MNI (Minimum Number of Individuals) were calculated. A 10X hand lens and strong light source were used for examination.

**Limitations:**

The small sample size (42 identified mammalian specimens) limits conclusions about subsistence patterns and prevents seasonality determinations. Only one immature specimen (cotton rat mandible) was recovered, and breeding season for this species is disputed in the literature. Fragmentary condition and calcium carbonate deposits obscured some identifications and potential cut marks. Species-level identification of [[Sylvilagus]] (desert vs. eastern cottontail) and [[Lepus]] (black-tailed vs. antelope jackrabbit) was not attempted due to small sample size and fragmentary condition.
