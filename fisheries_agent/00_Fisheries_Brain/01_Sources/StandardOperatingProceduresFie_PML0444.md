# Standard Operating Procedures: Field Manual for Long-Term Monitoring of Fish Populations in Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1995 field manual, issued by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office, sets out [[Standard Operating Procedures]] for long-term monitoring of fish populations in rivers and streams of the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona. It defines the field forms, station layout, sampling gears, voucher preservation, and quality-control steps needed so that data from different crews and years remain comparable and relational in a database. The core method is a quantitative 200 m station sampled by [[Electrofishing]] in an upstream or downstream progression, supplemented by other gears where electrofishing is inadequate and by qualitative searches for species not yet recorded.

## Background & Description

The manual is a standard operating procedure (SOP) document rather than a research study, so it reports no abundance or survey results. Its purpose is to standardize field methods for fish monitoring across the basin. Its structure is:

- **Introduction and objectives**: define the monitoring goal and the two field forms.
- **Sampling crews, design, and permits**: crew roles and the requirement for valid collecting permits.
- **Field forms**: a [[Habitat Sampling Form]] (station-level habitat and photo data) and a [[Fish Collection Form]] (gear, effort, and catch data per macrohabitat). Both share common header fields such as stream, reach, station, date, and habitat type so the data sets link in the database.
- **[[Station Location And Identification]]**: a lower boundary marked with labeled aluminum tags and flagged rebar, located by GPS and [[Universal Transverse Mercator]] coordinates, with an upstream boundary 200 stream meters above it.
- **[[Macrohabitat Identification]]**: each station is partitioned into [[Pool]], [[Riffle]], and [[Run]] units, numbered sequentially upstream from 1.
- **[[Fish Collections]]**: gear-specific methods (see Key Findings).
- **[[Voucher Collections]], [[Quality Control]], and [[Field Notes]]**.
- **Stepwise monitoring procedures**: a 25-step field sequence.
- **Appendix tables**: Table 1 gives variable definitions and codes; Table 2 lists travel routes, approximate UTM coordinates, and topographic quadrangles for fixed stations.

Two example species codes appear in Table 1 to illustrate the four-letter naming system: [[Cyprinella Lutrensis]] (CYLU) and [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (AGCH). The manual does not otherwise discuss these species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The manual's stream classifications and environmental measurements define the context for each sample:

- **Macrohabitat types** follow fixed definitions. A pool is a area of water column with low current velocity and near-zero surface gradient, often with fine substrates, including backwaters and pocket water. A riffle has fast current, steep gradient, and considerable turbulence, often with large substrates, including cascades and rapids. A run is rapid, non-turbulent flow, too deep to be a riffle and too fast to be a pool, including glides. A isolated pool has no surface water connection to the channel and no current.
- **Dominant substrate** is coded on a modified Wentworth-type scale from clay (<0.24 to 4.0 µm class in the table) through silt, sand, gravel, cobble, boulder, bedrock, detritus, and concrete (for lined canals).
- **Water conditions** recorded at each station include conductivity (µS/cm) and water temperature (°C), both needed to interpret electrofishing output and water quality.
- **Stream classifications** include the San Pedro, Gila, and Salt rivers, the Central Arizona Project (CAP) and Salt River Project (SRP) canals, and the FCG Canal. Canal stations are sampled as lined-channel habitats, which is why concrete is a substrate code.
- **Reach codes** in Table 1 divide each river into upstream-most, second, third, and fourth downstream reaches, with a random reach option.

## Key Findings

**Sampling design**

- Each fixed station is a 200 m reach, measured along the thalweg with a tape or hip chain. Upstream boundaries shift with channel sinuosity, so they are marked temporarily with flagged stakes, while downstream boundaries are permanently tagged.
- Photo documentation is mandatory: one 35 mm slide each of upstream and downstream views from both boundaries, with the station identifier written in the lower-left corner of each frame.
- Table 2 lists roughly two dozen fixed stations across the San Pedro (reach 1, nine stations listed), Gila (reach 2, twelve stations), and Salt (reach 3, partly legible) rivers. Station codes take the form reach-reach-station (for example, Hereford is 1-1-1).

**Habitat measurements (per macrohabitat, after fish collection)**

- Length to the nearest meter.
- Three equidistant wetted widths, measured to the nearest 0.01 m.
- Maximum depth.
- Dominant substrate category.
- Comments on irregularities or notable features.

**Electrofishing**

- Electrofishing is the primary quantitative method, unless habitats are not conducive to it.
- Pools are sampled upstream and swift-flowing habitats downstream, with single-pass shocking.
- Crews must record pulse rate, voltage, and amperage. Conductivity and temperature readings are required to calculate electrical power output (wattage), using a formula from Burkhardt and Gutreuter (1995).
- Crews also record seconds shocked and the estimated percentage of available habitat sampled.

**Other gears (used where electrofishing is inadequate)**

- **[[Seines Or Dip Nets]]**: record length, width or depth, number of hauls or sweeps (dip-net sweeps standardized to 1 m), and the percentage of habitat sampled.
- **[[Entrapment And Entanglement]]** (19 gear codes in total include gill, trammel, hoop, fyke, trap, minnow traps, and shock/gill combinations): record mesh size, net dimensions, and hours soaked.
- **[[Angling Or Trot Line]]**: record bait type (artificial lure, live bait, garbage, or stink bait), hooks per line, and hours fished.
- Gear and effort types should be kept consistent over time unless habitat changes or new classifications justify a change.

**Catch recording**

- Fish are held alive in buckets of stream water, then sorted by species and age class (age-0 or young-of-year, and age-1 or older) and counted.
- Each species is coded with a four-letter code (first two letters of genus plus first two of species). Unknown species are coded "????" and HYBR denotes hybrids; "0000" means no fish were caught.
- Length, weight, and external condition or parasites are optional fields.

**Qualitative sampling and vouchers**

- After quantitative sampling, crews search unusual upstream and downstream habitats with varied gear for species not caught in the 200 m reach. Effort need not be recorded for qualitative samples.
- Vouchers: at least three specimens of each permitted species are preserved in 10% formalin per station where possible. Specimens longer than about 3 cm are cut or injected to ensure fixation. Photo documentation is required for species that cannot be collected under permit.
- Specimens are deposited with a designated fish collection, with collection labels recording taxon, date, location, method, and collector.

**Quality control**

- Before leaving each station, the crew leader reviews every form for omissions, legibility, and errors, and initials the QC field.
- Field notes are required to capture conditions that data fields cannot record.

**Stepwise procedure (25 steps)**

The sequence runs from marking the lower boundary, recording header data, and measuring macrohabitats upstream, through electrofishing each unit, to resampling inadequate habitats with other gears, qualitative searches, photo documentation, form review, and removal of flagged stakes before moving to the next station.
