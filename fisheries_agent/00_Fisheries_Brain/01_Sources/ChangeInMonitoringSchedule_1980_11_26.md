## Executive Summary

This document is an internal memorandum from the California Department of Fish and Game, dated November 26, 1980, addressed to [[Razorback Sucker]] study participants. It does not present novel morphological or ecological findings but rather serves as an administrative update on a long-term telemetry-based monitoring program for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) at **Senator Wash Reservoir**, part of the Lower [[Colorado River]] system. The memo details a shift in monitoring frequency due to the onset of the spawning season, staffing changes, and continued use of sonic tagging technology to track fish movement patterns across a full year of study.

## Background & Description

The source text contains no direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, coloration, or anatomical descriptors). The only physical/technical detail relevant to the species' biology is indirect:
- Fish are large enough and robust enough to be surgically or externally fitted with **sonic transmitters** for long-term tracking (9+ months of continuous tag function reported).
- Additional fish were scheduled to be tagged (at least two individuals) on 12/9/80, suggesting a population capable of tolerating repeated capture and tagging without apparent immediate mortality noted in the text.

## Key Findings

- **Nine months of continuous movement data** had been collected from sonically-tagged razorback suckers as of late 1980, with a computer-based analysis report planned for the following summer.
- **Monitoring frequency reduced**: Spawning-period monitoring dives were reduced from **six dives/month to three dives/month**, made possible by hiring an additional temporary staff member.
- **Change effective 12/5/1980**: External assistance from the **USFWS**, **BLM**, and **WAPRS** (Western Area Power Administration) field personnel in Boulder City and Yuma was discontinued as no longer necessary under the reduced schedule.
- **Additional tagging planned**: At least two more fish were to be tagged on **12/9/1980** to ensure transmitter functionality throughout the 1980–81 spawning season, addressing possible equipment failure risks.
- **Methodological continuity emphasized**: A key goal for the 1980–81 season was to **verify results from the prior year**, correcting for a prior logistical issue where fish had already entered spawning condition before personnel training and equipment assembly were complete.
- **Multi-method approach**: The study incorporated diving surveys, sonic telemetry, netting (in both reservoir and river), and planned **underwater videography** in collaboration with WAPRS, representing a multi-modal approach to studying [[Fish Spawning Behavior]] in this species.
- **Collaborative, multi-agency effort**: The program relied on cooperation among California Department of Fish and Game, USFWS, BLM, and WAPRS — highlighting the interagency nature of endangered/native fish conservation monitoring in the [[Colorado River Basin]] during this period.
