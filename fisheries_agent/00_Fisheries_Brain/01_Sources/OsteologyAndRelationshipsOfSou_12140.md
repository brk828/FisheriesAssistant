# Osteology and Relationships of South American Characid Fishes of Subfamilies Lebiasininae and Erythrininae, with Special Reference to Subtribe Nannostomina

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 *Proceedings of the United States National Museum* paper by Stanley H. Weitzman presents a comparative osteological study of two characid subfamilies, [[Lebiasininae]] and [[Erythrininae]], with a focus on the small forest-stream characins of the subtribe [[Nannostomina]] (*Nannostomus* and *Poecilobrycon*). The work began as an attempt to place these small fishes, whose total cranial length is usually 5–10 mm, but its main result concerns the relationships between the two subfamilies, which earlier authors often grouped together. Weitzman concludes that the Lebiasininae are more closely allied to the [[Characinae]] than to the Erythrininae, and he proposes a revised classification with two tribes in the Lebiasininae and two subtribes in the Pyrrhulinini.

## Background & Description

The Nannostomina comprise about eight known species of small characids from South American forest brooks. The first species, *Nannostomus beckfordi*, was described by Günther in 1872. Earlier studies were superficial and often inaccurate, and their placement shifted between subfamilies (Anostominae, Nannostomatinae, Hemiodontidae, Hemiodontinae). The largest recorded specimen is 44.5 mm standard length.

Material came from museum collections (Stanford University, California Academy of Sciences, Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia, USNM, Chicago Natural History Museum). Most specimens were alizarin-stained skeletal preparations, supplemented by earlier osteological preparations ([[Weitzman 1962]]) and aquarium-reared fish. The drawings of [[Poecilobrycon Harrisoni]] were based on one 36.4 mm specimen, with some foreshortening of the snout region from optical drawing methods.

The paper's classification is:

- Subfamily Lebiasininae
  - Tribe [[Lebiasinini]]
  - Tribe [[Pyrrhulinini]]
    - Subtribe [[Pyrrhulinina]]
    - Subtribe [[Nannostomina]]
- Subfamily Erythrininae

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Nannostomina are described as specialized feeders on small animal organisms living on plants, rocks and other surfaces, and on slowly moving free-living organisms. Their movements are slow and precise. The [[Pyrrhulinina]] are more active and predaceous, with larger mouths, larger prey and harder strikes. The Nannostomina have smoothly fusiform bodies, while the Pyrrhulinina have a slight notch behind the dorsal fin, a flatter belly and a less arched back.

The Erythrininae (*Hoplias*, *Erythrinus*, *Hoplerythrinus*, and *Pseuderythrinus*) are carnivorous, with blunt, cylindrical bodies. Weitzman notes that the cellular anterior wall of the swimbladder, found in some Lebiasinini and Erythrininae, is probably an adaptation to stagnant water rather than evidence of close kinship.

Specimens came from localities including a creek near Yurimaguas (Peru), the Rio Negro (Brazil), the Rio Beni basin (Bolivia), the Mazaruni drainage of the Pakaraima region (British Guiana), the Rio Atrato tributaries (Colombia), and Cativá (Panama).

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic and historical review**
- The Nannostomina were placed in at least five different groupings by earlier authors: Lebiasina-related (Günther), Anostomatinae (Steindachner; Eigenmann & Eigenmann), Nannostomatinae (Eigenmann), Hemiodontidae (Regan), and Hemiodontinae (Gregory & Conrad).
- [[Hoedeman Classification|Hoedeman]] (1950a) established the tribe Nannostomini, then (1954a) placed it in the Erythrinidae, and finally (1956) returned it to the Hemiodontidae-related group. Weitzman judges that Hoedeman was correct in 1954 in suspecting a link with *Pyrrhulina*.
- Weitzman rejects the use of scale morphology alone for phylogeny because scale variation among characid groups is greater than Hoedeman assumed.

**Nannostomina osteology**
- Premaxillary-ethmoid joint is a loose, movable syndesmosis, but the premaxillary is not protractile.
- The posterior prevomer shaft is short and never reaches the parasphenoid.
- The lower jaw dentary has two regions, separated by a deep median groove, with two rows of teeth.
- The toothless [[Hoplias Malabaricus|Hoplias]]-like traits are absent; the fenestra between the quadrate, symplectic and metapterygoid is absent.
- Pharyngeal teeth are present (small, conical), contrary to Hoedeman's 1950 report.
- The frontal fontanel is present in some young fish (present in a 14.8 mm specimen, absent in a 21.8 mm specimen) and absent in adults.
- The pectoral girdle lacks the large foramen between coracoid and cleithrum, and the post-temporal is not forked.
- Nannostomus has a shorter snout and blunter ethmoid than *Poecilobrycon*.

**Lebiasininae vs. Erythrininae**
- The Lebiasininae have a large fenestra absent between the quadrate and metapterygoid, six infraorbitals, a short-based anal fin (8–14 rays), and branchiostegal rays of 3 or 4.
- The Erythrininae have a fenestra present, a supraorbital absent, a long-based anal fin shape with about 10–12 rays, five branchiostegal rays, and a long, slender lower cleithrum limb with a reduced coracoid.
- Weitzman concludes the Erythrininae are definitely characid but structurally more remote from the Characinae than any other living characid. He suggests they may deserve separate family status.
- The Lebiasininae, despite superficial resemblance to the Erythrininae, share more characters with the Characinae. Weitzman suggests they evolved from a common characid stock with the Characinae.

**Tribes and genera**
- Tribe Lebiasinini: *Lebiasina* (two species) and *Piabucina* (six or seven species). Weitzman notes the group needs revision.
- Tribe Pyrrhulinini (subtribes Pyrrhulinina and Nannostomina): shared traits include three branchiostegal rays, a unique dentary structure, short parietal and epiphyseal canals, and no supratemporal or dermopterotic canals. Weitzman argues these traits reflect common ancestry, not convergence.
- Subtribe Pyrrhulinina: *Pyrrhulina* (perhaps a dozen species), *Copeina* (two or three), *Copella* (four or five).
- Subtribe Nannostomina: *Nannostomus* (five or six valid species) and *Poecilobrycon* (three valid species). *Nannobrycon* is treated as a subgenus of *Poecilobrycon*, and *Archicheir* as a synonym, based on young *P. harrisoni*.
- Subfamily Erythrininae: *Hoplias* (one or two species), *Erythrinus* (one), *Hoplerythrinus* (one), and *Pseuderythrinus* (one, status uncertain).

**Specific anatomical points**
- Hoplias has a supraopercular bone and an accessory ectopterygoid, which Weitzman prefers to call an "accessory ectopterygoid."
- *Hoedeman's* genus *Pseuderythrinus* seems distinguishable from *Hoplerythrinus* only by tooth distribution. Weitzman suggests it may be a synonym of *Hoplerythrinus*, and at most a subspecies of *H. unitaeniatus*.
- Mesopterygoid teeth in *Hoplerythrinus* vary with size (a 115 mm specimen has well-developed teeth, a 120 mm specimen only a few scattered ones).
- Branchiostegal rays: Nannostomina 3, Pyrrhulinini 3, Lebiasinini 4, Erythrininae 5.

**Overall conclusions**
- The Nannostomina and Pyrrhulinina share a common ancestor, and neither is derived from the other. Their common ancestor differed from living Lebiasinini in jaw structure and in reduction of the head lateral-line system.
- The Lebiasininae are not closely related to the Erythrininae, as many earlier authors assumed.
- The Erythrininae were probably not derived from a Characinae-like ancestor. They may be the most conservative living characids in some respects, but are specialized in others.
- The Lebiasininae probably evolved from early Characinae or ancestral characids more like the Characinae than the Erythrininae.

**Limitations**
- Weitzman states that fossil evidence is absent, so phylogenetic inferences from recent material are only approximate.
- The Lebiasinini and the genera of the Pyrrhulinini require fuller revision, which was not attempted here.
- Weitzman notes that a Hoedeman claim of mid-Eocene characid fossils from France (Piton, 1938) lacks support.
