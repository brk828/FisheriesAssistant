# Arizona DEQ Assessment of Mine Tailings Impact on Aravaipa Creek Water Quality

#environmental-science #water-quality #mine-pollution #aquatic-habitat

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] documents a March 31, 1993 field assessment of mine tailings at [[Klondyke]] and their impact on [[Aravaipa Creek]] water quality. Twenty-four samples were collected including well water, surface water, subsurface water, streambed sediment, and tailings samples. Field measurements revealed evidence of tailings transport into the watercourse through rainwater runoff, with measurable effects on water chemistry including increased total dissolved solids and decreased pH. The assessment concludes that tailings pose environmental risks requiring remediation of surface berms by July 1, 1993.

## Background & Description

The assessment focused on two tailings piles at the [[Klondyke]] site near [[Thatcher, Arizona]]: a lower pile to the northwest and an upper pile to the southeast. The tailings originated from the [[Head Center Mine]] (predominantly zinc and lead oxide ores) and the [[Iron Cap Mine]] (sulfide ore, minority fraction). The site is located along [[Aravaipa Creek]] with HUC designation 15050203-004.

Sample collection on March 31, 1993 included:
- 1 well water sample
- 7 surface water samples (filtered for dissolved metals)
- 3 well-point samples (5-foot perforated pipes driven into wet sands)
- 3 streambed sediment samples
- 10 tailings samples

All samples were submitted to the [[Arizona Department of Health Services Laboratory]] on April 2, 1993, with results expected in two to three months.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Aravaipa Creek]] flows through the affected area with the right main braid measured at 1750 gallons per minute (gpm). The tailings site shows multiple erosional transport pathways:

**Lower tailings pile (northwest):**
- One drainage into stock pond
- One drainage into [[Aravaipa Creek]]
- One drainage onto mill site

**Upper tailings pile (southeast):**
- One drainage onto low ground east of pond
- One drainage into [[Aravaipa Creek]]
- One drainage onto mill site

The southern berm of the upper tailings had recent contact with flood waters from [[Aravaipa Creek]], though no erosional channels were evident at time of assessment. The site poses ongoing risk as subsequent floods could move the channel closer to tailings.

## Key Findings

**Water Chemistry Impacts:**
- Pond on upper tailings showed acidification capacity with pH of 4.85
- Far right braid of [[Aravaipa Creek]] below tailings (flow 16.4 gpm) showed:
  - 31% higher total dissolved solids compared to right main braid
  - More acidic pH of 7.76 versus 8.40 in main braid
- Shallow subsurface water along right cutbank below lower tailings contained 23% more total dissolved solids than sample above upper tailings

**Environmental Risk Assessment:**
- Severity of tailings effects assessed as "likely to be low" based on neutral to slightly alkaline pH values and relatively low electrical conductivities
- Predominance of oxide ores (zinc and lead) from [[Head Center Mine]] reduces acid mine drainage potential compared to sulfide ores
- Sulfide ore contribution from [[Iron Cap Mine]] thought to represent minority fraction

**Regulatory Findings:**
- Tailings discharge violates [[Arizona Revised Statutes]] § 49-263 prohibiting pollutant discharge into state waters without permit
- Considerable evidence of tailings transport via rainwater runoff observed
- Tailings adversely affecting water quality and at risk of further washout

**Recommended Actions:**
- Shore up berms on tailings surface to prevent runoff into [[Aravaipa Creek]], stock pond, and mill site
- Complete berm work during late spring dry season by July 1, 1993
- Develop comprehensive protection plan after laboratory analyses complete, commensurate with environmental threat posed
