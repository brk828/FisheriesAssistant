## Executive Summary

This 1997 annual report by W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) documents progress on the "Fishes of Arizona"/"Fishes of the American Southwest" project, funded via U.S. Bureau of Reclamation contract GR-5FG3200420. The project's core objective is compiling museum and literature-based distributional records of freshwater fishes into a Geographic Information System (GIS), covering not only Arizona but the entire "American Southwest" biogeographic region — defined as all watersheds draining into the northern Gulf of California, from the [[Rio Yaqui]] (Sonora, México) and Baja California habitats northward through the [[Gila River]] (Arizona) to the lower [[San Juan River]] (Utah–New Mexico) and the Pluvial [[White River (Nevada)]] system. The report is not a primary morphological or ecological study but a **data infrastructure and manuscript-progress document**, supporting an in-preparation book, *Fishes of the American Southwest*, targeted for publication in 1999. Embedded in the report are specimen-locality excerpts (heavily OCR-corrupted database exports) for three focal taxa: [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (longfin dace), [[Tiaroga cobitis]] (loach minnow), and [[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]] (Gila topminnow), illustrating the type of distributional records being mapped.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions or measurements of individual species. It references taxonomic identity only at the species/subspecies level for record-keeping purposes:

- **[[Agosia chrysogaster]]** (Cyprinidae) — longfin dace; records spanning 1959–1965 from Gila River basin tributaries (Maricopa/Pinal counties, Arizona).
- **[[Tiaroga cobitis]]** (Cyprinidae) — loach minnow; records from 1964–1967, Graham County, Arizona (Eagle Creek drainage, Gila basin).
- **[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]]** (Poeciliidae) — Gila topminnow, recorded at subspecies level (*P. o. occidentalis*, *P. o. sonoriensis*); records from 1972–1981, Pima/Santa Cruz/Yuma counties, Arizona (Gila and Santa Cruz basins, including springs and stock ponds).

No morphometric, meristic, or diagnostic character data are given in the extracted text; the manuscript-in-progress (Part III) is noted to include species narratives and identification keys once completed, but these are not reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not a primary ecological study, the report characterizes the **aquatic habitat framework** underpinning the forthcoming book:

- **Habitat diversity covered**: natural and artificial aquatic habitats across the Southwest, including springs, streams, stock ponds, irrigation ditches, and canal systems (e.g., the Central Arizona Canal system).
- **Human impacts**: Part II of the manuscript explicitly addresses how human intervention (impoundment, diversion, canal construction) has altered native aquatic habitats.
- **Species-specific habitat notes** (from locality records): 
  - *Agosia chrysogaster* — Gila basin tributary streams (e.g., Salt River, San Francisco River drainages).
  - *Tiaroga cobitis* — small perennial tributary streams within the Gila basin (Eagle Creek).
  - *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* — springs, spring-fed washes, and stock ponds/artificial impoundments within the Santa Cruz and Gila basins — reflecting this species' known reliance on isolated spring/refugium habitats, consistent with conservation concern for the [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Biogeographic scope**: The "American Southwest" region as newly defined encompasses the entire Gulf of California drainage basin — a unifying ichthyofaunal-biogeographic province linking Sonoran, Chihuahuan, and Colorado Plateau drainages.

## Key Findings

- **Database scale**: ~18,000 individual freshwater fish distributional records compiled and computerized from 20 specimen repositories across the U.S. and México.
- **Data quality control**: Estimated error rate up to 2.0% in some taxa/areas; ~2,500 duplicate literature records (redundant with museum specimen records) identified and removed; goal is to reduce overall error rate below 1.0%.
- **Manuscript progress** (*Fishes of the American Southwest*):
  - Part I (Introduction: physical/biological setting) and Part II (Aquatic Habitats) are in final preparation stages.
  - Part III (Fishes: Native + Non-native) — identification keys, glossary, and collection/preservation methods complete; ~60 native species narratives ~50% finished; notes on ~100 non-native species compiled.
  - A potential Part IV ("Recommendations to Perpetuate the Fauna") is under consideration.
  - Target: manuscript in publisher review by mid-1998, publication in 1999.
- **Applied/conservation use of database**: Distributional data and maps were directly provided to:
  - U.S. Bureau of Reclamation — [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)
  - U.S. Forest Service — [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), Gila topminnow
  - Arizona Game & Fish Department — [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- **Peer-reviewed contributions** citing database records:
  - Dowling et al. (1996, *Copeia*) — mitochondrial DNA diversity in [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - Dunham & Minckley (1997, *Biological Conservation*) — genetics of refugium populations of [[Desert Pupfish]]
  - DeMarais et al. (submitted 1997, *Copeia*) — allozyme variation/systematics of plagopterine cyprinid fishes
- **Ancillary training impact**: Project supported a graduate Research Assistant (M.S. Zoology) and an undergraduate work-study student in Conservation Biology.
- **Illustration development**: Negotiations with Randy Babb (Arizona Game & Fish Department) for line drawings and color plates of native and select non-native species; line drawings of anatomical structures and ecological concepts already drafted.
