# Contract YA-558-CT7-901: Fishes of the San Pedro River Chapter

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a federal procurement contract (Standard Form 26) issued by the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) Denver office to contractor W. L. Minckley of Tempe, Arizona, effective 12/9/86. The contract is for one job, a complete manuscript chapter on the fishes of the [[San Pedro River]], priced at $3,000.00. The chapter is intended for the San Pedro River Water Resources Assessment Strategy report. The scope covers native fish history and decline, reintroduction options for federally listed species, present fish species and abundance, flow and water-quality effects on fish life history, the effects of upstream mining, and water management for fish habitat. Final delivery was due March 15, 1987.

## Background & Description

**Contract particulars**
- Contract number: YA-558-CT7-901; requisition YA-470-87-041.
- Issuing office: BLM, Denver Federal Center, Building 50, Denver, CO.
- Contracting officer: Shirley Folger. Contracting Officer's Representative (COR): Bill Jackson.
- Contractor: W. L. Minckley, Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe.
- Payment terms: Net 30 days; invoices in four copies to the BLM Denver office.
- Contract type: fixed-price services, with standard Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and Department of Interior clauses incorporated by reference.

**Scope of work (Section C)**
The draft chapter was to cover:
1. History of San Pedro River native fish populations and causes of decline.
2. Possibilities for [[Reintroduction]] and survival of federally listed threatened or endangered fishes that historically inhabited the river.
3. Present species and relative abundance of fishes in the study area.
4. Water flow regimes and water quality affecting fish life history, including flooding, low flow, and intermittent flow.
5. Effects of upstream mining on stream water quality and fish populations.
6. Water management strategies to enhance fish habitat.

Formatting required 8" x 11" double-spaced draft text, single-spaced final copy, and references in the 1984 GPO Style Manual style.

**Attachment: partial bibliography**
The attached "Partial Bibliography, Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the San Pedro River, Arizona," compiled by Minckley, contains roughly 150 references (pages 20 to 25). It spans 19th-century exploration and boundary-survey accounts, early Arizona history, geology and arroyo-cutting studies, and fish and aquatic-biology literature. Representative fish-related entries include:
- Barber & Minckley (1966), *Fishes of Aravaipa Creek*.
- Barber, Williams & Minckley (1970), biology of the [[Gila Spikedace]].
- Minckley (1973), *Fishes of Arizona*.
- Minckley (1983), status of the [[Razorback Sucker]].
- Miller (1961), man and the changing fish fauna of the American Southwest.
- Schreiber & Minckley (1982), food interrelationships of native fishes in a Sonoran Desert stream.
- Constantz (1981), life history patterns of desert fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The contract frames the San Pedro as a Sonoran Desert river whose native fish fauna has declined. The specified topics point to several environmental pressures:
- **Hydrologic variability:** flooding, low flow, and intermittent flow are named as controls on fish life history.
- **Groundwater and arroyo cutting:** the bibliography includes many references on arroyo cutting (channel trenching) and groundwater-level change, which relate to riparian habitat loss. Examples include [[Arroyo Cutting]] studies by Bryan (1925) and Hastings (1959).
- **Mining:** upstream mining is identified as a potential source of water-quality change affecting fishes.
- **Riparian and wetland habitat:** cited sources include Minckley & Brown (1982) on wetlands, Hendrickson & Minckley (1985) on [[Cienega]] communities, and McNatt (1979) on preserving the San Pedro riparian community.
- **Aquatic invertebrates:** Bruns & Minckley (1980) document benthic invertebrate distribution in a Sonoran Desert stream, a relevant link in the food web.

## Key Findings

The document contains no new field data or quantitative results. Its main points are:

- **Deliverable and budget:** one chapter manuscript, $3,000.00 fixed price, due in three stages: draft by February 10, 1987; reviewed chapter returned by February 16, 1987; final by March 15, 1987.
- **Review process:** technical and subject-matter editors review the draft, and suggested changes return to the author.
- **Chapter content:** the chapter is to synthesize native fish decline, reintroduction prospects for listed species, current fish communities, flow and water-quality controls, mining impacts, and habitat-oriented water management.
- **Required submission standards:** 8" x 11" format, double-spaced draft, single-spaced final, and GPO-style citations.
- **Evaluation requirements:** the offeror must provide a list of reference materials and a resume.
- **Administrative terms:** Net 30 payment, prompt-payment interest under the Prompt Payment Act, and standard FAR clauses (including disputes, changes, termination for convenience, and default).
- **Historical framing:** the bibliography shows that the fish-related literature draws heavily on early explorer accounts and boundary-survey reports, alongside ecological studies of the San Pedro and nearby streams such as [[Aravaipa Creek]].
