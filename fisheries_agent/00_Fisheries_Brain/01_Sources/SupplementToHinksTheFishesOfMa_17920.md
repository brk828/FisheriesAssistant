# Supplement to Hinks' *The Fishes of Manitoba*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1957 supplement to David Hinks' *[[The Fishes of Manitoba]]* (1943), which had gone out of print. Written by J. J. Keleher of the [[Fisheries Research Board of Canada]] and B. Kooyman of the Manitoba Department of Mines and Natural Resources, it updates the freshwater fish fauna of Manitoba in three areas: scientific and common nomenclature, species distribution, and biological knowledge. It covers freshwater fishes only. It does not provide a full new fauna, but corrects and extends Hinks' accounts, notes recent Manitoba records, and lists locality records in an appendix.

## Background & Description

The supplement was prepared because Hinks' book remained in demand, yet a full successor was not feasible at the time. It was published in May 1957 by the Manitoba Department of Mines and Natural Resources. Its main changes are:

- **Generic names:** A movement, led by [[R. M. Bailey]] of the University of Michigan, to consolidate North American freshwater fish genera. Table I lists 19 proposed generic changes. These were not applied throughout the text, so Hinks' terminology stays for ease of reference.
- **Specific names:** Eight species names differ from Hinks' usage, including *Salmo gairdneri*, *Leucichthys artedii*, *Catostomus commersoni* and *Cottus bairdi*. Most changes follow anticipated revisions to the [[International Rules of Zoological Nomenclature]].
- **Authority citations:** Several species are now cited in parentheses, meaning they were transferred from their original genus. Examples include *Ictiobus bubalus* (Rafinesque) and *Notropis umbratilis* (Girard).
- **Common names:** The authors follow the standardized names of the [[American Fisheries Society]], published from 1948 and amended in later issues of its *Transactions*. They urge readers to adopt these names to reduce confusion from local usage.

The authors also state that distribution records are deliberately specific, because generalizations can discourage new publication and entrench errors. They invite contributions of new occurrence records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Biogeographic position.** According to published records, Manitoba marks the western limit of natural Canadian range for 17 species. These include *Ichthyomyzon castaneus*, *Salvelinus fontinalis*, *Semotilus atromaculatus*, *Notropis rubellus*, *Umbra limi*, *Lepomis gibbosus* and *Ambloplites rupestris*. It is the eastern limit for three species: *Platygobio gracilis*, *Notropis blennius* and *Thymallus arcticus*. The authors argue that such range limits reveal the ecological factors that restrict dispersal, so local records have wider significance.

**Major habitats and drainages.** The fauna spans the [[Hudson Bay Drainage]], which includes the [[Nelson River]], [[Churchill River]], [[Gods River]] and [[Hayes River]], and the southern drainages of the [[Red River]], [[Assiniboine River]] and [[Winnipeg River]]. Major lakes include [[Lake Winnipeg]], [[Lake Manitoba]], [[Lake Winnipegosis]], [[Lake Dauphin]] and [[Lake of the Woods]]. Many deep lakes lie within the [[Precambrian Shield]].

**Protected areas.** Several records relate to the [[Whiteshell Forest Reserve]], the [[Duck Mountain Forest Reserve]], the [[Porcupine Mountain Forest Reserve]] and [[Riding Mountain National Park]]. These areas were the sites of many stocking and range extension efforts.

**Introduced and cultured species.** Several species are non-native. [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]] were stocked, the former after toxicant eradication of native fish in some lakes. [[Smallmouth Bass]] spread from [[Lake of the Woods]] via the Winnipeg River system and was also transplanted. [[Largemouth Bass]] is present only in a few waters as introductions. [[Splake]], a hybrid of lake trout and eastern brook trout, was stocked in Hunt Lake in 1955 and 1956.

## Key Findings

**Nomenclature and taxonomy**

- Hinks' record of the **hog sucker** (*Hypentelium nigricans*) from Lake of the Woods was based on misidentification of *[[Catostomus commersoni]]*. The hog sucker should be omitted from the Manitoba checklist until it is actually collected.
- The **greater redhorse** (*Moxostoma rubreques*) is a synonym of *M. valenciennesi*. Its range probably does not include Manitoba. Hinks' illustration was regarded as *M. aureolum*.
- The **channel catfish** should not be called *Ictalurus lacustris*, because that name was based on a burbot description.
- The name *mollis* for the **tadpole madtom** (*Schilbeodes gyrinus*) appears unwarranted.
- The **ciscoes** (*Leucichthys* spp.) are still under study. Classification is difficult because diagnostic characters vary with age, growth rate and environment. Four species are supported in Lake Winnipeg, and a fifth has been questioned. *L. churchillensis* was described from the Churchill River but may not be valid.
- *Carpiodes velifer* is a valid species, but no Manitoba *Carpiodes* examined so far belongs to it.

**Records removed or unconfirmed**

- The **blue walleye** (*Stizostedion vitreum glaucum*) should be omitted from the checklist, since no specimens confirm its presence in eastern Lake Winnipeg.
- The **stoneroller** (*Campostoma anomalum*) remains unverified in Manitoba.
- The **silvery minnow** (*Hybognathus nuchalis*) has not been obtained locally and is not known from the Red River drainage.
- The **redfin shiner** (*Notropis umbratilis*) record from Lake of the Woods was shown to be in error.
- The **lake chubsucker** (*Erimyzon sucetta*) has no confirmed specimens in Manitoba.

**New or extended records**

- **Brassy minnow** (*Hybognathus hankinsoni*) was omitted by Hinks but has since been recorded.
- **Rosyface shiner** (*Notropis rubellus*) has been taken in the Winnipeg River system.
- **Silver chub** (*Hybopsis storeriana*) range has extended to St. Andrews Locks on the Red River.
- **Tadpole madtom** (*Schilbeodes gyrinus*) has been verified in the Red River and extended north to central Lake Winnipeg.
- **Silver lamprey** (*Ichthyomyzon unicuspis*) should be added to the fauna. Its Manitoba records are limited to the lower Nelson and Hayes rivers. Hinks' Winnipeg River record of chestnut lamprey should be verified.
- **Common carp** (*Cyprinus carpio*) is expanding its distribution. Specimens came from Lake Manitoba (1947), Lake Dauphin (1954), Dawson Bay (1955) and the upper Nelson River (1956).

**Salmonids and other game fish**

- **Lake trout** (*Salvelinus namaycush*) occurs naturally in deep lakes of the Precambrian Shield, east of Lake Winnipeg and north of the 54th parallel. Its range was extended artificially to several Duck Mountain and Porcupine Mountain lakes.
- **Eastern brook trout** (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) reaches the northwestern limit of its natural North American range in Manitoba. It is almost continuous along the Hudson Bay and James Bay coasts, and it is abundant in the Gods River system.
- **Goldeye** (*Hiodon alosoides*, in the source's usage *Amphiodon alosoides*) is taken commercially in the Churchill River system above Southern Indian Lake, at about 25,000 pounds annually.
- **Arctic grayling** (*Thymallus arcticus*) occurs in the major Hudson Bay coastal streams and far inland in northern streams such as the Seal, Wolverine and Cochrane rivers.
- **Burbot** (*Lota lota*) is generally a fish of lakes and large streams, but young specimens have been found in many small, fast-flowing streams.

**Range limits**

- **Smallmouth bass** (*Micropterus dolomieu*) has its most northern Canadian occurrence in Lake Athapapuskow (54°41′N, 101°40′W). It was introduced there in 1940 and survives.
- **Freshwater drum** (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) reaches its recorded northern limit at the mouth of the Limestone River (56°31′N).
- **Slimy sculpin** (*Cottus cognatus*) has its most northern Manitoba record at 58°33′N. It apparently replaces the mottled sculpin in the north.
- **Blacknose shiner** (*Notropis heterolepis*) has been taken as far north as latitude 55°.

**Methods and sources**

- Distribution statements are backed by literature citations, specimens in the appendix, or sight records considered reliable by the authors.
- The appendix lists selected locality records by section, township and range, or by latitude and longitude. Specimens are deposited at the [[Royal Ontario Museum]] in Toronto or the Fisheries Research Board Biological Station in Winnipeg.
- The reference list (about 60 entries) draws on regional faunal works, such as [[Fishes of the Great Lakes Region]] and [[Freshwater Fishes of Eastern Canada]], and on Manitoba fishery studies by Butler, Kennedy, Sprules, Hewson and Keleher.
