# Comparison Efficiencies of Three Sampling Techniques for Estimating Fish Populations in Small Streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #freshwater-fish-sampling

## Executive Summary

This study compares three methods for estimating the total number of fishes in short sections of a small, sluggish, silt-bottomed stream in north-central Oklahoma: [[Electrofishing]] with multiple-run [[Depletion Method]], [[Rotenone]] poisoning, and [[Primacord]] explosives. Electrofishing depletion estimates were judged the most reliable. Collections with rotenone were at least as efficient as electrofishing, while primacord performed poorly, which the authors attribute to the soft, mud-and-silt substrate absorbing the shock of detonation.

## Background & Description

Precise estimates of total numbers or biomass of fish are useful for management recommendations and for evaluating habitat modification. A major source of bias is variation in catchability among habitats and sampling gears (Hamley 1975).

Earlier work by [[Layher and Maughan]] (1981) found that standing crop per unit area declined as stream width increased. Biomass per unit area fell for [[Channel Catfish]] when stream width exceeded 15 m and for [[Spotted Bass]] when width exceeded 30 m. The authors suggested that part of this decline reflected reduced sampling efficiency in larger streams, a pattern also reported by Jacobs and Swink (1982).

The study aimed to quantify differential catchability bias among the three gears. Two 30-m stream sections in [[Stillwater Creek]] were isolated with 4-inch mesh block nets, and one section was sampled with rotenone and the other with primacord. Both sites were reshocked or electrofished as part of the protocol.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampled section had 100% pool habitat, a mean width of 9 m (range 5.75 to 6.9 m maximum and minimum given in the source), a mean depth of 2.2 m, a substrate of 50% silt and 50% mud, a water temperature of 24 °C, and a gradient of 1.55 m/km. Water chemistry included dissolved oxygen of 6.6 mg/L, pH 7.8, total alkalinity of 230 mg/L, total hardness of 220 mg/L, conductivity of 520 µmhos/cm, and nitrates of 0.034 mg/L. Flow volume and velocity values were not legible in the scan.

Fish community sampling yielded thirteen species at the rotenone site. Predators and scavengers (raccoons, *Procyon lotor*, and water snakes, *Nerodia* sp.) were actively excluded to prevent removal of stunned or dead fish.

## Key Findings

- **Electrofishing depletion** was judged the most reliable method. Depletion estimates for the rotenone site were generally equal to observed totals, with a standard error of 0 for most species (for example, [[Bluegill]] 27 fish, 95% CI 27.0–27.0; [[Green Sunfish]] 68 estimated, 95% CI 66.9–69.1). Catchability estimates for green sunfish and common carp were 0.84 and 0.68, respectively.
- **Rotenone** (2 mg/L, applied twice) captured unmarked fish of six species. For four of these species, catches did not exceed electrofishing totals. Unmarked mosquitofish (6) and yellow bullheads (2) exceeded marked fish, indicating depletion sampling underestimated these two species.
- **Primacord** performed poorly in this mud-bottomed stream. Reshocking recovered fish that had apparently survived detonation, showing that the explosive charge was not the failure point. The authors concluded that the mud and silt absorbed much of the shock, leading to low mortality and incomplete retrieval.
- **Substrate matters**: Earlier successful primacord results (Platts 1974; Platts et al. 1983, nearly 100% collection in streams up to order 4 to 5) likely came from gravel or cobble streams, so substrate may be a key factor in primacord efficiency.
- **Management implication**: Sampling efficiency should be validated for each habitat and gear before comparing standing crops across streams of different size or substrate.
