## Executive Summary

This entry documents a historical distribution map compiling **adult Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) capture records** across the Upper Colorado River Basin from 1975 to 1995. The source is a cartographic/legend dataset rather than a narrative study, showing capture locations and relative abundance classes (binned by number of adults captured) across major tributaries including the **Green River**, **San Juan River**, **Gunnison River**, and the mainstem **Colorado River**, extending toward **Lake Powell**. This map represents a foundational dataset used in endangered species status assessments and recovery program planning for this federally listed catostomid.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large-bodied, long-lived catostomid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel behind the head. This map compiles two decades of adult capture data (1975–1995), a period during which the species experienced severe population declines attributed to habitat modification, flow regulation, and interactions with non-native fishes, ultimately leading to its listing under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (listed 1991).

The dataset spans the **Upper Colorado River Basin (UC Basin)**, delineated at a mapping scale of 1:2,000,000, and includes key hydrological and geographic reference features:
- River networks (mainstem and tributary systems)
- Water bodies, notably [[Lake Powell]]
- State borders (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, Arizona, New Mexico)
- Regional towns including Glenwood Springs and Gunnison, Colorado, and reference to the La Plata River area

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The mapped region encompasses critical habitat for the [[Razorback Sucker]] within the broader [[Colorado River Basin]] ecosystem, including:

- **San Juan River** subbasin — a key spawning and recovery focus area for the species
- **Gunnison River** drainage
- **Green River** system (implied by broader UC Basin recovery context)
- **Lake Powell** — a major reservoir representing altered, lentic habitat conditions distinct from historic riverine flow regimes

This capture data reflects the fragmented and patchy remnant distribution of adult Razorback Suckers by the mid-1990s, occurring predominantly in specific mainstem reaches and reservoir margins rather than continuously throughout the basin — a pattern consistent with population declines linked to [[Dam Regulation]], loss of floodplain and backwater nursery habitat, and predation/competition from introduced non-native fishes such as [[Common Carp]] and centrarchids.

The binned abundance classes (1–15, 16–50, 51–100, 101–150 adults) suggest survey effort was concentrated in known aggregation sites, likely associated with spawning bars or deep-pool habitats used by adults during the reproductive season.
