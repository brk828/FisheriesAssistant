# Five-Year Flaming Gorge Flow Recommendation Investigation (FY 1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This FY 1994 proposal package, prepared under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin, describes the second year of a five-year research program on how seasonal releases from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] (FGD) affect the Green River ecosystem from the dam to [[Lake Powell]]. The program was created to implement the November 1992 Biological Opinion, whose Reasonable and Prudent Alternative was needed to avoid jeopardy to endangered fishes. The package contains a summary proposal, a Section 2 program plan, Section 3 scopes of work for ten studies, and a budget. The proposed FY 1994 RIP-funded budget is $418,700 (excluding Bureau of Reclamation–Fish and Wildlife Service transfer and overhead costs), with projected totals of $435,000 for FY 1995 and $448,000 for FY 1996.

## Background & Description

The Upper Colorado River Basin supports four endangered fishes: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail]] (*G. elegans*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The RIP guiding document (1988) identified reoperation of major Colorado River Storage Project impoundments as a high priority to offset depletion impacts. FGD, the [[Wayne Aspinal Unit]] and [[Ruedi Dam]] were named for this purpose.

Closure of FGD in 1962 altered the magnitude and timing of Green River flows, modifying fish habitat. Section 7 consultation began in 1980, and studies from 1978–1991 led to the November 1992 Biological Opinion. Because data were limited, the Opinion gave only partial seasonal flow recommendations, covering the Green River from FGD to Jensen, Utah. The proposal lists five elements of the Reasonable and Prudent Alternative:

1. Change FGD operations so flow and temperature regimes more closely resemble historic natural conditions.
2. Initiate a five-year research program starting in spring 1992, including test flows in spring and winter.
3. Determine feasibility of releasing warmer water in late spring/summer and of retrofitting bypass tubes for power generation.
4. Establish legal protection of Green River flows below FGD, which Utah has tied to quantifiable flow recommendations throughout the river.
5. Hold discussions with the Service after five years to refine flows.

The proposal is submitted by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Larry Crist) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Robert D. Williams), with original submittal March 30, 1993 and resubmittal August 18, 1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** The study area includes the lower Yampa River, the lower 15 miles of the Duchesne River, the Green River from FGD to its confluence with the Colorado River, and the Colorado River to the Lake Powell inflow area. Areas of particular interest are:

- Colorado squawfish spawning in the lower Yampa River and Desolation/Gray canyons.
- Razorback sucker spawning in the lower Yampa River and the Green River above Jensen.
- Colorado squawfish nursery areas near Ouray–Jensen and below the town of Green River.
- Desolation/Gray canyons, where humpback chub are present.

**Flow recommendations.** The Biological Opinion sets seasonal targets measured at the USGS gauge at Jensen, Utah:

- **Spring:** releases rise from April to a peak of about 4,000–4,700 cfs, held for 1–4 weeks between May 15 and June 1, timed to the Yampa River runoff peak. The post-peak decline should not exceed 400 cfs/day and should hold at least 2,000 cfs for one more week. Average water years give a spring peak of 6–8 weeks.
- **Summer:** releases decline to 1,110–1,800 cfs to maximize [[Nursery Habitat]] for young-of-year (YOY) Colorado squawfish. Hourly fluctuations stay within 25% of the maximum flow.
- **Fall:** a continuation of summer flows. In high-water years the upper limit may rise to 2,400 cfs. The target is held until November 1.
- **Winter:** flows stabilize once ice cover forms and remain stable until spring ice breakup, with possible exceptions.

**Dam-induced geomorphic change.** The proposal argues that understanding the role of FGD relative to other drivers is essential. Other drivers include climate change, [[Tamarisk]] invasion, changes in tributary sediment delivery, and channelization and diking. Cited literature (Andrews 1986; Graf 1978; Graf 1987; Hereford 1984, 1986) gives conflicting accounts of channel narrowing and aggradation, and the dam's role has not been established.

**Core context.** The proposal notes that the Green River below FGD historically supported warmwater species, but after dam closure these species disappeared from the dam to the Yampa confluence. Cold temperatures are presumed to be the main limiting factor.

## Key Findings

**Program structure**

- Ten studies are proposed for FY 1994. Studies 1–4 are Core-Research Studies (annual, long-term). Studies 5–10 are Hypothesis-Testing Studies (1–5 years).
- Core-Research follows four life-history elements: reproduction, age-0 recruitment, age-1 recruitment, and adult recruitment.
- The research team includes Reclamation, the Service, the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at Colorado State University, the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], the National Park Service, and [[Western Area Power Administration]].
- A Technical Coordination Team (Williams, Crist, Lentsch, Muth in FY 1994) integrates findings and advises the Service.
- The field investigation runs from FY 1992 to FY 1996.

**Program goal and objectives**

- Goal: determine biological and physical responses of the Green River ecosystem to implemented seasonal flows, and investigate effects of warmer FGD releases.
- Objective 1: track reproductive success and relate it to flow.
- Objective 2: evaluate recruitment from age 0 onward.
- Objective 3: monitor relative abundance and population structure.
- Objective 4: determine how FGD and tributary flows form and maintain habitat.

**Research flows.** Research flows anticipated include:

- At least one year of stable winter releases at or below 2,000 cfs.
- At least one year of very high spring releases through the jet tubes.
- Two to three years of relatively high flows below the Green–Yampa confluence (about 18,000 cfs at Jensen).

**Study-specific findings and plans**

- **Study 1 (squawfish reproduction):** drift-net sampling at three sites, with a new station proposed upstream of Jensen. Sampling runs from mid-to-late June to early August, with daily dawn sampling. The 1990 data showed about 60% of drifting larvae were caught at two stations over 30 days on only four sampling days, so daily sampling over 6–8 weeks is needed.
- **Study 3 (razorback sucker):** in 1993 significant spawning may have occurred in the Green/Yampa system, but larval fate was unclear. Sampling was expanded after 1992/1993 effort was judged inadequate.
- **Study 4 (overwinter survival):** fall and spring sampling compares year-class strength with overwinter mortality, using ISMP-consistent methods.
- **Study 6 (geomorphology):** a new-start study by Utah State University aims to establish the dam's role in channel change over the past 30 years, compare it with other factors over the past century, and assess habitat significance. Mapping is planned at 1:12,000 scale on reaches including Browns Park, Dinosaur National Monument, Jensen, Ouray, Desolation Canyon, Mineral Bottom, and Cataract Canyon.
- **Study 9 (restoration potential):** a new start to survey fish fauna between FGD and the Yampa River and test whether warmer water could restore habitat for rare fishes. Holden and Crist (1981) showed reproduction was limited between 1967 and 1977.
- **Study 10 (ice and nursery habitat):** tests the hypothesis that surface and frazil ice reduce backwater habitat and winter survival of age-0 squawfish. The study area runs from Echo Park to Ouray in three reaches. Warm (about 4°C) fluctuating releases from FGD and steep gradients in Lodore and Whirlpool canyons leave ice-free conditions for roughly 65 miles downstream.

**Related research (Section 4.0.2).** NPS sediment transport work in Lodore Canyon, neutrally buoyant bead drift studies, and synthesis of winter movement and habitat studies are coordinated with the program. CUWCD also funds Duchesne River studies (historic flow, habitat change, baseline survey) for the Uinta Basin Replacement Project.

**Deliverables and schedule**

- Yearly progress reports for each study, and final reports for completed projects.
- An interim report by December 1994 synthesizing results to date.
- A final report after five years, recommending refinements to FGD operations.
- Core-Research studies run annually until 1996. Hypothesis-Testing studies run 1–5 years.

**Budget outlook.** FY 1994: $418,700 (RIP). FY 1995: $435,000. FY 1996: $448,000. Excluding BOR–FWS transfer and CUWCD contributions.

**Open issues noted in the proposal**

- The state of Utah will not pursue legal flow protection until quantifiable flow recommendations exist for the full Green River.
- Winter and spring flow recommendations rest on the least data, so research emphasizes those seasons.
- Overwinter survival estimates depend on assumptions about catchability and movement that the proposed mark–recapture work would test.
