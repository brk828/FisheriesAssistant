# Sexual Dimorphism in the Loach Minnow (Tiaroga cobitis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cypriniformes

## Executive Summary

This 1965 *Copeia* note by W. L. [[MincklBy]] describes sexual dimorphism in the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*, order [[Cypriniformes]]), a fish that once occurred widely in the [[Gila River]] system of Arizona and New Mexico but had become increasingly rare in Arizona. The study is based on 46 specimens (17 males, 29 females) collected on 25–26 April 1964 from the Gila River near [[Cliff New Mexico]] and deposited in the [[Arizona State University]] fish collection (ASU 629). The author documents breeding coloration, morphometric differences, and male-specific head and fin tubercles, and describes the species' riffle habitat and associated fauna.

## Background & Description

The Loach Minnow was previously known from many Gila River streams. The author notes that little specific information existed on the species, which motivated the report. Color notes and an illustration had earlier been published by [[Gilbert and Scofield]] (1898).

Key morphological points:

- **Male coloration (breeding condition):** Head black dorsally; intense orange on the posterior two-thirds of the upper lip and the lower lip tip; red-orange spot at the dorsal fin origin and posterior dorsal fin base; orange-yellow caudal lobes with whitish tips; intense red-orange inguinal region and axillae; orange blotches on pelvic fin margins; reddish anal fin base.
- **Female coloration:** Same basic pattern but more mottled. Lower caudal peduncle yellow to whitish yellow. Axillae and inguinal regions generally yellow, and canary yellow in 4 females. A black caudal spot is intense in females.
- **Tubercles:** Males have minute, scattered head tubercles concentrated on the [[Opercles]], and tubercles on the distal two-thirds of pectoral rays 2–4, largest on the posterior branch. Caudal peduncle tubercles sit one per scale in regular rows. Some females have a few head tubercles; otherwise they are smooth.
- **Pectoral fins:** Male pectoral fins are upcurved distally, similar to some species of [[Rhinichthys]].
- **Paired and vertical fins:** Paired fins are larger in males. The dorsal fin shows no dimorphism, while the female anal fin is larger. Both sexes have thickened fin bases forming a soft, mucus-like pad.
- **Meristics:** Fin ray and scale row counts were similar between sexes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site.** The Gila River at Cliff was 40 to 75 ft wide, with larger pools exceeding 5 ft deep. Pool bottoms were sand, while riffles mixed sand and gravel. Some swift riffles lacked vegetation, but turbulent areas less than 6 inches deep supported [[Zannichellia Palustris]], [[Potamogeton Foliosus]], and [[Cladophora]] sp. Water temperature ranged from about 52 °F in early morning to near 65 °F in the afternoon.

**Habitat association.** *T. cobitis* occurred only in shallow, turbulent riffles with roughly 50% of the bottom covered by [[Macrophytes]]. It was absent from unvegetated riffles and rare in riffles choked with plants. Most collections also contained [[Plecoptera]] (stonefly) nymphs and [[Baetid Mayflies]] nymphs.

**Associated fishes.** [[Pantosteus Clarkii]] was often taken with *Tiaroga* but was more abundant in swift, open riffles. [[Meda Fulgida]] appeared in some seine hauls that included areas of eddying current.

**Conservation context.** The author cites [[Miller and Lowe 1964]], who reported that the species had become increasingly rare in Arizona streams in recent years.

## Key Findings

- The study examined 46 specimens (17 males, 29 females) from the Gila River near Cliff, Grant County, New Mexico, collected 25–26 April 1964 and held at ASU (ASU 629).
- Males and females share the same basic color pattern, but males show brighter breeding colors, especially orange and red-orange on the lips, fin bases, inguinal region, and axillae.
- Males have head and pectoral ray tubercles and caudal peduncle scale tubercles that females largely lack.
- Males are thicker-bodied overall, with larger paired fins, longer heads, and greater pectoral and pelvic fin lengths relative to standard length. Female body depth is greater due to mature ova, and female anal fins are larger.
- Both sexes stripped gametes readily when handled, but turbulence and vegetation prevented observation of spawning behavior.
- *T. cobitis* is restricted to shallow, partly vegetated, turbulent riffles, and its presence is associated with stonefly and mayfly nymphs.
- The fish's declining abundance in Arizona motivated the study, which the author frames as filling a gap in basic biological information about the species.
