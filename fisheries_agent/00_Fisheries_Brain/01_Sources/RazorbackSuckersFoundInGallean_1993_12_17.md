# Razorback Suckers Found in Galleano Reservoir, Imperial County (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This December 1993 California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG) memorandum documents the incidental capture of three [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) during a routine fish survey of [[Galleano Reservoir]] conducted by the [[Imperial Irrigation District]] (IID) in Imperial County, California. Given the species' federally protected status, biologists coordinated with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to process the fish according to established protocols (measurement, weighing, PIT tagging, and scale sampling for aging) before releasing them into [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] on the [[Colorado River]], near [[Imperial Dam]]. This record represents a recurring monitoring effort, as the note references identical handling of razorback suckers "captured last year," suggesting an ongoing population assessment in the Lower Colorado River region.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** The species was found in [[Galleano Reservoir]], an off-channel water body associated with the Imperial Irrigation District infrastructure in the Lower Colorado River drainage.
- **Release habitat:** Individuals were relocated to [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], a reservoir situated immediately west of [[Imperial Dam]] on the [[Colorado River]] — a known refugial habitat for endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **Regional context:** The Lower Colorado River system, including reservoirs like Senator Wash, is managed cooperatively by CDFG, IID, and USFWS due to the presence of multiple federally listed fish species, including the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]].
- **Conservation management:** The involvement of USFWS in directing disposition protocol reflects the endangered status of *Xyrauchen texanus* under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, and the reservoir's role as both incidental capture site and translocation/refuge destination.

## Key Findings
- Three razorback suckers were captured during a routine IID fish survey of Galleano Reservoir on November 22, 1993 — an unexpected occurrence given the species' rarity and endangered status.
- Standardized handling protocol (per USFWS direction) included: total/fork length measurement, weight recording, PIT tagging, and scale sample collection for age analysis.
- All three fish were processed on December 7, 1993, and successfully released alive into Senator Wash Reservoir on the Colorado River near Imperial Dam.
- This event mirrors a similar capture-and-release effort from the previous year, suggesting Galleano Reservoir may serve as an incidental collection point for monitoring razorback sucker populations dispersing from or connected to the Colorado River system.
- Multi-agency coordination (CDFG, USFWS, IID) exemplifies interagency cooperation in managing and monitoring endangered fish species in the Lower Colorado River Basin.
- Data and scale samples were distributed to relevant regional biologists and databases (e.g., [[Natural Diversity Database]]) for record-keeping and long-term population tracking.
