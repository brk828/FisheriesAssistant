## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific research paper** but rather a **Statement of Work (SOW)** contracted between the Arizona Nature Conservancy and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Office) for a **status survey of the [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*)**, a freshwater cyprinid fish native to the Gila River basin of Arizona and adjacent Mexico. The SOW outlines a structured research plan to compile existing literature, conduct field surveys, and produce a report to inform a potential **Federal listing decision** (i.e., Endangered Species Act status review). No original morphological, ecological, or population data are presented in this text itself; instead, it prescribes the methodology and deliverables required to generate such data. The draft report was due November 30, 1993, with a total contract value of $12,000.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data are provided in this document. However, the SOW explicitly requires the eventual report to include:
- A **brief description of the species** (Item 4.F)
- **Citation of the original published description** of *Gila intermedia* (Item 4.D)
- **Location and data on type specimens** (Item 4.E)
- A **summary of the taxonomic history** of the species (Item 4.C)

This indicates that, at the time of writing, the taxonomic status and morphological diagnosis of the [[Gila Chub]] required consolidation, suggesting some ambiguity or dispersion of taxonomic records within the [[Gila Robusta]] species complex and broader [[Cyprinidae]] of the American Southwest.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The SOW frames the [[Gila Chub]] as a species of concern within freshwater habitats of the **Gila River basin**, including Arizona and historically Mexico. Key ecological parameters to be documented per the survey design include:

- **Habitat associations**: streams, springs, and cienega-type wetland habitats typical of desert Southwest fish assemblages.
- **Associated fish species**: field teams were directed to record collection data on co-occurring fish species, implying the Gila chub occupies a broader native fish community context (e.g., alongside other [[Gila Robusta]] complex members and desert cyprinids/catostomids).
- **Land and water use pressures**: the survey explicitly targets identification of **habitat loss, alteration, or improvement**, and both natural and anthropogenic threats to survival — implicating water diversion, grazing, groundwater pumping, and non-native species introduction as probable stressors (typical of Southwestern freshwater fish declines, though not stated verbatim in this excerpt).
- **Land ownership context**: survey sites span **Federal, State, and private lands**, indicating a fragmented distribution across multiple jurisdictions, a common ecological/management challenge for the [[Gila Chub]] and other imperiled Southwestern fishes.

## Key Findings

Since this document is a scope-of-work rather than a results-bearing paper, the "findings" are procedural and administrative rather than scientific:

- The status of *Gila intermedia* was **insufficiently known** at the time (1993) to make a Federal listing determination, necessitating this dedicated status survey.
- The project mandated a **rangewide review**, including Mexico, combining museum records, Arizona Game and Fish Department data, gray/published literature, and personal communications with knowledgeable biologists.
- Field surveys were restricted to the **U.S. portion of the range** due to permitting and logistical constraints.
- **Voucher specimens** from undocumented or taxonomically uncertain populations were to be deposited at the **Arizona State University fish collection**, reflecting standard practice for confirming species identity in cases of range/distribution uncertainty (relevant to distinguishing *Gila intermedia* from congeners such as [[Gila Robusta]] and [[Gila Nigra]]).
- All positive and negative survey results were to be mapped on **USGS 7.5' or 15' topographic quadrangles**, establishing a georeferenced baseline for the species' known range — a precursor step to formal conservation status assessment.
- The survey's ultimate deliverables (conclusions on status, management recommendations, and further research needs) were explicitly designed to feed into a **potential Endangered Species Act listing decision** for the [[Gila Chub]].
