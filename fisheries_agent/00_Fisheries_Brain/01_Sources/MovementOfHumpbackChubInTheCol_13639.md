# Movement of Humpback Chub in the Colorado River, Grand Canyon, Arizona

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #radiotelemetry #dam-impacts

## Executive Summary

This study examined movements of [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona from October 1990 through November 1993, evaluating effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on movement patterns. Using radiotelemetry (75 radio-tagged adults) and PIT-tag recaptures (1,572 tagged fish), researchers documented limited long-range movement within the mainstem (mean net displacement 1.49-1.64 km) but substantial spawning migrations to the warmed [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR, mean 6.72 km). Movement between nine distinct aggregations spanning 99.8 km was minimal (2.9% of movements). Daily movement patterns were significantly affected by flow regime, turbidity, and spawning season, with higher movement rates associated with high-fluctuating flows from dam operations.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] is an endangered cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system, representing one of six remaining populations. This relatively large cyprinid has declined concurrent with construction and operation of large dams. The species has been described as relatively sedentary compared to other Colorado River fishes like [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*).

The study area encompassed 225 miles (362 km) of mainstem Colorado River from [[Lees Ferry]] to [[Diamond Creek]], with humpback chub scattered in nine aggregations over 295 km (River Mile 29.8-213.2). The largest mainstem aggregation occurs at the LCR inflow (RM 61.3). Flows are regulated by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] at the upper end of Grand Canyon.

Two flow regimes occurred during the study: "research flows" (high-fluctuating releases, 3,000-31,500 ft³/s) from October 1990-July 1991, and "interim flows" (reduced fluctuations, 5,000-20,000 ft³/s) from August 1991 onward. Average on-site ramping rates decreased from 886 ft³/s/h under high-fluctuating flows to 378 ft³/s/h under interim flows.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water temperature in the mainstem ranged 7-11°C from dam releases, warming downstream to 15°C in summer at the lowest aggregation. In contrast, the [[Little Colorado River]] exhibited temperatures similar to pre-dam conditions (2-25°C), providing suitable spawning habitat. This temperature difference is critical, as hypolimnetic releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] create suboptimal spawning temperatures in the mainstem, limiting hatching success and larval survival.

The study area contains diverse macrohabitats including eddies, runs, pools, and riffles. Adult humpback chub demonstrated strong affinity for large recirculating eddies and associated eddy return channels, areas that provide low-velocity habitat and greater food availability from entrainment of drifting material.

Turbidity significantly influenced fish behavior, with high turbidity (>30 NTU, <0.5 m Secchi disk) versus low turbidity (≤30 NTU, >0.5 m Secchi disk) affecting near-surface activity patterns. The aquatic community includes predators on humpback chub: large [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Osprey]] (*Pandion haliaetus*), and [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*).

## Key Findings

**Long-Range Movement Patterns:**

- Radio-tagged adults (N=69) in the LCRI aggregation showed mean net displacement of 1.49 km (range 0.00-6.11 km), mean gross displacement of 5.13 km (range 0.32-16.93 km), and mean displacement between contacts of 0.26 km over an average of 93 days
- PIT-tagged fish (≥150 mm TL, N=238, 285 movements) exhibited mean net displacement of 1.64 km (range 0.0-59.8 km) in the mainstem
- Strong spatial fidelity: 51% of radio-tagged fish had net displacement <1 km, 84% <3 km
- Movement between aggregations was rare: only 7 of 356 (2.0%) PIT-tagged fish moved between the nine aggregations
- Three fish made extensive movements: one adult moved 99.8 km downstream, another 87.6 km, and one moved 30.7 km upstream between aggregations
- No significant differences in net or gross displacement between males and females
- No significant differences in long-range movement between high-fluctuating and interim flow regimes

**Spawning Migration to Little Colorado River:**

- Mean net displacement between mainstem and LCR (6.72 km) was significantly greater than within-mainstem movement (1.64 km) (t=9.96, P<0.00005)
- 35 of 69 (51%) radio-tagged adults contacted in lower LCR or LCR inflow at least once
- 70% of migrants returned to sites within 2 km of pre-migration mainstem locations, demonstrating strong homing ability
- Four phases of spawning migration identified:
  1. Local aggregations in mainstem eddies (February)
  2. Movement to staging area near LCR inflow (March-May)
  3. Ascent into LCR during declining flows, decreased turbidity, and rising temperatures (primarily February-May)
  4. Extended return to mainstem (May-September)
- Peak staging observed March 8-11, 1991 and March 11, 1992, when 60% of radio-tagged fish located in deep eddy above LCR inflow
- 55% of fish moved into LCR during decreasing flows (213-1,760 ft³/s), 30% during steady low flows (198-276 ft³/s)
- 74% moved during rising water temperatures (9.6-22.7°C)
- 20 of 23 PIT-tagged movements from mainstem to LCR within 30 days occurred during spawning period (February-June)

**Juvenile Movement:**

- Eleven juveniles (<150 mm TL) marked with fin clips in LCR were recaptured in mainstem in 1993
- Juvenile dispersal from LCR to mainstem documented, coinciding with large floods in LCR (January-February 1993)
- Five juveniles recaptured below LCR confluence (RM 61.9-64.9), one recaptured 1.8 km above confluence
- Juveniles originated from as high as 14.9 km into the LCR
- Movement likely active rather than passive, as most fish were >40 mm TL, well past larval stage vulnerable to passive transport

**Daily Movement and Activity Patterns:**

- Near-surface activity (APFC) significantly higher during high turbidity (ANOVA, F=99.41, P<0.00001), consistent with negative phototactic behavior
- APFC significantly higher during spawning than non-spawning season (ANOVA, F=19.97, P<0.00001)
- No significant difference in APFC between day and night (ANOVA, F=2.16, P=0.141)
- Lowest near-surface activity during daytime under low turbidity conditions
- Horizontal movement (Pm) significantly higher during spawning season (0.21) than non-spawning (0.13) (χ²=22.25, P<0.00001)
- Pm significantly higher during high turbidity (0.18) than low turbidity (0.12) (χ²=10.89, P=0.001)
- No difference in Pm between day (0.16) and night (0.16) (χ²=0.02, P=0.887)

**Effects of Flow Regime and Characteristics:**

- Pm significantly higher during high-fluctuating flows (0.21) than interim flows (0.16) (χ²=5.18, P=0.023)
- Pm approximately three times higher at flows >10,000 ft³/s (χ²=39.31, P<0.00001)
- Higher movement at on-site ramping rates >300 ft³/s/h, particularly at flows >10,000 ft³/s (ANOVA, F=15.37, P<0.00005)
- Pm highest (0.21) during rising and falling flows, remained high (0.16) during high flow period, lowest during low flow period
- Total distance traveled increased with magnitude of flow change, particularly when daily difference exceeded 4,000 ft³/s
- Higher movement rates hypothesized to result from fish relocating to more favorable microhabitats after flow-induced habitat changes, particularly velocity alterations

**Population Structure and Connectivity:**

- Home range for LCRI aggregation approximately 28.4 km (13.5 km mainstem, 14.9 km LCR)
- 92 fish originally tagged with Carlin or Floy tags in LCR (1980-90) were recaptured in mainstem; 49 of 50 with records recaptured between RM 57.0-65.0
- Average distance between original capture and recapture: 4.29 km (range 0.1-14.4 km)
- Average elapsed time between captures: 2,990 days (range 304-4,496 days)
- Fish dispersed approximately evenly above and below LCR inflow
- Downstream dispersal of juveniles from LCRI aggregation hypothesized as primary source for downstream aggregations
- Cold mainstem temperatures (10-15°C summer) likely preclude successful spawning, making LCR critical for reproduction

**Condition and Predation Risk:**

- High condition factors for adults throughout study indicated no negative energetic effects from increased movement demands
- Increased movement rates under high-fluctuating flows may increase predation risk, particularly for juveniles
- Predators capable of consuming humpback chub up to 340 mm TL documented
- Reduced turbidity may increase predation potential while negative phototaxis reduces available foraging time

**Management Implications:**

- Spawning migrations to LCR appear to be response to suboptimal mainstem temperatures from hypolimnetic dam releases
- Flow conditions reducing turbidity may increase predation potential
- Effects of flow regime on juvenile dispersal and survival remain unclear due to variable densities across years
- Questions remain about flow levels necessary to passively transport juveniles and at what point transported juveniles are lost from reproducing population
- Low mainstem temperatures may reduce juvenile swimming ability under large flow fluctuations; at 14°C, sustained swimming speed (0.51 m/s) maintained only 2.22 minutes versus 90 minutes at 20°C
