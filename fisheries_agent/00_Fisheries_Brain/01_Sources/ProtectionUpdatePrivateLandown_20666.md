# Community Conservation Protection Update: Private Landowners Pitch-In

#ichthyology #conservation #endangered-species #private-lands #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

This community conservation update highlights collaborative efforts between private landowners in Arizona and conservation organizations to protect federally listed and rare aquatic species. Multiple landowners across Arizona have voluntarily implemented habitat protection measures including cattle exclusion from riparian areas, spring fencing, and species reintroduction programs. Key partnerships involve The Nature Conservancy, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Partners in Wildlife program, Arizona Game and Fish Department, and Arizona State University.

## Background & Description

The document profiles several private landowner conservation initiatives across Arizona focused on protecting rare and endangered aquatic species and their habitats. On the [[Verde River]], a landowner excludes cattle from riparian areas to benefit [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Near [[Patagonia]], landowner Davis U. Merwin manages his property to protect [[Ciénaga]] wetland habitat supporting [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), [[Huachuca Spring Snail]], and ciénaga false rush. In the [[Chiricahua Mountains]], rancher Josiah Austin has worked for years to protect [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), softshell mud turtle, and thick-billed parrots.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The conservation efforts focus on critical [[Riparian Habitat]] and spring-fed wetland systems in Arizona. [[Ciénaga]] wetlands represent valuable and increasingly rare habitat types requiring mature vegetation stages to support listed species. The [[Turkey Creek]] watershed in the Chiricahua Mountains experienced significant disturbance from the Rattlesnake Canyon fire followed by heavy rains, creating ash-laden runoff that impacted aquatic species populations. Cattle grazing pressure on riparian areas and springs represents a primary management concern requiring fencing and alternative watering systems to allow habitat recovery.

## Key Findings

**Patagonia Ciénaga Project (Davis U. Merwin property):**
- Landowner fenced spring and wetland to exclude cattle grazing
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Partners in Wildlife program funded fencing installation and solar-powered pump with watering system for cattle
- Project now in second year with successful habitat recovery
- The Nature Conservancy conducts ecological monitoring and assists with cattle trespass management
- Success has led to plans for expanding protection to additional riparian corridor stretches
- Site supports federally listed [[Gila Topminnow]] plus two other rare species

**Chiricahua Mountains Project (Josiah Austin property):**
- Rancher introduced [[Yaqui Chub]] into ponds fed by [[Turkey Creek]] with Arizona Game and Fish Department assistance
- Rattlesnake Canyon fire followed by heavy rains created ash-laden runoff
- Runoff may have caused loss of Yaqui Chub from the site
- Current discussions underway for reintroduction of [[Yaqui Chub]] and [[Yaqui Catfish]] with Arizona Game and Fish Department and Arizona State University
- Landowner working to improve Turkey Creek watershed conditions
- Long-term goal to re-create conditions necessary for chub reintroduction to stream

**Additional Conservation Actions:**
- Altar Valley rancher collaborating with researchers on [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] management
- Northeastern Arizona rancher protecting Peeble's Navajo cactus from collectors
- Multiple landowners implementing voluntary species protection measures across Arizona
