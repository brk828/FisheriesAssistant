# A Record of a Great Siberian Sturgeon (Huso dauricus) off Niigata, Sea of Japan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary
This paper documents a rare occurrence of a [[Great Siberian Sturgeon]] (*Huso dauricus*), also known as [[Kaluga]], caught on 17 April 1994 by a motor trawler fishing for [[Olive Flounder]] off Neya Village, Sanpoku Town, Niigata Prefecture, in the [[Sea of Japan]]. The specimen — a 1765 mm total length, 32.56 kg immature (subadult) male — represents the **southernmost documented record** of the species both within the Sea of Japan and across the entire Japanese archipelago. Only five individuals of this species had been previously documented from northeastern Japanese waters, underscoring the biogeographic significance of this find and supporting the hypothesis of southward coastal migration by immature individuals prior to river-spawning migrations in the Russian Far East.

## Background & Description
*Huso dauricus* is a member of the family [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons), a group whose global occurrences have become increasingly rare. In Japanese waters, sturgeon records had been sparse and geographically clustered in the north, with prior notable records including:
- First Japanese record of *H. dauricus* from Hokkaido waters ([[Amaoka & Nakaya 1975]]), collected via gill net off **Oshoro Bay** in February 1975 — the first Sea of Japan record for the species.
- A record of green sturgeon (*[[Acipenser Mediostris]]*) off Aomori Prefecture (19 May 1979).
- A *H. dauricus* caught in a salmon gill net off Wakinosawa, Mutsu Bay, Aomori Prefecture (26 January 1989).
- An additional *H. dauricus* record from Sanriku-chou, Iwate Prefecture (Pacific side), 20 May 1984.

### Morphological Description of the Niigata Specimen
- **Diagnostic features**: 5 rows of unique bony scutes; 2 pairs of long barbels on the ventral snout surface; heterocercal caudal fin; strongly curved (semicircular), ventrally positioned, shark-like mouth.
- **Gill morphology**: Left and right opercular (gill) membranes free from the isthmus and broadly joined with each other; operculum incompletely covers the branchial cavity, making gill filaments visible to the naked eye.
- **Fin/body positioning**: Ventral fins situated postero-ventrally near the cloaca; cloaca positioned closer to ventral fin bases than to the anal fin origin.
- **Coloration (fresh)**: Dark grayish-green dorsally, white ventrally; all scutes and the anterior margin of the pectoral fins white.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution & Habitat**: *H. dauricus* is primarily associated with the [[Amur River]] basin, [[Ussuri River]], [[Lake Khanka]], and [[Songhua River]] in the Russian Far East / Manchuria region, with additional records from [[Sakhalin Island]] and the [[Sea of Okhotsk]] (including immature specimens collected by Kostarev and Turnin, 1970).
- **Migratory behavior**: The species is believed to be anadromous or partially so — immature individuals migrate through coastal waters of the northern Sea of Japan and western Sea of Okhotsk before entering Russian Far East rivers to spawn. The Niigata specimen suggests that a small number of individuals travel further south into the middle regions of the Sea of Japan, likely following coastal routes along southern [[Hokkaido]] and northern [[Honshu]] in cold-water conditions.
- **Sympatric species considerations**: The Niigata region has also yielded records of a second sturgeon genus, *[[Acipenser Multiscutatus]]* (originally described by Tanaka), collected off Naoetsu (southern Niigata Prefecture) and possibly off Kakizaki (identity considered doubtful by Nakabo, 1993). This suggests at least two sturgeon genera (*Acipenser* and *Huso*) have historically occurred in the Niigata region, likely via similar cold-water coastal migration corridors along Sakhalin, Hokkaido, and northern Honshu.
- **Conservation context**: The paper notes a general worldwide decline in sturgeon occurrences, with no sturgeon reports from Japanese coastal waters between prior records and 1980, highlighting the rarity and biogeographic value of each new specimen record.

## Key Findings
1. This record constitutes the **southernmost known occurrence** of *Huso dauricus* both in the Sea of Japan and across the Japanese archipelago as a whole.
2. Only **five individuals** of this species had been documented from northeastern Japanese waters prior to/including this record, making each occurrence scientifically significant for understanding range limits.
3. The specimen's immature (subadult) male status and morphometrics support the hypothesis that **juvenile/immature Huso dauricus undertake southward coastal migrations** in the Sea of Japan and Sea of Okhotsk before returning north to Russian Far East rivers for spawning.
4. The find reinforces evidence of **two distinct sturgeon genera** (*Acipenser* and *Huso*) historically present in the Niigata coastal region, pointing to shared but genus-differentiated migratory corridors along the Sakhalin–Hokkaido–northern Honshu coastline.
5. Diagnostic morphological features consistent with *Huso dauricus* were confirmed: 5 scute rows, 2 pairs of barbels, semicircular ventral mouth, and opercular (gill) membranes free from the isthmus and joined medially — distinguishing this species from other Acipenseridae members such as [[Acipenser Mediostris]] and *Acipenser Multiscutatus*.
