# A Redescription of the Scorpion Vejovis Bilineatus Pocock

#arachnology #scorpiones #vejovidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Stanley C. Williams]] redescribes the scorpion [[Vejovis Bilineatus]] (order Scorpionida, family [[Vejovidae]]), a species first described by [[Richard Irvine Pocock]] in 1898 from "San Diego, Texas." Its identity had been uncertain for decades, and [[Hoffmann]] (1931) had only tentatively assigned Mexican material to it. Williams examined the holotype, a poorly preserved mature female borrowed from the [[British Museum of Natural History]] through [[Willis J. Gertsch]] of the [[American Museum of Natural History]]. The redescription confirms the species' diagnostic characters, corrects two details of the original description, and supports Hoffmann's identification of [[Aguascalientes]] specimens as the same species.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** Pocock described eight new North American *Vejovis* species in 1898. [[Hoffmann]]'s monograph of Mexican scorpions (1931) treated the species but was unsure whether its material matched Pocock's type. The paper was written to clarify the status of the species, which matters for understanding the scorpion fauna of the [[United States]] and northern [[Mexico]].

**Diagnosis.** A small member of the *spinigerus* subgroup, probably rarely exceeding 35 mm in total length. Key characters include:
- Mesosomal dorsum with one pair of distinct dark longitudinal stripes; carapace with dark underlying marbling.
- Metasoma with dark stripes along the inferior median and inferior lateral keels.
- Inferior median keels obsolete; inferior lateral keels irregularly smooth and fairly developed.
- Dorsal and dorsolateral keels on segments II to III more crenulate than serrate.
- Vesicle not granular but may bear punctiform depressions.
- Small pedipalp chela with a palm that is not very swollen.
- Pectines with 13 to 16 teeth in females and 15 to 17 in males.

**Holotype (female).** Preservation is poor and the coloration is obscured. The cuticle is deep yellow with dark underlying carapace markings and a pair of submedian dorsal stripes on the mesosoma. The carapace is granular and slightly lustrous, with three lateral eyes per group and median eyes on a subtly elevated ocular tubercle. Key measurements from [[Table 1]] are:

| Character | Value (mm) |
|---|---|
| Total length | 35.0 |
| Carapace length / width | 3.5 / 2.9 |
| Metasoma length | 11.5 |
| Telson (vesicle length / width) | 3.5 (2.3 / 1.6) |
| Aculeus length | 1.2 |
| Pedipalp chela length / width | 4.1 / 1.1 |
| Movable finger | 2.4 |
| Fixed finger | 1.7 |
| Pectines (left / right) | 14 / 14 |

The metasomal keels are irregularly crenulate on segments I to III, and the inferior intercarinal spaces of segment V bear many granules. The vesicle is smooth and lustrous with several punctate depressions, and the aculeus is very short. The movable finger of the chelicerae lacks denticles, and the pedipalp fingers meet closely along their length when closed.

**Type data.** The holotype is labelled "Texas, North America (San Diego), W. Taylor 1889." It is poorly preserved: the telson is essentially detached from the metasoma, and one pedipalp and one walking leg are separated from the prosoma.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The source gives no information on habitat, microhabitat, behaviour, diet, reproduction, or population ecology. Its only geographic data are the type locality (San Diego, Texas) and the Aguascalientes, Mexico, material discussed by Hoffmann. The paper's ecological relevance is biogeographic: knowing the species' identity helps clarify the composition of the scorpion fauna of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico.

## Key Findings

- **Redescription confirmed:** The holotype agrees with the original description in most respects, and the paper provides a fuller diagnosis than Pocock's two-paragraph original.
- **Pectine count corrected:** The holotype has 14 pectinal teeth per comb, not the 15 Pocock reported.
- **Length discrepancy noted:** Total length is 35 mm, not the 25 mm given originally. Williams suggests the membranes may now be stretched.
- **Sex clarified:** Pocock did not specify sex. The holotype is clearly a female, probably mature.
- **Mexican records supported:** Comparison of the holotype with Hoffmann's diagnosis indicates that his Aguascalientes specimens were *Vejovis bilineatus*, although Hoffmann had not seen the type or material from near the Texas type locality.
