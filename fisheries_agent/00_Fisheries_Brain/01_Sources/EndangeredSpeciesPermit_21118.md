# Federal Subpermit PRT-676811: Authorization for Endangered Fish Research and Recovery at Arizona State University (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) correspondence package, not a research study. It is a subpermit letter, dated July 18, 1994, from the Regional Director to Dr. W.L. Minckley of [[Arizona State University]]. It authorizes Minckley and named colleagues and graduate assistants to take, hold, maintain, and propagate [[Endangered Species Act]]-listed fishes for scientific research and recovery, under the Regional Director's blanket [[Federal Fish and Wildlife Permit]] PRT-676811. The package also contains the permit's species list, the Service's Special Conditions, and a separate letter (July 19, 1994) that declined to authorize collection of [[Gila Trout]] in New Mexico pending a study plan.

## Background & Description

- **Permit holder:** Regional Director, FWS Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico. The blanket permit was issued 4/15/93 and expires 4/15/98.
- **Subpermittee:** Dr. W.L. Minckley, Professor of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe.
- **Named colleagues:** Michael E. Douglas, Thomas E. Dowling, Philip Hedrick, and Paul C. Marsh.
- **Graduate assistants:** Glen W. Knowles, David Oakey, Christopher R. Reimus, Ruby Scheffer, C. Alana Tibbets, Ross J. Timmons, and Anthony L. Velasco.
- **Validity:** Authorized through December 1994. Any activity not specifically permitted is prohibited.
- **Jurisdiction:** Work in Arizona and New Mexico is covered by this subpermit. Work in Nevada requires a subpermit from the Portland, Oregon regional office, and work in Utah requires one from the Denver office.

The permit is governed by the Special Conditions (implemented June 1982), which apply to marine mammals and native endangered and threatened species. The blanket permit also incorporates 50 CFR 13 general conditions and NEPA requirements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authorized species are native fishes of the arid American Southwest. Many are restricted to small desert rivers, springs, and reservoirs. The permit's conservation focus reflects this vulnerability. The species list includes:

- **Cyprinids (Gila and related minnows):** Virgin River chub, Bonytail chub, Humpback chub, Yaqui chub, Colorado squawfish, Woundfin, and others such as Spikedace and Loach minnow.
- **Pupfishes and topminnows (Cyprinodontidae and Poeciliidae):** Desert pupfish, Quitobaquito pupfish, Gila topminnow (including Yaqui), and several other pupfish and gambusia taxa.
- **Catostomids:** [[Razorback Sucker]], with the Lake Mohave population as the main focus of field work.

The species list also covers listed taxa outside fishes, including mammals, birds, amphibians, reptiles, crustaceans, molluscs, plants, insects, and arachnids. Many of these are federally listed cave or desert organisms.

Key sites named in the letter include Lake Mohave, Arizona, the source of wild razorback sucker adults and larvae, and Quitobaquito, the home of the Quitobaquito pupfish. Ephemeral ponds adjacent to Lake Mohave were approved as grow-out facilities.

## Key Findings

**1. Authorized take limits (maximum from wild populations, per the bracketed values):**

| Species | Maximum take |
|---|---|
| [[Virgin River Chub]] | 20 per locality (1993–1994 year-classes) |
| [[Bonytail Chub]] | 10 per locality |
| [[Humpback Chub]] | 10 per locality |
| [[Yaqui Chub]] | 10 per locality |
| [[Woundfin]] | 5 per locality |
| [[Desert Pupfish]] | 10 per locality |
| Quitobaquito pupfish | 20 per locality |
| [[Gila Topminnow]] (incl. Yaqui) | 20 per locality |
| [[Colorado Squawfish]] | 5 per locality, captive, refugia, or nonessential experimental stocks only |

**2. Razorback sucker (Lake Mohave, Arizona) provisions:**

- Unlimited survey, monitoring, capture, and immediate release unharmed at the capture site.
- Stripping of no more than 100 ripe females, without supplemental hormone injections.
- Milt may be collected from adult males in Lake Mohave.
- Eggs are transported in suitable containers to Arizona State University for hatching and research.
- Larvae may be taken from the wild Lake Mohave population and moved to Arizona State University or the approved ephemeral-pond grow-out facilities.
- All adults are released at the capture site after egg and milt removal.

**3. Mortality and reporting rules:**

- If 10 inadvertent adult razorback sucker mortalities occur, operations must stop immediately. The FWS Division of Endangered Species/Permits must be contacted within 5 working days.
- Unexpected death or escape of authorized wildlife must be reported to the Office of Management Authority before the end of the next business day.
- A complete annual report, with any published or unpublished reports and data, and three copies of USGS 7.5-minute quad sheets showing sites where species were found or not found, is due **December 1, 1994**. Late reports must be sent immediately after completion. Failure to report can lead to revocation or denial of future subpermits.

**4. Restrictions on propagated fish:**

- Reintroduction or translocation of captive-propagated fish to the wild requires prior written approval from the Service.
- Live wildlife may not be disposed of or released without written approval. Dead specimens may be destroyed or transferred to a public institution for research or education if the permit does not specify disposal.
- Every shipment must carry a copy of the permit, the letter, and a cover letter naming the recipient and numbers involved. A copy of that cover letter must go to the Service.

**5. Legal and access conditions:**

- The subpermit is valid only together with a valid state permit.
- It does not grant trespass rights. Written landowner or agency permission is needed for private, federal, state, or local property.
- Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act must be completed first. No action may violate Section 7(a)(2).
- Tissue sampling and invasive procedures require trained personnel and disease-transmission safeguards. Live wild specimens must be held and transported per professional protocols.

**6. Gila trout, New Mexico (July 19, 1994 letter):**

- The Service did **not** authorize collection of [[Gila Trout]] from New Mexico waters at that time.
- Authorization may be added by amendment after a completed study plan is reviewed.

**7. Species list (current as of 4/22/94):**

The permit covers about 130 federally listed taxa. The main groups are:

- **Mammals:** About 13 entries, including [[Black-Footed Ferret]], [[Mexican Gray Wolf]], and several bats.
- **Birds:** About 16 entries, including [[Whooping Crane]], [[Bald Eagle]], and [[Yuma Clapper Rail]].
- **Amphibians:** 4 entries, including the Houston toad and Texas blind salamander.
- **Reptiles:** 3 entries, including [[Desert Tortoise]].
- **Fishes:** About 31 entries, including [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]].
- **Crustaceans and molluscs:** 1 crustacean (Socorro isopod) and 2 molluscs (Kanab ambersnail and San Xavier talussnail).
- **Plants:** A long list of cacti and other species, mainly from Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona.
- **Insects and arachnids:** 4 insects and 3 arachnids, mainly cave-dwelling species from Texas.

**Overall conclusion:** The letter confirms that Arizona State University research on these fishes is authorized, with strict take limits, mandatory reporting, and restrictions on releasing captive-bred fish. Research beyond the listed activities, including New Mexico Gila trout collection, needs further FWS approval.
