# Some Implications of the Carbon-14 Dates from a Cave in Coahuila, Mexico

#archaeology #radiocarbon-dating #desert-culture

## Executive Summary

Walter W. Taylor reports radiocarbon dates from [[Frightful Cave]] (site CM-68) in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] of central [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The dates show that the cave's artifacts and biofacts are far older than Taylor expected, and that the deposits record a single cultural continuum of roughly 8,000 to 10,000 years. Taylor argues that the cave's culture is a typical variant of a widespread "Desert Culture" and that its shift from animal to plant foods reflects post-Pleistocene aridification. The document is a 1956 archaeology paper. It contains no aquatic, fish, or invertebrate data beyond a land snail, so the aquatic-ecology framing does not apply to most of its content.

## Background & Description

Taylor began work in Coahuila in 1937. His team had expected only modest antiquity and had no dating control beyond stratigraphy. A request for [[Carbon-14 Dating]] was initially deferred. A second submission to the University of Michigan Memorial-Phoenix Project Radiocarbon Laboratory was accepted, and dates began to return about a year before the 1956 reading to the [[American Anthropological Association]] at Santa Monica.

Eleven dates come from Frightful Cave. Four had been published by Crane (1956) and the rest were supplied by correspondence. All derive from human artifacts or biofacts (human feces). The table below summarises the dates by level.

| Level | Dates (years B.P.) | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| Top (4 dates) | 1770 ± 250 to 3620 ± 350 | Fiber sandal, cut wood, fiber rosettes, human feces |
| Middle (4 dates) | 6170 ± 300 to 9540 ± 550 | Cut wood, human feces, twill-pad fiber sandals |
| Bottom (3 dates) | 8023 ± 350 to 8870 ± 350 | Human feces, fiber scuffer-sandals, cut wood |

Two dates on middle-level wood (9300 ± 400 and 9540 ± 550) are older than any bottom-level date. Taylor attributes this to possible secondary deposition. He stresses that the oldest dates come from artifacts, so they date the artifacts directly. A check-run on a middle-level specimen confirmed the 9300 ± 400 result after one bottom-level specimen was accidentally included in the 9540 sample.

The cave's region is arid. The northern half of Coahuila lies in the Sierra Madre Oriental and shows Basin and Range topography, with fault-block mountains and narrow alluvial basins. Apart from higher elevations, it belongs to the [[Chihuahua Desert]] province.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Taylor reads the deposits as recording a climate change from cooler and moister to warmer and drier conditions. Several lines of evidence support this:

- **Fauna:** Over 2,000 identified mammal bones include no extinct species. They do include bison, grizzly bear, elk, and yellow-haired porcupine, which no longer occur in Coahuila or at such low elevation. Their remains thin out in higher deposits.
- **Mollusks:** The land snail *Humboldtiana* (described as *Humboldtiana taylori* by Drake, 1951) follows the same trend.
- **Flora:** Floral species show comparable shifts.
- **Pleistocene fauna:** Elephants and other extinct forms occur in Coahuila near the cave, yet none appear in its deposits. Taylor suggests that the Pleistocene fauna had already disappeared from northern Coahuila by about 9,000 years ago, and that warming and desiccation under the [[Anathermal Climate]] may have hit the most arid areas first.
- **Geology:** Taylor links the desiccation to an erosion cycle between the Neville and Calamity formations in the Big Bend region, which he sees as the start of a long aridity that continues today.

Taylor notes a contrast with [[Danger Cave]] in Utah, which shows no comparable extinction pattern.

## Key Findings

- **Dating:** Eleven radiocarbon dates from Frightful Cave range from 1770 ± 250 to 9540 ± 550 years B.P. Taylor concludes that the artifacts are "the two oldest dated human artifacts in the Western Hemisphere," with the bottom level at about 8,000 to 10,000 years old.
- **Collection size:** About 55 per cent of the site was excavated. Bottom level has roughly 1,000 artifacts (about 950 of fiber and wood). The middle level has about 1,600 (about 50 imperishable). The top level has over 2,100 (more than 2,000 perishable). The full catalog is estimated at about 9,000 specimens, excluding over 20,000 typed and counted [[Quids]].
- **Material orientation:** The culture emphasised fiber and wood over stone and bone. The fiber-to-stone ratio is about 16:1 in the bottom level and 22:1 in the top.
- **Projectile points:** Bottom level holds 7 pointed wood foreshafts (64 per cent of site total), 8 stone points, and 2 notched wood foreshafts. Points and foreshafts total 17 (bottom), 21 (middle), and 40 (top). Stone points gain at the expense of wood points, but wood points persist to the top level.
- **Subsistence shift:** Taylor documents a move from animal to vegetal foods through time, shown by metates, manos, edible seeds, and quids. He attributes it to increasing aridity.
- **Cultural continuity:** One sandal type, made of the same material by the same technique, appears from bottom to top in quantities of 89, 235, and 231 across a span of about 8,000 years. Twining, coiling, netting, plaiting, atlatls, fire-drills, and grooved clubs all occur throughout. Taylor concludes that a single cultural continuum existed for 8,000 years and probably 10,000.
- **Historical link:** Of the archaeologically discernible traits attributed to the Coahuiltecan tribes by [[Alonso de Leon]] before the mid-17th century, 63 per cent are known from Frightful Cave's remains.
- **Regional relations:** The Coahuila culture is closely related to the [[Pecos River Focus]] of the Big Bend, Texas, and both are phrasings of the [[Desert Culture]]. Taylor suspects the Pecos River cave sites are no earlier than Frightful Cave. He also argues that the Desert Culture's traits (atlatl, stemmed points, grooved club, metate and mano, fiber sandals) are widespread across Arid America.
- **Challenge to Clovis-Folsom framing:** Taylor proposes that differences between the [[Folsom Culture]] and [[Clovis Culture]] "hunting emphasis" and the Desert-Cochise "gathering emphasis" may be temporal and environmental rather than strictly culture-historical. Neither gathering nor hunting cultures may be the earliest in western North America.
- **Response to Heizer:** Taylor disputes the view that a single culture persisted unchanged for 10,000 to 11,000 years in the Great Basin, arguing the Coahuila evidence supports continuity over 8,000 to 10,000 years.
- **Editor's note:** No small "arrow points" occur in Frightful Cave, suggesting occupation ended before such points appeared regionally. This is notable given the youngest date of 1770 ± 250 years B.P.
