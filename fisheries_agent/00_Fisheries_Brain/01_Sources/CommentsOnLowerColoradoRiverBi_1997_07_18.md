# Comments on Lower Colorado River Biological Opinion

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species #[[Catostomidae]] #[[Cyprinidae]] #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal comment letter submitted by American Rivers (a river conservation NGO) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regarding a 1997 Biological Opinion ("BO") governing Bureau of Reclamation operations on the Lower Colorado River. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological field data, the letter critiques the regulatory and scientific adequacy of a Section 7 Endangered Species Act consultation covering three listed species: the [[Bonytail Chub]], the [[Razorback Sucker]], and the (non-fish) Southwestern Willow Flycatcher. The core scientific-management concerns raised include the inadequacy of a five-year "interim" consultation period for long-lived fish species, over-reliance on the yet-unrealized Lower Colorado River [[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP) to achieve recovery, and specific critiques of proposed research provisions related to native-nonnative fish interactions and razorback sucker recruitment dynamics.

## Background & Description

The source document is a policy/advocacy letter and does not provide morphological descriptions, meristic data, or physical characteristics of the species discussed. No data on body form, coloration, size classes, or anatomical features of the [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] are present in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: Both focal fish species inhabit the mainstem and associated backwater habitats of the [[Lower Colorado River]], including reaches through the Grand Canyon and [[Lake Mead]].
- **Recruitment dynamics (Razorback Sucker)**: The letter notes unresolved scientific disagreement over whether successful recruitment has occurred in the Grand Canyon/Lake Mead population of [[Razorback Sucker]]. It identifies **predation by non-native fish** as the primary obstacle to recruitment in Lower Colorado River razorback sucker populations, suggesting that any observed recruitment would likely reflect a temporary reduction or cessation of non-native predation pressure on young razorbacks, conditions considered very difficult to identify or replicate.
- **Native-nonnative fish interactions**: The letter asserts that competitive and predatory interactions between native and non-native fish species in the system are already scientifically well-characterized, arguing that further research (Provision 4 of the RPA) is largely redundant and that management should instead focus on actions reducing detrimental interactions and providing competitive advantages to native fish such as the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Habitat management proposals**: Provision 3 of the Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) references creation of isolated impoundments (totaling no less than 300 acres) intended to provide refugial habitat protected from negative impacts (presumably predation and competition from non-native species), tied to drafts of the Lower Colorado River Basin Management Plan.
- **Ecological restoration**: Provision 14 of the RPA is noted as a positive step toward broader ecological restoration within the Lower Colorado River system, though details of the restoration actions are not specified in this excerpt.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory/temporal concern**: A five-year "interim" consultation period is argued to be scientifically and legally inadequate for long-lived fish species like the bonytail chub and razorback sucker, as it cannot meaningfully advance recovery within that timeframe.
- **Jeopardy status unresolved**: The Fish and Wildlife Service's own Biological Opinion acknowledges that the RPA will not remove jeopardy to the [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], or Southwestern Willow Flycatcher within the consultation period — improvement below the jeopardy threshold is only anticipated in the long term, contingent on the future [[Multi-Species Conservation Program]].
- **Over-reliance on future planning**: The RPA is critiqued for depending heavily on "paper" actions (studies, reviews, research provisions 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 13, 15) rather than immediate, on-the-ground conservation actions (provisions 1, 3, 5, 11) needed to arrest species decline.
- **Predation as primary limiting factor**: Non-native fish predation is identified as the dominant bottleneck limiting recruitment success in Lower Colorado River razorback sucker populations, more so than other habitat or water-management variables.
- **Recommendation against redundant research**: American Rivers recommends against funding new research on native/non-native fish interactions (Provision 4) and recruitment condition studies (Provision 10), arguing existing expert knowledge is sufficient and that management actions should be prescribed directly rather than further studied.
- **MSCP should not substitute for regulatory standards**: The letter emphasizes that Reclamation's obligations under the Endangered Species Act constitute a higher and legally distinct standard from voluntary participation in the multi-stakeholder MSCP, and that participation in MSCP should not itself be credited as an action improving species status absent demonstrated results.
