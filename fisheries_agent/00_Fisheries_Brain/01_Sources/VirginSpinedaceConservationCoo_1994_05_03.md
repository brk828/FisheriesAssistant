# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Coordination (1994)

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative/regulatory letter (dated May 3, 1994) from the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR) rather than a primary scientific study. It announces the formation of a **Virgin Spinedace Conservation Coordinating Team**, aimed at developing a multi-agency conservation agreement for the [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*). The stated goal is to reduce or eliminate biological threats to the species and to preempt the need for federal listing under the Endangered Species Act. While it contains no direct morphological, ecological, or population data, it provides important context on the conservation status and management history of this imperiled cyprinid fish native to the [[Virgin River]] drainage.

## Background & Description
No morphological data are provided in this source. The [[Virgin Spinedace]] is known from other literature to be a small-bodied member of the family [[Cyprinidae]], characterized by a slender body and, as its common name suggests, spinous anterior dorsal fin rays — a diagnostic feature of the genus *Lepidomeda*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The species is endemic to the [[Virgin River]] system, which spans portions of Utah, Arizona, and Nevada — a tributary of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Range/Jurisdiction**: Multi-state distribution necessitating coordination among Utah, Arizona, and Nevada wildlife agencies, as well as federal partners (Bureau of Land Management, U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service).
- **Threats**: The letter references unspecified "biological threats" to the species, consistent with known stressors for Virgin River native fishes such as habitat alteration, water diversion, and competition/predation from introduced non-native species.
- **Conservation Status Context**: The species was, at the time, a candidate for consideration under federal listing processes, prompting proactive multi-agency conservation planning to avoid formal Endangered Species Act listing.

## Key Findings
- UDWR formally initiated the **Virgin Spinedace Conservation Coordinating Team**, with Leo Lentsch designated as Team Leader.
- The purpose of the team was to draft a **conservation agreement** among relevant agencies and stakeholders to reduce biological threats to the [[Virgin Spinedace]].
- A key motivation was to prevent the need for federal Endangered Species Act listing by proactively addressing conservation concerns.
- The first organizational meeting was scheduled for **May 16, 1994**, at the Washington County Water Conservation District Office in St. George, Utah, running from 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.
- Meeting objectives included finalizing official team membership, establishing the basis and goals for the conservation agreement, and beginning prioritization of management actions.
- Participating/invited organizations included:
  - Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR)
  - Arizona Game & Fish Department
  - Nevada Department of Wildlife
  - U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service
  - Bureau of Land Management
- This correspondence represents an early administrative step in a broader multi-agency **Conservation Agreement and Strategy** framework for the [[Virgin Spinedace]], reflecting a collaborative, proactive approach to native fish conservation in the [[Virgin River]] ecosystem.
