# Quaternary Paleoclimatology of the Pacific Coast of North America

#paleoclimatology #marine-biology #climate-history #ichthyology #zooarchaeology

## Executive Summary

This paper reviews Quaternary paleoclimatic trends along the [[Pacific Coast]] of [[North America]], emphasizing long-term cooling and increasing aridity from the Tertiary through the Pleistocene to Recent times. [[Carl L. Hubbs]] synthesizes evidence from paleobotany, paleontology, archaeology, oxygen-18 isotope analysis, and historical records to reconstruct temperature and precipitation patterns over the past several million years, with particular focus on the last 11,000 years (Postglacial period). The analysis integrates marine and terrestrial data, including fish distributions, mollusk assemblages from Indian middens, and physiographic evidence of former lake levels and stream flows. Hubbs argues that the [[Wisconsin Glaciation]] represented extreme cold with far-reaching effects, and that the trend toward greater aridity has continued through the Holocene, possibly accelerating in recent centuries.

## Background & Description

The study area encompasses the [[Pacific Coast]] from Canada to Mexico, including offshore islands, the [[Great Basin]], [[Colorado Desert]], and adjacent interior regions. The temporal scope extends from the Tertiary Period through the Quaternary (Pleistocene and Recent/Holocene), with detailed focus on the past 7,000 years based on radiocarbon dating.

Hubbs employs multiple lines of evidence: (1) paleobotanical data on the [[Arcto-Tertiary Geoflora]] and [[Madro-Tertiary Geoflora]]; (2) vertebrate and invertebrate paleontology; (3) oxygen-18 paleotemperature estimates from marine mollusks; (4) zooarchaeological analysis of fish and mollusk remains from Indian middens with associated carbon-14 dates; (5) physiographic evidence including pluvial lake shorelines, bajada structure, and erosional features; (6) historical weather records from [[San Diego]] (beginning 1849) and [[Monterey]]; and (7) biogeographic distributions of extant and extinct species.

The [[Wisconsin]] (Würm) glaciation is identified as a period of extreme cold extending approximately 11,000 years before present. Evidence includes southward displacement of boreal forests to the Gulf of Mexico, establishment of disjunct populations of northern freshwater fishes in New Mexico and northeastern Mexico, and the presence of cool-water marine species (e.g., [[Pacific Sardine]], [[Giant Kelp]] *[[Macrocystis]]*) connecting temperate regions across then-cooled tropical waters. Hubbs estimates that sea-surface temperatures dropped approximately 3°C in winter and 8°C in summer during Wisconsin time to allow such transtropical dispersal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The long-term climatic trend shows general cooling and increasing aridity from the Tertiary through the Quaternary, punctuated by major glacial-interglacial cycles. During the [[Pluvial Period]] accompanying the last glaciation, the [[Great Basin]] contained extensive lakes, rivers flowed full, and precipitation was "vastly greater than at present" with much reduced evaporation. Pluvial lakes included [[Lake LeConte]] in the [[Salton Sink]], which persisted until approximately 300 years ago based on radiocarbon evidence.

Marine temperature patterns show remarkable persistence. Pleistocene fossil assemblages and oxygen-18 estimates indicate maintenance of the north-south temperature gradient and the [[Point Conception]] faunal break throughout the Quaternary. Upwelling zones appear to have remained in consistent locations, suggesting persistent northwesterly winds.

Postglacial (Recent) climate shows continued fluctuation. About 7,000 years B.P., Indians on [[Santa Rosa Island]] consumed primarily [[Red Abalone]] (*[[Haliotis rufescens]]*), a cold-water indicator, suggesting persistence of glacial cooling. Later periods show alternating warm and cool phases. A notably cool period occurred approximately 900 years B.P., when Indians along the Baja California coast harvested [[Giant Chiton]] (*[[Cryptochiton stelleri]]*), a species now confined to waters north of [[Point Conception]]. Oxygen-18 estimates from this period show below-present temperatures. This cool phase may correlate with the "green" period in Greenland when Scandinavians colonized that region, suggesting negative correlation between Pacific Coast and Arctic temperatures.

A warm period occurred approximately one century ago (1850s), documented by the [[Pacific Railroad Survey]] of 1853-57, which found warm-water fish species at [[San Diego]] (including a seahorse) and southern species at [[Monterey]] that no longer occur at those latitudes. Weather data from San Diego and Monterey confirm a cooling trend through the second half of the 19th century, particularly in late spring and early summer months.

## Key Findings

**Long-term Temperature Trends:**
- General cooling from warm, moist early Tertiary conditions (when deep ocean temperatures reached only ~10°C and no polar ice caps existed) through progressively cooler Pleistocene glaciations
- [[Wisconsin Glaciation]] represented extreme cold, with evidence including: southward extension of boreal biota to Gulf of Mexico; establishment of disjunct northern fish populations in New Mexico and northeastern Mexico; transtropical dispersal of temperate marine species; and widespread pimple-mound topography attributed to freeze-thaw cycles in waterlogged soils
- Oxygen-18 estimate of ~12°C from Illinoian-age mollusks in northwestern Baja California (compared to present temperatures)
- Postglacial warming with fluctuations, including a cool period ~900 years B.P. and a warm period ~100 years ago

**Long-term Aridity Trends:**
- Consistent trend toward greater aridity from Tertiary through Quaternary, interrupted by pluvial periods during glacial advances
- Pluvial conditions during Wisconsin time produced: extensive lakes in the [[Great Basin]] and [[Range and Basin Province]]; full-flowing rivers; ponds and marshes on the [[Great Plains]]; and montane glaciers extending toward the Mexican border
- Rapid desiccation in Postglacial time, with evidence of "Little Pluvial" period mid-Holocene and severe drought in the 13th century
- Past three decades (as of 1959) may represent "the most severe" drought since the Pluvial period

**Archaeological and Zooarchaeological Evidence:**
- [[Fish Creek]] site (Salton Basin): hearths dated to 1,000 years B.P. contained bones of young [[Humpbacked Sucker]] (*[[Xyrauchen texanus]]*) and [[Bonytail]] (*[[Gila robusta elegans]]*), indicating sufficient stream flow for spawning runs from [[Lake LeConte]]; estimated rainfall ~10 inches/year (vs. <5 inches presently)
- Multiple coastal sites show human populations at 7,000, 5,000, 4,000, 3,000, and 2,500 years B.P. in areas where present water supply would be inadequate
- [[North Coronado Island]] site dated <400 years B.P. shows extensive habitation requiring more fresh water and firewood than currently available
- Midden sites along Baja California coast dated ~300 years B.P. contain abundant small [[Pismo Clam]]s, associated with warm period requiring greater surface water

**Marine Temperature Indicators:**
- Santa Rosa Island middens (~7,000 years B.P.): predominantly [[Red Abalone]], indicating cold water
- Later Santa Rosa middens: shift to warm-water [[Black Abalone]] (*[[Haliotis cracherodii]]*)
- Oxygen-18 estimates from surf-zone mollusks in northwestern Baja California middens indicate warmer-than-present temperatures at ~4,000, ~2,500, and ~300 years B.P.
- Cool period ~900 years B.P. documented by oxygen-18 estimates and abundant [[Giant Chiton]] remains in middens along Baja California coast where species no longer occurs
- [[Pacific Railroad Survey]] (1853-57) found warm-water fish fauna at San Diego resembling [[Turtle Bay]] and [[Magdalena Bay]] assemblages; southern species at Monterey not present in recent times

**Physiographic Evidence:**
- [[Lake LeConte]] shore bars at 45 feet above sea level show minimal erosion over past ~300 years, indicating extreme aridity
- Recession lines spaced ~5 feet apart, suggesting evaporation rate about double present rate
- Bajada structure in [[Colorado Desert]] shows fine sediments capped by desert shingle, with secondary fans of coarse rock fragments, indicating recent (late Pleistocene/early Holocene) stripping of soil mantles from mountains
- Rapid gullying throughout Southwest attributed to drought-induced destruction of ground cover

**Biogeographic Evidence:**
- [[Woodchuck]] ranged through northern Arizona in past few millennia, now limited by reduced moisture and higher temperatures
- Fish mummies from Nevada caves indicate permanent lake in [[Humboldt Sink]] within past few millennia
- Extirpation of isolated fish populations during 1930s drought that "must have persisted since Pluvial time"

**Climate Pattern Persistence:**
- Geographic pattern of temperature and precipitation gradients maintained throughout Tertiary and Quaternary despite magnitude changes
- Upwelling zones remained in consistent locations, indicating persistent northwesterly winds
- Temperature gradient from cold-water western [[San Miguel Island]] to warm-water southern [[Santa Cruz Island]] (~10°C summer difference) maintained in recent millennia

**Predictions:**
- High probability that climate will continue to change with wide fluctuations
- "Very strong possibility, if not a probability" that trend toward greater aridity will continue
- Future fluctuations will likely be superimposed on geographic pattern similar to present
- Temperature trends unclear; some evidence of negative correlation between Baja California coast and Arctic regions
