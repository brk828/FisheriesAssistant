## Executive Summary

This document is a **1993 station profile** for the **D.C. Booth Historic Fish Hatchery**, located in Spearfish, Lawrence County, South Dakota, and operated under the authority of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6). Rather than presenting biological or morphological data on a specific fish taxon, the source describes the administrative, historical, and infrastructural profile of a **fish culture station** established in 1896 under the original authority of the [[U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries]]. The station's core mission is not active fish production but historical preservation — to assemble, protect, and interpret the history and technology of American [[Fish Hatchery|fish culture]]. As such, the record functions more as an institutional and heritage-management profile than a species-level ichthyological study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While the source lacks species-level ecological data, the **facility's operational niche** within the freshwater fish culture system can be characterized:

- **Function**: Historic and (at time of writing) still partially operational fish hatchery, transitioning toward a museum/interpretive role for the history of [[Fish Culture]] technology.
- **Habitat infrastructure**:
  - 7 raceways
  - 5 ponds
  - Associated with a natural freshwater spring/creek system typical of the Black Hills (Spearfish, SD) region, historically used for [[Salmonid Aquaculture|salmonid rearing]].
- **Institutional lineage**: Originally founded under the **U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries** (est. 1896), later folded into the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service**, reflecting the broader historical shift in federal aquatic resource management from production-focused hatcheries to conservation and public education.
- **Public interaction**: Functions as a heritage/education site — FY1993 recorded **140,000 visitors**, indicating a significant public engagement role beyond fish production, linking it to broader themes in [[Public Aquatic Education]].

## Key Findings

- **Establishment**: The hatchery was established in **1896**, making it one of the earliest federal fish culture stations, predating the creation of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service itself.
- **Administrative Authority**: Historical authority originates from the **U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries**, later administered under **USFWS Region 6**.
- **Physical Infrastructure** includes:
  - 1 hatchery building
  - 1 historic home (original Superintendent's residence)
  - 1 additional residence
  - 1 four-stall garage
  - 2 stone garages
  - 2 wooden garages
  - 1 fuel storage building
  - 7 raceways and 5 ponds for fish rearing
