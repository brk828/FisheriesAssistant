# Dexter National Fish Hatchery Annual Narrative Report, FY 1981

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This annual narrative report from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in Dexter, New Mexico, covers fiscal year 1981. The station's program consists entirely of holding, spawning, rearing and distributing endangered, threatened and candidate southwestern fishes. Hatchery staff distributed 130,000 native fishes to waters within their historic range in Arizona and sent 30,000 young [[Colorado Squawfish]] to California for further rearing. The report documents razorback sucker spawning, successful reproduction of several listed species in holding ponds, and recommendations for broodstock acquisition and changes to federal listing status.

## Background & Description

The hatchery holds 18 species and hybrid groups, grouped in the report under federal status codes: (E) endangered, (T) threatened, (P) proposed, (C) candidate, (U-SC) unlisted species of concern. The source location for each stock is listed after the status.

Fiscal year 1981 brought staff changes. Hatchery Manager Jensen attended the Fish Hatchery Managers Course at the National Fisheries Center in [[Leetown, West Virginia]]. Theop Inslee served as acting manager during that time and then remained as Chief Fish Culturist. The station lost its YACC (Youth Conservation Corps) staff at the end of the fiscal year.

The station operated 20 ponds after the YACC pond project was completed in early June. Ponds, tanks and a flow-through system held the stocks. Stocks came from [[Lake Mohave]], [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], the wild, and Mexican and Texan springs and rivers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most stocks are imperiled. The hatchery acts as a refuge and source for restoration, so its outputs go to natural waters and cooperating agencies:

- **Stocking:** Razorback suckers went to the Gila, Salt and Verde rivers in Arizona. Bonytail chubs went to [[Lake Mohave]]. Yaqui chubs and Yaqui topminnows went to waters on the San Bernardino Ranch in southeastern Arizona.
- **Reproduction in ponds:** [[Woundfin]] spawned naturally in Pond D. [[Fountain Darter]] reproduced in Pond A. [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] numbered in the hundreds of thousands.
- **Recovery teams:** The [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]] and the [[San Marcos Recovery Team]] influenced decisions on removing and reclassifying species.
- **Environmental risks:** Winter temperature was identified as the main cause of losses for [[Big Bend Gambusia]]. Flooding in the San Marcos area may have eliminated part of the [[San Marcos Gambusia]] population. Several small populations showed winter crashes.

Cooperating agencies included the Arizona Game and Fish Department, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, the Region 6 office, [[Arizona State University]], [[Oklahoma State University]] and the University of Maryland.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker (E, Lake Mohave stock)**
- 134 adult fish arrived January 15–16 in ripe condition. Spawning began January 17 and produced about 40,000–45,000 fry.
- On June 30, 7,000 fish (3.2 in) were stocked in the Gila, Salt and Verde rivers. On September 8, 8,000 fish (5 in) were stocked in the same rivers. Fish were marked with [[Oxytetracycline]].
- About 4,000 fish were held back for Lake Mohave stocking the next spring. Fin-clip cauterization trials with liquid nitrogen were run; final results were pending.
- The station held 75 adults and recommended more broodstock from Lake Mohave.

**Yaqui Sucker (U-SC, Rio Sirupa, Mexico)**
- Only two individuals are held. They have not spawned and their sex is undetermined. The report recommends acquiring more broodstock from Mexico or sacrificing and preserving the two fish.

**Colorado Squawfish (E, Willow Beach stock)**
- 271 fish arrived from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] in March 1980, averaging 14.74 in and 305 g. By September 1980 they averaged 16.45 in and 567 g.
- Fish ripened in early June 1981. About 20,000 fingerlings were produced.
- Hatchery shipped 10,000 fry and 5,000 eggs to Joe Valentine in Region 6 for research. It received 10,000 fry from Willow Beach and reared them.
- In September, 100 domestic broodstock, 13 wild broodstock and 1,000 young-of-year fish arrived from Willow Beach. The adults will supply the FY82 commitment of 100,000 fingerlings.

**Bonytail Chub (E, Willow Beach stock)**
- About 50,000 fry arrived May 19 and were split into four C-series ponds.
- In October, 43,000 fish (733 lb, averaging 4.35 in) were harvested and marked with oxytetracycline before stocking in Lake Mohave.
- Ten broodfish were transferred to Dexter for FY82 production.

**Chihuahua Chub (PE, Mimbres River, New Mexico, and Rio Piedras Verde, Mexico)**
- The Mimbres stock rose from 10 fish in 1979 to a high of 102. Most exceed five inches; the largest are about 12 in. Spawning next year could raise the population four- to five-fold.
- The Rio Piedras Verde stock was not counted. Ten adults were brought in 1978, and about 30 were seen swimming together.

**Yaqui Chub (C, Leslie Creek, Arizona)**
- 225 fish arrived in 1976 and did very well in ponds. 32,000 were stocked on the San Bernardino Ranch in October 1980.

**Woundfin (E, Virgin River)**
- 240 fish arrived in 1979. Pond D fish did not reproduce, and 25 were removed the next spring. Stream-released fry washed out through screens.
- Additional fish arrived in April 1981. Fish spawned naturally in Pond D; other induced-spawning efforts failed.
- On May 16 all woundfin were moved to Pond 5C, including 185 fry taken from Pond D, confirming successful reproduction. Young schools were also seen in Pond 5C.

**Beautiful Shiner (CE, Arroyo Moctezuma, Mexico)**
- Sixteen fish arrived in June 1978. The population now numbers in the thousands during summer.

**Bluntnose Shiner (CE, Pecos River, New Mexico)**
- Ten fish arrived in November 1980. On August 18, six adults were removed (one died). Five remaining fish were moved to another pond. No reproduction was observed.
- The station hopes to obtain more fish from the Pecos River but notes that the FY82 workload is already overcrowded.

**Comanche Springs Pupfish (E, Diffen Spring, Texas)**
- About 35 fish arrived in 1974 from the outflow of Giffen Spring near Balmorhea, Texas. Numbers reached the hundreds of thousands in summer and fall.

**Leon Springs Pupfish (E, Leon Creek, Texas)**
- Introduced in 1974. Numbers are much lower than Comanche Springs pupfish, attributed to space and competition. Fish were moved to Pond 7C.

**Pecos Gambusia (E, Leon Creek, Texas)**
- Abundant. The Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team recommended removing it from station inventory at year's end, because it is secure in four widely separated natural habitats. The team also recommended downlisting it from endangered to threatened.

**Big Bend Gambusia (E, Big Bend National Park, Texas)**
- Stock was lost three consecutive winters. New stock from the park arrived August 27, 1981. Fish are now held in Pond D and in a tank in the holding house. Staff believe temperature is the primary cause of losses.

**Amistad Gambusia (E, Texas; extinct in the wild)**
- Very abundant and held in Pond 12C.

**San Marcos Gambusia (E, San Marcos River, Texas)**
- Overwintered successfully in the holding house. Flooding in the San Marcos area may have eliminated part of the wild population, so additional fish are being held as a precaution.

**Gila Topminnow (E, Boyce Thompson Arboretum, Arizona)**
- 200 fish arrived in 1976. Populations peak in summer and fall and crash in winter. Overwintering in the holding house is planned.
- 600 live fish went to Dr. Wendell Minckley at ASU.

**Yaqui Topminnow (E, San Bernardino Ranch, Arizona)**
- 200 fish arrived in October 1980. Although not extremely abundant, they have built a substantial population. Overwintering in the holding house is planned.

**Fountain Darter (E, San Marcos River, Texas)**
- One of the first six species brought in 1974. Fish reproduced in Pond A, but not in Pond 7B, and none were found when Pond 7B was drained. The San Marcos Recovery Team recommended removing the species from the station if fish remained present.

**Gila Hybrids I and II**
- Made at Willow Beach NFH and shipped as fry in May–June 1980 (Hybrid I: *G. robusta × G. elegans*; Hybrid II: *G. cypha × G. elegans*). Planned 1982 crosses (six in total) will test fertility, to help clarify the complex Gila problem in the upper Colorado River Basin.

**Other Activities**
- In April, 511 [[Socorro Isopod]] (E) were brought in by the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish for holding. They reproduced, but all died by late October.
- Inslee began bioassays on all station species, to continue in FY82.
- Construction of a visitors center was about 65% complete when the YACC program shut down in September. Visitor records were not kept for the first half of the year; the estimate is 500–600 visitors for that period.
