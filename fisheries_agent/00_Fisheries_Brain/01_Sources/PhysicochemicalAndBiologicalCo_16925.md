# Physicochemical and Biological Conditions in Two Oklahoma Reservoirs Undergoing Artificial Destratification

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #clupeidae #sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This 1977 Bureau of Reclamation technical report (REC-ERC-77-6), prepared by the Oklahoma Water Resources Research Institute at Oklahoma State University, evaluates the [[Garton Pump]], a low-energy, large-diameter axial-flow device that pumps surface water downward to destratify and reaerate reservoirs. The study compares two lakes: [[Ham's Lake]] (40 ha) and [[Lake of the Arbuckles]] (951 ha), tracking temperature, dissolved oxygen (D.O.), algae, zooplankton, benthic macroinvertebrates, and fish across 1972–1975. Complete thermal destratification was achieved in Ham's Lake, while the larger Arbuckle Lake was only partially affected. The report concludes that pumping can improve water quality and fish growth in mesotrophic reservoirs, but that its effectiveness depends on pump capacity, lake size, and wind assistance.

## Background & Description

Stratified lakes can develop anoxic hypolimnia, producing ammonia, sulfide, dissolved iron and manganese, and fishkills. Artificial mixing has long been used to counter these effects, using either compressed air or mechanical pumping. Before 1971, reservoirs were typically destratified by bubbling air or pumping bottom water upward. In 1971, Garton and Quintero designed a downflow mechanical pump, which was installed in Ham's Lake in 1972 and later enlarged.

Study sites:
- **Ham's Lake**: a 40 ha flood detention reservoir near Stillwater, Payne County, with a mean depth of 2.9 m and a maximum depth of 10 m. Previously used for a caged catfish farm, it suffered a catastrophic fishkill in August 1972 (about 150,000 channel catfish died after an overturn).
- **Lake of the Arbuckles**: a 951 ha municipal water-supply reservoir near Sulphur, Murray County, with a maximum depth of 27.4 m and a mean depth of 9.4 m. It is a warm, monomictic lake that stratifies from mid-May to mid-October.

Pump evolution:
- 1.07 m pump (373 W, about 0.76 m³/s), tested 1972–1974 in Ham's Lake.
- 1.83 m pump (746 W, about 1.7 m³/s), used in 1975 in Ham's Lake.
- 5 m propeller (5.6 kW, about 13 m³/s), used in Lake of the Arbuckles from 1974 to 1975.

The report presents a new Garton-developed oxygen distribution index, analogous to the stability index for temperature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physicochemical conditions.** In Ham's Lake, a 10.1 °C surface-to-bottom difference before pumping in 1975 fell to 1.2 °C after four days of pumping. D.O. was maintained above 3 mg/L throughout the water column except for a brief August dip. In Lake of the Arbuckles, the 2 mg/L D.O. isopleth defined suitable fish habitat. Thermal destratification was not achieved there, but the fall overturn advanced by about one month in 1975.

**Algae.** Ham's Lake showed no bloom formation under mixing. The dominant taxa included *Anabaena*, *Melosira*, *Cyclotella*, and *Aphanizomenon*. Dissolved organic carbon and chlorophyll *a* declined between 1974 and 1975, suggesting a shift toward a less eutrophic state. Lake of the Arbuckles recorded no nuisance blue-green algal blooms during pumping.

**Zooplankton.** Ham's Lake yielded 25 taxa and Lake of the Arbuckles 24 taxa. Both reservoirs had mean diversity values near 2.2–2.3. Zooplankton distribution was not closely related to D.O.

**Benthos.** Ham's Lake contained 76 benthic macroinvertebrate taxa and Lake of the Arbuckles 95. Dominant taxa included chironomid midges, oligochaetes, and the phantom midge *Chaoborus punctipennis*. Diversity generally declined with depth in both reservoirs.

**Fish.** Largemouth bass in Ham's Lake grew below the state average before and during mixing. Fish rapidly reoccupied previously anoxic depths after mixing began. In Lake of the Arbuckles, the 1973–1975 gill-net catch totaled 5,046, 3,351, and 2,926 fish, with catch per unit effort falling from 13.13 to 7.61 fish per net-day.

## Key Findings

- **Thermal destratification:** Complete thermal destratification was achieved in Ham's Lake in 1975 with the 1.83 m pump (4 days), compared with about 14 days in 1973 with the 1.07 m pump.
- **Pump scaling:** The 5 m pump at Lake of the Arbuckles (about 13 m³/s, 5.6 kW) did not fully destratify the lake. The authors note the lake has about 75 times the volume of Ham's Lake, while the pump has less than 7 times its capacity. A 38 m³/s, 18.7 kW pump was under development for 1977 testing.
- **Lake of the Arbuckles habitat:** The average volume with more than 2 mg/L D.O. over the 163-day interval (May 11–October 20) was 72.9% (1973), 82.7% (1974), and 72.6% (1975). In 1975, the 2 mg/L isopleth rose from 20.7 m (May 28) to 4.5 m (August 11).
- **Fish growth:** Instantaneous growth was often negative in stratified summers. Lowest summer growth rates for most species occurred in 1975, and highest in 1974, the year with the largest habitat volume.
- **Fish species shift:** Gizzard shad fell from 49.98% to 17.43% of the gill-net catch (1973–1975), coinciding with a rise in white bass from 3.15% to 9.47%.
- **Benthos:** Diversity in Ham's Lake increased after pumping. No significant change in diversity was detected in Lake of the Arbuckles.
- **Water quality:** BOD₅ in Ham's Lake fell to under 2 mg/L during pumping. The authors attribute this to oxidation of the accumulated organic load.
- **Management guidance:** The authors advise that lakes with high algal biomass, low light, calm winds, high BOD, and a high volume-to-surface-area ratio should be kept mixed from spring onward, not destratified at midsummer. Pumping of highly eutrophic lakes (chlorophyll *a* above 40 mg/m³) should begin in spring. Combining pumping with artificial aeration was recommended to ensure fish survival.
- **Recommended research:** Priorities include determining the pumping rates needed for different lake morphometries, comparing single large versus multiple small pumps, developing hydrodynamic theory, assessing long-term effects on biota and water quality, and testing the pump on cold-water release reservoirs.
