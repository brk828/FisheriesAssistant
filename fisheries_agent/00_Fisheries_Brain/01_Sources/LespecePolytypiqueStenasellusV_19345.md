# Polytypic Stenasellus Virei: Subspecies Variation in a Hypogean Isopod

#crustacea #isopoda #stenasellidae #subterranean-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1968 monograph supplement by [[Guy Magniez]] in *Annales de Spéléologie* re-examines the hypogean isopod [[Stenasellus Virei]], first described by [[Dollfus]] in 1897 from a single damaged specimen. Drawing on 77 known localities, the author recognises the species as polytypic and diagnoses five subspecies that differ in several independent anatomical characters. The study concludes that the species is not a restricted relict of fossil karst networks but an expanding, dynamic inhabitant of the hyporheic zone (river underflow) across southwestern France and northwestern Spain. Subspecies are never found together, and the author revises the diagnostic criteria used by [[Racovitza]] (1924, 1950), particularly the dactylopodite spine count and the morphology of the second and fourth pleopods.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** The species was described from a single adult collected at the [[Gouffre De Padirac]] (1896), with the body in poor condition. [[Hansen]] (1904) showed it was a true Asellota rather than a new suborder. Detailed study followed from [[Jeannel]] and [[Racovitza]] using material from Pyrenean caves, culminating in Racovitza's 1950 monograph. That work rested mainly on two closely spaced caves, Mont de Chac and l'Estelas, which masked the species' variability. Magniez also argues that *[[Stenasellus Buchneri]]* Stammer (1936), from Cantabrian caves, is not a valid species but a geographic variant of *S. virei*.

**Morphology.**
- **Size:** Males 7–9 mm and females 8–11 mm (Racovitza's averages). At the pristine Goueil di Her siphon, males measured 6–9.9 mm (mean 8.04 mm) and females 7.8–12.1 mm (mean 9.66 mm). The largest specimen recorded was a 12.2 mm female from Bas-Nistos. Size varies strongly between stations, so it is a weak identification criterion.
- **Body form:** About five times longer than wide, rising to six in slender hyporheic populations.
- **Cuticle:** Roughly 11–12 µm thick dorsally and about 5 µm sternally, firmer than in true asellids. The hyporheic *boui* lineage has a less hardened exoskeleton.
- **Eyes:** No ocular apparatus was found at any stage.
- **Pigmentation:** Live animals are pink, pale in alcohol. Magniez shows the pigment is dissolved in the haemolymph, not tied to specific organs, and is absent in very young animals. Hyporheic *boui* is nearly unpigmented.

**Appendages.** Key diagnostic structures include the antennules (up to 12 articles and 12 aesthetascs), the antennae (flagellum of 40–45 articles in males, 35–40 in females), the mandibles and maxillules, and the pereopods. The male second pleopod (the copulatory organ) is described as highly constant within the species and is the main criterion for species-level identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occupies three habitat types: karst caves (cavernicolous), groundwater (phreatic) and river underflow (hyporheic, Orghidan 1959). Populations from the hyporheic zone are smaller, more slender and less pigmented than cave populations. Cave populations of *S. virei hussoni* and *S. virei buchneri* show extreme endemism, with differences between neighbouring karst massifs and even between cavities within a single massif. Magniez links the late-appearing dactylopodite spine to ontogenetic delay (protero-genesis), which he interprets as an evolutionary adaptation to cave life. Repeated sampling of cave animals may also reduce adult size in some stations.

The author notes that smaller, earlier-maturing individuals may colonise interstitial habitats that large cave-dwelling adults cannot tolerate. Some populations show a shortened uropod length, possibly linked to slower growth of the posterior pereopods, which Magniez calls "endemic growth dysharmony."

Distribution follows karst and alluvial aquifers across the Aquitaine Basin, the Pyrenees, Roussillon and the Cantabrian Mountains. The author argues that the river underflow of the Garonne, Tarn, Salat and Tet-Tech systems acts as a corridor for dispersal. Related *Stenasellus* species from Corsica, Sardinia and Tuscany are cited as evidence that distribution is only weakly tied to present-day geography.

## Key Findings

**Subspecies (five, all with new subspecific names):**

1. ***S. v. virei*** (type subspecies): Padirac and the Causses, and the underflow of the Tarn in the northeastern Aquitaine Basin. Live animals are bright pink, with two dactylopodite spines on pereopods II–VII.
2. ***S. v. hussoni***: Cavernicolous lineage of the northern central Pyrenees, recorded from more than 55 stations (the largest subspecies, including l'Estelas and Mont de Chac). Typically has only one dactylopodite spine (rarely two). Pleopod IV exopodite is a crescent-shaped blade bearing up to 9 distal plumose setae.
3. ***S. v. buchneri* (= *S. buchneri* Stammer, 1936)**: Cavernicolous lineage of seven Cantabrian caves (Spain), including Altamira, Santa Isabel and Cullalvera. Two dactylopodite spines. Its sister subspecies, *hussoni*, shows no overlap in range with it.
4. ***S. v. angelieri***: Phreatic alluvial aquifers of the Tet and Tech rivers (Roussillon). Pleopod IV endopodite is especially small and the exopodite broad, with at most 4 distal setae. Adult body size does not exceed 8 mm in any capture.
5. ***S. v. boui***: Hyporheic lineage of the Salat underflow and its tributaries (Ariège). Almost unpigmented, with remarkable uniformity across stations. Pleopod IV exopodite is very stylet-shaped.

**Key results:**

- No cohabitation of two subspecies was ever observed.
- The *boui* lineage shows remarkable morphological homogeneity, while cave lineages (*hussoni*, *buchneri*) show considerable variation.
- Dactylopodite spine count is the most significant discontinuous character. In the l'Estelas cave, of 240 adult females only 4 of the 40 largest had a second spine (only on pereopod II), and none of the 40 largest males (from 500) had one. The character is therefore age-dependent and was used to revise the diagnosis of the species.
- The ratio of pereopod VII to pereopod IV length ranges from 1.39 to 1.58. Populations of the Estelas massif (Peyort) usually fall below 1.4, while those of the Sourroque massif (Sauvajou, Moulis) often exceed 1.5.
- Pleopod II in females is truncated in *virei* and *buchneri* and tapering in *hussoni* and *angelieri*. This shape is a major subspecific marker.
- Pleopod IV exopodite shape varies from crescent to stylet-shaped, with five structural types corresponding to the five subspecies. The author warns that this character should be used with caution for species definition.
- Pereopod IV is not sexually specialised, unlike male asellids. Magniez proposes that this reflects juvenilisation (neoteny), not a primitive state.
- The subspecies correspond to geographic and ecological units: karst massifs for the cave forms, and alluvial aquifers for the interstitial forms.

**Conclusions and taxonomic implications:**

- The species is "not a fossil living relict": it is an actively expanding species that colonises the hyporheic domain across a large area. The author suggests that the river underflow moved the lineage over geological time from coastal interstitial habitats into the karst massifs.
- The diagnosis of *S. virei* should rely mainly on the male second pleopod and the character set given by Racovitza, updated for the dactylopodite and pleopod IV variation.
- The author proposed that *[[Stenasellus Nobrei]]* Braga (1942) from Portugal may be a giant, isolated *virei* lineage, though specimens were not examined.
- Interbreeding is possible between the Padirac and Pyrenean forms, so the subspecies are not reproductively isolated, although this requires further crossing experiments.
