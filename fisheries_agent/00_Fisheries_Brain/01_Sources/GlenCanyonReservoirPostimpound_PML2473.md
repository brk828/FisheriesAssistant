# Lake Powell Post-Impoundment Investigations 1966 (Progress Report No. 4)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #salmonidae #ictaluridae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This progress report from the Utah Division of Fish and Game, a contribution to the [[Glen Canyon Unit]] of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]], documents the fourth study year of the cooperative [[Lake Powell]] investigation (February 1966 to January 1967). Utah covered chemical and physical regimens and fishery development; Arizona covered creel census, harvest and plankton. The reservoir was in a drawdown year after a poor runoff (about 6.7 million acre-feet versus a normal near 11 million), and the lake fell about 18 feet. The report covers stocking, water chemistry, thermal and density-current dynamics, and age, growth, condition, food habits and harvest for [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Walleye]] and the non-game species, with [[Common Carp]] and [[Green Sunfish]] dominant among the latter.

## Background & Description

The investigation began in July 1963, following the closure of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] in January 1963. Objectives and agency responsibilities had remained unchanged since project initiation. Sampling used gill nets, seines, an electro-shocker barge and hook and line, with creel census and stomach analysis supporting the work. Age was estimated from scales read on a microprojector using the Fraser-Lee back-calculation method, with a body-scale intercept assumed at 15 mm for centrarchids and 20 mm for trout. Condition factors (K) were computed from standard length.

Water chemistry was sampled at 17 fixed stations, with analyses for dissolved oxygen, pH, alkalinity, hardness, sulfate, silica, chloride, iron, turbidity, phosphate, organic solids, organic nitrogen and ash, following standard methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and thermal regime.** Lake Powell is monomictic, with a permanent thermocline forming as early as June and persisting to November. The 1966 thermocline was unusually deep (over 70 feet thick, spanning about 32 °F), and penstock withdrawal levels amplified the effect. Surface temperatures in early 1966 fell about six degrees below prior years near the dam and ten in the upper reservoir. A cold [[Colorado River]] density current flowed along the bottom from late December 1966 into March 1967. Runoff-driven turbidity from the Colorado River affected more than two-thirds of the reservoir during spring runoff.

**Water quality.** Dissolved oxygen fell sharply in the thermocline during summer, with values as low as 2.6 ppm near the dam in August, and the report links this to organic loading carried by the warm river. Bicarbonate alkalinity dominated, and total hardness fell during runoff in the upper reservoir. Phosphate concentrations declined downreservoir, indicating uptake as water moved through the lake.

**Littoral and vegetation.** Rising water flooded steep canyon walls and gentler slopes, creating littoral habitat for young fish. Rooted aquatic plants were sparse; [[Russian Thistle]], [[Tamarix]] and saltbush established between high and low water levels and offered cover to juvenile game fish.

## Key Findings

**Stocking**
- Only [[Rainbow Trout]] were stocked in 1966: about 2.1 million two- to three-inch fingerlings, dropped by aircraft from May 6 to May 11.
- Cumulative stocking to date is about 13.2 million rainbow or [[Kokanee Salmon]] trout and 2.9 million largemouth bass, plus smaller [[Black Crappie]] and kokanee plants.
- In October and November 1964, about 365,000 five- to eight-inch rainbow trout were stocked in Wahweap Bay, the largest single difference in trout plantings that year.

**Largemouth Bass**
- Growth was faster in the lower reservoir in later years, reversing the earlier pattern of faster growth upreservoir. First-year mean lengths for the 1963 and 1965 year classes were 209 mm and 226 mm in the lower reservoir, and 265 mm and 234 mm in the upper reservoir.
- Length-weight relationships compared favorably with bass from Norris Reservoir and Iowa and Minnesota waters.
- Condition factors averaged 1.8 for fish of about 120 mm, 2.35 at 255 mm, 2.66 at 420 mm, and 3.27 at 495 mm. Factors were highest in March and April and lowest in July and August.
- Spawning occurred from late April through July, with most reproduction from nests in deeper water; early-April nests in shallow water failed.
- Harvest from February through October 1966 was an estimated 57,363 fish. The 1965 year class supplied 53% of the harvest and the 1963 class was nearly absent after June.
- Diet was dominated by fish (60.6% by occurrence in the lower reservoir), largely centrarchids, with crayfish present only upreservoir.

**Rainbow Trout**
- Trout concentrated near the dam and in lower Wahweap Bay. Vertical net catches placed them within the thermocline (below about 70 °F) in summer and near penstock depth (62 to 80 feet) during netting.
- Returns from planted fish were low: an estimated 0.3%, 1.4% and 0.7% of the 1963, 1964 and 1965 plants respectively, though the report notes higher return rates if only Wahweap-area plants are considered.
- Growth was slower than in Flaming Gorge Reservoir; first-year mean length was about 7 inches versus over 9 inches there. Natural reproduction was largely unsuccessful.
- Zooplankton (mostly cladocerans) made up the majority of diet by occurrence in the 1966 sample; fish were absent from smaller trout.
- Condition factors in 1966 were lower than in 1964 and 1965 for most lengths.

**Black Crappie**
- Introduced in 1964 (about 3,500 fish) and 1965 (about 34,700 young-of-the-year). Twenty-one fish were aged; two-year fish averaged about 258 mm at capture.
- Early March 1967 young-of-the-year collections were the first indication of successful reproduction.

**Channel Catfish**
- 189 fish were collected from May to October 1966. The 150 to 250 mm length class was abundant, indicating the 1965 year class was the first clearly successful one since impoundment.
- Internal parasitism by a nodule-forming organism was noted in larger fish.

**Walleye**
- Four walleye (379 to 560 mm) were netted in 1966, aged IV to VI. Their presence was attributed to spawning before dam closure. The species was not considered a significant fishery.

**Non-Game and Other Species**
- [[Common Carp]] dominated non-game catches, with peaks at 170, 260, 300, 330 and 370 mm; the population showed slow growth and the report judged the lake less than favorable carp habitat.
- Green sunfish declined sharply in large-size classes but reproduction appeared unaffected. Bluegill increased upreservoir, with a median and mode of 220 mm.
- Colorado chub, bonytail chub, Colorado squawfish and flannelmouth sucker were taken in small numbers. Flannelmouth sucker numbers appeared to be increasing. A red shiner was collected in Wahweap Bay, the first lower-reservoir record since 1964.

**Water Chemistry Highlights**
- Surface temperatures ranged from 39 °F (January) to 80 to 83 °F (July to August); the thermocline was 30 to 100 feet deep by late July.
- Surface bicarbonate reached 144 ppm (December average); total hardness fell during runoff.
- Secchi readings averaged 12 feet in March, 27 feet in April, and under 2 feet near the Colorado confluence.
- Colorado River turbidity was the main concern, with a slight effect on fishes that inhabit the summer thermocline.

**Management Implications**
- The report recommends that future trout plantings include a marked percentage by reservoir area to evaluate planting success.
- Consistent annual monitoring is needed to track year-class strength, given the variable spawning season and drawdown effects.
