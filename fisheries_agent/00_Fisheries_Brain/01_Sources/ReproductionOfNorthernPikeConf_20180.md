# Reproduction of Northern Pike Confirmed at Parker Canyon Lake

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Esocidae #invasive-species #fishery-management

## Executive Summary

This document reports the confirmed reproduction of illegally introduced [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) in [[Parker Canyon Lake]], Arizona. First detected in summer 1997, juvenile pike were sampled by September 1998, confirming successful reproduction. The 124-acre lake southwest of [[Sierra Vista]] supports important recreational fisheries for [[Rainbow Trout]], sunfish, and other species now threatened by pike predation. Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) personnel are monitoring the expanding pike population and assessing management options to address potential impacts on the existing fish community and angler satisfaction.

## Background & Description

[[Northern Pike]] is a large soft-rayed predaceous fish characterized by an elongated oval body, large flattened head, and duckbill-like snout. The species is well-armed with razor-sharp teeth and functions as an ambush predator, lying sedentary in wait for prey. Color pattern consists of horizontal rows of yellow to white bean-shaped spots on an olive green to brown background. The species has a deeply forked tail and cylindrical body form.

[[Northern Pike]] is circumpolar in distribution, typically inhabiting cool water lakes and slow-moving rivers in Canada and the northeastern United States. The species has been both legally and illegally introduced throughout much of the United States, greatly expanding beyond its historic range. Individual fish can live over twenty years, reaching lengths exceeding five feet and weights over 50 pounds. The current world record is a 55-pound specimen caught in Germany in 1986. The Arizona State record is a 47.5-inch fish weighing 24 pounds 3 ounces from [[Upper Lake Mary]] near Flagstaff in 1981.

[[Northern Pike]] feed almost exclusively on other fish but occasionally consume small birds and frogs. In their native northeastern range, pike typically spawn in March and April as ice melts and water temperatures warm into the mid-forties. At [[Parker Canyon Lake]], pike may spawn as early as mid-January when water temperatures reach the mid-forties.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Parker Canyon Lake]] is a 124-acre impoundment completed in the mid-1960s, located southwest of [[Sierra Vista]] in southern Arizona. The lake represents an extremely important fishery for anglers throughout southern Arizona and is known regionally for producing large-sized sunfish and supporting a substantial trout angling following.

The first [[Northern Pike]] was identified at [[Parker Canyon Lake]] in summer 1997. By summer 1998, anglers reported small northern pike chasing schools of smaller fish in shallow shoreline areas. Extremely low water levels and unusable boat ramp conditions prevented Department surveys until September 1998. During this survey, three juvenile northern pike were sampled with an average length of 12 inches, estimated at approximately one year old. This sampling confirmed that the illegal introduction had provided suitable conditions for reproduction within the lake.

The presence of reproducing [[Northern Pike]] poses significant threats to the existing fish community. In the [[Coeur d'Alene River]] system of Idaho, biologists estimated that a population of 600 to 1,000 northern pike could consume between 2,500 and 4,200 trout per year. Such predation rates at [[Parker Canyon Lake]] could result in loss of as much as 25 percent of the stocked [[Rainbow Trout]] population. In Wisconsin, fish biologists reported that as northern pike populations increased, once-numerous [[Smallmouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]] sunfish became virtually absent from affected lakes.

## Key Findings

**Confirmed Reproduction**: Sampling of three juvenile northern pike (average 12 inches length, approximately one year old) in September 1998 confirmed successful reproduction following the initial 1997 detection of illegally introduced adults.

**Documented Predation on Trout**: In October 1999, an eight-pound northern pike was sampled containing an 8-inch trout in its stomach, providing direct evidence of predation on stocked [[Rainbow Trout]]. No evidence of predation on warm water fish species within [[Parker Canyon Lake]] has been documented to date.

**Potential Population Impact**: Based on predation rates from the [[Coeur d'Alene River]] system in Idaho, a pike population of 600 to 1,000 individuals could consume 2,500 to 4,200 trout annually, potentially representing a 25 percent loss of the stocked rainbow trout population at [[Parker Canyon Lake]].

**Ongoing Monitoring**: AGFD personnel continue to monitor pike expansion within the lake. Recent surveys have documented ten-pound northern pike, indicating establishment of larger size classes beyond the initial juvenile cohort.

**Management Assessment**: AGFD is conducting a management assessment to more closely evaluate impacts and management options. The Department acknowledges uncertainty regarding ultimate effects on the fish community, noting that impacts could range from negligible to large-scale decline in fishery quality and angler satisfaction.

**Spawning Phenology**: At [[Parker Canyon Lake]], northern pike may spawn as early as mid-January when water temperatures reach the mid-forties, considerably earlier than the March-April spawning period typical of northeastern populations.
