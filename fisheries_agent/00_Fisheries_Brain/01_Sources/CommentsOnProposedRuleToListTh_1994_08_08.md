# Comments on Proposed Rule to List the [[Virgin Spinedace]] as a Threatened Species

## Executive Summary
This document is a formal comment letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), responding to a proposed rule (Federal Register, May 18, 1994) to list the [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) as a Threatened Species under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. The letter supports the listing action and the associated decision to postpone critical habitat designation, while correcting a factual error in the proposed rule regarding Arizona's existing protections for the species. The AGFD also endorses a coordinated, ecosystem-based approach to critical habitat designation encompassing the [[Virgin Spinedace]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Virgin River Chub]], rather than three separate regulatory actions.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristic counts, coloration) is provided in this regulatory correspondence. The document is administrative/legal in nature rather than a taxonomic or anatomical description. Species-level taxonomic identity is confirmed via scientific nomenclature:
- **[[Virgin Spinedace]]**: *Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*
- **[[Woundfin]]**: *Plagopterus argentissimus*
- **[[Virgin River Chub]]**: *Gila robusta seminuda*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The [[Virgin Spinedace]] is native to the [[Virgin River]] drainage system, historically occurring across three U.S. states (implied: Arizona, Utah, and Nevada, based on the historic range of the Virgin River).
- **Community Context**: Co-occurs with other imperiled native fishes in the same drainage, notably the endangered [[Woundfin]] and endangered [[Virgin River Chub]], supporting a shared "aquatic ecosystem approach" to conservation and habitat management.
- **Threats to Niche Stability**:
  - **Habitat loss** — cited as a primary and well-documented driver of decline over recent decades.
  - **Competition and predation by nonnative fishes** — identified as a co-equal primary threat, indicating significant biotic disruption of the native fish assemblage in the Virgin River system.
- **Conservation Framework**: The [[Virgin Fishes Recovery Plan]] (in draft form at time of writing) is identified as the appropriate vehicle for incorporating multi-species recovery actions across the Virgin River ecosystem.

## Key Findings
- **Decline Documentation**: Multiple authors (Valdez et al. 1991; Hardy and Addley 1993) have documented significant declines in [[Virgin Spinedace]] populations and habitat quality over "the past several decades."
- **Causal Factors**: Declines are attributed primarily to (1) habitat loss and (2) competition/predation from nonnative fish species — both considered "fairly well known" causal mechanisms.
- **Regulatory Correction**: The AGFD identifies a factual error in the proposed federal rule, which incorrectly stated "no protection is provided for the Virgin spinedace in Arizona." In fact, the species has been listed as **Endangered** under Arizona's 1988 Threatened Native Wildlife list, with take prohibited under state law.
- **Multi-State Protection Status**: As of 1994, all three states within the historical range of the [[Virgin Spinedace]] have independently enacted protective (take-prohibition) status for the species, predating the federal listing action.
- **Ecosystem-Level Management Support**: AGFD explicitly supports a combined critical habitat designation for the [[Virgin Spinedace]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Virgin River Chub]] as ecologically more effective than three independent single-species rules, reflecting an early example of ecosystem-based endangered species management in the Colorado River Basin.
- **Recovery Planning Integration**: A recommendation is made to fold [[Virgin Spinedace]] recovery actions into the broader, still-in-development [[Virgin Fishes Recovery Plan]], reinforcing a coordinated multi-species conservation strategy rather than isolated single-species recovery efforts.
