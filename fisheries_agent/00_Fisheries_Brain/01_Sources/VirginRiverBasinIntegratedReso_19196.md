# Virgin River Basin Program: Environmental Community Letter to Utah DNR (1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This August 28, 1997 letter from four conservation groups to [[Utah Department of Natural Resources]] Director Ted Stewart responds to the [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program]]. The groups welcome the Program's mission to recover endangered species and enhance the river ecosystem, but say the Program was built without direct environmental input. They therefore decline to commit to its constraints until they understand it fully and can propose changes. They nominate [[Rick Johnson]] and [[Pam Hyde]] as environmental representatives and request equal, good-faith treatment, including serious consideration of their concerns before any Program documents are signed.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Letter of correspondence, not a research report. It contains no survey data, sample sizes, or species-level measurements.
- **Authors:** [[Grand Canyon Trust]], [[American Rivers]], [[Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance]] and [[Southwest Resource Council]]. Signatories include Lin Alder, Pam Hyde, Rick Johnson, Lupton (Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance) and Jane Whalen (Southwest Resource Council).
- **Addressee:** Ted Stewart, Director, Utah Department of Natural Resources.
- **Subject Program:** The Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program. Its stated mission is to "implement actions that recover, conserve, enhance and protect native species and their habitat, while balancing recreational and consumptive needs" of a growing human population in the basin.
- **Timeline:** In February 1997, environmental groups were told by department staff that the Program would have at least two committees with an "environmental representative" seat.
- **Distribution:** The letter was copied to about 38 named recipients (counted from the cc and bcc lists). They include directors and staff of the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[National Park Service]], [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]], [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], the Nevada Division of Wildlife, and the [[Virgin River Fish Recovery Team]]. University and conservation-group staff are also listed, including [[The Nature Conservancy]] offices in Utah and Nevada. The [[Council on Environmental Quality]] and Governor [[Michael Leavitt]] were also copied.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System of concern:** The [[Virgin River Basin]] contains endangered species and a river ecosystem that the Program aims to recover and enhance. The letter names no specific fish or other species; the recovery team's membership and the "Native Aquatics" section in the cc list indicate that native aquatic fauna are central to the effort.
- **Competing uses:** The Program must balance recreation and consumptive water use against habitat protection. The letter identifies growing population pressure in the basin and the complexity of resource management in Washington County.
- **Multi-agency setting:** Management involves federal land and wildlife agencies, state wildlife and natural resource agencies, a local water district, a national park, and a regional water authority. The groups also point to their members' experience with the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]].
- **Conservation framework:** The Program is framed as a consensus-oriented process for [[Endangered Species Recovery]] and ecosystem protection.

## Key Findings

1. **Program potential, with reservations:** The groups see the Program as a possible "valuable forum" for recovering endangered species in the Virgin River system. They have "several concerns" about its details and say it was developed without direct environmental-community input.
2. **Conditional participation:** Environmental groups will not agree to be bound by the Program's constraints until they fully understand how it operates and can suggest changes to its structure.
3. **Representation proposal:** The groups propose Rick Johnson (Director, Ecosystem Protection Program, Grand Canyon Trust; ecology training; prior work with The Nature Conservancy in Minnesota and New Mexico) and Pam Hyde (Southwest Director, American Rivers; law and natural resource economics; Steering Committee member of the Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program) as their representatives. They say both have support from the wider basin environmental community.
4. **Requests for briefing:** The groups ask that Johnson and Hyde meet with the involved agencies to learn the Program's history, purpose, scope and function, and that they be included in all ongoing discussions about its status, changes and implementation.
5. **Conditions of good faith:** The groups expect equal treatment with other participants, an opportunity to present concerns and improvements, serious consideration of their suggestions, and no signing of Program documents until significant efforts have been made to build a Program the environmental community can join.
6. **Tone:** The letter stresses cooperation. It commends the resource managers for handling "complex issues" and says the groups "may not always agree" with their decisions.
