# Temperature Preference and Avoidance by Adult Razorback Suckers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This laboratory study measured the temperatures that adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered [[Colorado River]] catostomid, avoid and prefer. Fish were acclimated to 8, 14, 20, or 26 C and tested in an electronic [[Shuttlebox]]. The authors estimated a [[Final Preferendum]] of about 23 C, with a best-estimate range of 22 to 25 C, and an optimum summer water temperature of 22.9 to 24.8 C. The study links the species' disappearance from the [[Green River]] tailwaters below [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] to the cold releases created by dam impoundment, and it provides temperature design criteria for water-development projects in the Colorado River drainage.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker has declined across the upper Colorado River basin. The main causes cited are impoundments, water withdrawals, and altered thermal regimes from dams. The species is one of several native Colorado River fishes listed as endangered or threatened, along with the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. Earlier work had documented the species' thermal responses, but the authors aimed to define its specific [[Temperature Preference]] and [[Avoidance Temperature]] for use in design criteria.

The study used two main behavioral measures:

- **Avoidance temperatures:** the upper and lower temperatures fish moved away from during the first 24 hours in the shuttlebox.
- **Final preferendum:** the temperature fish selected after orientation, estimated from the modes of temperatures occupied during the second 24-hour period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Experimental conditions**

- Fish were held in flow-through tanks with dechlorinated water, dissolved oxygen near saturation, ammonia below 0.2 mg/L, and pH of about 8.0.
- The photoperiod was 14 hours light and 10 hours dark.
- Fish with disease were not tested during treatment or for 21 days afterward, so the authors concluded disease did not affect thermal responses.
- The shuttlebox was a 45-liter plexiglass chamber in which fish triggered photocells to control warm and cold inflows. The apparatus could range from 4 to 31 C, with a maximum rate of change of 0.2 C/minute and a precision of ±1 C.
- At least 19 fish per acclimation temperature were tested.

**Dam effects on the Green River**

- After [[Dam Impoundment]] at Flaming Gorge in 1963, mean summer (June–September) water temperature below the dam fell from about 18 C to 6.8 C.
- Annual post-impoundment temperatures ranged from 1.2 to 10 C, which was below the calculated lower avoidance temperatures of 8.0 to 14.7 C.
- Razorback suckers were no longer captured for 105 km downstream, to the confluence with the [[Yampa River]].
- Below the confluence, the 1964 mean summer temperature was 16.9 C (monthly range 15.0–18.9 C), and razorback suckers were still found there.

## Key Findings

**1. Upper avoidance temperature**

- Upper (acute) avoidance temperatures ranged from about 27 to 32 C among acclimation groups and averaged 28.6 C overall.
- Acclimation temperature significantly affected upper avoidance (ANOVA, P < 0.01).
- Fish acclimated to 14 C avoided a significantly higher upper temperature than fish acclimated to 8, 20, or 26 C (P < 0.05).

**2. Lower avoidance temperature**

- Lower avoidance temperatures averaged 11.8 C, ranging from 8 to about 15 C.
- Fish acclimated to 8 C had a mean lower avoidance of 8.0 C, versus 14.7 C for fish acclimated to 26 C.
- Lower avoidance was positively correlated with acclimation temperature (P < 0.01, r = 0.39, df = 52).

**3. Behavioral responsiveness and mortality**

- Fish acclimated to 8 and 14 C showed [[Low Thermal Responsiveness]], with sluggish movement between chambers.
- Of 19 fish acclimated to 8 C, 10 raised the chamber temperature so rapidly that they died, 5 stayed in one chamber without moving, and only 4 operated the apparatus successfully.
- Of 21 fish acclimated to 14 C, 6 died or jumped out, 5 showed no preference, and 10 operated the shuttlebox successfully.
- Maximum temperatures recorded for fish that died ranged from 30 to 41 C (mean 35 C, SD 3.7).
- No mortality occurred in fish acclimated to 20 or 26 C.
- Coefficient of variation in occupied temperatures during orientation was 16.7% for the 8 C group and 7.6% for the 26 C group, which suggests fish acclimated closer to their final preferendum selected it more rapidly.

**4. Final preferendum**

- Modal temperatures selected by individual fish ranged from 22.7 to 29.0 C.
- Day and night preferences did not differ significantly, so data were combined.
- Fish acclimated to 14 C preferred significantly higher temperatures than fish acclimated to 20 and 26 C (Fisher's LSD).
- Fish acclimated to 20 and 26 C gave the most reliable estimate, with modal means of 22.7 and 23.1 C (mean 22.9 C). This value falls midway between their acclimation temperatures, which meets the equality criterion of [[Richards et al. Final Preferendum Criterion]].
- The geometric mean across all four acclimation groups was 24.8 C.
- The authors concluded the true value for adult razorback suckers lies close to 23 C, between 22 and 25 C.

**5. Comparison with other species**

- The [[White Sucker]] final preferendum (24.1 C) falls within the 94% confidence limits for razorback suckers.
- Other Colorado River natives measured in the same laboratory gave: Colorado Squawfish 25.4 C, Humpback Chub 24.0 C, and Bonytail 24.2 C.
- The upper avoidance temperature of razorback suckers averaged 3.7 C above the final preferendum (range 2.4–5.3 C). This is higher than the roughly 2 C reported by [[Coutant]] as a general pattern. The authors attribute the difference to using first-day data, when fish had not yet narrowed their range.

**6. Interpretation of overshoot and mortality**

- Fish acclimated to cold temperatures may overshoot their final preferendum, a pattern documented in earlier work on [[Final Preferendum]] paradigms.
- Upper avoidance in cold-acclimated fish sometimes exceeded the [[Critical Thermal Maximum]], which means these fish did not avoid lethal heat.
- The authors suggested that reduced metabolic and cerebral activity at low temperatures slows behavioral responses. Similar losses of orientation occur in [[Threadfin Shad]] at low temperatures and in humans with hypothermia.

**7. Conclusions and design criteria**

- Summer water temperatures of 22.9 to 24.8 C should be optimal for adult razorback suckers.
- Temperatures much above 29 C will probably be avoided if cooler water is available.
- Temperatures below about 12 C during summer will probably be avoided.
- The authors recommend that alterations in water temperature outside these values should not be allowed in new [[Water Development Projects]] in the Colorado River drainage if habitat preservation for the razorback sucker is a goal.
