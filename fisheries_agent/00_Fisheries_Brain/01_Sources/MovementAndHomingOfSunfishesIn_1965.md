# Movement and homing of sunfishes in Clear Lake

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 study by J.J. Kudrma investigated the movement and homing behavior of three sunfish species — [[Pumpkinseed]] (*Lepomis gibbosus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) — in [[Clear Lake (Iowa)]] during summer 1964. Fish were captured via electric shocking, marked with numbered jaw tags, and experimentally displaced to different lake areas to test homing fidelity. Results indicate that discrete, largely independent sub-populations occupy different littoral zones of the lake. Recapture and homing rates were higher for fish returned to their home area than for those displaced elsewhere, though homing success showed no correlation with displacement distance (up to 2,200 feet). Larger fish exhibited stronger homing tendencies, and homing percentage declined with increasing time-at-large, suggesting that home ranges are maintained only for short durations. Direct behavioral observation revealed that green sunfish orient and return to capture sites with greater precision and directness than pumpkinseed or bluegill.

## Background & Description

- **Size classes examined** (Total Length, inches):
  - Green sunfish: 3.4–5.7 in
  - Pumpkinseed: 3.4–6.5 in
  - Bluegill: 3.0–8.1 in
- Tagging method: Size 1, Number 4 Monel metal self-piercing jaw tags (¼ inch closed length), affixed to the lower jaw.
- No detailed external morphological descriptions were provided beyond size-class data used for correlating body length with homing tendency; larger individuals across all three [[Lepomis]] species (particularly pumpkinseed and bluegill) showed increased homing success with increasing size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[Clear Lake (Iowa)]] is a shallow, eutrophic, north-central Iowa lake (~3,643 acres, max depth ~20 ft). Littoral substrate is sand/gravel; deeper zones are silt.
- **Vegetation associations:**
  - Emergent: giant bulrush (*Scirpus validus*)
  - Submerged: *Potamogeton* spp., *Myriophyllum*, *Ceratophyllum*, *Carex*, *Vallisneria*
  - Vegetation density varied strongly by site (e.g., Area 3 had sparser vegetation than Areas 2A/2B), correlating with lower catch-per-effort.
- **Species-specific habitat preferences:**
  - Pumpkinseeds preferred densely vegetated Areas 2A/2B over sparser Area 3.
  - Bluegills preferred Area 2A over Areas 2B and 3.
  - Green sunfish were notably present in rocky, non-vegetated zones (e.g., Island West) unlike the other two species.
- **Distinct capture zones** included Areas 2A, 2B, 3, McIntosh Point, Lone Tree Cove/Point, Farmers Beach/Cove, Dodge Point, Island East/West, South Bay/Dock — each supporting apparently semi-independent populations, given limited inter-area movement detected via tagging.
- **Catch-per-effort (shocking, fish/hour)** varied substantially by area and year (e.g., Lone Tree Cove had highest pumpkinseed catch rates in 1964: 5.62/hr; South Bay had near-zero catch in 1963).

## Key Findings

- **Population structure:** Tagging data suggest sunfish in different major lake areas (e.g., Area 2/3 complex, Lone Tree Cove/Point, Farmers Beach/Cove, Island East/West) form largely separate, semi-independent populations, with limited cross-area movement.
- **Recapture rates by species:**
  - Pumpkinseed: 44% (163/368) recaptured after displacement.
  - Bluegill: 44% (73/166) recaptured after displacement.
  - Green sunfish: 74% (17/23) recaptured after displacement — attributed to possibly greater hardiness.
- **Homing percentages (of recaptured fish):**
  - Pumpkinseed: 64% homed.
  - Bluegill: 60% homed.
  - Green sunfish: 76% homed — highest homing fidelity among the three species.
- **Homing and habitat preference:** Fish displaced into less-preferred habitats (e.g., Area 3) tended to return to preferred vegetated habitats (Areas 2A/2B), regardless of original capture site — evidence of active habitat selection rather than pure site-fidelity.
- **Homing and distance:** No clear correlation between displacement distance (200–2,200 ft) and homing success — contrary to general literature trends, likely because distances tested were too short to reveal an effect.
- **Homing and body size:** Larger pumpkinseeds and bluegills showed a general increase in homing tendency with size; green sunfish sample size was too small for a valid trend assessment.
- **Homing and time-at-large:** Homing percentage *decreased* with increasing days-at-large for pumpkinseed and bluegill (e.g., pumpkinseed: 90% homed at 0–5 days vs. 38% at 24+ days) — this contradicts [[Parker and Hasler (1959)]], who found the opposite trend in other centrarchids. This suggests Clear Lake sunfish home ranges are maintained only briefly, with fish becoming more nomadic over time rather than more oriented toward "home."
- **Direct behavioral observations (balloon-tracking method, adapted from [[Hasler and Wisby (1958)]]):**
  - Green sunfish swam directly toward the home shoreline (Island) immediately upon release and remained stationary once there — indicating precise, direct orientation.
  - Pumpkinseed and bluegill exhibited "searching" behavior (circling near release boat) before moving toward shore, and continued moving/reversing direction along the shoreline after arrival — indicating less precise orientation.
- **Stay-at-home (non-displaced) controls:** Non-displaced pumpkinseed (64% recaptured) and bluegill (80% recaptured) had higher recapture rates than displaced conspecifics, supporting that displacement reduces overall recapture probability, likely due to stress, mortality, or dispersal.
- **Temporal pattern of recapture:** Fish recaptured within 1–2 weeks tended to have stayed within their capture area; those recaptured after ~3 weeks were more likely to have strayed — reinforcing the conclusion that home range fidelity in these [[Sunfish]] populations is a short-term phenomenon rather than a stable, long-term behavior.
