## Executive Summary

This 1961 Royal Ontario Museum publication by [[W.B. Scott]] and [[E.J. Crossman]] constitutes a comprehensive **taxonomic checklist of the fish fauna of Ontario**, Canada. The list enumerates **133 species and subspecies** distributed across **26 families**, ranging from primitive jawless fishes ([[Petromyzonidae]]) to advanced teleosts ([[Percidae]], [[Centrarchidae]], [[Cottidae]]). The document explicitly flags **introduced (non-native) species** with an asterisk and notes taxonomic deviations from the **American Fisheries Society's 1960 standardized nomenclature list**, making it as much a work of nomenclatural reconciliation as of faunal inventory. This checklist later formed the foundation for Scott and Crossman's landmark 1973 work *Freshwater Fishes of Canada*.

## Background & Description

The source is a nomenclatural checklist and does not provide morphological descriptions, meristic counts, or diagnostic characters for individual species. Taxonomic distinctions are indicated only at the genus/species/subspecies level (e.g., differentiation of numerous [[Leucichthys]] cisco species, or subspecies such as [[Salvelinus Timagamiensis|Aurora Trout]] vs. [[Salvelinus Fontinalis|Brook Trout]], and [[Stizostedion Vitreum Glaucum|Blue Pike]] vs. [[Stizostedion Vitreum Vitreum|Walleye]]).

Notable taxonomic/nomenclatural notes extracted from the text:
- **[[Entosphenus Lamottei]]** (American brook lamprey) — listed as *Lampetra lamottei* in the A.F.S. list.
- **[[Salvelinus Timagamiensis]]** (aurora trout) — treated as a form of *S. fontinalis* by A.F.S.
- **[[Leucichthys]]** species — treated as synonymous with *Coregonus* in the A.F.S. classification, reflecting ongoing generic-level disagreement over cisco taxonomy.
- **[[Megastomatobus Cyprinella]]** (bigmouth buffalo) — listed as *Ictiobus cyprinella* in A.F.S.
- **[[Moxostoma Macrolepidotum]]** (northern redhorse) — synonym *aureolum* noted.
- **[[Stizostedion Vitreum Glaucum]]** — recognized as a distinct subspecies ("blue pike, blue walleye") from the nominate **[[Stizostedion Vitreum Vitreum]]** (walleye).
- **[[Triglopsis Thompsoni]]** (deepwater sculpin) — listed as *Myoxocephalus quadricornis* in A.F.S.
- Multiple minnow genera (*Hybopsis*, *Chrosomus*, *Semotilus*) reassigned to alternate genera (*Couesius*, *Erimystax*, *Nocomis*, *Hybognathus*, *Margariscus*, *Pfrille*) relative to the A.F.S. standard, indicating an era of active cyprinid systematic revision.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although the document does not detail habitat preferences per species, the taxonomic composition indicates a diverse freshwater ecosystem spanning:

- **Anadromous/diadromous lineages**: [[Petromyzonidae|Lampreys]] (including the invasive [[Petromyzon Marinus|Sea Lamprey]]), [[Anguillidae|American Eel]], and clupeids such as [[Alosa Pseudoharengus|Alewife]] and [[Alosa Sapidissima|American Shad]].
- **Coldwater lake specialists**: [[Salvelinus Namaycush|Lake Trout]], numerous [[Coregonidae|whitefish and cisco]] species (e.g., [[Leucichthys Kiyi|Kiyi]], [[Leucichthys Zenithicus|Shortjaw Cisco]]), and [[Cottidae|sculpins]] including the deepwater-adapted [[Triglopsis Thompsoni|Deepwater Sculpin]].
- **Riverine/benthic specialists**: [[Catostomidae|Suckers]] (e.g., [[Hypentelium Nigricans|Northern Hog Sucker]], [[Moxostoma|Redhorses]]) and [[Percidae|darters]] (subfamily Etheostomatinae), indicating rich benthic invertivore guilds.
- **Piscivorous top predators**: [[Esox Lucius|Northern Pike]], [[Esox Masquinongy|Muskellunge]], [[Micropterus Salmoides|Largemouth Bass]], [[Stizostedion Vitreum Vitreum|Walleye]], and [[Lota Lota|Burbot]].
- **Introduced/stocked species** (marked with asterisk), reflecting anthropogenic modification of Ontario's ichthyofauna: Pacific salmonids (*[[Oncorhynchus Gorbuscha|Pink Salmon]]*, *[[Oncorhynchus Keta|Chum Salmon]]*, *[[Oncorhynchus Kisutch|Coho Salmon]]*, *[[Oncorhynchus Tshawytscha|Chinook Salmon]]*), *[[Salmo Gairdneri|Rainbow Trout]]*, *[[Salmo Salar|Atlantic Salmon]]*, *[[Salmo Trutta|Brown Trout]]*, *[[Cyprinus Carpio|Carp]]*, and *[[Carassius Auratus|Goldfish]]*.
- The presence of the invasive **[[Petromyzon Marinus|Sea Lamprey]]** is ecologically significant, as this species was undergoing a well-documented invasion of the Great Lakes at the time of publication, decimating native lake trout and whitefish populations.

## Key Findings

- The Ontario fish fauna as catalogued in 1961 comprised **133 species/subspecies across 26 families**, representing a benchmark inventory prior to major Great Lakes ecosystem disruptions.
- The **[[Coregonidae]]** (whitefishes and ciscoes) show the greatest species-level diversity among coldwater lake fishes, with **10 recognized *Leucichthys* forms**, reflecting the historic diversity of Great Lakes ciscoes (a group later subject to severe declines and extinctions, e.g. *Leucichthys johannae*, *Leucichthys nigripinnis*, *Leucichthys reighardi*).
- Significant **generic and specific taxonomic disagreement** existed between Scott & Crossman's usage and the 1960 American Fisheries Society standardized list, particularly within [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows) and [[Coregonidae]] (whitefishes), underscoring a period of active systematic revision in North American freshwater ichthyology.
- **Nine introduced species** are explicitly flagged, dominated by anadromous Pacific salmonids and European salmonids, indicating substantial early-20th-century stocking programs in Ontario waters.
- The list documents the co-occurrence of the **[[Stizostedion Vitreum Glaucum|Blue Pike]]** as a distinct subspecies alongside the walleye — a form that would be declared extinct later in the 20th century, making this record historically valuable as a baseline biodiversity snapshot.
- This checklist served as a direct precursor to the definitive reference work *[[Freshwater Fishes of Canada]]* (Scott & Crossman, 1973).
