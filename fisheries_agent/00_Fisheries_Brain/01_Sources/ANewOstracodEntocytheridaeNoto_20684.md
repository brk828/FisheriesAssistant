# A New Ostracod (Entocytheridae, Notocytherinae) Commensal on New Zealand Crayfish

#ostracod #marine-biology #entocytheridae #commensal #taxonomy #new-species

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes *Laccocythere aotearoa*, a new genus and species of entocytherid ostracod commensal on the freshwater crayfish [[Paranephrops planifrons]] from [[Pelorus Valley]], [[South Island]], [[New Zealand]]. The new genus *Laccocythere* is placed in the subfamily [[Notocytherinae]] and represents the first entocytherid ostracod described from New Zealand crayfishes. Twelve specimens were recovered from museum material collected by Charles Chilton (USNM 18173), though subsequent 1967 field collections failed to yield ostracods, possibly due to competition with heavily infesting temnocephalid worms.

## Background & Description

*Laccocythere* represents a new genus within the entocytherid subfamily [[Notocytherinae]]. The genus is diagnosed by: antennule with six podomeres; dorsal antennal claw truncate and devoid of setae; mandibular protopodite with distal row of five teeth; peniferum terminating bluntly with two possibly opposable processes; penis short, ventrally directed, and situated entirely above mid-length of peniferum. The genus name derives from Greek *lakkos* (pond) + *cythere*.

The type species *L. aotearoa* (named from the Maori "the long white cloud" for [[South Island]]) has a subelliptical shell with eyespot situated approximately 4% shell length from anterior end. Measurements of 7 adult male specimens: length 280μ-310μ (average 298μ), height 160μ-190μ (average 170μ). Adult triunguis females average 22μ longer than males (320μ length, 180μ height). Immature biunguis females (n=4) measured 240μ-300μ length (average 272μ), 150μ-160μ height (average 157μ).

Detailed morphological descriptions include: antennule with six podomeres bearing specific setal arrangements; antenna with four podomeres and tapering exopodite extending from basal podomere; mandibular protopodite with five-toothed distal row (proximal tooth bicuspid, teeth 2-4 tricuspid, distal tooth with five cusps); copulatory complex with blunt peniferum terminating in two sclerotized tapering processes; clasping apparatus falcate with four terminal denticles and four irregular teeth on internal surface.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Laccocythere aotearoa* is commensal on the freshwater crayfish [[Paranephrops planifrons]] White. The species is known only from the type locality in [[Pelorus Valley]], [[South Island]], [[New Zealand]].

The authors note an interesting ecological observation: their 1967 field collections of crayfishes from North and South Islands failed to yield commensal ostracods. Instead, virtually every crayfish examined was heavily infested with [[Temnocephalid Worms]]. The authors surmised the absence of ostracods may be correlated with competition between the two commensal organisms. However, they did not collect from [[Pelorus Valley]] during this expedition, leaving the current status of the ostracod population uncertain.

The difference between average instar lengths of six-legged immature females and adult triunguis females (48μ) appears similar to the 44μ difference noted for *[[Microsyssitria indica]]*.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Description**: *Laccocythere* new genus and *L. aotearoa* new species described from 12 specimens collected from [[Paranephrops planifrons]] crayfish in [[Pelorus Valley]], [[South Island]], [[New Zealand]] (USNM 18173).

**Morphological Characteristics**: Six antennular podomeres (vs. five in Indian forms); five mandibular palp teeth (vs. six in Australian forms); truncate dorsal antennal claw devoid of setae; bifid peniferum with two sclerotized processes that may or may not be apposable.

**Phylogenetic Relationships**: The species shows close affinities with subfamily [[Notocytherinae]] of [[Australia]] and [[New Guinea]], as well as with Indian subfamily [[Microsyssitrinae]]. Placement in [[Notocytherinae]] is based on six antennular podomeres (shared with Australian and New Guinea species) despite having five mandibular teeth (shared with Indian and New Guinea forms).

**Ecological Observation**: 1967 field collections failed to yield ostracods; heavy infestation of crayfishes with [[Temnocephalid Worms]] suggests possible competitive exclusion between the two commensal organisms.

**Type Material**: Male holotype, female allotype, and paratypic series deposited in [[Smithsonian Institution]]; paratypes in [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]].

**Morphological Variation**: Terminal denticles on clasping apparatus show variation in distinctiveness from internal margin teeth; paratypic specimen shows four possible terminal denticles and 2-3 internal teeth.

**Sexual Dimorphism**: Female shell similar to male but averaging 22μ longer; females possess conspicuous slender conical apparatus probably homologous with amiculum of genus *[[Dactylocythere]]* in [[North America]].
