# Grass Carp Stocking for Aquatic Weed Control in an Irrigation Canal (1974 Preliminary Report)

#ichthyology #aquatic-biology #ctenopharyngodon-idella

## Executive Summary

This 1974 preliminary report by [[William J. Hauser]], Assistant Research Biologist at the [[University Of California-Riverside]], was sent under a cover letter dated 15 October 1974 to three Southern California irrigation districts. It evaluates whether [[Grass Carp]] can control nuisance aquatic weeds in irrigation canals. Earlier trials had been less encouraging than expected, but one stocking in the [[Flox Canal]] in the [[Imperial Irrigation District]] gave "very exciting preliminary results." Some numbers in the scan are garbled, and they are reported here only where legible.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Biological control of aquatic weeds in irrigation canals, using grass carp as the agent.
- **Correspondence:** Cover letter from Hauser to R. F. Carter and J. N. Sheldon (Imperial Irrigation District), L. O. Meeks and K. H. Ainsworth (Coachella Valley County Water District), and J. Blakemore and G. Davisson (Palo Verde Irrigation District). The letter was copied to about ten researchers and staff, including T. W. Fisher, E. F. Legner, and R. J. Pelzman.
- **Status:** Marked as an informal publication, not to be quoted without the author's prior approval.
- **Test site:** A canal section in the Flox Canal, Imperial Irrigation District, about 9 miles west of Imperial, California.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target vegetation:** Dense growth of aquatic macrophytes filling the canal from bank to bank and extending through the water surface. Four weed species were recorded in October. The dominant species was thin, poor-stand [[Eurasian Watermilfoil]], which grass carp are known to eat readily in aquaria but apparently reject in more natural conditions.
- **Native-like fish community:** Large numbers of an OCR-garbled fish species and other fish-eaters were observed in the canal.
- **Canal management:** Vegetation was mechanically cleared by the area supervisor during the summer, a practice that [[Mechanical Weed Clearance]] would supplement or replace.

## Key Findings

1. **Limited prior success:** Earlier studies and experiments on the effectiveness of [[Biological Control]] agents in canal weed control were less encouraging than expected.
2. **Stocking event:** On 11 June 1974, 59.2 pounds of grass carp were stocked in a 0.612-acre test section of the Flox Canal. The stocking density was reported as about 0.7 lb or roughly 1,920 fish per acre, though this figure is partly garbled in the scan.
3. **Fish loss:** Fish were initially observed in the test section, but some were later seen downstream or in lateral canals. The author believed most had eventually been lost from the section.
4. **Vegetation regrowth:** By August, vegetation had spread and become dense, nearly reaching pre-stocking levels. The area supervisor then carried out mechanical clearance.
5. **October condition:** On 8 October 1974, the test section was nearly choked with weeds, with Eurasian watermilfoil as the only species present in any abundance. Grass carp appeared to be controlling it only partially, keeping growth in check rather than eliminating it.
6. **Overall assessment:** The author described the preliminary results as very encouraging and reported increased optimism. Water in the canal would be "drawn down" in several weeks, after which a quantitative evaluation of the stocking would be made.
