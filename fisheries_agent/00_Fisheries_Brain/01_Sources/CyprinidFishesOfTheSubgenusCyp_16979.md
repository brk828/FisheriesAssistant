# Blacktail Shiner (*Notropis venustus*): Variation, Subspecies, and Taxonomy

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1957 Tulane Studies in Zoology paper by [[Robert H. Gibbs Jr.]] (Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution) revises the taxonomy of the [[Blacktail Shiner]], *Notropis venustus* (Girard), a cyprinid minnow of the subgenus *Cyprinella*. Long confused by many synonymous names, the species is recognized as a single species with three subspecies and five races, based on meristic and proportional character variation across [[Gulf of Mexico Drainages]]. The paper also re-examines the nomenclatural history, including the [[Principle of Priority]] controversy, and offers a conjectural biogeographic history involving Gulf coast origin, dispersal through estuaries, and Pleistocene isolation.

## Background & Description

*Notropis venustus* was originally described as *Cyprinella venusta* by Girard (1856). Over the next century, ten additional names were proposed for populations of this minnow, several of which were treated as species or subspecies. Bailey, Winn and Smith (1954) first questioned the status of four of these names and recognized only one species pending investigation. Following changes in [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]] rulings, the name *notatus* (Girard, 1856) was initially applied but was later placed in synonymy with *venustus* by [[Clark Hubbs]] (1954b).

The species is distinguished by a pronounced black basicaudal (caudal peduncle) spot, which varies in intensity and dimensions among races and streams. Diagnostic features include:

- Teeth 1,4-4,1, hooked, with narrow concave cutting surfaces
- Anal rays usually 8 (often 9 in the upper Chattahoochee River)
- Lateral-line scales usually 36 to 43
- Predorsal circumferential scales usually 15-2-11
- Pectoral rays usually 14 or 15
- Terminal or subterminal mouth
- Dorsal fin pigmentation gradually increasing toward the rear, except in *N. v. stigmaturus*
- Breeding males with yellow unpaired fins and tubercles on the head, snout, notal ridge, and chin rami

Methodology followed [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1947) for counts and measurements. Proportional measurements used specimens of 50 to 60 mm standard length, with no more than ten specimens counted per collection where adequate samples existed. The author explicitly acknowledged that subspecies designation is uncertain without genetic evidence, but adopted it on the assumption that the segments have a genetic basis. Subspecies were defined by consistent modes and distributions across adjacent river systems, following the [[Ginsburg Criteria]] (1938) with the author's slightly higher threshold; races were defined at a lower level, with separation falling below an average of about 80 per cent.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and tolerance.** *N. v. venustus* prefers rather clear streams and tolerates warmth and temporary stagnation; it was found more commonly in clearer water downstream from spring sources, whereas [[Red Shiner]] (*N. lutrensis*) more often inhabits murky waters. Where the two were sympatric, *venustus* was "more abundant in swifter water." *N. v. cercostigma* is found mainly in clear, sandy-bottomed streams but also in extremely turbid water, which may reduce the size and intensity of the caudal spot. *N. v. stigmaturus* occurs in clear, cool streams with gravel to pebble bottoms.

**Caudal spot and turbidity.** The darkness of the basicaudal spot appears to depend on water turbidity, which may be the major factor in reducing its dimensions and intensity in the Chattahoochee and Flint rivers. The author suggested a possible genetic basis but noted that proof was lacking.

**Brackish tolerance.** [[Jordan]] (1928) observed the species in Perdido Bay alongside a marine goby and a sea eel. [[Bailey, Winn and Smith]] (1954) recorded it in the tidal portion of the Escambia River, where surface salinity was 1.8 parts per thousand and bottom salinity was 22.6, indicating tolerance of brackish water that likely facilitated dispersal.

**Associated species.** In much of its range *N. venustus* occurs adjacent to or with *N. lutrensis*. In lower Mississippi tributaries it is associated with a race of *N. camurus*, and in the Ouachita, Black, and St. Francis rivers with *N. whipplei*. In the Alabama system, [[Notropis callitaenia]] and [[Notropis trichroistius]] are sympatric congeners.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic revision**
- *Notropis venustus* is recognized as one species with three subspecies and five races.
- Subspecies: *N. v. venustus* (Girard), *N. v. cercostigma* (Cope), and *N. v. stigmaturus* (Jordan).
- Races: *Red-Lower Miss. Race* and *Western Gulf Race* (within *venustus*); *Eastern Gulf Race* and *Upper Chattahoochee Race* (within *cercostigma*).
- *Cyprinella notata* (*notatus*), *C. texana*, and *Cliola urostigma* are placed in synonymy with *venustus*. *Cyprinella texana* is included only as a misidentification after Royal D. Suttkus found that its syntypes are not *Cyprinella*.
- *Photogenis eurystomus*, *P. leucopus*, and *Luxilus chickasavensis* are synonymized with *N. v. cercostigma*; *Cyprinella calliura* with intergrades between *cercostigma* and *stigmaturus*.
- Lectotypes were designated for *N. v. venustus* (ANSP 3035), *Cyprinella notata* (USNM 136), *Cliola urostigma* (USNM 20446), *N. v. cercostigma* (*Photogenis leucopus*, USNM 31124; *Luxilus chickasavensis*, USNM 27419; *Photogenis eurystomus*, MCZ 24388), and *N. v. stigmaturus* (USNM 17891; *Cyprinella calliura*, USNM 6865).

**Subspecies diagnoses**
- *N. v. venustus*: deeper-bodied and larger-scaled than other subspecies; lateral-line count seldom exceeds 38; body depth usually 24 to 27 percent of standard length; a modally 8 anal ray form with a black basicaudal spot.
- *N. v. cercostigma*: body depth 21 to 25 percent of standard length; lateral-line counts rarely below 39; larger basicaudal spot, usually twice as long as wide.
- *N. v. stigmaturus*: the most slender form, with body depth usually 19 to 22 percent; lateral-line scales usually 40 to 43, up to 48; the best-developed caudal spot; prominently pigmented last two dorsal membranes with the anterior three typically unpigmented.

**Racial distinctions**
- *Red-Lower Miss. Race* (*N. v. venustus*): body depth averages over 27 percent; predorsal scale mode 13-2-11 tendency; modal 14 pectoral rays; short, wide caudal spot. Described as *Notropis cooglei* by Hildebrand and Towers (1928).
- *Western Gulf Race* (*N. v. venustus*): two geographically separated populations (Rio Grande to Sabine, and Ouachita, Black, and St. Francis rivers) showed no significant differences in analyzed characters.
- *Upper Chattahoochee Race* (*N. v. cercostigma*): polymorphic anal rays (8 and 9 in nearly equal numbers); modal pectoral rays 16 though commonly 14 or 15. The Fall Zone rapids near Columbus appear to be an effective barrier.

**Distribution and sampling**
- Collections examined by river system included 39 from the lower Mississippi tributaries and 12 from the Red River for the Red-Lower Miss. Race, and 24 from the Brazos and 8 from the Colorado (Texas) among western Gulf systems.
- *N. v. cercostigma* totals included 26 collections from the Apalachicola system, 16 from the Escambia, and 14 from the Pascagoula.
- *N. v. stigmaturus* was examined from 30 collections in the upper Alabama and 21 in the Tombigbee.
- Lateral-line counts in the upper Alabama averaged 42.4 (SD 1.50), the highest among systems sampled, while Rio Grande and western Gulf systems averaged about 37.

**Infraspecific variation**
- Variation among river systems showed no consistent pattern and was attributed to sampling error, slight genetic drift, or environmental phenotypic response; further study was needed.

**Relationships and biogeography**
- *N. venustus* is most closely related to [[Notropis spilopterus]] on morphological grounds, sharing yellow breeding fins, similar tubercle patterns, and reduced dorsal pigmentation.
- Center of origin is hypothesized as the Gulf coast drainages, with dispersal east and west through bays and estuaries.
- *N. v. venustus* is hypothesized to have dispersed up the west side of the Mississippi, with the Red River as the likely origin of the deep-bodied Red-Lower Miss. Race during Pleistocene isolation.
- The Fall Zone and rapids appear to act as partial barriers, with differentiation accelerated above them, giving rise to *N. v. stigmaturus* and the Upper Chattahoochee Race.
- Intergrades between *cercostigma* and *stigmaturus* in the lower Alabama system are based mainly on intermediate lateral-line counts. Proportional data for these intergrades were not analyzed.

**Conclusions**
- The caudal spot is the most recognizable character, but its variation in shape and intensity, along with body proportion variation, caused previous unwarranted species names.
- The author states that subspecies are "a real and important category," and that their naming has value even if some are later judged wrong.
