# Cliola (Hybopsis) topeka Gilbert, 1884 — Specific Name Conserved

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cypriniformes #Leuciscidae #Nomenclature

## Executive Summary
This document is not a traditional morphological or ecological study but an **official taxonomic ruling** by the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN), published as **Opinion 1821**. The ruling resolves a naming conflict between two historical species names — *Moniana tristis* Girard, 1856, and *Cliola (Hybopsis) topeka* Gilbert, 1884 — both of which refer to what is now recognized as **[[Notropis Topeka]]** (Topeka Shiner), a small North American freshwater cyprinid fish. Despite *tristis* being the senior (older) name under the Principle of Priority, the Commission voted to **suppress *tristis*** and **conserve *topeka*** as the valid specific name, prioritizing long-established scientific usage over strict chronological priority.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements, coloration) are provided in this nomenclatural text. The only physically referenced specimen data pertains to **type material**:

## Key Findings

### Nomenclatural Ruling (ICZN Opinion 1821)
1. **Suppression of *tristis*:**
   The specific name **tristis** Girard, 1856 (as in *Moniana tristis*) is **suppressed** for Priority purposes (but *not* for Homonymy), effectively removing it as the valid name despite being published earlier.

2. **Conservation of *topeka*:**
   The specific name **topeka** Gilbert, 1884 (as in *Cliola (Hybopsis) topeka*) is placed on the **Official List of Specific Names in Zoology**, becoming the accepted valid name — now used in the combination **[[Notropis Topeka]]**.

3. **Official Rejection of *tristis*:**
   *Moniana tristis* Girard, 1856, as defined by its lectotype (MNHN 427, designated by **Mayden & Gilbert, 1989**), is placed on the **Official Index of Rejected and Invalid Specific Names in Zoology**.

- **Published**: *BZN* 49: 268–270 (December 1992).

- **Clarifying comment**: Dr. Reeve M. Bailey noted the correct original publication date of Girard's work as **1856**, not 1857 as originally cited.

### Commission Vote (1 March – 1 June 1995)
- **Affirmative**: 17 votes (Bock, Cocks, Corliss, Hahn, Heppell, Holthuis, Kraus, Mahnert, Martins de Souza, Minelli, Nielsen, Nye, Ride, Savage, Schuster, Starobogatov, Stys)
- **Negative**: 6 votes (Bouchet, Dupuis, Kabata, Lehtinen, Macpherson, Thompson)
- **Abstained/No vote**: Cogger, Halvorsen, Trjapitzin, Uéno
- **On leave**: Bayer

### Notable Commissioner Remarks
- **Bock**: Emphasized that nomenclatural stability is paramount; unused senior synonyms should be suppressed through Commission action rather than reintroduced into literature.
- **Cocks**: Framed the case as a conflict between strict rules and established usage, siding with usage per Bailey's argument.
- **Dupuis (against)**: Cited taxonomic uncertainty regarding syntypes/lectotypes; considered listing *tristis* premature.
- **Thompson (against)**: Argued that "usage" is not clearly quantifiable and that widespread adoption of *topeka* in a popular Peterson field guide should not override the Principle of Priority.

### Taxonomic Significance
This ruling exemplifies the ICZN's **plenary power** to override strict priority rules in favor of nomenclatural stability, ensuring continued and unambiguous use of **[[Notropis Topeka]]** in scientific and conservation literature (the species remains ecologically significant as a **federally protected minnow species** in parts of its range within the central United States).
