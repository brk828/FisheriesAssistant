# MHC Variation and Parasite Resistance in Endangered Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #poeciliidae #conservation-genetics #immunology #parasitology

## Executive Summary

This dissertation proposal by Timothy J. Kim (Arizona State University, 1998) outlines research to investigate Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) genetic variation and parasite resistance in two federally-listed endangered fish species: the [[Sacramento Winter-Run Chinook Salmon]] and the [[Gila Topminnow]]. The research addresses critical conservation questions about how genetic variation, particularly at immune system genes, affects disease resistance in threatened populations. The proposal includes three projects: (1) characterizing MHC class II variation across four runs of Sacramento chinook salmon, (2) determining resistance of winter-run and fall-run chinook to [[Rosette Agent]] (a systemic protist parasite), and (3) assessing [[Gila Topminnow]] resistance to [[Gyrodactylus turnbulli]] (a monogenean fluke). The research will also examine inbreeding effects on parasite resistance using established inbred lines of both species.

## Background & Description

**Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC):**
The MHC is a group of genes present in all vertebrates that code for cell-surface glycoproteins central to immune response. Class I MHC proteins bind peptides from intracellular sources and present them to cytotoxic T cells, while Class II MHC products bind peptides from extracellular sources (bacteria) and present them to helper T cells. MHC loci are highly polymorphic, with evidence suggesting this polymorphism is maintained by selection. Amino acids in antigen-binding sites are approximately ten times more variable than non-binding sites, and non-synonymous substitution rates are elevated, indicating selection for amino acid change.

**Sacramento Chinook Salmon:**
[[Chinook Salmon]] (Oncorhynchus tshawytscha) are semelparous, anadromous fish of the Pacific Ocean. The [[Sacramento River]] system historically supported four distinct runs defined by migration timing: late fall-run, winter-run, spring-run, and fall-run. The winter-run was listed as endangered by California in 1989 and federally in 1994. Historical run size approached 100,000 fish in the 1960s; current effective population size is estimated at 200-600 individuals. Habitat loss from hydroelectric development, irrigation, and flood control has severely restricted spawning grounds. [[Shasta Dam]] construction forced spring and fall runs to share spawning grounds, causing hybridization concerns.

**Rosette Agent:**
A systemic protist parasite recently identified in salmonid populations. Taxonomic classification not established, but successfully cultured and shown to be eukaryotic, obligate intracellular parasite. Detected in captive winter-run broodstock at [[Bodega Marine Laboratory]] (BML) in 1994 and in returning late fall-run salmon in Sacramento River in 1995. Disease progression is slow but infected fish show higher mortality rates. In comparative studies, chinook salmon (late fall-run) were most heavily infected among five salmonid species tested.

**Gila Topminnow:**
[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis]] is a small livebearing fish closely related to guppies. Historically found in springs and streams of the [[Gila River]] system of Arizona and New Mexico. Now exists naturally in only four isolated Arizona watersheds due to habitat loss and introduction of non-native species (particularly western mosquito fish, Gambusia affinis). Federally and state-listed as endangered. Conservation efforts focus on translocation management to reestablish declining populations.

**Gyrodactylus turnbulli:**
A monogenean fluke parasite native to wild guppies (Poecilia reticulata) in Trinidad. Lives on body surface, mainly gills, and multiplies asexually. Guppies have spread worldwide and now persist in several warm springs in Arizona, potentially bringing this parasite into contact with [[Gila Topminnow]] populations. Previous research demonstrated other Sonoran topminnows are susceptible to common guppy parasites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Conservation Threats:**
Exposure to pathogens introduced by non-native species represents a critical factor for endangered species. Current management efforts including preservation, translocation, and captive breeding can be severely affected by host-pathogen interactions. Parasites may become especially problematic in populations restricted to reduced habitat or suffering from loss of genetic variation due to small population size and inbreeding. Disease outbreaks introduced from other species may push vulnerable species toward extinction.

**Genetic Variation and Disease Resistance:**
Populations with limited genetic variation may not adapt to environmental variation, including parasites, whether occurring spatially or temporally. Genetic variation allowing adaptation to parasites and disease is among the most important types of variation in many species. However, the genetic basis for parasite resistance in vertebrates is relatively unknown except in humans and mice, and effects of parasites on endangered species with lowered genetic variation is virtually unstudied.

**Inbreeding Effects:**
Inbreeding results in loss of fitness (inbreeding depression), potentially increasing extinction probability in endangered populations. Environmental stress, including parasites and disease, may further lower fitness in inbred individuals. While many biologists support the supposition that inbred animals have lowered parasite resistance, little evidence confirms this. Lyles (1990, 1996) found that inbreeding generally increased susceptibility to parasitic infection in guppies infected with [[Gyrodactylus turnbulli]], though other factors also influenced resistance.

**Habitat Context:**
For chinook salmon, water development for hydroelectric production, irrigation, domestic water supplies, and flood control has restricted or eliminated much spawning habitat in the [[California Central Valley]]. [[Coleman National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Bodega Marine Laboratory]] maintain captive breeding programs for winter-run salmon. For [[Gila Topminnow]], habitat loss and non-native species introductions have created scattered remnant populations in isolated watersheds. Evidence suggests feral guppies are being found in Arizona habitats that may contain topminnows, raising concerns about parasite transmission.

## Key Findings

**Research Objectives:**

1. **Project 1 - Chinook Salmon MHC Variation:**
   - Determine level and characteristics of class II MHC variation in four runs of Sacramento chinook salmon
   - Genomic DNA samples acquired from fish captured at Red Bluff or Keswick Dam
   - PCR primers derived from Atlantic salmon will amplify MHC class II Beta 1 chain exon region
   - SSCP (Single-Strand Conformation Polymorphism) will genotype all individuals
   - Preliminary investigation demonstrated four different alleles isolated; runs substantially differ in allelic frequencies

2. **Project 2 - Chinook Salmon and Rosette Agent:**
   - Determine resistance of winter-run and fall-run chinook salmon to rosette agent
   - Compare with brown trout as species comparison
   - Determine extent and type of MHC-related resistance within fall and winter runs
   - Winter-run salmon successfully maintained at BML since 1991 as captive broodstock
   - 1996 broodstock large enough for four full-sib matings and outcrosses
   - Compare resistance of inbred offspring (from full-sib matings of 1992 broodstock) versus outbred offspring
   - Use established disease challenge protocol developed at BML
   - Mortality rates of both groups will be tested using G test

3. **Project 3 - Gila Topminnow and Gyrodactylus turnbulli:**
   - Determine resistance of topminnows from four remaining watersheds to the fluke
   - Determine extent and type of MHC-related resistance
   - Test whether homozygosity at MHC has effect independent from generalized inbreeding effects
   - Topminnows maintained at ASU for approximately 28 of last 33 years
   - Stocks from four watersheds maintained in 400-gallon raceways (census numbers averaging several thousand fish)
   - Five inbred lines developed by four generations of brother-sister mating for Sharp Spring, Bylas Spring, and Cienega Creek populations
   - MHC variation already characterized using DNA cloning, sequencing by PCR, and SSCP analysis
   - Will determine appropriate infection level (comparing 1, 2, or 4 parasites per fish) and observation period
   - For each population: infect 100 fish with 100 uninfected controls (10 per 20-gallon tank per group)
   - MHC genotype for first random half will determine MHC-parasite resistance associations
   - Specific experiments planned:
     * Cienega Creek population segregating for two alleles (Pooc-1 and Pooc-5) in equal frequencies; will establish crosses producing homozygous and heterozygous progeny to test heterozygote advantage
     * Bylas Spring fixed for allele Pooc-1 with five inbred lines; will test differential resistance among inbred lines versus outbred population to determine if inbreeding itself (not just MHC variation) contributes to differential resistance; will cross inbred lines with low resistance to test if resistance can be restored by heterotic effect

**Significance:**
This research represents a unique opportunity to study relationships between MHC variation and parasite resistance in two endangered species where parasite challenge protocols are established and inbred/outbred lines exist. Results can influence current and future conservation biology practices generally and provide useful information for conservation of chinook salmon and [[Gila Topminnow]] specifically. Hughes (1991) argued that preservation of MHC variability should be first priority in designing captive breeding programs for endangered vertebrates.

**Facilities and Methods:**
Fully equipped molecular genetics laboratory with all protocols successfully tested. Techniques include: DNA extraction, PCR amplification, SSCP analysis, DNA cloning and sequencing, disease challenge protocols, and statistical analysis of mortality rates. Collaborative work with Kristen Arkush at [[Bodega Marine Laboratory]] for salmon disease challenges. Animal Care Facility at ASU maintains topminnow stocks and breeding lines.
