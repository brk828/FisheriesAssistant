# A Revision of the Lake Victoria Haplochromis Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This excerpt from a taxonomic revision of the [[Lake Victoria]] [[Haplochromis]] species flock (Greenwood, the third part of a series) redescribes several species and describes three new ones: [[Haplochromis Theliodon]], [[Haplochromis Empodisma]] and [[Haplochromis Saxicola]]. Each account gives type material, morphometric and meristic descriptions, pharyngeal dentition, coloration, distribution, diet, breeding notes and phylogenetic affinities. The author links feeding specialisation (insect-eating, mixed, mollusc-crushing, piscivorous) to the structure of the [[Lower Pharyngeal Bone]], arguing that several species form a sequence from a generalised stem toward the extreme pharyngeal hypertrophy seen in [[Haplochromis Ishmaeli]] and [[Haplochromis Pharyngomylus]].

## Background & Description

The revision covers species from the Lake Victoria cichlid radiation, with type material held mainly at the British Museum (Natural History) and the Genoa Museum. Key taxonomic actions in this section include:

- **Redescription of Haplochromis riponianus**: Lohberger (1929) resurrected this name from synonymy with *H. cinereus*, and the author endorses that decision.
- **Naming of new species**: *H. theliodon*, *H. empodisma* and *H. saxicola*. Specimens of *H. empodisma* had previously been called *H. michaeli* in earlier papers, and those references are to be corrected.
- **Nomenclatural notes**: The genus *Labrochromis* (Regan, 1920) is not maintained; *H. ishmaeli* syntype counts are reconciled (11 found against 13 listed in the original description).

**Haplochromis martini** is a female mouth-brooder that reaches sexual maturity at about 80 mm standard length (S.L.). Its bright yellow coloration, strongly decurved snout and large eye separate it from related species. Although its dentition and body form are generalised, it is a piscivore, and the author considers it a trophically distinct branch from a generalised *H. macrops*-like ancestor.

**Haplochromis humilior** (Boulenger, 1911) is based on a lectotype male from Kakindu, Victoria Nile, and a description of 30 specimens of 65–90 mm S.L. Its lower pharyngeal bone is relatively massive, and it has a low modal gill raker count (6–7).

**Haplochromis riponianus** (Boulenger, 1911) has a lectotype male of 95.5 mm S.L. from Jinja, and a description based on 28 specimens of 57–104 mm S.L. Its neurocranium departs from the generalised type toward the form of elongate piscivores.

**Haplochromis saxicola** sp. nov. has a holotype female from Ramafuta Island and a description based on 27 specimens of 106–123 mm S.L. It closely resembles *H. riponianus* in dentition and neurocranium, but its lower pharyngeal bone is typically slender.

**Haplochromis theliodon** sp. nov. has a holotype from Majita, Tanganyika, and a description based on seven specimens of 79–95 mm S.L. Its pectoral scales are small and deeply embedded, a feature shared with some generalised and fluviatile East African species.

**Haplochromis empodisma** sp. nov. has a holotype adult male of 117 mm total length, caught on the bottom in 90 feet of water off Kibibi Island. It is based on 39 fishes of 65–117 mm S.L.

**Haplochromis obtusidens** Trewavas (1928) has a lectotype male of 107 mm total length and a description based on 44 specimens of 60–114 mm S.L.

**Haplochromis pharyngomylus** Regan (1929) has a description based on 38 specimens of 70–126 mm S.L. Its pharyngeal mill is extremely hypertrophied.

**Haplochromis ishmaeli** Boulenger (1906) is described from the Bunjako syntypes. Its lower pharyngeal bone is massive and almost all its teeth are molariform.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The species occupy a range of substrates:

- *H. martini*: no habitat data supplied in this excerpt.
- *H. humilior*: confined in Lake Victoria to shallow water over sandy beaches, both exposed and sheltered.
- *H. saxicola*: shallow water over exposed sand or shingle, amongst dense plant stands near rocky shores, and over shingle in 10–30 feet. One specimen was taken more than a mile offshore on mud at about 180 feet. Hard substrates (sand, rock or shingle) are the most consistent feature of these habitats.
- *H. theliodon*: the few known specimens came from two exposed, shallow, sandy beaches.
- *H. empodisma*: generally restricted to areas of organic mud, caught at 10–90 feet.
- *H. obtusidens*: predominantly shallow water (less than 30 feet) on soft organic mud, with a few individuals taken over sand and at about 60 feet.
- *H. pharyngomylus*: essentially a species of hard substrates.
- *H. ishmaeli*: inshore areas under 30 feet deep with a soft, organic mud bottom.

**Diet and trophic specialisation.** The author groups the species by feeding strategy:

- Mollusc-eaters: *H. obtusidens*, *H. pharyngomylus*, *H. ishmaeli*.
- Mixed insect and mollusc feeders: *H. humilior*, *H. theliodon*, *H. riponianus*.
- Piscivore: *H. martini*.
- Insectivore/omnivores: the remaining seven species.

Diet details include:

- *H. humilior*: of 32 fishes from two localities, 24 contained bottom debris, larval and pupal insects (mainly Diptera and Trichoptera), and sand. Only 10 had molluscs, so Mollusca are not a major food item. The sand in caddis-larva cases suggests the pharyngeal mill may also crush these cases.
- *H. saxicola*: of 26 fishes from nine localities, only 12 contained food. Sand grains were found in 11, and insect larvae (probably chironomids) in eight.
- *H. theliodon*: all seven specimens ate bottom debris, small cichlids (20–23 mm), gastropods, bivalves and insect larvae.
- *H. empodisma*: of 37 fishes, the main food was dipteran larvae and diatoms from the bottom mud. Only two contained molluscan remains.
- *H. obtusidens*: of 46 fishes, the main foods were insects and the bivalve *Corbicula*. Diatoms in the bottom mud are digested, while blue-green algae pass through undigested.
- *H. pharyngomylus*: heavy mollusc feeding, with fragments of *Sphaerium*, *Corbicula*, *Melanoides tuberculata*, *Bellamya* and *Biomphalaria*. Some empty shells pass intact, suggesting that digestive enzymes break down snail bodies.

**Breeding.** *H. obtusidens* and *H. pharyngomylus* are female mouth-brooders. Breeding sites were not found for most species. Size at maturity recorded in the excerpt:

- *H. martini*: females at about 80 mm S.L.; no male data.
- *H. humilior*: smallest specimens (65 mm S.L.) already adult.
- *H. saxicola*: smallest specimen (106 mm S.L.) sexually mature.
- *H. theliodon*: a 75 mm S.L. female is mature.
- *H. empodisma*: smallest sexually active female 84 mm S.L.
- *H. obtusidens*: smallest mature male 83 mm S.L.; smallest mature female 89 mm S.L.
- *H. pharyngomylus*: smallest adult female 90 mm S.L.; smallest adult male 94 mm S.L.
- *H. ishmaeli*: smallest adult recorded 98 mm S.L.

**Distribution.** *H. humilior* occurs in Lake Victoria and the Victoria Nile. *H. saxicola* is definitely known from Lake Victoria and possibly the Victoria Nile. *H. theliodon*, *H. empodisma* and *H. obtusidens* are known only from Lake Victoria. *H. ishmaeli* occurs in Lakes Victoria and Edward. *H. niloticus* (nom. nov. for *Tilapia bayoni*) is known only from the Victoria Nile.

## Key Findings

1. **Pharyngeal hypertrophy as an evolutionary axis.** The lower pharyngeal bone and its teeth vary from slender to massive. Enlargement generally correlates with body size and with a shift to mollusc-crushing. The author argues that *H. empodisma* may represent the ancestral condition from which *H. obtusidens* arose, and that *H. obtusidens* gave rise to the extreme forms *H. ishmaeli* and *H. pharyngomylus*.
2. **Phylogenetic affinities.** *H. humilior* resembles *H. pallidus*, a species with incipient pharyngeal hypertrophy, and may be a specialised offshoot of that stem. *H. riponianus* and *H. microdon* are not closely related, contrary to Boulenger's view, because both the teeth and habits differ. *H. cinereus* is no longer regarded as a sufficiently generalised basic type.
3. **Sister-species relationships.** *H. saxicola* is so similar to *H. riponianus* in dentition and neurocranium that it might be large *H. riponianus*. However, its slender lower pharyngeal bone and the lack of enlargement with size argue against that. *H. pharyngomylus* differs from *H. ishmaeli* in outer tooth count (30–42 vs 38–66), pectoral fin length (68–91% vs 75–102% of head) and male breeding coloration.
4. **Diagnostic characters.** Taken together, the diagnostic features of *H. pharyngomylus* against *H. ishmaeli* are reliable, though no single character is decisive. Male breeding colours differ in life.
5. **Piscivory without elongate body form.** *H. martini* is a piscivore despite its generalised dentition and body form, and it differs from the larger, elongate piscivorous *Haplochromis* of Lake Victoria.
6. **Anomalous specimens.** Four *pharyngomylus*-like fishes from Jinja (115–120 mm S.L.) with weakly enlarged pharyngeals are placed in an appendix, as are three *ishmaeli*-like fishes from the Nzoia River mouth with bright pink male coloration. The author does not resolve their status.
7. **Summary of the revision.** Eleven species are redescribed and three are newly described, giving fourteen species in the group. Three species are mainly mollusc-eaters, three feed on insects and molluscs, one is piscivorous, and seven are insectivore/omnivores.
