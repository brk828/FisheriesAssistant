## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific or ichthyological paper**; it is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) policy memorandum (dated September 12, 1996) directing Regional Directors to organize stakeholder meetings for the agency's [[National Fish Hatchery System]] and broader [[Recreational Fisheries Program]]. It contains no morphological, taxonomic, or ecological data on fish species. Instead, it documents the administrative history, budgetary pressures, and planning requirements shaping U.S. federal recreational fisheries policy in the mid-1990s, driven by the [[Sport Fishing and Boating Partnership Council]] (SFBPC), Congress, and Executive Order 12962 ([[Recreational Fisheries Executive Order]]). ## Key Findings

- **Directive**: Each of FWS's seven regions was required to hold at least one stakeholder meeting by end of October 1996, per the Acting Director's July 11, 1996 memorandum, in response to direction from the [[Senate Appropriations Committee]] (FY1996 and FY1997 reports) and recommendations from the [[Sport Fishing and Boating Partnership Council]].
- **Purpose of meetings**: To establish program priorities, set regional goals/objectives, and develop cooperative strategies — explicitly *not* meant to be dominated by cost-cutting discussions, though cost-savings identification was a parallel goal.
- **Practical constraints**: Meetings involving more than approximately 40 people were found to become "unwieldy"; recommended approach was inviting stakeholder-group spokespersons or holding smaller serial meetings (as piloted by Region 3).
- **Historical/budgetary context**:
  - Multiple historical reductions in the number of [[National Fish Hatcheries]] (Reagan-era and FY1996 Clinton-era).
  - A 1994 16-member National Fish and Wildlife Foundation panel report on the NFH System's future role was seen by stakeholders as unrepresentative, prompting formation of the broader SFBPC review process.
  - Flat/cut FWS Fisheries budgets from FY1984–FY1995 led to operating deficits in four FWS Regions by FY1994.
  - October 1994 Matagorda ARD meeting: Fisheries Assistant Regional Directors attempted an internal 10% funding redirection toward highest-priority programs, recommending stakeholder consultation before facility changes.
  - Subsequent unilateral REGO I/II budget-reduction decisions (hatchery closures/transfers) were made without external stakeholder notification until the President's FY1996 Budget was published, damaging trust with recreational fisheries stakeholders.
- **Policy milestones**:
  - **June 1995**: President Clinton signed [[Executive Order 12962]] (Recreational Fisheries), directing Federal agencies to build partnerships and produce a 5-year Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan.
  - **August 1995**: FWS formally tasked SFBPC with (1) recommending FWS's appropriate role in recreational fisheries, (2) evaluating effectiveness of FWS facilities/policies, and (3) recommending operational/funding changes.
  - **December 29, 1995**: SFBPC delivered its *Cost Savings Report*, finding the FWS Fisheries Program budget had lagged behind other Service resource programs (FY1984–FY1995), and recommending interim cost savings without prescribing specific facility closures (deferring those decisions to regional stakeholder meetings).
  - **August 7, 1996** (Birmingham BASS Masters Classic): SFBPC approved a draft definition of FWS's appropriate recreational fisheries role and forwarded recommendations to the Director on August 20, 1996.
- **Expected deliverables**:
  1. FWS Implementation Plan (under E.O. 12962's Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan).
  2. FWS Fisheries Strategic Plan (merged with the Implementation Plan per recommendations).
  3. Identification of cost savings via program reduction/elimination and hatchery transfer/closure, to be reported to Congress.
- **Recommendation logic**: Any identified cost savings should be redirected within the same FWS Region to higher-priority fisheries projects, rather than lost from the program entirely — intended to incentivize stakeholder cooperation in identifying low-priority activities.
- **Key contacts/administrative infrastructure**: Bob Batky (Division of Fish Hatcheries) served as the point of contact; Helen Sevier (SFBPC Chair) and Gary Edwards assisted in identifying regional stakeholder contacts.
