# Notes on Fishes Collected from Lake Winnipeg Region

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This short faunal note reports fishes collected in the [[Lake Winnipeg]] region of Manitoba during field work for a research project at the [[Central Fisheries Research Station]], Winnipeg. The author, J. J. Keleher, notes that the most recent provincial checklist relied partly on disputed literature records and that collected material for the region was sparse, which made new collections worthwhile. The annotated list covers 14 species drawn from specimens in Table 1, which lists 13 to 14 collection localities with fish counts, length ranges and dates. Commercial species and four abundant minnows and perches were excluded. All specimens were deposited at the [[Royal Ontario Museum]]. Several records extend the known northern or provincial distribution of species, including the [[Golden Shiner]], [[Tadpole Madtom]] and [[River Darter]].

## Background & Description

The note builds on the [[Manitoba Fish Checklist]] by Hinks (1943), whose distributional statements were often generalized, and on the cautious distributional records in Dymond (1947), which revealed how few Manitoban fishes were backed by collected material. Field collecting was carried out mainly by J. E. Matheson and the author, with additional specimens contributed by L. C. Hewson, K. G. Roberts and K. H. Doan.

Methods were simple. Fish length was measured as the straight-line distance from the tip of the snout to the end of the middle caudal fin rays. Localities were given with approximate latitude, ranging from the [[Red River]] (49° 48' to 50° 6') to Warren Landing (53° 42'). Sites included [[Victoria Beach]], [[Black Island]], [[Loon Straits]], [[Bullhead]], [[Black Bear Island]], [[Dauphin River]], [[Mukatawa River]] and [[Spider Island]]. Collection gear included gill-nets, angling, seining over rocky bottoms and still fishing with earthworms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fishes were taken across a wide latitudinal gradient in both lake and river habitats. Several species were associated with specific substrates or depths:

- Longnose Dace were seined on rocky bottoms at every locality.
- Log-Perch occurred on sandy bay bottoms at Victoria Beach and on rocky bottoms elsewhere.
- Tadpole Madtom was taken at the surface in shallow water, with one exception at 33 feet at Black Bear Island.
- Muddler was caught in Lake Winnipeg, including one specimen 54 feet deep 7 miles west of the Mukatawa River mouth.
- Young Brown Bullheads formed schools in quiet bays at Victoria Beach.

Chestnut Lamprey was found attached to a pike in the Red River, showing a parasitic association with a host fish. Several records were from the Winnipeg and Assiniboine river systems, linking the lake fauna to these tributaries.

## Key Findings

The author's species accounts and collection records document the following:

1. **Chestnut Lamprey** (*Ichthyomyzon castaneus*): Two specimens, one from the Red River (attached to a pike, between Selkirk and St. Andrews Locks) and one from Black Island (commercial gill-net). The author notes its very limited Canadian distribution.
2. **Mooneye** (*Hiodon tergisus*): Ten fish at Victoria Beach (47 to 61 units length). Occurs in Lake Winnipeg alongside the goldeye.
3. **Longnose Dace** (*Rhinichthys cataractae*): 62 fish across three localities. About 10 at Black Bear Island had a bright orange tinge on the pectoral fins and premaxillary.
4. **Golden Shiner** (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*): Two specimens (93 to 95) at the Mukatawa River mouth in four feet of water. This extends the Manitoban range beyond Hinks's record of the southern half of Lake Winnipeg.
5. **Channel Catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus*): Five fish (248 to 356) from gill-nets at Victoria Beach. The author explains the changed trivial name, citing Speirs (1952).
6. **Brown Bullhead** (*Ameiurus nebulosus*): Five fish (32 to 42) at Victoria Beach. Black Bullhead (*A. melas*) is reported by Hinks but has no Dymond records; specimens should be retained if collected.
7. **Tadpole Madtom** (*Schilbeodes mollis*): Eight fish across the Red River and Black Bear Island. Lake Winnipeg record extends known distribution.
8. **River Darter** (*Etheostoma shumardi*): Nine fish from Bullhead and the Mukatawa River, extending its northern distribution. The author argues that *E. guntheri* was incorrectly synonymized with *Hadropterus maculatus* by Hinks (1943), following Hubbs and Greene (1928) and Carlander (1941).
9. **Log-Perch** (*Percina caprodes*): 28 fish across Victoria Beach, Bullhead and Black Bear Island.
10. **Iowa Darter** (*Poecilichthys exilis*): One specimen from a small pool at Black Bear Island.
11. **Muddler** (*Cottus bairdi*): Taken at Loon Straits, Black Bear Island, Dauphin River and the Mukatawa River, including a gill-net catch one mile off the Dauphin River mouth.
12. **Rice's Sculpin** (*Cottus ricei*): One dead specimen on the bank about 5 miles upstream of the Mukatawa River mouth.
13. **Ninespine Stickleback** (*Pungitius pungitius*): Records from Loon Straits (3), Black Bear Island (1), Mukatawa River (2) and Spider Island (4).
14. **Brook Stickleback** (*Culaea inconstans*): Four fish (30 to 38) from the same small pool at Black Bear Island as the Iowa Darter.

Overall, the note adds several new or extended provincial records and corrects a synonymy in the Manitoban literature. It also highlights how sparse collected material was for the region, supporting the case for continued specimen-based surveys.
