# First-Form Male of *Orconectes bisectus*, a Poorly Known Kentucky Crawfish

#carcinology #freshwater-biology #cambaridae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes the previously unknown first-form (form I) male of the crawfish [[Orconectes Bisectus]], a species known until then only from the [[Rhoades Holotype]] and a unique female [[Annulus Ventralis]]. Form I males are needed for reliable identification and for judging relationships, so the authors' collections from western Kentucky provided the basis for a redescription. On the strength of the first pleopod morphology of the form I male, the species is moved from the Rusticus Group of the Propinquus Section to the Limosus Section of the genus [[Orconectes]]. The paper also reports the habitat conditions during collecting, which were severely stressed by drought, ice, and oxygen depletion.

## Background & Description

- **Taxon and prior work:** [[Rhoades 1944]] described the species from Kentucky, recognising it mainly by its female annulus ventralis. He could not obtain form I males, and the only later collections are those reported here.
- **Systematic context:** The authors note that competitive exclusion is known between closely related crayfish, citing [[Bovbjerg 1961]] for *Orconectes* and [[Penn and Fitzpatrick 1963]] for the dwarf crawfishes *Cambarellus shufeldti* and *C. puer* under laboratory conditions. Rhoades had suggested possible exclusion between *O. bisectus* and *O. kentuckiensis*, which the authors could neither support nor refute.
- **Type material:** Holotype USNM 81322 (form II male) and allotype USNM 81323 (female). Type locality is Brushy Fork (Creek), 1 mi west of Repton, Crittenden County, Kentucky (about 5 mi north of Marion on U.S. 641).
- **Form I male morphology:** Carapace subcylindrical, with a strong acute lateral spine on each side. The areola averages 34.0% of carapace length (range 32.3–34.8%). The rostrum averages 30.4% of carapace length (range 29.2–31.2%), and the acumen averages 38% of rostrum length (range 32.9–40.8%). The first pleopod has a blunt, laterally compressed central projection and a non-corneous mesial process, with the pleopod reaching about the middle of the coxopodite of the second pereiopod when the abdomen is flexed.
- **Form II male:** Differs mainly in less acute, more spiculiform spines, weaker ischiopodite hooks on the third pereiopods, and less stout first pleopods with a more laterally directed mesial process. The authors had too few form II males for a full analysis of variability.
- **Female:** Annulus ventralis is immovable, bisected by a fossa, and fused cephalically with the sternum. The authors judged it unique within *Orconectes*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collecting conditions:** On 25 and 26 January 1964, Brushy Creek and most smaller tributaries were nearly dry. Surviving water persisted only as pools about 5–10 feet wide and 5–20 inches deep. The drought was attributed to low rainfall in western Kentucky during fall and early winter 1963–1964.
- **Stressors:** Ice about one-fourth inch thick covered the pools on 25 January. The pools were choked with detritus and decaying leaves, and crawfishes were taken from this debris and the rubble beneath it. Water temperature was 4 °C. Malodorous debris and numerous dead fishes indicated severe oxygen depletion.
- **Mortality and state:** At least ten crawfishes from Brushy and Skew creeks were dead, and most of the rest were torpid from cold.
- **Reproduction:** Of 16 adult females collected on 25–26 January 1964, 12 carried sperm plugs, which the authors interpret as fall mating followed by spring spawning. Three adult females from August 1961 had none.
- **Distribution:** All specimens came from the Crooked Creek drainage, a tributary of the Ohio River, in Crittenden County. Skew Creek is a tributary of Brushy Creek.

## Key Findings

- **Form I male described:** This is the first description of the form I male, which the authors consider essential for accurate identification and for studies of evolution and dispersal.
- **Reassignment:** *O. bisectus* is moved from the Rusticus Group of the Propinquus Section to the Limosus Section, based chiefly on the morphology of the form I male first pleopod (short rami, laterally compressed central projection, and deflected terminal elements).
- **Closest relatives:** The pleopod resembles that of [[Orconectes Sloani]] and [[Orconectes Shoupi]] but has a more flattened central projection, which is more like [[Orconectes Lancifer]]. The hand resembles that of [[Orconectes Shoupi]] and the tuberosities of the fingers resemble [[Orconectes Indianensis]]. Overall, the authors judge the relationship to *O. lancifer* to be closer than Ortmann (1905) had indicated.
- **Specimen base:** The new material comprises 12 form I males, two form II males, five immature males, 14 adult and three immature females from the type locality on 25 January 1964, plus further specimens from Skew Creek. Holotype and allotype are deposited at the [[United States National Museum]].
- **Sample statistics:** Morphometric ranges are reported for the areola, rostrum, acumen, and first pleopod. The central projection averages 24.2% of pleopod length (range 22.7–26.4%), and pleopod length fits into carapace length an average of 3.1 times (range 3.0–3.4).
- **Habitat and survival:** Drought, ice cover, detritus, and oxygen depletion in shallow pools were severe; at least ten crawfishes died during the sampling period.
- **Future work:** The authors call for further studies of evolution, dispersal, and interspecific relationships, which now rest on a properly characterised form I male.
