# Native Fishes in the Lower Colorado River Mainstem, American Southwest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document, attributed to P.C. Marsh and W.L. Minckley of the [[Arizona State University]] Department of Biology, reviews the status of [[Native Fishes]] in the [[Lower Colorado River]] mainstem. A century ago the free-flowing river and its [[Colorado River Delta]] supported a diverse indigenous fauna, including many freshwater endemics. Today the reach is dominated by [[Non-Native Species]], native fishes are essentially gone, and no native population is improving in conservation status. The authors attribute this collapse to the combined effects of exotic species and habitat alteration, and they report that the river's lowermost reaches in Mexico are also declining from water deprivation.

## Background & Description

The river historically hosted a suite of native freshwater fishes, many of them endemic to the basin. The excerpt does not give species counts, sample sizes, or survey dates.

The authors describe the current native community as a "tattered remnant" of its former self. Most species are extirpated, others are threatened or endangered, and none is self-sustaining.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document identifies several pressures that have driven native fish decline:

- Introduction of exotic species, which now dominate the fauna
- [[Dam Construction]] and [[Water Diversion]]
- Channelization and flow regulation
- Pollution and other perturbations

Management is complicated by many overlapping jurisdictions. These include:

- **States:** Arizona, California, Nevada, Baja California, and Sonora
- **Federal agencies:** [[Bureau Of Land Management]], [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], [[Fish And Wildlife Service]], and [[National Park Service]]
- **Other parties:** [[Native American Tribes]], municipalities, and private interests, mainly in agricultural irrigation and domestic supply

The authors report frequent conflict among these groups. They also state that, from a fishery management perspective, [[Sport Fishing]] goals and native fish needs are in direct opposition, with little hope of compromise or of successfully maintaining native faunal components.

## Key Findings

- Native fishes, including many freshwater endemics, were once common in the free-flowing lower Colorado River and delta. Today they are essentially absent from the mainstem.
- Introduced species dominate the current fish fauna.
- Most native species are extirpated. The remaining species are threatened or endangered, and none is self-sustaining.
- The conservation status of no native fish population is improving.
- The lowermost river and delta in Mexico are also degrading because of water deprivation.
- Sport fishing priorities and native fish conservation are in direct conflict, and the authors see little prospect of compromise.
