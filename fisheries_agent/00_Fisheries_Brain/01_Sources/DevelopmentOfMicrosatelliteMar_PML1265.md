# Development of Microsatellite Markers in Bonytail (Gila elegans) with Cross-Species Amplification in Humpback Chub (Gila cypha)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This primer note reports the development of microsatellite (simple sequence repeat) markers for the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), an endangered freshwater fish of the [[Colorado River]]. Markers were cloned from an enriched genomic library, and 17 loci were designed. Ten loci were used to screen 160 bonytail broodstock held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (Dexter NFHTC). The same loci were tested for cross-species amplification in 10 [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The markers were intended to give the genetic resolution needed to monitor and manage a critical broodstock and to serve similar purposes in other *Gila* species.

## Background & Description

- **Focal species:** The bonytail is described as one of the most endangered fish in the USA. It persists only as a refugium population at Dexter NFHTC and through rare takes of hatchery-produced fish in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Broodstock:** The Dexter stock is 22 years old. Developing a new broodstock required markers that are species-specific and highly polymorphic.
- **Other holdings:** Dexter NFHTC also maintains refugium populations of Pahranagat roundtail chub (*G. robusta jordani*), Virgin River chub (*G. r. seminuda*) and Chihuahua chub (*G. nigrescens*). It may hold humpback chub broodstock in the future.
- **Rationale:** The authors chose to build an enriched [[Microsatellite Library]] to provide the required level of genetic resolution for this broodstock and for other *Gila* species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses genetic monitoring of captive and refugium populations rather than wild habitat conditions. The bonytail's native range is the Colorado River, and the species survives mainly in hatchery refugia. Management of these small, managed populations depends on accurate estimates of [[Genetic Diversity]], which the markers were designed to provide. Environmental or habitat data are not reported.

## Key Findings

**Library construction and enrichment**
- Enrichment followed a modified version of the [[Streptavidin-Biotin Enrichment]] method of Hamilton et al. (1999). Genomic DNA was restricted with BsfUI (fragments 200–1000 bp), ligated to a universal linker, and incubated overnight.
- Oligonucleotide probes were (GATA)n at 43 °C and (GACA)n at 48 °C.
- Enrichment success was 61% for (GATA)n and 30% for (GACA)n.
- Sequencing yielded 67 clones from the (GATA)n enrichment and 64 from the (GACA)n enrichment.
- Primers were designed with Primer3 software.

**Bonytail results (broodstock screening)**
- 17 microsatellite loci were developed and screened in bonytail.
- The number of alleles per locus ranged from three to 18 (the scanned value reads "1g" and is probably 18).
- Across the 10 loci used to screen 160 broodstock, the mean expected heterozygosity was 0.58 and the observed heterozygosity was 0.59.

**Humpback chub cross-amplification**
- Of the 17 loci, 10 amplified successfully in humpback chub. Several loci did not amplify in the 10 individuals surveyed, and the protocol was not adjusted for them.
- The mean expected heterozygosity was 0.69 and the observed heterozygosity was 0.60. The authors note these results are less consistent, reflecting the small sample size (n = 10).
- The allele range for humpback chub is illegible in the scanned text.

**Amplification protocol**
- PCRs contained 100 ng template DNA, 1.5 mM MgCl₂, 5 pmol of each primer, and 0.88 U AmpliTaq Gold polymerase in a 10 µL volume. DNA was extracted with DNeasy kits.
- The program used a 9-min denaturation at 95 °C, then 33 cycles, with an initial annealing temperature of 64 °C that declined by 0.02 °C per cycle, followed by a final extension at 70 °C for 10 min.
- Genotypes were scored on an ABI Prism 3730XL sequencer, and heterozygosities were calculated with [[CERVUS]] 2.0.

**Conclusions**
- The loci are suitable for monitoring bonytail broodstock.
- Cross-species amplification in humpback chub is partial, and the markers require further validation in larger humpback samples before routine use.
