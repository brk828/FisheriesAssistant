# Two New Species of *Cleidodiscus* (Monogenea) from the Gills of Costa Rican Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #monogenea #dactylogyridae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 taxonomic paper by [[Charles E. Price]] and [[William A. Bussing]] describes two new monogenean (flatworm) gill parasites, *Cleidodiscus costaricensis* and *Cleidodiscus strombicirrus*, both recovered from the characid teleost [[Astyanax fasciatus]] collected in Costa Rica. The authors state that the new species raise the known freshwater monogenean fauna of Central America to six. The paper is labelled "Part 1" of a series on Costa Rican fish monogeneans, and it also introduces a concise format for presenting new species descriptions.

## Background & Description

**Previous records.** Before this study, four monogeneans were known from Central America. Three came from Costa Rican hosts and one from Guatemala:
- [[Price 1938]] described two *Cleidodiscus* species from a single *Rhamdia rogersi* specimen collected near San Pedro Montes de Oca, Costa Rica. Price and McMahon (in press) later transferred these to the genus *Urocleidus*.
- *Dactylogyrus mizellei* (Price, in press) was recovered from *Xiphophorus helleri* in Guatemala.
- [[Palombitrema Heteroancistrium]], a new genus proposed by the same authors (Price and Bussing, in press), was described from *Astyanax fasciatus* in Costa Rica.

**Materials and methods.** Host fish were collected in Costa Rica, frozen for several hours, and preserved in 32% formalin before shipment to North Texas State University. Gill parasites were dissected and processed following [[Price 1966]]. Measurements were taken with a calibrated filar micrometer ocular and illustrations were drawn with a camera lucida. All measurements are in microns, reported as an average followed by minimum and maximum values in parentheses.

**Format note.** To shorten the paper, the authors list characters shared by both new species once, in a preliminary section, and give only differential characters under each species heading. The shared set includes two pairs of eyespots, a muscular pharynx, simple intestinal crura, a copulatory complex of cirrus and accessory piece, a vagina opening in the left body half, a peduncle separating haptor from trunk, two pairs of anchors, seven pairs of hooks following the pattern of [[Mizelle and Crane 1964]], and poorly defined head organs. The list contains 15 numbered items.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host:** *Astyanax fasciatus* (Cuvier), a freshwater teleost native to Costa Rica. Both new species were collected from its gills, the site where monogeneans attach.
- **Collection locality:** Rio Montenegro, Guanacaste Province, 23 km NW of Canas, along the Interamerican Highway. Both species were recovered from this same locality.
- **Biogeography:** The paper frames its contribution as adding to the known freshwater monogenean diversity of [[Central America]]. Host records from Costa Rica and Guatemala are the basis for the regional count.
- **Morphological context:** The authors note that anchors, bars and hooks of *Cleidodiscus* are widely shared among congeners and offer little diagnostic value. Their own taxonomic arguments therefore rest mainly on the copulatory complex and the vagina.

The source gives no data on water chemistry, habitat conditions, prevalence or infection intensity.

## Key Findings

### 1. *Cleidodiscus costaricensis* n. sp.
- **Material:** Five specimens studied. Holotype is deposited in the Helminthological Collection of the [[U.S. National Museum]]; the first paratype is in the Museo de Zoologia, Universidad de Costa Rica.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Cirrus arises from an expanded base and tapers to a narrow tube. The accessory piece has a hollow portion within its main shaft that guides the cirrus. The vagina is not sclerotized, and a hyaline atrium opens near the left body margin. The authors consider this non-sclerotized vagina unusual for the genus.
- **Measurements (mean, range):** Body length 322 (293–350); greatest body width 76 (67–85); dorsal anchor length 34 (32–37); ventral anchor length 28 (27–30); dorsal bar 25 (23–28); ventral bar 26 (25–28). Hook no. 5 is much smaller (10, range 9–12) than the other hooks, which range from 16 to 23.
- **Relationships:** The closest relative cannot be determined with confidence because no other species has a comparable copulatory complex. The authors judge the species valid because the cirrus and accessory piece are unknown in previously described *Cleidodiscus*.
- **Etymology:** Named after the country of collection, Costa Rica.

### 2. *Cleidodiscus strombicirrus* n. sp.
- **Material:** Four specimens studied, with the same type-deposition arrangement as the first species.
- **Diagnostic traits:** The cirrus forms a coil of about two turns, a feature the authors describe as rare in *Cleidodiscus*. The vagina has a heavily sclerotized orifice and a slender vaginal duct. Anchor bases, including superficial roots, are hollow. The accessory piece looks like two pieces but is one structure. A single prostatic reservoir folded on itself gives a false impression of two reservoirs.
- **Measurements (mean, range):** Body length 380 (347–399); greatest body width 118 (99–135); cirrus coil diameter 30 (27–32); estimated cirrus length 190–210; accessory piece 44 (41–46); dorsal anchor 46 (44–49); ventral anchor 28 (26–31); dorsal bar 44 (41–46); ventral bar 33 (30–36). Hooks range from 20 to 28.
- **Relationships:** The authors place *C. costaricensis* as its nearest relative. They support the distinction with three factors: the coiled cirrus, the atypical vagina and duct, and the hollow anchor bases.
- **Etymology:** From the Latin prefix *strombi-* ("coiled" or "spiraled"), so the name means "coiled cirrus."

### 3. Taxonomic conclusions
- The two species bring the known freshwater monogenean count for Central America to six.
- Both species are closely related within *Cleidodiscus* and share the same host and collecting locality.
- Genus-level changes proposed by [[Price and McMahon]] (in press) are cited but not adopted in this paper.

### 4. Italian summary
The paper includes an Italian abstract (Rivista di Parassitologia, Vol. XXVIII, No. 2, June 1967) that repeats the main results: two new *Cleidodiscus* species from *Astyanax fasciatus* in Costa Rica, bringing the Central American freshwater monogenean count to six.
