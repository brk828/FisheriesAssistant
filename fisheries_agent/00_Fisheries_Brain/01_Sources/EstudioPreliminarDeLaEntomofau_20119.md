## Executive Summary

This preliminary protocol, authored by Rolando Mendoza Trejo and Francisco Soberón Mobarak, proposes a multi-year systematic survey of the entomofauna of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Valley]], an arid endorheic basin (~1500 km²) in central Coahuila, Mexico, within the Chihuahuan Biotic Province (per Morafka, 1977). Despite decades of biological exploration since 1939 by both Mexican and foreign institutions (Arizona State University, University of South Texas, Carnegie Museum of Natural History/Pittsburgh, University of Kansas, Universidad de Monterrey), prior work has concentrated on vertebrates, gastropods, and crustaceans, with **no published studies on the insect fauna**. The proposal outlines objectives, field and lab methodology, and a budget (~$50,000 USD for year one) to fill this gap, emphasizing the valley's exceptional endemism and its vulnerability to accelerating economic and demographic pressures since the 1960s.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document characterizes the physical and habitat template of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] relevant to any aquatic taxa (including the basin's endemic fish community, though not discussed directly here):

- **Geography:** Valley situated at the eastern edge of the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]], bounded by 27°00'00"–26°45'00" N latitude and 102°17'53"–101°48'39" W longitude.
- **Climate:** Extreme arid climate; annual precipitation <200 mm, concentrated in summer; temperatures range from 0°C in winter to >40°C in summer.
- **Elevation gradient:** Basin floor at ~700 msnm, surrounding mountain massifs exceeding 2500 msnm, creating a strong altitudinal environmental gradient.
- **Habitat diversity:** Canyons, arroyos, alluvial fans, and gypsum dunes support vegetation ranging from pine-oak forest to halophytic (salt-tolerant) plant communities.
- **Aquatic/semi-aquatic habitats:** Explicitly enumerated as [[ciénegas]] (marshy spring-fed wetlands), manantiales (springs), pantanos (swamps), lakes, ponds (estanques), canals, and shoreline/beach zones (playas) — the same interconnected spring-fed system known to harbor the basin's famous endemic fish, snails, and crustaceans.
- **Biotic significance:** The valley supports "a varied biota characterized by a high level of endemism," consistent with its recognition as a globally significant center of aquatic endemism (relevant to [[Cyprinodon]] and other endemic Cuatro Ciénegas fish taxa, though not named in this text).

## Key Findings

- **Research gap identified:** No prior entomological survey exists for Cuatro Ciénegas, despite extensive vertebrate, gastropod, and crustacean inventories dating to 1939.
- **Conservation urgency:** Rapid economic and population growth since the mid-1960s is degrading natural habitats, threatening endemic biota (including presumably the endemic aquatic fauna of the ciénegas system).
- **Proposed methodology:**
  - Field sampling across the elevational gradient using direct and indirect collection methods suited to aquatic, terrestrial, and aerial insect habitats.
  - Monthly 15–20 day field campaigns over the first two years to capture full seasonal cycles, timed to lunar phases (full/new moon) to optimize light-trap collecting efficiency.
  - Concurrent recording of phenology, biology, and plant-association data for dominant insect groups.
  - Laboratory-based taxonomic identification phase in Mexico City, requiring institutional partnerships for facilities (microscopy, reagents, computing, literature).
- **Broader value:** The planned entomological collection (housed under the association NATURALIA) is framed as a resource for allied disciplines — agronomy, forestry, ecology, biogeography, and ethology — and as a baseline for future faunal publications.
- **Budgetary scope:** Estimated first-year budget of $50,000 USD, itemized elsewhere in the full project document (not detailed in this excerpt).
