## Executive Summary

This document is a 1992 Bureau of Reclamation memorandum from the **Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)** program, summarizing a coordination meeting regarding graduate research proposals under the Cooperative U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Research Unit (AZCFWRU) at Arizona State University. The primary research focus is Nathan Allan's graduate study, "Habitat Use by Native Fish in Havasu and Shinumo Creek," intended to support endangered fish conservation efforts for the **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) in the Grand Canyon. The memo does not present primary morphological or ecological data itself, but establishes the research framework, methodology constraints, and administrative agreements guiding tributary habitat studies critical to Conservation Measure 7: establishing a second spawning aggregation of humpback chub in the Grand Canyon.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data on *Gila cypha* or *Catostomus discobolus* is presented in this administrative document. The memo references methodological tools relevant to individual identification and tracking rather than morphology per se:

- **Marking/Tagging Methods**: Discussion of **Passive Integrated Transponder (PIT) tags** and **fin-marking** techniques as non-lethal methods for individual identification, movement tracking, and recapture studies.
- **Species Differentiation Concern**: Fin-marking patterns must be distinct across research groups (Bio/West, Arizona Game & Fish, Arizona State University) to avoid confounding humpback chub identification with other tagged native fish species.
- **Electroshocking**: Noted as an effective bank-shocking technique for sampling in small tributaries, with limited injury reported to native fish in mainstem applications (citing Valdez et al. 1992).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Focal Species**: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a Colorado River Basin endemic and federally endangered cyprinid, is the central conservation target.
- **Secondary Species**: [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*) is studied concurrently for comparative habitat utilization data, though not the primary research objective.
- **Habitat Focus**: Four key tributaries were designated for seasonal ichthyofaunal census and habitat assessment:
  - [[Shinumo Creek]]
  - [[Tapeats Creek]]
  - [[Kanab Creek]]
  - [[Havasu Creek]]
- **Reference System**: The [[Little Colorado River]] serves as the baseline/reference habitat dataset, given ongoing FWS/Pinetop research there on humpback chub habitat requirements.
- **Additional Tributary Context**: The [[Paria River]] is mentioned as a separate study site (Ted Otis, CFWS/Tucson) relevant to the broader tributary research network.
- **Habitat Use Patterns**: Research aims to determine seasonal tributary use, habitat availability, and whether physical barriers at tributary confluences are total exclusions or species-specific barriers to fish movement — a key question for understanding [[Native Fish Assemblage]] dynamics in the Grand Canyon.
- **Spawning Ecology Context**: The study directly supports **Conservation Measure 7**, aiming to establish a second humpback chub spawning aggregation in the Grand Canyon, distinct from the known Little Colorado River aggregation.

## Key Findings

1. **Research Approval**: GCES granted tentative approval for Nathan Allan's graduate study on native fish habitat use in Havasu and Shinumo Creeks, to be completed by December 1993.
2. **Standardized Methodology**: Habitat data collection must strictly follow protocols already established by the USFWS/Pinetop Office in the Little Colorado River, with no unauthorized methodological deviation.
3. **Division of Responsibility**: Graduate researchers are tasked only with habitat data collection, not with directly determining humpback chub habitat requirements — that interpretive analysis falls to FWS/AFO.
4. **Bluehead Sucker Data Integration**: Habitat utilization data for *Catostomus discobolus* in Havasu and Shinumo Creek will be incorporated into a broader habitat model and compared against Little Colorado River data.
5. **Hydrological Characterization**: Where available, researchers will incorporate discharge and dendrological (tree-ring based hydrological) records to characterize tributary hydrology, though this was not an original research goal.
6. **Permit Constraints**: Existing AZCFWRU collecting permits prohibited electroshocking and tagging/marking, creating a significant methodological bottleneck; the group recommended permit modification to allow PIT tagging and standardized fin-marking.
7. **Inter-Agency Coordination Protocols**: GCES established formal requirements for permit coordination, correspondence sharing, and logistics support (PIT tagging equipment and river logistics) to be handled through designated GCES contacts (Alan Haden, Michael Yard).
8. **Conservation Linkage**: All proposed tributary studies are explicitly framed as foundational for [[Conservation Measure 7]] and broader endangered species research under the GCES program, supporting long-term recovery planning for the humpback chub in Grand and Glen Canyons.
