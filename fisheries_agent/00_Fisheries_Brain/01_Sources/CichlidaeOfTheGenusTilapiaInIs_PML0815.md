# Tilapia Reproductive Behaviour, Development and Hybridization in Israel

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scientific paper on the cichlid genus [[Tilapia]] in Israel, written by L. Fishelson of the Department of Zoology, Tel-Aviv University. It covers thermal limits on distribution, the two reproductive strategies (mouth-breeding and bottom-spawning), territorial and courtship behaviour, embryonic and larval development, the hypothesised evolutionary origin of mouth-breeding, and [[Interspecific Hybridization]] experiments aimed at aquaculture. The author concludes that the bottom-spawning ancestor gave rise to mouth-breeders and that hybrid Tilapia grow considerably faster than pure-bred fish.

## Background & Description

- **Family relationships:** Cichlids differ morphologically and physiologically from other Israeli freshwater families and are closely related to several marine Perciformes, notably the Pomacentridae. Shared traits include mating behaviour, territoriality, brood care and embryonic development.
- **Thermal biology:** Cichlids are tropical in origin. In *Tilapia*, minimum temperature for normal growth is above 16 °C and normal reproduction occurs above 22 °C. Below 16 °C the fish become inactive; at about 20 °C they begin breeding behaviour and colour change. The author proposes that this narrow thermal tolerance limited northward distribution into temperate zones.
- **Distribution history:** Israeli Tilapias likely spread from a tropical centre of origin during the Upper Miocene or Upper Pliocene, reaching near the northern limit of their range along the Jordan Valley and coastal plain, where low temperature is probably the limiting factor.
- **Reproductive groups:** The genus has two behavioural forms:
  - *Mouth-breeders* (e.g. [[Tilapia Nilotica]], [[Tilapia Aurea]]): females (or both sexes) take eggs into the mouth immediately after spawning and incubate them there.
  - *Bottom-spawners* (e.g. [[Tilapia Zillii]]): eggs are laid on the substrate and guarded, not mouthed.
- **Species descriptions:**
  - *T. zillii*: upper lip blueish, lower lip white, dark iris, black pelvic fins; males have elongated dorsal and anal fin tips. Prefers shallow, warm, vegetated water with stone and sand bottom.
  - *T. aurea*: blue body, lighter silvery abdomen, reddish fin margins, reddish eye crossed by a dark line. Was long confused with *T. nilotica*. Most common mouth-breeder in Israel.
  - *T. nilotica*: reddish colour, regular vertical white stripes on the caudal fin, red eye without a cross-line. Found in small numbers in coastal rivers such as the Yarkon.
  - *T. galilaea*: yellowish-silvery body, orange iris with a black line, black opercular spot (visible in young fish from about 3 cm). Distributed in all Israeli water bodies; intermediate between the two reproductive types.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Territoriality:** Breeding males are aggressive and defend territories. In open water a male defended about 5–6 m²; in plant-covered areas about 2–2.5 m². In dense ponds and aquaria, territories shrank to roughly 0.1–1.0 m². Surplus males could fail to secure territory and so did not reproduce.
- **Seasonal timing:** Males start reproductive behaviour when water rises above 20 °C. Near warm-water springs (Beth Shean, Lake Kinneret), reproduction began about one month earlier than in other locations.
- **Pairing and spawning:** Pairs form after days of display, and the bond can last days to weeks. Nests are dug by the male with fins and mouth. Spawning occurs in batches of 20–40 eggs in *T. aurea*, with clutches of 600–1,000+ eggs reported for bottom-spawning clusters.
- **Egg predation and competition:** Small *Haplochromis* and *T. zillii* can enter *T. galilaea* territories and eat eggs, which the author links to the low number and biomass of *T. galilaea* in mixed populations. Intensive stocking of Lake Kinneret with *T. aurea* fingerlings was followed by a decline in the *T. galilaea* yield.
- **Population dynamics:** *T. aurea* is described as more aggressive and reproductively more flexible than *T. galilaea*, and may increase at the expense of the latter when both occur together.
- **Salinity:** Hybrids also grew well in two fish ponds with high-salinity water near the Dead Sea.

## Key Findings

1. **Thermal limits:** Growth above 16 °C and reproduction above 22 °C; behavioural switch near 16–20 °C.
2. **Two reproductive strategies:** Mouth-breeding versus bottom-spawning, with *T. galilaea* showing intermediate traits (eggs left 10–15 minutes before mouthing, and mouthing by male, female, or both).
3. **Egg incubation requirement:** Mouth-breeder eggs must be rolled by jaw and throat movements; stationary eggs in experiments did not develop, whereas bottom-spawner eggs did.
4. **Brood size:** A mouth-breeding female may hold up to about 2,000 eggs; fry are released after about 14 days at 25–27 °C.
5. **Larval development:** Bottom-spawner larvae attach to substrate by cephalic adhesive glands for 3–5 days, then swim after 5–6 days; those glands disappear around day 6–8.
6. **Evolution:** Adhesive glands in mouth-breeder embryos are rudimentary and lost by day 4, suggesting mouth-breeders evolved from a bottom-spawning ancestor. The author proposes that prolonged "mouth-taking" in dense populations led to true mouth-keeping.
7. **Laboratory fecundity:** One female produced 11 clutches per year at Tel-Aviv University, with total egg output about 10 times the usual number.
8. **Hybrid growth:** *T. aurea* × *T. nilotica* F1 hybrids were all male in a sample of 700 and grew faster than pure-bred fish (600–650 g vs 400–450 g in one year in aquaria).
9. **Field trials:** Three years of Gan Shmuel pond observations matched laboratory results. Hybrid females also outgrew pure males. There was no significant wild reproduction of F1 hybrids.
10. **Commercial output:** Hybrids were reared alongside common carp at 4,000 per hectare without reducing carp growth. Gan Shmuel produced 600,000 hybrids in 1965/66 for sale to several farms.
11. **Earlier hybridization:** Hickling (1959) produced a hybrid generation of 98.7% males (9,870 fish) from two strains of *T. mossambica*, reported in the source as "9870 males".

**Note:** The source text contains a passage describing a hybrid generation of "9870 males", which appears to be a transcription artefact; the 98.7% figure is not stated in the source. Number cited as given.
