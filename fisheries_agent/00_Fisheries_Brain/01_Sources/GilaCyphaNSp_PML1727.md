# *Gila cypha*: A New Cyprinid from the Colorado River, Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes *Gila cypha*, a new species of cyprinid fish from the [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona. The holotype, a specimen about 305 mm standard length, was collected near [[Phantom Ranch]]. The author distinguishes the species from congeners mainly by its greatly reduced squamation, its prominent occipital hump, and the proportions of its [[Pharyngeal Arches]]. The paper was published in the *Journal of the Washington Academy of Sciences* (vol. 36, no. 12, December 15, 1946). The author links the scale loss to adaptation to swift-water habitat. The description is based on the holotype and two non-type specimens.

## Background & Description

The fish came to the author's attention in the Grand Canyon collection of the Colorado River. Study showed it to be of unusual interest for its adaptations to life in swift waters. The author notes that fewer than 12 nominal species in the genus *Gila* had been attributed to the Colorado River fauna by Jordan, Evermann, and Clark (1930).

**Diagnosis:** A strongly compressed [[Gila]] with a slightly convex body profile, a prominent abrupt hump over the occiput, and a body almost entirely devoid of scales. The lateral line has about 80 scales with basal radii. The fins are expansive and falcate, the snout is fleshy, the mouth is inferior, and the eye is very small.

**Type material:**
- **Holotype:** U.S.N.M. no. 131839, about 305 mm standard length. Collected by N. N. Dodge near Phantom Ranch, western Grand Canyon, taken on hook and line in swift water, presumably at or near the mouth of [[Bright Angel Creek]].
- **Non-type specimens:** U.S.N.M. no. 20083 (fair condition, no data, assigned on the basis that cyprinids of this type occur only in the Colorado basin; standard length about 315 mm) and U.S.N.M. no. 15349 (head, nape, and pectoral fins only, no locality, assigned on the basis of the occipital hump).

**Fin and meristic counts (holotype):**
- Dorsal 9; anal iii, 11; pectoral 18 in each fin; pelvic 9 in each fin
- Principal caudal rays 20 (18 branched, plus one full-length unbranched ray above and below)
- Lateral-line scales about 80
- Total gill rakers: 9 on the left side, 11 on the right; short and dimorphic
- Dental formula 2,5-4,2 as usual in *Gila*. The holotype has several teeth missing, and the author suggests the original formula may have been 2,5-4,2.

**Squamation:** Scales are embedded and only slightly imbricated anteriorly, becoming scarcely evident on the caudal peduncle. Back, breast, and belly are completely scaleless. The author notes that this degree of scale loss is approached only by *[[Gila Robusta Elegans]]* and, to a lesser extent, by *[[Gila Robusta Seminuda]]*. It is exceeded only by two unrelated genera, *Pimephales* and *Meda*, in which scales are usually absent.

**Pharyngeal arches:** Unusually small for a fish of this size. Left and right arch lengths are 21.8 mm and 22.7 mm, compared with 31.8 mm and 32.6 mm in the largest cotype of *[[Gila Robusta]]* of about the same length. The arches of *G. cypha* more closely resemble those of *G. r. elegans*. The ratio of least lower-limb width to total arch length is 0.9 (holotype) and 1.2 (U.S.N.M. no. 20083), compared with 1.5 and 1.4 in *elegans*.

**Coloration:** In formalin, the holotype was brownish-pinkish brown on the sides and belly and yellowish brown along the back. Dark puncticulations densely cover the head and dorsal sides and extend below the lateral line near the pectoral base and caudal fin.

**Morphometrics (holotype, ratios as given):** Body depth 4.25 in standard length; head length 4.1 in standard length; head depth 1.5 and head width 1.7 in head length; snout about 2.7 in head length; eye about 13.0 in head length; dorsal and anal bases each 1.6 in head length. Full values are in the paper's Table 1 and Figure 1.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author attributes the reduced squamation to adaptation to a swift-water habitat, which smooths body surfaces and reduces friction. Most of the associated Colorado River fishes also have streamlined bodies. Other fishes in the same habitat show partial scale loss, including [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]], [[Tiaroga Cobitis]], and [[Xyrauchen Texanus]]. This interpretation is the author's hypothesis and is not tested directly in the paper.

The author also notes that the teeth of cyprinids are modified during the fish's lifetime, which explains the wear and missing teeth in the holotype. Cyprinids of this type are known only from the Colorado River basin, which supports the assignment of the non-type specimens to the new species.

## Key Findings

- *Gila cypha* is described as a new species, distinguished by a prominent occipital hump, extensive scale loss (scaleless back, breast, and belly), and small pharyngeal arches.
- The holotype (U.S.N.M. no. 131839, about 305 mm SL) was collected near Phantom Ranch, Grand Canyon, from swift water.
- Two non-type specimens (U.S.N.M. nos. 20083 and 15349) are tentatively referred to the species, one by body form and arch proportions and one by the occipital hump.
- Of the squamation-reduced congeners, *G. cypha* is most closely approached by *G. robusta elegans*. Its arch proportions also resemble those of *elegans* more than those of *G. robusta*.
- The author links the scale reduction to adaptation for swift-water habitat, reducing friction on the body.
- Fewer than 12 nominal *Gila* species had previously been attributed to the Colorado River fauna, so this addition is significant for regional diversity.
