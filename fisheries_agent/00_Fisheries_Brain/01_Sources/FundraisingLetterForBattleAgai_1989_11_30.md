## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 fundraising letter authored by E. P. Pister, Executive Secretary of the **[[Desert Fishes Council]]**, soliciting donations to subsidize publication of the book *Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West* (University of Arizona Press; editors **[[W. L. Minckley]]** and **[[James E. Deacon]]**). The letter is not a primary scientific paper but a historical/organizational record documenting the activities, scale, and funding challenges of a multi-agency, multinational consortium dedicated to the conservation of threatened and endangered desert fish species in the American Southwest. It references the 20th and 21st annual **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** symposia, a site visit to the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, and a day-long symposium specifically addressing desert fish extinction crises and management strategies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The focal ecosystems are **desert aquatic habitats** of the American West — isolated springs, streams, and relict waters historically associated with pluvial lake systems — which host highly endemic, often single-locality fish species vulnerable to extinction.
- The **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]** (Dexter, New Mexico) is identified as a facility "devoted to the rearing of threatened and endangered fishes," indicating active *ex situ* conservation/propagation efforts for desert fish taxa.
- The breadth of participating agencies — **[[Bureau of Land Management]]**, **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]**, **[[Bureau of Reclamation]]**, **[[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]]**, **[[U.S. Geological Survey]]**, state fish and wildlife agencies, **[[The Nature Conservancy]]**, universities, and Indian tribes — reflects the multi-jurisdictional, fragmented nature of desert spring/stream habitat management, where water allocation, groundwater pumping, and habitat isolation are central threats to native fish persistence.
- The inclusion of a philosophical paper by **[[Holmes Rolston]]** (Colorado State University) on the ethics/value of desert fish conservation suggests the symposium framed the ecological niche issue within a broader conservation-ethics context, not purely biological management.

## Key Findings

- The **[[Desert Fishes Council]]**'s 21st annual symposium (Albuquerque, 1989) drew over 200 attendees from 37 universities (Mexico, U.S., Canada) and numerous federal/state agencies, underscoring the scale of collaborative desert fish conservation research.
- 40 research and management papers were presented at the 21st symposium; a field trip to the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]** highlighted applied propagation of threatened/endangered species.
- The 20th anniversary meeting (Death Valley, 1988) featured a dedicated day-long symposium, "**[[Native Fish Management in the American West: Battle Against Extinction]]**," comprising 20 papers spanning historical, technical, and philosophical perspectives on desert fish decline.
- These proceedings formed the basis for the book *Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West*, edited by **[[W. L. Minckley]]** and **[[James E. Deacon]]**, published by the **[[University of Arizona Press]]**.
- Publication economics were a major concern: unsubsidized retail price was projected near $100/book; the Council sought $8,000–$10,000 in donations to reduce the price to an affordable $45–55 range for students and field biologists.
- The Council's total annual operating budget was under $10,000 at the time, illustrating the resource constraints facing grassroots desert fish conservation organizations despite their broad institutional reach.
- The letter signals continuity of the organization's mission, referencing a planned 1990 meeting in **[[Ensenada]]**, Mexico, reflecting the binational (U.S.-Mexico) scope of desert fish conservation work.
