# Palaemonetes paludosus in the Rio Hardy and Colorado River, Baja California

#marine-biology #decapoda #palaemonetes

## Executive Summary

This short field note reports collections of the freshwater shrimp [[Palaemonetes Paludosus]] from the [[Rio Hardy]] and the combined flow of the Rio Hardy and the [[Colorado River]] in [[Baja California]], Mexico. The authors conclude that the species is well established in these reaches. The note also documents a 1958 introduction of the shrimp by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] into the lower Colorado River in California, where it is established but not abundant.

## Background & Description

The identification was tentative until confirmed by Allyn G. Smith (California Academy of Sciences) and Tom Cukr (Scripps Institution of Oceanography). The authors were James A. St. Amant and Robert G. Hulquist of the Inland Fisheries program, Region 5, California Department of Fish and Game. The note was accepted in March 1969.

Collections included adult males, females carrying eggs, and juveniles. Several successive spawnings were observed in freshwater aquaria at the Chino Fish and Wildlife Base over a period of several months.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Collection sites (1968):**
- Rio Hardy, 42 miles south of [[Mexicali]], at Del Prado Camp (March 11). Shrimp were collected in large numbers.
- Combined Rio Hardy and Colorado River flow, 60 miles south of Mexicali, at [[Vuelta La Corvina Camp]] (March 11 and May 6).
- Combined flow, 70 to 80 miles south of Mexicali, at Las Panzas Camp (March 11 and June 8).

**Water conditions:** A water sample at Vuelta La Corvina had a specific gravity of 1.020 at 23 °C. The shrimp occurred in waters also inhabited by marine shrimps and fishes.

**Habitat:** Large numbers were collected with a dip net in all areas where suitable cover was present, mainly [[Sago Pondweed]] (*Potamogeton pectinatus*).

**Introduction history:** In 1958 the California Department of Fish and Game introduced the species into the lower Colorado River (reference: Hayden, 1963). About 225 individuals were planted in a pond adjoining [[Lake Havasu]], and 1,000 were planted in three ponds in the [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]]. The species is now established but not abundant in parts of the Colorado River north of the Mexican border to [[Davis Dam]].

## Key Findings

- *Palaemonetes paludosus* was collected in large numbers at three sites in the Rio Hardy and the combined Rio Hardy–Colorado River flow south of Mexicali in 1968.
- All three areas held marine shrimps and fishes alongside the freshwater shrimp. One water sample had a specific gravity of 1.020 at 23 °C.
- Adults, ovigerous females, and juveniles were present, and several successive spawnings occurred over several months in captivity.
- The authors conclude the species is well established south of the border, where sago pondweed provides cover.
- The 1958 stocking (about 225 individuals in one pond and 1,000 in three ponds) has resulted in an established but not abundant population north of the border, along the Colorado River to Davis Dam.
