## Executive Summary

This short field report, issued from the [[Fish Cultural Laboratory]], describes a survey of [[Channel Catfish]] in the [[Colorado River]] near [[Parker Dam]]. The document is a partially damaged scan, so several passages are incomplete. Its clearest findings are that adult channel catfish occur in the study area, that juveniles are rare and probably drift in from [[Drain Canals]], and that a single small side bay held an unusually dense concentration of adults, many of them ripe females in good condition.

## Background & Description

The survey was conducted by a field branch of the laboratory. The text describes the work as a study of the Colorado River fishery, with fish located through several sampling methods. The report notes that the channel contains an extensive channel catfish population, with a normal distribution of age classes, although the scan does not give the sampling gear or dates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Age structure:** Adult channel catfish dominate the main channel. Only two juvenile fish were captured.
- **Origin of juveniles:** The author attributes the juveniles to [[Drain Canals]], because they were small and found near the confluence of drains with the main river. This suggests that the main-river population is not reproducing strongly.
- **Reproduction:** A minor reproducing population is reported to exist in the river, though the surrounding text is illegible.
- **Concentration:** The report states that the concentration of fish in one small area could not be fully explained by migration alone. It points to [[Spawning Migration]] as a likely contributing factor.

## Key Findings

1. **Side-bay sample:** A sample from a 1.5-acre side bay (OCR read "1 \/2-acre") produced 92 adult channel catfish weighing approximately 285 pounds in total.
2. **Sex composition:** Of the 92 fish, only five were males. The remaining fish were females at various stages of maturity.
3. **Spawning condition:** Thirty of the adult females were ripe. The author describes the fish as being in excellent body condition, with well-developed, normally colored gonads.
4. **Interpretation:** The author could not fully explain the heavy concentration in this one small area other than through migration combined with spawning-related aggregation.
5. **Juvenile scarcity:** Only two juvenile fish were captured across the study area, and both are believed to have come from drain canals.
6. **Study scope:** The survey covered roughly 25 miles of the Colorado River. The page 2 scan contains no legible text.
