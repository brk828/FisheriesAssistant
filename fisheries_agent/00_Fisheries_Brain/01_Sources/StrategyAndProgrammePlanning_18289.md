# Station Biologique de la Tour du Valat: Strategy and Programme Planning 1992–1996

#ichthyology #marine-biology #waterbirds

## Executive Summary

This strategy document, prepared by the [[Station Biologique de la Tour du Valat]] (Tour du Valat), sets out the mission, research agenda, partnerships, training, awareness and internal structure of the Station for 1992–1996. The mission, redefined by the Board in February 1991, is "to halt and reverse the destruction and degradation of Mediterranean wetlands and their natural resources." The document is mainly a planning and policy text rather than a research report. Its ichthyological content is limited: a fish monitoring and research programme in the [[Camargue]], the [[Prespa Project]], an endemic fish study, and a fish-related component of [[Eutrophication]] and [[Aquaculture]] assessments. Most of the agenda concerns waterbirds, especially [[Greater Flamingo]] and [[Dalmatian Pelican]] populations, and the broader socio-economic and policy context of wetland loss. The plan states that the programme is probably too ambitious for available human and financial resources.

## Background & Description

The Station was established in 1954 by Dr. Luc Hoffmann as a private research institute, initially for field ornithology, and later expanded into invertebrate and plant ecology, soils, hydrology and hydrochemistry. In 1978 the Station and 1,500 ha of estate passed to the [[Fondation Sansouire]] (created 1976 under French law). In 1983 the Petit Badon estate was added, bringing the total to 1,850 ha. The central 1,100 ha were recognised as a Réserve Naturelle Volontaire in 1984.

Core funding comes mainly from the [[Fondation Tour du Valat]] (Swiss law, created 1974), which promotes research on wetland communities in the Camargue and around the Mediterranean. Project funding comes from the European Commission, French Ministry of the Environment, WWF, Fondation de France, regional governments and the Conservatoire du Littoral et des Rivages Lacustres.

The 1986–1991 programme covered:
- ecology and management of fish and invertebrates
- plant ecology and management
- biogeochemistry and nutrient cycles
- breeding biology of flamingoes
- fundamental processes maintaining wetland ecosystems

The document lists staff by discipline (avian, plant and fish ecology, plus management, statistics, library and administration). It also names associated scientists, scientific advisers and consultants, and the boards of the two foundations. These lists are summarised here rather than reproduced.

The strategy was developed in the context of the [[Grado Declaration]] (Grado, Italy, February 1991). That meeting, convened by the [[International Waterfowl and Wetlands Research Bureau]] (IWRB) with about 280 participants from 28 countries, set the goal "to stop and reverse the loss and degradation of Mediterranean wetlands." Twelve Mediterranean states were then Contracting Parties to the [[Ramsar Convention]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document identifies several features of Mediterranean wetlands:
- Long human settlement has affected almost all remaining wetlands, and artificialisation is widespread and growing.
- Wetlands are mainly valued as habitat for migratory and breeding waterbirds. Their roles as fish, plant and invertebrate habitat, and in groundwater recharge, flood attenuation, water purification and food-chain support, receive less recognition.
- Irrigation policy places heavy demands on limited water resources. Summer tourism multiplies resident populations and intensifies pressure on water.
- Laws to protect wetlands exist but are not adequately enforced.
- Population growth and urban expansion are strongest in the east and south of the region.

Local significance is high. The Tour du Valat–Petit Badon estate lies on the edge of the more saline [[Réserve Nationale de Camargue]] and includes freshwater and brackish marshes, notably St Seren and the Baisse Salée. These hold 10,000–15,000 wintering waterbirds and, in cold winters, up to 20% of the c. 100,000 waterfowl in the Camargue.

On eutrophication, the document reports that agricultural and urban nutrient inputs cause periods of severe anoxia and dystrophic crises in advanced stages. Consequences include fish kills, disappearance of fish species, decline in fisheries, bans on leisure activities and reduced drinking water quality. Tour du Valat has been invited to join an interdisciplinary programme on dystrophic crises in southern French lagoons.

Temporary oligohaline marshes in Mediterranean deltas are described as unique, rare and highly adapted to salinity and flooding cycles. They provide feeding grounds for ducks, herons and flamingoes, and their food value is considered to have been underestimated. Vegetation development follows three pathways: terrestrial plants, emergent macrophytes (Scirpus, Phragmites, Typha), and submerged macrophytes. Fish and invertebrate predation varies with these scenarios, which in turn alters food for birds.

## Key Findings

### Fish and aquatic research

- **Camargue fish monitoring:** The programme includes testing a predator–prey hypothesis in the [[Fumemorte]] by rebuilding the [[Pikeperch]] population through stocking. Sampling is monthly but only every three years.
- **Prespa Project:** Planned to end in 1996, this study links nine years of catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) data with environmental factors, fish-eating birds and pelican breeding success. The hypothesis is that pelicans are not limited by fish numbers. A secondary aim is to examine the fate of introduced exotic species.
- **Endemic fish species:** Phase I was to finish early in 1993. Phases II and III depend on funding.
- **Coto Doñana:** Participation in a University of Córdoba project on fishes of the Coto Doñana marshes.
- **Aquaculture and lagoon fisheries:** An evaluation is proposed to test whether aquaculture can offset declines in traditional catches, to assess employment and environmental consequences, and to establish the true costs and benefits of aquaculture.

### Waterbirds

- **Herons:** Data from 30 nesting sites in southern France, Spain, Algeria, Tunisia, Israel, Greece and Italy show freshwater habitat area and quality are the main limits on colony size and diversity. A threshold of 800 ha is identified, below which only very small colonies exist. Camargue colony data from 1968–1991 are to be analysed with remote-sensing imagery.
- **Greater Flamingo:** Listed in Annex 1 of EC Directive 79/409. A 1990 Antequera seminar recommended IWRB-coordinated annual censuses in mid-January and May, ring-reading surveys, and joint analysis of France and Spain data as one dataset. Salines loss is a particular concern.
- **Dalmatian Pelican:** An international programme since 1983 is considered to have halted the decline, with encouraging signs of increase. Long-term security still requires more data on breeding, migration and wintering. A draft Action Plan is due in 1994, implementation is planned for 1995–1996, and a review is scheduled for the end of 1996. About two-thirds of the world population of the species breeds in the USSR.
- **Atlas of Breeding Colonial Waterbirds:** A Mediterranean-wide census is planned, with a team leader in each country.

### Resources, restoration and policy

- **Integrated management:** Field projects are planned at El Kala (Algeria), Göksu Delta (Turkey, a Special Protection Area), Vigueirat Marshes (Camargue) and Tour du Valat–Petit Badon. A cross-site evaluation of management-plan effectiveness is proposed for later in the plan period.
- **Lake Karla (Greece):** Restoration is widely supported locally, but the Station will only engage on invitation from the Ministry of Environment and/or the Prefects of Volos and Larissa.
- **Hunting and tourism:** Both are flagged as needing external funding. The document notes that no Mediterranean studies yet quantify waterfowl responses to disturbance levels.
- **Salt industry:** Investigation of salines' production, viability and ownership is proposed, given their importance for flamingoes and Artemia.
- **Policy influence:** The Station will work through partners (IWRB, WWF, IUCN, ICBP) rather than lobbying governments directly. The Mediterranean Wetlands Forum is a priority.

### Monitoring and training

- Long-term monitoring (Table 1) covers flamingoes, herons, egrets, waterfowl, laro-limicoles, herring gulls, water levels and salinity, boars, hunting statistics, vegetation and seedbank germination, and fish. Surveys run from weekly to triennial.
- Training includes up to three PhD studentships per year (mostly for Mediterranean nationals), producing six to nine young researchers. Small grants are normally below 5,000 FF.
- The annual activity report, due by 31 March each year, is for internal use only.

### Organisational assessment (SWOT)

- **Strengths:** An experienced, credible research team; the long-term studies; the network of contacts; Dr. Luc Hoffmann's leadership and the independence it gives the Station.
- **Weaknesses:** Lack of integration between researchers and groups, limited long-term strategic planning, small size, and weak communication with local and wider scientific communities.
- **Opportunities:** Applying research results, freedom to choose work, and substantial external finance.
- **Threats:** Over-ambition that over-stretches resources, becoming an out-station of a larger body, and drift into consultancy and superficiality.
