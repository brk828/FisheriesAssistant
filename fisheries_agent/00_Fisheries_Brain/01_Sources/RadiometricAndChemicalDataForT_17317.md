# Radiometric and Chemical Data for Rocks of the Tortolita Mountains 15' Quadrangle, Pinal County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #geology-not-fish

> Note: this document is a geological data report, not a study of fish or aquatic organisms. The tags above reflect the required format, but the content is entirely about rock geochronology and geochemistry.

## Executive Summary

This report (Banks, McKee, Keith, Shafiqullah and Damon; Isochron/West no. 22, August 1978) presents potassium-argon (K-Ar) ages and rapid-rock chemical analyses for Precambrian basement, middle Tertiary volcanic and clastic rocks, and a middle Tertiary [[Metamorphic Core Complex]] in the Tortolita Mountains quadrangle. Five K-Ar ages are reported on biotite and hornblende: one Precambrian (1438 ± 10 m.y.) and the rest Tertiary (23.5 to 26.7 m.y.). Chemical and normative data are given for five samples (Table 1).

## Background & Description

**Geologic framework (from reconnaissance mapping by the [[US Geological Survey]] and [[University of Arizona]]):**
- The quadrangle is underlain mainly by Precambrian-Y [[Oracle Granite]] (Peterson, 1938), Tertiary volcanic and sedimentary rocks, at least one metamorphic core complex, and unconsolidated Quaternary deposits.
- The Oracle Granite contains pendants of Precambrian-X [[Pinal Schist]] and is cut by tourmaline-bearing pegmatite dikes and Precambrian-Y diabase dikes. The pegmatite and diabase also cut quartz diorite in the central quadrangle.
- The quartz diorite resembles the Precambrian [[Madera Diorite]] (1630-1660 m.y.), but its contact with the Oracle Granite was not observed.
- Tertiary volcanic and clastic deposits overlie basement across the northern two-thirds of the quadrangle and are intricately faulted and broadly folded.

**Generalized middle Tertiary section (Banks and others, 1977):**
- Basal clastic units: 0-65 m thick.
- Basal volcanic rock (about 150 m): basalt or trachyandesite flows, dense to amygdaloidal.
- Overlying 800+ m: flows and flow breccias of phenocryst-poor quartz latite, rhyodacite, and possibly rhyolite, dacite and trachyte.
- At least 900 m of middle Tertiary clastic deposits above the volcanics; basal 100-300 m dominated by volcanic clasts, upper several hundred meters by granitic and metamorphic clasts. Alkali basalt flows (21.0 ± 0.5 m.y.; Jennison, 1976) lie about 700-800 m above the base of the clastics.

**Analytical methods:**
- Rock chemistry by the rapid-rock methods of Shapiro and Brannock (1962) and Shapiro (1975); analysts Z. A. Hamlin and F. Brown.
- K-Ar ages for TMN-93, TMN-62 and TMN-92 were done at USGS Menlo Park (argon by isotope dilution; Nier-type 60°-sector, 15.24-cm mass spectrometer in static mode; potassium by lithium metaborate fusion flame photometry; analyst G. Ambats).
- Samples UAK 75-86 and UAK 75-87 were analyzed in duplicate at the University of Arizona (similar mass spectrometer; potassium by atomic absorption).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Not applicable in the ecological sense. The setting is the Tortolita Mountains and adjacent features (The Huerfano, Chief Butte, Owl Head Buttes, Desert Peak, Durham Hills, Suizo Mountains) in Pinal County, Arizona, with Quaternary basin fill, sheetwash and alluvium surrounding bedrock outcrops. Regional comparisons are made to [[Picacho Peak]] (12 km west), the [[Vaca Hills]] (50 km south of Picacho Peak), the [[Tucson Mountains]], and the larger [[Santa Catalina-Rincon Complex]] to the southeast. Sample localities fall around 32°30'-32°39'N, 111°00'-111°11'W.

## Key Findings

**K-Ar ages (all biotite unless noted):**

| Sample | Rock | Age |
|---|---|---|
| TMN-93 | Quartz diorite (Precambrian) | 1438 ± 10 m.y. |
| TMN-62 | Quartz latite flow, The Huerfano | hornblende 25.1 ± 0.4 m.y.; biotite 23.5 ± 0.4 m.y. |
| TMN-92 | Quartz latite flow, Chief Butte | 26.7 ± 0.5 m.y. |
| UAK 75-86 | Porphyritic sphene-bearing granodiorite (pluton of Chirreon Wash) | 25.1 ± 0.5 m.y. |
| UAK 75-87 | Quartz monzonite porphyry dike (Bass Canyon) | 24.0 ± 0.5 m.y. |

**Interpretations:**
- **Quartz diorite (TMN-93):** its age is indistinguishable from that of the Oracle Granite (1420-1460 m.y.), so it cannot resolve their relation. Two interpretations: (1) a large pendant of Madera or similar diorite reheated to several hundred degrees Centigrade during granite intrusion, or (2) a pluton of Oracle Granite age. The sample site was not appreciably heated by the metamorphic core complex 6 km south or by similar rock in the Suizo Mountains 4 km northwest.
- **Volcanic sequence:** the Huerfano sample shows slightly discordant ages (hornblende older than biotite), possibly reflecting heating from an extensive middle Tertiary thermal, metamorphic and plutonic event. The Chief Butte flow (26.7 m.y.) was collected slightly above the middle of the volcanic section.
- **Chemistry caution:** TMN-56 (Owl Head Buttes, petrographically identical to TMN-92) suggests possible trachytes or potassic quartz latites, but devitrification, clay and sericite alteration, and calc-alkaline rhyodacite of the same age 50 km west mean regional generalizations should be made cautiously.
- **Regional correlation:** the western volcanic sequence near Picacho Peak and the Vaca Hills is much thicker than the dated alkali basalt and is not underlain by older (about 25 m.y.) volcanics correlative to those of the Tortolita quadrangle.
- **Core complex:** the pluton of Chirreon Wash (25.1 m.y.) is the oldest pluton involved in the cataclastic metamorphism; the youngest postfoliation dike type (quartz monzonite porphyry, 24.0 m.y.) shows only a small age difference from the deformed rock, a pattern also noted in the Santa Catalina-Rincon complex. At least three postfoliation dike types cut the metamorphic rocks; one mafic dike (TMN-58, Durham Hills) is less mafic than the alkali basalt flows.

**Selected analytical data:**
- TMN-93: K2O = 8.99 percent; radiogenic Ar40 = 2.8458 x 10^-10 mole/gm; 99.1 percent radiogenic.
- TMN-62 hornblende: K2O = 1.022 percent, 49.9 percent radiogenic Ar; biotite: K2O = 8.62 percent, 59.5 percent.
- TMN-92: K2O = 8.43 percent; 51.4 percent radiogenic Ar.
- UAK 75-86: K2O = 8.722 percent; 80.9 percent radiogenic Ar. UAK 75-87: K2O = 9.011 percent; 86.6 percent.

**Chemical data (Table 1, OCR-degraded; values approximate):** five samples (TMN-56, TMN-83E, TMN-93, TMJ-28, TMN-58) with major-oxide and normative mineral values. SiO2 ranges from roughly 49 to 75 percent, with the mafic dike and Chirreon Wash border-facies sample (TMJ-28) near 49 percent and the more silicic samples near 67-75 percent. Several individual values are unreliable from the scan and are not reproduced.
