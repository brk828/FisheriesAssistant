# Stocking of Sport Fish Species (Rainbow Trout and Channel Catfish) in the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #conservation-biology #Salmonidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 Draft [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services State Office) under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It evaluates the ecological consequences of stocking hatchery-raised, catchable-size (8–10 inch) [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Channel Catfish]] into the Lower Colorado River (from Hoover Dam to the International Boundary) for recreational fishing purposes. The central finding is that stocking of non-native Rainbow Trout is likely to **jeopardize** the continued existence of two endangered native fish species — the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail]] — and adversely modify their designated [[Critical Habitat]]. In contrast, stocking of Channel Catfish into isolated, non-connected waters was found **not** to jeopardize these species. Two Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs) were proposed to mitigate jeopardy from trout stocking, primarily by restricting or reintroducing stocking practices in [[Lake Mohave]].

## Background & Description
The document is primarily regulatory/ecological rather than anatomical, but it specifies key identifying and management-relevant morphological traits:

- **Rainbow Trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)**: Stocked at a "catchable size" of **8–10 inches**, indicating sub-adult hatchery-raised fish sourced from the Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
- **Channel Catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*)**: Also stocked at catchable size; introduced only into isolated waters lacking direct hydrological connection to the mainstem river.
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: Named for its distinctive dorsal keel/ridge behind the head (a diagnostic feature of the [[Catostomidae]] family), though not elaborated upon in this text.
- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)**: A member of the [[Cyprinidae]] family (genus *Gila*), native to the Lower Colorado River Basin; specific morphology not detailed in this administrative document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Endangered Species**: The [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] occupy critical habitat in [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]], and the Colorado River reach between [[Parker Dam]] and [[Imperial Dam]]. These species represent remnant, declining populations of the historic native fish assemblage of the Lower Colorado River.
- **Introduced Predator Dynamic**: Stocked [[Rainbow Trout]] function as a non-native competitor/predator-prey link within the reservoir ecosystem. The opinion notes that [[Striped Bass]] (also non-native) prey heavily on stocked rainbow trout, indicating a complex multi-species predation web unique to this altered reservoir system.
- **Predation Pressure on Native Fish**: While Rainbow Trout are implicated in the decline of Razorback Sucker and Bonytail populations, the report explicitly states they are **not the sole non-native species** responsible—implying a broader guild of introduced predators (e.g., striped bass, other sport fish) contribute to suppression of native fish recruitment and survival.
- **Habitat Compartmentalization**: Channel Catfish stocking is ecologically isolated by design—placed only in backwaters/ponds without direct connectivity to the mainstem river, thus limiting niche overlap with native endangered fish and reducing pathways for negative interaction.
- **Reservoir vs. Riverine Habitat**: The critical habitat spans both lentic (reservoir: Lake Mohave, Lake Havasu) and lotic (riverine: Parker Dam to Imperial Dam) environments, reflecting the dual habitat requirements of these native Colorado River endemics.

## Key Findings
- **Jeopardy Determination (Rainbow Trout)**: The continuing program to stock Rainbow Trout from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] is likely to jeopardize the razorback sucker and bonytail and adversely modify their designated critical habitat.
- **No Jeopardy Determination (Channel Catfish)**: Stocking Channel Catfish in isolated, non-connected waters is **not likely** to jeopardize either endangered species or destroy/adversely modify critical habitat, provided monitoring continues.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs)**:
  - **RPA 1**: Prohibits all Rainbow Trout stocking in Lake Mohave; permits stocking elsewhere in the action area.
  - **RPA 2**: Permits limited Rainbow Trout stocking in the upper portion of Lake Mohave and other approved sites; includes an aggressive **reintroduction program** for Razorback Sucker and Bonytail to augment declining wild populations.
  - Both RPAs mandate a research/monitoring program studying stocking effects and native species biology.
- **Management Tension**: The opinion acknowledges the socio-economic importance of the trout stocking program to recreational fishing and to the mission of Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery, creating a direct conflict between recreational fisheries management and endangered species conservation mandates.
- **Incidental Take Statement**: The level of anticipated incidental take could not be quantitatively estimated at the time; compliance is instead defined by adherence to the RPA and associated terms/conditions.
- **Conservation Recommendations** (discretionary): Recommended evaluation of the broader sport fishing program on the lower Colorado River and exploration of **alternative stocking sites** for Willow Beach-produced Rainbow Trout to reduce range overlap with endangered native fish.
- **Multi-Agency Coordination**: Concurrent Section 7 consultation was anticipated with the State of Nevada regarding its own independent Rainbow Trout stocking of Lake Mohave, indicating cross-jurisdictional conservation coordination needs.
- **Species of Concern Taxonomy**: Confirms scientific nomenclature — [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) — both federally listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
