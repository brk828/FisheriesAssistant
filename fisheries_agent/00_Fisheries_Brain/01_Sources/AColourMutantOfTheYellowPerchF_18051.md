# A Colour Mutant of the Yellow Perch from Lake Erie

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae #freshwater-biology #colour-mutation

## Executive Summary

This 1962 note by E. J. Crossman documents a single anomalously coloured specimen of [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*) collected from [[Lake Erie]] near Point Pelee, Ontario. The specimen displayed a bright orange ground colour in place of the typical yellow-to-yellow-green, and completely lacked the species' characteristic dark vertical bars. Aside from pigmentation, the fish was morphologically typical of *P. flavescens*, including a standard fin ray formula, leading the author to conclude it represented a **colour mutant** rather than a hybrid or distinct taxon.

## Background & Description

- **Specimen origin**: Collected on 4 June 1962 by Wm. Krause in a pound net set six miles north of the tip of Point Pelee, on the west side, in [[Lake Erie]].
- **Provenance**: Forwarded to the Royal Ontario Museum (R.O.M. Cat. No. 21833) by Dr. H. Regier of the Research Division, Ontario Department of Lands and Forests, Wheatley, Ontario.
- **Condition**: Shipped frozen/on ice; arrived in very fresh condition. Documented via Ektachrome slide and colour notes/sketch made after thawing.

### Colouration Details (Fresh Specimen)
- **Dorsum, sides (above an irregular wavy line below lateral line), top of head**: Solid, bright, transparent orange — matched to Villalobos Colour Atlas reference **017-12°**.
- **Lower sides/ventral surface**: Milky white.
- **Head markings**: Black speckle patches between eyes and between nares; black mid-dorsal line from occiput to origin of spiny dorsal (same speckled composition).
- **Back/sides**: Finely speckled with black; some areas darker due to speckle coalescence.
- **Diagnostic absence**: No vestiges of the typical broad, dark green vertical bars — present neither fresh nor after formalin preservation.
- **Cheek/lower operculum**: Brilliant metallic-silver, mirror-like; upper 1/5 of operculum orange.
- **Fins**:
  - First dorsal spine: black.
  - Subsequent spines: orange-to-yellow bases/tips, clear in between.
  - First interspinous membrane: totally black; others show a black terminal band decreasing in depth posteriorly.
  - Soft dorsal: first ray orange; other ray tips orange/yellow with black speckling; black-bordered appearance on dorsal edge; orange patch impression at upper rear edge.
  - Caudal fin: ray tips pigmented black (rear edge appears black); ray bases orange/yellow; membrane unpigmented.
  - Pectoral fin rays: orange tips with black specks.
  - Pelvic fins: mid-section of first ray deep orange; otherwise clear.
  - Anal fin: central third of each ray (2nd ray onward, excluding last two) bright orange; membrane clear.
- **Eyes**: Pupil black; upper crescent of sclera orange and speckled with black.
- **Jaws**: Black pigment patches at tips of upper and lower jaws.
- **Skeletal features**: Preopercular and subopercular bones typically serrated (normal for species).

### Post-Preservation Changes
- After **10% formalin** preservation for 2.5 days, most orange pigment disappeared, leaving a colourless body retaining only the black speckling pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Captured in a pound net in the nearshore/littoral waters of [[Lake Erie]], a Great Lakes freshwater ecosystem — habitat typical for [[Yellow Perch]] populations.
- **Population context**: Only a single individual of this colour type was observed in the catch; the collector (Mr. Krause) reported never having seen this form before, suggesting **extreme rarity** of this phenotype within the local population.
- **Taxonomic implication**: The normal body shape, meristic counts (fin formula), and skeletal features matching typical *P. flavescens* argue against this being a [[Hybridization|hybrid]] between yellow perch and another species — supporting the interpretation of a spontaneous **colour mutation** rather than introgression.

## Key Findings

1. A single anomalous *Perca flavescens* specimen from Lake Erie exhibited **bright orange body colouration** (Villalobos Atlas 017-12°) replacing the normal yellow/yellow-green ground colour.
2. The specimen completely **lacked the diagnostic dark vertical bars** typical of yellow perch, both fresh and post-preservation.
3. Black pigmentation was present as **discrete speckle patterns** on the head, dorsum, fin rays, and fin membranes, differing qualitatively from the typical banding pattern.
4. All meristic (fin ray counts) and morphological features (body shape, serrated preopercular/subopercular bones) were **consistent with typical *P. flavescens***, ruling out obvious hybrid identity.
5. Formalin preservation caused **near-total loss of orange pigmentation**, leaving only the black speckle pattern — an important note for future museum specimen interpretation.
6. The rarity of the phenotype (a single fish, never previously observed by an experienced local fisherman) supports classification as a naturally occurring **colour mutant** in the wild population.
