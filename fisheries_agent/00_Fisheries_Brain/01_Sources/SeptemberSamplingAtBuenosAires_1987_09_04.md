# September 1987 Sampling at Buenos Aires NWR

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #Ictaluridae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary

This field report documents a September 1987 survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], Arizona, with two primary objectives: (1) assessing stock status and growth of reintroduced [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations across multiple ponds, and (2) evaluating the efficacy of October 1986 ichthyocide treatments intended to eradicate non-native fishes from three impoundments. Results confirmed survival and substantial growth of razorback sucker stocked in November 1986 and March 1987, while reclamation efforts showed mixed success — non-native [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]] were successfully extirpated, but [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]] persisted despite treatment.

## Background & Description

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Mormon Lake (August 1987 seine/trammel net sample):** Total length (TL) range 71–122 mm; small size suggests either sampling bias toward smaller individuals or factors limiting growth/survival, as mean length appeared to decrease relative to a February 1987 assessment.
- **Mormon Lake (supplemental netting):** 9 individuals, TL 268–311 mm (mean 290 mm), attributed to the November 1986 stocking cohort. All fish were described as **robust and healthy**, confirming ~10 months of survival with substantial growth.
- **Rock Tank:** 107 individuals collected in 3 seine hauls; TL 38–172 mm (mean 113 mm). Fish in excellent condition.
- **Tequila Tank:** 102 individuals collected in 6 seine hauls; TL 73–176 mm (mean 126 mm). Fish in excellent condition.

### Non-Native Species (Post-Reclamation Survivors)
- **Black Bullhead** (*Ameiurus melas*, [[Airport Tank #3]]): Abundant population, all individuals <100 mm TL.
- **Yellow Bullhead** (*Ameiurus catus*, [[State Tank]]): Moderate population, all individuals <150 mm TL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Mormon Lake]]:** Recipient of razorback sucker fry (November 1986) and later fry stocking (March 1987); supports cohort-based growth assessment.
- **[[Rock Tank]]:** Circular impoundment, ~20 m diameter; moderate *Spirogyra* algal growth; common adult ranid frogs; abundant aquatic insects. Stocked with 30,000 razorback sucker fry in March 1987.
- **[[Tequila Tank]]:** Circular impoundment, ~40 m diameter; abundant adult ranid frogs and larval *Ambystoma* (tiger salamander). Also stocked with 30,000 razorback sucker fry in March 1987.
- **[[Little Mormon Lake]]:** Treated with ichthyocide October 1986; previously hosted fathead minnow and mosquitofish. Post-treatment survey found no fish but abundant larval/adult aquatic insects and crustaceans — indicating successful eradication and recovering invertebrate community.
- **[[Airport Tank #3]]:** Treated October 1986; supported black bullhead both before and after poisoning, but mosquitofish (previously abundant) were eliminated.
- **[[State Tank]]:** Treated October 1986; supported yellow bullhead before and after poisoning, but fathead minnow and mosquitofish (previously abundant) were eliminated.

## Key Findings

1. **Confirmed growth and survival:** Razorback sucker from the November 1986 stocking at Mormon Lake grew to 268–311 mm TL within ~10 months, demonstrating strong post-stocking survival and growth rates under refuge pond conditions.
2. **Possible sampling bias or differential mortality:** The August 1987 Mormon Lake sample (71–122 mm TL) showed an apparent decrease in mean length compared to February 1987 data, suggesting either a sampling artifact favoring smaller fish or an unidentified factor limiting growth/survival of the March 1987 fry cohort.
3. **High fry-to-juvenile survival in tank habitats:** Both [[Rock Tank]] and [[Tequila Tank]], despite only partial-habitat sampling, yielded over 100 razorback suckers each from 30,000 fry stocked in March 1987, indicating substantial, though unquantified, standing populations.
4. **Ichthyocide treatment was effective against cyprinids and poeciliids:** Fathead minnow and mosquitofish were successfully extirpated from all three treated ponds ([[Little Mormon Lake]], [[Airport Tank #3]], [[State Tank]]).
5. **Ichthyocide treatment failed against ictalurids:** Both black bullhead and yellow bullhead persisted post-treatment, reaffirming the well-documented difficulty of eradicating bullhead species with standard piscicide protocols.
6. **Management recommendation:** Future reclamation efforts targeting bullhead-infested waters should incorporate pumping ponds down to the lowest practicable water level prior to toxicant application to improve eradication efficacy.
7. **Site suitability for conservation aquaculture:** [[Airport Tank #3]] and [[State Tank]] were identified as strong candidates for future native fish stocking, grow-out, and subsequent translocation to reintroduction sites, owing to uniform morphometry and good site access.
8. **Invertebrate community persistence:** Aquatic insects and crustaceans remained abundant in treated ponds even where fish were fully extirpated, suggesting the ichthyocide treatment was relatively selective or that invertebrate recovery was rapid.
