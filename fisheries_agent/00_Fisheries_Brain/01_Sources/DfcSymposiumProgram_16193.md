# Desert Fish Conservation: Institutional Roles in Captive Breeding, Research, and Public Education

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontiformes

## Executive Summary

This document has two parts. Page 1 to 3 is a letter dated 24 November 1992 from Paul V., Curator of Freshwater Fishes at the [[New York Zoological Society]] / [[New York Aquarium]], to [[William L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University. The letter follows up on the 1992 [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC) Symposium and sets out a proposed role for the [[AAZPA Desert Fishes Working Group]] in [[North American Desert Fishes]] conservation. Page 5 is a brochure for the [[Aquatic Conservation Network]] (ACN), a freshwater fish conservation initiative focused on amateur aquarists. Page 4 is inverted and largely unreadable OCR text, so its content is not summarized here.

The letter argues that habitat preservation is the best conservation option, but captive breeding may be the only immediate response when a species faces imminent extirpation. It also argues that AAZPA member institutions should not lead hatchery-based breeding of salmonids or large riverine fishes. Instead, they should focus on three contributions: long-term captive husbandry of small desert fishes, applied laboratory research, and [[Conservation Education]].

## Background & Description

**Letter (pp. 1–3).** The letter was written to thank Dr. Minckley for a returned copy of the 1992 DFC Symposium Program, which had been left in a hotel restaurant after a working lunch the previous Saturday. The writer notes that Dr. Minckley had signed the cover and that the publication contained inserts. The letter also encloses an ACN brochure and states that Rob Huntley would send a fuller information packet.

The letter addresses several topics:

- **Public perception:** The writer reports that a substantial number of government-sector professionals hold a serious misperception of AAZPA goals and activities. [[Paul Reinhardt]] and [[David Kuehn]] reportedly reached the same conclusion after informal conversations with symposium participants.
- **[[IUCN]] request:** The IUCN is expected to ask for a [[Species Survival Program]] for these fishes. The writer requests input from Dr. Minckley, given his familiarity with the agencies involved.
- **Conservation philosophy:** The writer's stated aim is "maximizing options while minimizing regrets."
- **Genetic management:** AAZPA terrestrial vertebrate coordinators have begun planning for maintenance of genetic diversity in captive populations over periods of "fifty to two hundred years."

**ACN brochure (p. 5).** The ACN sets three immediate objectives for freshwater fish species:
1. **Networking:** a worldwide communications mechanism linking amateur and professional aquarists.
2. **Captive Breeding:** a formal program for amateur participation in breeding endangered freshwater fishes, in cooperation with existing professional and amateur programs.
3. **Conservation:** mechanisms for amateur participation in field conservation issues.

The ACN has a Steering Committee of scientific, hobby, and industry authorities. Its stated goals include establishing communication mechanisms, ensuring sound scientific principles, obtaining endorsement from professional groups, and building cooperative relationships with aquarist organizations worldwide. The brochure invites volunteers (administration, research, writing, translation, communications, data management, fundraising, legal advice) and financial supporters. Annual membership is listed as $25 (Cdn. or U.S.), and the membership form returns to Ottawa, Ontario.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat as the primary conservation tool:** The letter identifies secure and preserved viable habitat as the best conservation approach. It notes that, given conditions in the intermountain west and northern Mexico, habitat protection "is not always viable in the near term."
- **Fish groups named:** Southwestern salmonids (endangered); large riverine cyprinids, catostomids, and catfishes; cyprinodontiform fishes (including pupfish); cichlids; and smaller cyprinids.
- **Public-acceptance challenge:** Pupfish are described as species "popularly perceived to lack societal utility."
- **Ecosystem scope:** The letter states that "any efforts to preserve viable desert ecosystems" depend on changing public behavior in both the U.S. and Mexico.
- **Protection of critical aquatic habitats:** Public constituency building is presented as necessary for securing protection of "critical aquatic habitats."

No water chemistry, population counts, or habitat measurements are reported.

## Key Findings

1. **Habitat first, captivity as a bridge:** Habitat preservation is the preferred conservation strategy, but captive breeding may be the only immediate response when a species faces imminent extirpation. Any captive program should preserve the option to reintroduce species to natural settings, which may require very long-term captive husbandry.
2. **Division of labor:** AAZPA member institutions are not in a position to lead captive breeding of endangered Southwestern salmonids or large riverine cyprinids, catostomids, and catfishes. These efforts require full hatchery facilities and are "properly the responsibility" of government agencies.
3. **Contribution area 1 – Long-term captive husbandry:** AAZPA institutions, as mostly private foundations, can focus on smaller desert fishes such as cyprinodontiforms, cichlids, and smaller cyprinids. Their funding insulation allows long-term work on species without perceived "societal utility," such as pupfish.
4. **Contribution area 2 – Applied research:** On-site or affiliated laboratories support research in nutrition, behavior, genetics, reproductive biology, and pathology. The writer offers in-house applied research capacity to agencies involved in desert fish conservation.
5. **Contribution area 3 – Conservation education:** AAZPA institutions run ongoing educational programs that "reach millions of people annually," supported by professional staff, volunteer groups, and public relations expertise. Conservation education is described as "our main mission."
6. **Perception problem:** Government-sector professionals hold a "serious misperception" of AAZPA goals and activities. The writer expects this to take time to reverse.
7. **Upcoming IUCN planning:** The IUCN is expected to request a Species Survival Program for these fishes, and early input from Dr. Minckley is requested.
8. **Collaboration over isolation:** The letter concludes that no participant in desert fish conservation "can afford the luxury of going it alone."
9. **ACN objectives:** The Aquatic Conservation Network aims to mobilize amateur aquarists in networking, captive breeding of endangered freshwater fishes, and field conservation. It invites professionals, scientists, conservationists, industry members, and general supporters to join.
