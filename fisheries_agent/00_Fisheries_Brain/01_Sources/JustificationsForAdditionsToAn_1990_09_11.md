# Justifications for Additions to Animal Notice of Review (1990)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #malacology #Sonorellidae

## Executive Summary

This September 11, 1990 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix Field Office) to the Regional Director in Albuquerque provides scientific and administrative justification for adding thirteen taxa to the federal **Animal Notice of Review (NOR)** candidate species list. The taxa span vertebrates and invertebrates of the arid Southwest — one amphibian, two freshwater fishes ([[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]), two caddisflies, seven land snails (primarily *Sonorella* talussnails), and one freshwater mussel ([[California Floater]]). Central themes across nearly all taxa are severe range contraction, small/isolated relict populations, taxonomic uncertainty (cryptic or undescribed sister species), and habitat degradation from anthropogenic pressures including impoundment, exotic species introduction, and urban recreational use. Most recommendations were coordinated with state wildlife agencies (AGFD, NMGF, UDWR, Colorado DOW) and other FWS regional offices prior to submission.

## Background & Description

The source document is a conservation-status/regulatory memorandum, not a morphological study, so detailed anatomical descriptions are absent. Only taxonomically diagnostic notes are provided:

- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*): A cyprinid minnow; the subspecies *G. robusta grahami* was under taxonomic review at the time, with its validity questioned and recommended for absorption into the species-level listing.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*): A catostomid sucker; the Little Colorado River population is noted by some authorities as a distinct, undescribed species separate from *C. latipinnis*.
- **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]** (*Rana chiricahuensis*): Part of the broader *Rana pipiens* species complex; populations formerly assigned to this taxon at Ramsey Canyon and in the sub-Mogollon Plateau region are now considered distinct, undescribed species — indicating unresolved cryptic diversity within the complex.
- Invertebrate taxa (*Sonorella*, *Oreohelix*, *Protoptila*, *Metrichia*, *Anodonta*) are referenced only by taxonomic identity and locality; no morphological detail is given in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Freshwater fishes:**
- **Roundtail Chub** — historically distributed across the **Colorado River Basin** (mainstem Colorado, Green, and San Juan Rivers) and the **Rio Yaqui** and adjacent drainages in Mexico. Extirpated from large portions of the **Gila River Basin**; remaining Gila populations declining. Mainstem Colorado, Green, and San Juan River populations also declining, though healthy populations persist in some upper Colorado River tributaries. Status in Mexico is largely unknown but believed less severely impacted than in the U.S.
- **Flannelmouth Sucker** — historically basin-wide in the Colorado River system; still thriving in the **upper Colorado River basin**, but the **lower basin** (below Glen Canyon Dam) has suffered severe decline, with only three extant populations: the **Virgin River**, the **Colorado River in the Grand Canyon**, and the **Little Colorado River**. Completely extirpated from the **Gila River Basin**.

**Amphibian:**
- **Chiricahua Leopard Frog** — inhabits stock tanks, canyons, and riparian systems in southeastern Arizona (e.g., Sulfur Springs Valley, Sycamore Canyon); a 1983–1987 survey found the species at only 2 of 36 historically occupied localities, alongside 2 newly discovered sites (Ramsey Canyon, Rucker Lake) later attributed to distinct undescribed taxa.

**Invertebrates:**
- **Caddisflies** (*Protoptila balmorhea*, *Metrichia volada*) — restricted to spring-fed stream systems; *P. balmorhea* known only from Oak Creek (central Arizona) and Balmorhea State Park (west Texas); *M. volada* known only from Page Springs, Arizona — both narrow-range spring endemics.
- **Land snails** (*Sonorella* spp., *Oreohelix grahamensis*) — montane talus-slope endemics restricted to isolated mountain ranges (e.g., Pinaleno Mountains, Squaw Peak/Phoenix, Wet Canyon), making them vulnerable to habitat disturbance and, in the case of Squaw Park Talussnail, heavy recreational human traffic in an urban park setting.
- **California Floater** (*Anodonta californiensis*) — freshwater mussel under active 90-day petition review as of the memo date.

## Key Findings

- **Rangewide decline drivers (general theme):** habitat loss, fragmentation, and degradation; competition/predation from exotic species; potential heavy metal contamination; and river regulation (dams, impoundments) fragmenting historic ranges.
- **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]**: Found at only 2 of 36 historically occupied sites in a 1983–1987 statewide survey; recommended for **Category 2** listing; AGFD already lists it as threatened.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]**: Recommended for **Category 2**; already listed as endangered (NM), threatened (AZ), sensitive (NV — Moapa form), species of special concern (CO), and protected/sensitive (UT).
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (lower Colorado River Basin population): Recommended for **Category 2**; reduced to only three remaining populations in the lower basin; Little Colorado River population possibly a distinct undescribed species; listed as a candidate species by Arizona.
- **Balmorhea Saddle-case Caddisfly & Spring Micro Caddisfly**: Recommended for **Category 2**; both are narrow spring endemics with no existing state regulatory protection, as Arizona's insect authority (Agriculture and Horticulture Commission) focuses on pest control rather than conservation.
- **Pinaleno Mountainsnail & Mimic Talussnail**: Recommended for **Category 3C** (removed from further consideration but publicly documented), following a completed multi-species land snail status survey.
- **Ladybug Saddle, Clark Peak, and Pinaleno Talussnails**: Recommended for **Category 2** due to restricted range and incomplete status data.
- **Wet Canyon Talussnail**: Recommended for **Category 1** (highest-priority candidate), based on a detailed status outline submitted previously.
- **Squaw Park Talussnail**: Recommended for **Category 2**; endemic to Squaw Peak in the urban Phoenix area, where recreational pressure and habitat loss pose acute threats.
