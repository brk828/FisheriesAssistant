## Executive Summary

This document is a 1999 administrative memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) confirming the appointment of Jeff Simms (Bureau of Land Management, Safford, AZ) to the **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]**. The memo outlines the team's membership, its regulatory mandate, and the list of federally listed and candidate fish species under its purview. It is primarily an administrative/organizational record rather than a primary field or laboratory study, but it provides valuable taxonomic and conservation-status data for a suite of imperiled southwestern desert fishes.

## Background & Description

The **[[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]** was formed in 1985, initially tasked with preparing recovery plans for endangered and threatened fish species native to Arizona and New Mexico. Over time:
- Draft recovery plans were completed for several species.
- Some species were transferred to other, more specialized recovery teams.
- The team's primary charge shifted from *plan development* to *plan implementation*, serving as an advisory body to the Service on biological and management strategies.

**Team Leadership & Membership (as of 1999):**
- **Dr. W.L. Minckley** — Arizona State University, Team Leader (Dept. of Zoology)
- **Jeff Simms** — Bureau of Land Management, Safford, AZ (newly appointed)
- **Lourdes Juarez-Romero** — Centro Ecológico de Sonora
- **Jerome Stefferud** — U.S. Forest Service
- **David Propst** — New Mexico Game and Fish Department
- **Tom Burke** — Bureau of Reclamation
- **Francisco Abarca** — Arizona Game and Fish Department

The team operates under the Service's **Recovery Planning Guidelines** (Appendix II), meeting once or twice annually (typically in Phoenix or Tempe, AZ), with meetings lasting approximately two days.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species under this team's charge inhabit **desert aquatic ecosystems** of the American Southwest and adjacent Sonora, Mexico — including the [[Gila River Basin]], isolated desert springs, and remnant riverine habitats. These systems are characterized by:
- Fragmented, isolated habitats vulnerable to desiccation and water diversion
- High endemism and narrow ranges (many taxa restricted to single drainages or springs)
- Pressure from habitat alteration, non-native species introductions, and water use conflicts typical of arid-land fish conservation

This memo reflects the institutional and multi-agency (Federal, state, and Mexican) collaborative framework built to address these ecological vulnerabilities, involving the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[New Mexico Game and Fish Department]], and the Mexican [[Centro Ecológico de Sonora]].

**Administrative/Programmatic Findings:**
- The recovery team's scope spans **8 federally listed species** and **4 candidate species**, reflecting a broad, multi-taxon conservation program rather than a single-species focus.
- The team's function has evolved from *recovery plan drafting* to *recovery plan implementation oversight*, indicating a maturing conservation program by the late 1990s.
