# Experimental Introduction of Fish Species (Texas, 1956-1957)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Sciaenidae #Moronidae #Atherinopsidae #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This Texas Game and Fish Commission segment report (Project F-7-R-4, Job F-1) documents an experimental fish introduction program aimed at establishing new sport fisheries and biological control agents in inland Texas waters. The primary effort involved transplanting marine [[Redfish]] (*Sciaenops ocellata*) and [[Speckled Trout]] (*Cynoscion nebulosus*, referred to as "weakfish") from Gulf of Mexico coastal waters into the hypersaline [[Lake Kemp]] and upper Big Wichita River. A secondary program tracked the establishment of [[White Bass]] (*Roccus chrysops*) in [[Buffalo Lake]] as an open-water predator to control gizzard shad and other rough fish. A tertiary, unplanned event saw [[Brook Silverside]] (*Labidesthes sicculus*) naturally colonize the Big Wichita and Little Wichita River lake systems via upstream migration during 1957 spring floods, preempting planned introductions of this species. No confirmed reproduction or reliable growth data were obtained for redfish or speckled trout during this period, though white bass showed strong condition factors and evidence of natural spawning in Buffalo Lake.

## Background & Description

- **Redfish (*Sciaenops ocellata*)**: Marine sciaenid; no morphological or growth-rate data collected in this study due to sampling difficulties (see Key Findings).
- **Speckled Trout / Weakfish (*Cynoscion nebulosus*)**: Marine sciaenid; noted by researchers to be more readily captured by hook-and-line along the Texas coast than redfish, and reportedly easier to transplant than expected based on prior assumptions by marine biologists.
- **White Bass (*Roccus chrysops*)**: Individuals recovered by gill net during the June 1955–May 1956 period exhibited condition ("K") factors ranging up to 3.5, indicating excellent physiological condition and favorable growth in [[Buffalo Lake]].
- **Logperch (*Percina caprodes*)**: Repeatedly misidentified by bait seiners as juvenile redfish or trout fingerlings; correct identification made by project biologists on each occasion, underscoring a recurring field identification confound.
- **Brook Silverside (*Labidesthes sicculus*)**: Small-bodied atherinopsid; capable of substantial upstream migration during spring flood conditions, self-stocking into new lake habitats without human intervention.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Redfish & Speckled Trout**: Introduced into the atypically saline waters of the upper [[Big Wichita River]] and [[Lake Kemp]] to exploit the lake's unusual salinity regime, intended to function as novel game/sport fish species in an otherwise freshwater system.
- **White Bass**: Introduced into [[Buffalo Lake]] to fill a vacant open-water predator niche, specifically targeting control of [[Gizzard Shad]] and other rough fish populations, while also providing a new sport fishery.
- **Brook Silverside**: Intended as an additional open-water forage fish species for the Big Wichita and Little Wichita River lake systems (Lake Wichita, Kickapoo, Diversion), supporting predator populations at a lower trophic level.
- **Rough fish control conflict**: Chemical (rotenone-based) selective-kill treatments targeting gizzard shad and carp in Buffalo Lake (May 1956, June 1957) were found to be non-selective, killing white bass alongside target rough fish, creating a direct management conflict between predator-stocking and chemical rough-fish control strategies.
- **Environmental transport stress**: Success of the redfish/trout transplant program was constrained by the need to carefully manage temperature and gradually acclimate salinity over the 500-mile transport route from the Texas coast to Lake Kemp; failure to do so increased mortality.

## Key Findings

- **Redfish stocking totals**: A cumulative minimum of 1,358 redfish were transferred to Lake Kemp by the end of the segment period (only 8 successfully stocked during this specific reporting period, reflecting high capture/transport mortality).
- **Speckled trout stocking totals**: 52 speckled weakfish were successfully stocked during this segment, bringing the cumulative total to 184 (reported elsewhere in the abstract as 182).
- **No confirmed reproduction**: No evidence of natural reproduction was found for either redfish or speckled trout in Lake Kemp during the study period.
- **Growth rate undetermined**: Reliable growth-rate data could not be obtained for either marine transplant species because seining was impractical in Lake Kemp's 22,800-acre area with heavy submerged structure (trees, rocks, stumps), and gill netting was avoided to prevent killing the introduced fish.
- **Angler report reliability**: Most angler-reported catches of redfish/trout were determined to be false reports, practical jokes, or misidentifications (frequently confused with [[Logperch]]); some reports could not be verified because specimens had already been consumed.
- **White Bass establishment success**: Initial stocking (28 adults from Lake Diversion, summer 1954; 78 additional individuals, July 1955) resulted in confirmed natural reproduction, evidenced by anglers catching numerous small white bass by August 1955 and strong "K" condition factors (up to 3.5) in gill-net samples through May 1956.
- **Chemical treatment risk to White Bass**: Rotenone-based selective-kill treatments in Buffalo Lake (1956, 1957) inadvertently killed white bass, halting further stocking decisions pending development of more selective control techniques.
- **Unplanned Brook Silverside colonization**: Spring floods of 1957 allowed brook silversides to migrate upstream and self-stock into Lake Wichita and possibly Kickapoo and Diversion lakes, rendering planned manual introductions unnecessary.
- **Public outreach program**: Newspaper articles and posters (in Wichita Falls, Lake Kemp area, Amarillo, and Canyon, TX) were used to inform anglers of the experimental stockings, instruct catch-and-release of introduced species, and encourage reporting to authorities; a closed season was implemented for white bass at Buffalo Lake.
- **Collection logistics**: A custom transport trailer system (detachable from pickup truck, battery-equipped) was developed to support marine fish capture and live transport operations along the Texas coast, capable of carrying two average hatchery vat loads simultaneously.
