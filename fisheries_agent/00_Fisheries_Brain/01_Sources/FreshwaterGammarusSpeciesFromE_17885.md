# Gammarus roeseli-Group: Taxonomic Revision of Freshwater Gammarids (Part II)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1977 taxonomic revision (Part II of a series on freshwater [[Gammarus]] from Europe, North Africa and adjacent Asia) treats the *Gammarus roeseli*-group, an artificial grouping defined by a middorsal process (carina) on each of the metasome segments. The authors re-examined type material of all known taxa, along with more than 500 samples and material from major European museums. They conclude that *G. triacanthus* and its subspecies are synonymous with [[Gammarus Roeseli]], raise *G. argaeus* stojicevici to species rank, and describe four new species, bringing the group to seven species. The group appears to originate in southeastern Europe and Asia Minor, with *G. roeseli* a relatively recent colonist of western Europe.

## Background & Description

- **Group definition:** Members carry carinae on metasome segments 1 to 3. Pereiopod 3 and 4 setation and uropod 3 setation can be long (as in the [[Gammarus Pulex-Group]]) or short (as in the [[Gammarus Balcanicus-Group]]).
- **Nomenclatural history:** [[Roesel von Rosenhof]] described *Squilla fluviatilis* in 1755, but the keeled form was long confused with *G. pulex*. [[Gervais]] (1835) separated the keeled *G. roeselii* from *G. pulex*. Prior to this study, three species were recognised (*G. roeseli*, *G. triacanthus* Schäferna, 1922, and *G. argaeus* Vávra, 1905) with at least 15 subspecies attributed to them, many poorly described.
- **Methods:** Morphological characters follow the earlier pulex-group revision. Fifteen characters were scored, including body length, dorsal armature, eye and cephalic lobe shape, antennal setation, mouthparts (mandibular palp), coxal plates, gnathopods, pereiopods 3 to 7, retinacular hooks, epimeral plates, uropod 3, telson, sexual dimorphism and live colour.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Pronounced in all species. Females are smaller, have shorter appendages, wider basal segments on P5 to P7, no calceoli, reduced gnathopod propodus spination, and brood plates (oostegites) on thoracic segments 2 to 5. Dimorphic differences in P3/P4 setation and uropod 3 do not follow a regular pattern.
- **Variability:** Setal and spine counts increase with age, so all conclusions apply to adults only. Stable characters include the mandibular palp structure, antenna 1 armature, the medial palmar spine on gnathopods 1 and 2, and the ratio of endopodite to exopodite on uropod 3. Variable characters include flagellar segment number, dorsal process length and urosome armature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Six of seven species are confined to southeastern Europe and Asia Minor. *G. roeseli* ranges from eastern Turkey to the Atlantic. The group is absent from northern Europe, the British Isles, Italy and the Iberian Peninsula.
- **Co-occurrence:** Members often occur with the pulex- or balcanicus-groups. Experimental work showed *G. roeseli* is more [[Eurybiont]] than these groups, with higher tolerance of low oxygen and elevated temperatures.
- **Subterranean forms:** None found so far.
- **Habitat notes:** *G. roeseli* occurs in small mountain streams, large rivers and lakes, and tolerates higher organic pollution, which the authors suggest explains its competitive success over related forms. *G. goedmakersae* is known from clear, fast-running water on stony substrate. *G. argaeus* is usually in slowly to moderately running water with high calcium ion content. *G. pavo* and *G. mladeni* ecology is unknown.
- **Colour:** Live *G. roeseli* ranges from green or yellow to brown or grey, often with red dots on the epimeral plates.

## Key Findings

1. **Synonymy:** *G. triacanthus* and all its subspecies are synonymous with *G. roeseli*. Intermediate forms (three- and four-keeled) co-occur in Lakes Ohrid and Skadar.
2. **Status change:** *G. argaeus stojicevici* (S. Karaman, 1929) is raised to species rank as *G. stojicevici*.
3. **New species:** Four species described: *G. goedmakersae* (Turkey, Greece), *G. mladeni* (southern and southeastern Turkey), *G. obnixus* (Turkey, Lake Acigöl and Lake Dinar area) and *G. pavo* (central Turkey, Burdur). The group now comprises seven species.
4. **Neotype designation:** Because the original *G. roeseli* locality was not specified, a neotype was designated from the river Yonne at Coulanges-sur-Yonne, France (ZMA Amph. 103.574).
5. **Other nomenclatural acts:** *G. argaeus* var. *brachyurus* is invalid (a specimen with lost and regenerated uropods). *G. argaeus burduri* is identical to the nominal form. A neotype of *G. argaeus* was selected from topotypical material near Develi, Turkey.
6. **Key distinctions (adult males):**
   - *G. goedmakersae*: P3/P4 and uropod 3 densely setose, no anterior setae on P5 to P7.
   - *G. obnixus*: no setae on basal segment of P6/P7 inner surface, uropod 3 endopodite under 3/5 of exopodite.
   - *G. stojicevici*: very sharp epimeres 2 and 3, straight coxal plate margins 3 and 4.
   - *G. argaeus*: moderately pointed epimeres, convex coxal plate margins 3 and 4.
   - *G. mladeni*: setae on dorsal surface of metasomites 1 to 3, sharp epimeres, poorly setose antenna 2.
   - *G. pavo*: calceoli absent, dense setation on antenna 2 peduncle, endopodite of uropod 3 almost as long as exopodite.
   - *G. roeseli*: large (male up to 22 mm), dense antenna 2 setation, uropod 3 endopodite at most 3/4 of exopodite.
7. **Biogeographic conclusion:** The group originated in southeastern Europe and Asia Minor. *G. roeseli* only recently invaded northeastern France and continues to expand westward and southward, according to Pacaud (1952), Wautier & Roux (1959) and Roux (1969).
8. **Ecological tolerance:** *G. roeseli* is more eurybiont than its congeners, a plausible reason for its widespread expansion.
