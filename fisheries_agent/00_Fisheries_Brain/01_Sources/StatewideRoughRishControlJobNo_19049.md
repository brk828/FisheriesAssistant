# Fish Eradication in Century Lake

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #freshwater-ecology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This job completion report documents the chemical treatment of [[Century Lake]] (555 acres, reduced to 463 acres at treatment) and 1.5 miles of [[White Oak Creek]] in [[Hopkins County]], Texas on May 15-16, 1963. The treatment used 995 gallons of 5% rotenone to remove the existing fish population, which consisted of 72% rough fish by number and 87% by weight. The operation was conducted under Federal Aid Project F-14-D-7 (Statewide Rough Fish Control) and successfully eliminated the fish population, with approximately 4,000 spectators recovering edible fish. Post-treatment monitoring confirmed successful eradication, and restocking recommendations were provided.

## Background & Description

[[Century Lake]] is a 555-acre impoundment serving as the first of three reservoirs supplying water to the city of [[Sulphur Springs]], Texas. At the time of treatment, water levels were 30 inches below normal, reducing the effective surface area to 463 acres and creating extensive mud flats on the south and west sides. Fisheries collections conducted in October 1961 revealed a fish community dominated by rough fish species, comprising 72% of individuals and 87% of total biomass.

The treatment targeted the entire lake plus 1.5 miles of lower [[White Oak Creek]] and three small tributaries. Environmental conditions during treatment included strong southwest winds (25-30 mph), surface water temperature of 70°F, and shallow water conditions throughout much of the reservoir.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The pre-treatment fish community was heavily dominated by undesirable rough fish species. [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) comprised approximately 85% of the total fish killed, with most individuals measuring 2-4 inches in length. [[Freshwater Drum]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) was the second most abundant species, ranging from 3 inches to 17.5 pounds, though most were 5-6 inches long.

Large-bodied rough fish included 400-500 [[Smallmouth Buffalo]] (*Ictiobus bubalus*) and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), mostly 10-15 pounds. The catfish community included 15-20 [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) weighing 20-42 pounds, only 6-8 [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) at 8-10 pounds, and abundant [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*) at 6-8 inches.

Game fish were poorly represented. Only 10-15 [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) were recovered from the lake (mostly 4-6 pounds), with very few small bass or [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*) observed. Other species included small sunfish (*Lepomis* spp.), [[Gar]] (*Lepisosteus* spp.), and [[Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*).

## Key Findings

**Treatment Methodology:**
- Creek treatment (April 15, 1963): 1.5 miles of [[White Oak Creek]] plus three tributaries treated with 5% emulsifiable rotenone applied via high-pressure spray mounted on pickup truck
- Lake treatment (April 16, 1963): 995 gallons total rotenone applied over 6 hours using gravity flow drums mounted in four outboard motorboats running criss-cross pattern, with chemical flowing into propeller wash; high-pressure spray used in shallow areas

**Treatment Efficacy:**
- Fish began surfacing quickly due to high winds dispersing chemical
- [[Gizzard Shad]] surfaced within 15 minutes of treatment initiation
- Immediate observations indicated successful kill
- Post-treatment monitoring (3 weeks later, after heavy rainfall): gill netting (900 feet overnight) captured 4 gizzard shad, 2 carp, 49 black bullheads, 1 largemouth bass, 1 [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), 4 [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), 1 [[Longear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis megalotis*)
- Seining collected 200 bass fry (released same day by [[Lewisville State Fish Hatchery]]) and 1 gizzard shad

**Public Participation:**
- Estimated 4,000 people present at peak
- 90-100 boats on lake simultaneously (limited by high winds)
- Better than average recovery of edible fish by spectators
- No quantitative estimate of fish recovered possible

**Restocking Recommendations:**
- 200 [[Largemouth Bass]] fry per acre
- 50 [[White Crappie]] fingerlings per acre
- 50 [[Channel Catfish]] fingerlings per acre
- Fish to be supplied by State fish hatcheries
- Periodic monitoring recommended under Job B-21 (Fisheries Reconnaissance)

**Project Costs:**

- Travel expenses: $353.18
- Equipment (two gravity flow drums): $50.00
- Equipment operation: $50.00
- Materials (1,105 gallons 5% rotenone @ $4.10/gallon): $4,530.50
- **Total project cost: $5,631.77**
