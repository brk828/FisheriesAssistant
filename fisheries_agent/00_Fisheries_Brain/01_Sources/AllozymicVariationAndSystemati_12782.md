# Allozymic Variation and Systematics of North American Plagopterin Fishes

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #plagopterini #phylogenetics #allozyme-electrophoresis

## Executive Summary

This study used allozyme electrophoresis to assess phylogenetic relationships within the cyprinid tribe [[Plagopterini]] of western North America. The research examined 28 presumptive gene loci across multiple genera including [[Lepidomeda]], [[Meda]], [[Plagopterus]], and [[Gila copei]], plus five divergent lineages of [[Gila]]. Results challenge the traditional view of [[Plagopterini]] as monophyletic, instead revealing polyphyly with two independent monophyletic lineages: [[Lepidomeda]] and [[Meda]] + [[Plagopterus]]. The [[Meda]] + [[Plagopterus]] clade shows closer affinity to the genus [[Gila]] (excluding [[Gila copei]]) than to [[Lepidomeda]]. Phylogenetic placement of [[Gila copei]] relative to other genera was poorly resolved despite allozymic similarity to [[Lepidomeda]]. The authors propose that plagopterins originated in [[Oligocene]] paleorivers, with genera and species diverging over [[Miocene]] to [[Pleistocene]] times due to drainage disruptions resulting from regional tectonism.

## Background & Description

The tribe [[Plagopterini]] has long been recognized as monophyletic based on the shared presence of ossified, spine-like rays in both dorsal and pelvic fins, distinguishing them from other New World cyprinids. As constituted, the group includes two monotypic genera ([[Meda]] Girard and [[Plagopterus]] Cope) and the polytypic [[Lepidomeda]] Cope. Morphological similarities between [[Lepidomeda]] and [[Gila copei]] are particularly striking, though relationships to other western cyprinids remain unclear.

The study examined specimens from across the geographic ranges of plagopterins and [[Gila copei]], including populations from the [[Colorado Plateau]], [[Basin-and-Range Province]], [[Bonneville Basin]], and [[Gila River]] system. Taxonomy of [[Gila copei]] is complicated, with recent work demonstrating polyphyly between populations in the upper [[Snake River]]-[[Bear River]] drainages and [[Bonneville Basin]]. All material used in this study came from the [[Sevier River]] sub-basin of the [[Bonneville]] system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study region encompasses two major physiographic provinces: the [[Colorado Plateau]] and [[Basin-and-Range Province]]. The [[Colorado Plateau]] is characterized by elevated beds of mostly sedimentary rocks forming an almost-horizontal surface projecting westward from southern flanks of the [[Rocky Mountains]]. [[Basin-and-Range]] topography consists of parallel mountain ranges separated by deeply alluviated basins, further subdivided into the endorheic [[Bonneville Basin]] and an exorheic zone where the [[Colorado River]] enters the [[Gulf of California]].

Geologic evidence indicates extensive [[Oligocene]] paleorivers drained westward from this landscape, entering the Pacific Ocean through what is now the [[Los Angeles Basin]]. Regional drainages were disrupted in the [[Miocene]] by extensional faulting and uplift of the [[Colorado Plateau]] margin. Extension began >20 million years ago (Ma), ultimately surrounding the Plateau on west and south. The [[Bonneville Basin]] was isolated in the north by the [[Wasatch Front]] >30 Ma; the [[Mogollon Rim]] formed 22-25 Ma; and the southwestern margin differentiated as recently as 6.0 Ma. The lower [[Colorado River]] formed when the [[Los Angeles Basin]] transferred northward between 6-12 Ma, with integration across the Plateau margin through [[Grand Canyon]] occurring ~5.0 Ma.

[[Meda]] and [[Plagopterus]] are restricted to the southern [[Basin-and-Range Province]] (lower [[Colorado River]] basin). [[Meda]] occurs only in the [[Gila River]] basin, while [[Plagopterus]] historically occupied both mainstem [[Gila River]] and [[Colorado River]], upstream to the lower [[Virgin River]]. The species exhibit different habitat preferences: [[Meda]] prefers small, clear, rock-bottomed streams while [[Plagopterus]] inhabits large, silty, sand-bottomed rivers.

## Key Findings

**Molecular Analysis:**
- Products from 28 presumptive gene loci were resolved; 26 were polymorphic
- Only CBP-2 and LDH-C were invariant across all taxa
- Modified Rogers' genetic distances ranged from 0.060 (between [[Meda fulgida]] populations) to 0.844 (between [[Gila robusta]] and [[Richardsonius balteatus]])

**Phylogenetic Relationships:**
- [[Plagopterini]] is polyphyletic with two independent monophyletic lineages
- First lineage: [[Lepidomeda]] (also includes [[Gila copei]])
- Second lineage: [[Meda]] + [[Plagopterus]], showing closer affinity to [[Gila]] than to [[Lepidomeda]]
- Within [[Lepidomeda]]: L. m. mollispinis and L. m. pratensis are most similar, L. vittata is sister to L. mollispinis
- Genetic distance between two populations of [[Meda fulgida]] is of similar magnitude to that separating species of [[Lepidomeda]]
- All [[Gila]] form an independent cluster, with G. atraria and G. robusta + G. pandora most distinctive

**Parsimony Analysis:**
- FREQPARS analysis produced topology with tree length = 131.5 steps
- [[Gila copei]] placed as sister to [[Gila]] + [[Plagopterus]] + [[Meda]] clade rather than to [[Lepidomeda]]
- Traditional arrangement of monophyletic [[Plagopterini]] was nearly five steps longer (less parsimonious)
- Monophyly of [[Gila]] strongly supported when [[Gila copei]] excluded
- Moving G. atraria outside the [[Gila]] + [[Plagopterus]] + [[Meda]] clade produced tree ~10 steps longer

**Biogeographic Interpretation:**
- [[Plagopterini]] progenitor likely occupied paleoriver watershed
- [[Meda]] and [[Plagopterus]] isolated >20 Ma south of [[Colorado Plateau]] as margin differentiated
- Divergence of [[Lepidomeda]] and [[Gila copei]] attributed to separation of Plateau and [[Bonneville Basin]] drainages
- Accrued allozymic differences imply [[Meda]] + [[Plagopterus]] separated from [[Lepidomeda]] lineage before [[Gila copei]]
- [[Lepidomeda]] species diverged later in evolutionary history

**Taxonomic Implications:**
- Most parsimonious explanation is evolution of spiny-rayed characters in ancestral plagopterin, subsequently lost in lineage leading to [[Gila copei]]
- [[Gila copei]] may be a member of [[Plagopterini]] despite lacking ossified dorsal and pelvic fin rays
- Basal relationships of [[Gila copei]] to either [[Lepidomeda]] or [[Gila]] remain poorly resolved
- Resolution requires additional data from more comprehensive survey of North American cyprinid genera
