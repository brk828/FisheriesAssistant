# Chemical treatment of the Merced River, Mariposa County

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries-management #rotenone-treatment #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 California Department of Fish and Game administrative report documents a large-scale rotenone chemical treatment of the [[Merced River]], its North and South Forks, associated tributaries, and [[Exchequer Reservoir]] in Mariposa County, California. The project was undertaken to eliminate an overabundant population of rough fish (primarily suckers, squawfish, hardhead, and carp) prior to and during the Merced Irrigation District's construction of the New Exchequer Dam. The drawdown of the reservoir to elevation 445 feet for diversion tunnel construction made the treatment economically feasible. Approximately 75 miles of stream and river habitat plus the 40-acre reservoir pool were treated using 248 gallons of rotenone (Pro-Noxfish) in the lower phase and 196 gallons in the upper phase, with potassium permanganate (KMnO4) used for downstream detoxification. The kill was judged thorough via visual survey, and the river was subsequently restocked with over 182,000 rainbow trout across fingerling, subcatchable, and catchable size classes. Total project cost was $9,993.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose**: Remove overabundant "rough fish" populations (non-game cyprinids, catostomids, and ictalurids) competing with game fish, coordinated with reservoir drawdown for [[New Exchequer Dam]] construction.
- **Study area**:
  - ~40 miles of [[Merced River]] main stem (El Portal to Exchequer Reservoir)
  - ~10 miles of the [[South Fork Merced River]] (from a barrier 2 miles above Peachtree Bar downstream)
  - ~10 miles of the [[North Fork Merced River]] (from Bull Creek downstream)
  - ~15 miles of small, intermittent tributaries (34 tributaries surveyed and treated)
- **Access**: Highway 140 (El Portal–Briceburg); beyond Briceburg access limited to old Yosemite Railroad bed and Forest Service/private roads. Much of the river system was surveyed on foot or trail bike.
- **Timing**: Conducted during a very dry water year; most tributaries were intermittent except the South Fork and Crane Creek.
- **Two-phase treatment**:
  1. **Phase 1** (Sept 14–18): Upper river from El Portal to a barrier 1 mile above the North Fork, plus South Fork from a barrier 2 miles above Peachtree Bar.
  2. **Phase 2** (Sept 29–Oct 3): Barrier above North Fork downstream through Exchequer Reservoir.
- **Chemical agents**:
  - **Rotenone (Pro-Noxfish)** — primary piscicide, target concentration 0.05 ppm active ingredient.
  - **Potassium permanganate (KMnO4)** — detoxification agent applied 2 miles below Exchequer Dam.
- **Application methods**: Drip stations at ~4-mile intervals along the river (6-hour metering cycles), backpack pumps/squirt bottles for isolated pools and potholes, and a commercial spray helicopter for the reservoir pool and adjacent mudflats/river bed, plus transport into difficult-access areas (Peachtree Bar).
- **Total rotenone used**: 196 gallons (upper river phase) + 248 gallons (lower river/reservoir phase) = ~444 gallons across the whole project (including tributary pre-treatment of 125 gallons).
- **Detoxification**: KMnO4 crystals dissolved directly in river riffle (after an impractical metered-solution method failed); dosed at 2 ppm (16 lbs/hr) initially, reduced to 1 ppm (9 lbs/hr) until water tested nontoxic.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology**: Reservoir drawdown to elevation 445 ft (for diversion tunnel construction) reduced the reservoir pool to ~40 surface acres / 210 acre-feet, max depth 12 ft, with no thermocline (71°F surface, 65°F at depth) — enabling cost-effective treatment.
- **Flow conditions**: Main river flow measured at 40 cfs above the South Fork; South Fork flow at 10 cfs. Fluorescein dye tracer used to estimate rotenone travel time, though actual rotenone movement was slower than dye due to pool/riffle heterogeneity and reduced gradient in some reaches.
- **Water temperature range during treatment**: 67–71°F.
- **Downstream fishery protection**: A KMnO4 detoxification station was placed 2 miles below Exchequer Dam, with an additional safety buffer of 700–900 acre-feet of dilution water in [[Merced Falls Reservoir]] 6 miles downstream.
- **Bioassay methodology**: Golden shiners used in graded KMnO4 concentration jars (1–5 ppm) to bracket rotenone toxicity levels in real time; highest recorded toxicity fell between 1–2 ppm rotenone equivalent.
- **Non-target impacts**: Damage to other aquatic organisms was limited to large larvae of [[Hellgrammites]] (Megaloptera), [[Stoneflies]] (Plecoptera), [[Mayflies]] (Ephemeroptera), tadpoles, and salamanders — and only in the immediate vicinity of drip stations where rotenone concentrations were highest. No effects were observed at lower concentrations downstream.
- **Fish kill extent from detoxification lag**: Approximately 2 miles of fish kill occurred below the detoxification station, attributed to difficulty achieving proper KMnO4 dosing during the first 6 hours of operation.
- **Pre-existing mortality**: Large numbers of dead carp, threadfin shad, and largemouth bass were already observed in the reservoir prior to treatment, attributed to adverse water conditions from the dam construction project (likely low water/oxygen stress from drawdown).

## Key Findings

- **Species composition of the kill** (from Table 1, estimated by sample counts at representative pools/riffles) included:
  - **Game/sport fish**: [[Rainbow Trout]] (fingerling–18"), [[Brown Trout]] (fingerling–13"), [[Smallmouth Bass]] (fingerling–10"), [[Largemouth Bass]] (fingerling–10"), [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Black Crappie]] (fingerling–16")
  - **Rough/non-game fish (dominant by abundance and weight)**: [[Western Sucker]] (fry–26"), [[Sacramento Squawfish]] and [[Hardhead]] (combined, fingerling–30"), [[Carp]] (6–30"), [[Sculpin]] (2–4.5", incidental), [[White Catfish]], [[Brown Bullhead]]
  - Rough fish (suckers, squawfish/hardhead, and carp) consistently dominated percent abundance and weight across nearly all treatment reaches, confirming the "overabundant rough fish population" rationale for the project.
  - Trout (rainbow and brown) were largely absent or present only in small numbers in the lower and middle river reaches, but present in the upper river/South Fork sections (e.g., Hite Cove).
- **Reservoir kill composition**: Black crappie, white catfish, brown bullhead, bluegill, green sunfish, and carp — carp dominating by weight (~90% of estimated weight in the reservoir pool).
- **Total rotenone volumes**:
  - Tributaries (initial survey): 125 gallons
  - Upper river phase (El Portal to North Fork barrier + South Fork): 196 gallons (121 gal main river + 55 gal South Fork + 15 gal isolated pools)
  - Lower river/reservoir phase: 248 gallons (98 gal metered river stations + 70 gal reservoir spray + 50 gal upstream river bed spray + 30 gal isolated pools)
- **Helicopter application**: Used to treat the 40-acre reservoir pool and 90 acres of mudflats/shallow pools above the pool; also used for chemical/personnel transport to difficult-access sites (Peachtree Bar). Cost: $4.85/acre for 130 acres treated.
- **Detoxification outcome**: KMnO4 successfully neutralized toxic water within ~2 days of dosing adjustments (2 ppm → 1 ppm), with live-car and bioassay tests confirming nontoxicity by October 6.
- **Restocking results**: Merced River restocked with 182,524 rainbow trout total (100,844 fingerlings, 48,671 subcatchables, 33,009 catchables), weighing 21,674 lbs combined. Reservoir slated for restocking with largemouth bass, white catfish, black crappie, and threadfin shad (forage fish) once water storage began. Smallmouth bass restocking planned for the Merced River and both forks in 1965.
- **Public reaction**: Overwhelmingly favorable; sportsmen's clubs, businesses, and residents supported the project, particularly after observing the extent of rough fish abundance. No adverse public reaction reported.
- **Total project cost**: $9,993.00 (pretreatment survey/sampling: $3,720; chemical treatment: $6,273), funded via Dingell-Johnson Project F-14-D, Region 4 Fisheries Management Support funds, and $2,000 in Mariposa County Fish and Game fine monies.
- **Management recommendations**: Future KMnO4 dispensing should use an adjustable dry paddle-wheel-powered dispenser (as used in the 1963 Nebraska Snake River renovation project) rather than a liquid metering system, which proved impractical for dissolving sufficient KMnO4 crystals. Marker dye tests for calculating rotenone travel speed should use longer test sections spanning multiple representative stream segments.
