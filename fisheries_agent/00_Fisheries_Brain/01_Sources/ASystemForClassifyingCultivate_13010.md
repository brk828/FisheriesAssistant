# A System for Classifying Cultivated and Cultured Lands Within a Systematic Classification of Natural Ecosystems

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ecosystem-classification #biogeography #wetland-ecology

> **Note on scope:** This source is **not** a fish- or aquatic-organism-specific study. It is a biogeographic/land-classification paper by David E. Brown (1980) that extends an existing hierarchical natural vegetation classification system (Brown & Lowe 1974a,b; Brown, Lowe & Pase 1979, 1980) to cover human-cultivated and cultured lands, including a formal but largely hypothetical category for **submergent aquatic crops** and other wetland cultivation types. Because the paper's substantive content concerns terrestrial/wetland land classification rather than fish biology, the note below is synthesized to reflect the actual content, with aquatic/wetland-relevant elements highlighted for cross-reference to related aquatic ecology notes.

## Executive Summary

Brown (1980) proposes a formal, numerically-coded hierarchical scheme for classifying **cultivated and culturally-modified lands** (agricultural, urban-residential, and wetland-agricultural systems) within the pre-existing [[Brown-Lowe-Pase Natural Ecosystem Classification]] framework used for North American natural vegetation. The system uses a seven-level digit hierarchy (Biogeographic Realm → Formation-type → Climatic Zone → Biotic Province → Series/Crop → Association → Composition-structure-phase) to allow cultivated and cultured lands to be mapped, compared, and stored in the same computer-compatible data structure as natural biomes. Critically for aquatic/wetland ecology, the system explicitly separates **Upland Cultures** (1,300 series) from **Wetland Cultures** (1,400 series), with wetland formation types including Marsh Crops (e.g., rice), Swampscrub Crops (e.g., cranberry bogs), Swamp Tree Farms (hypothetical), Strand Crops (hypothetical), and **Submergent Aquatic Crops** — cultivated stands of mostly-submerged or non-emergent aquatic plants. The paper argues that classifying cultivated wetlands systematically improves prediction of wildlife (including presumably fish and waterfowl) habitat value relative to adjacent natural ecosystems and ecotones.

## Background & Description

*Not applicable in the conventional (organismal/anatomical) sense — this source contains no fish, invertebrate, or plankton morphological data.* Instead, the paper describes the **"morphology" of land-cover units**, which may be of interest for adapting land/habitat classification metadata in aquatic ecology notes:

- **Formation-type (2nd digit level):** Structural/physiognomic categories analogous to natural vegetation formations — e.g., Urban-Residential, Tree Farms/Orchards, Shrub Crops, Graminoid Crops, Row Crops, Vacant/Fallow (uplands); Urban-Residential Wetland, Swamp Tree Farms, Swampscrub Crops, Marsh Crops, Strand Crops, **Submergent Aquatic Crops** (wetlands).
- **Series/Crop (5th digit level):** The actual monoculture or crop type (e.g., cotton, alfalfa, pecans, maize, rice, cranberry), treated as equivalent to a natural vegetation "series" or cover type.
- **Association (6th digit level):** Plant association-level detail, e.g., *Citrus* sp.–*Cynodon dactylon* Association; *Myriophyllum brasiliense* (water-milfoil) Association for a recreational lake.
- **Composition-structure-phase (7th digit level):** Fine-scale quantitative descriptors such as percent canopy cover, height, or stand age (e.g., "cultivated deciduous trees between 50 and 100 years of age").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although centered on terrestrial/agricultural land classification, the paper touches on aquatic and wetland ecological niches in several ways relevant to aquatic ecologists:

- **Cultivated Wetlands** are defined as croplands "normally flooded or occurring in water" (e.g., rice, *Oryza sativa*), placing them within the same hierarchical logic as natural [[Wetland Ecosystems]] and marsh biomes.
- **Submergent Aquatic Crops** formation (wetland formation-type 6) is described as containing "cultivated crops of aquatic plants mostly submerged or lacking emergent structures" — a category the author flags as largely **hypothetical**, with the worked example being an urban recreational lake (Encanto Lake, Phoenix, Arizona) classified down to the *Myriophyllum brasiliense* (water-milfoil) Association, a submerged/floating aquatic macrophyte relevant to freshwater habitat structure and potentially to fish/invertebrate cover assessment.
- **Climatic (Thermal) Zone (3rd digit level)** determines which wetland/crop types can support certain wildlife guilds — the author notes that "wintering waterfowl can usually only use croplands in warm temperate and tropic-subtropical zones," implying analogous thermal constraints likely apply to fish and other aquatic fauna using cultivated wetland habitats.
- **Ecotone significance:** The paper explicitly discusses how proximity of wetlands to different cultured land types (cropland vs. urban-residential vs. wilderness) alters their biotic carrying capacity — wetlands near croplands may support more wintering waterfowl than wilderness wetlands, while urban-adjacent wetlands may support fewer individuals but have greater social/human importance. This ecotone concept is transferable to aquatic habitat quality assessments near [[Agricultural Runoff]] and urbanized watersheds.
- **Biotic Provinces (4th digit level):** North American provinces mapped in Figure 1 (e.g., Sonoran, Chihuahuan, Californian, Gulf Coastal, Great Basin) provide the biogeographic template within which both natural aquatic biomes and cultured wetland systems are nested, linking this classification conceptually to regional [[Freshwater Biogeographic Provinces]].

## Key Findings

- Introduces a **7-level digitized numerical hierarchy** (Realm → Formation → Climatic Zone → Biotic Province → Series → Association → Structure-phase) extending the Brown-Lowe-Pase natural ecosystem classification to cultivated/cultured lands.
- Cultivated lands are split at the top level into **Upland Cultures (x,300)** and **Wetland Cultures (x,400)**, mirroring natural upland/wetland biome divisions.
- Six upland formation-types defined: Urban-Residential, Tree Farms/Orchards, Shrub Crops, Graminoid Crops, Row Crops, Vacant-Fallow.
- Six wetland formation-types defined: Urban-Residential (wetland converted to upland use, e.g., marinas/docks), Swamp Tree Farms (hypothetical), Swampscrub Crops (e.g., cranberry bogs), Marsh Crops (e.g., rice), Strand Crops (hypothetical), **Submergent Aquatic Crops** (mostly/fully submerged aquatic plant cultivation).
- Four global climatic (thermal) zones — Arctic-Boreal, Cold Temperate, Warm Temperate, Tropical-Subtropical — constrain which crop/formation types and associated fauna (e.g., wintering waterfowl) can occur.
- Worked example (Table 10) classifies **Encanto Lake**, a recreational urban lake in Phoenix, Arizona, down to the *Myriophyllum brasiliense* (water-milfoil) Association — the only fully aquatic-vegetation example given in the paper, situated within the Sonoran Biotic Province and Tropical-Subtropical urban wetland category.
- The author emphasizes that cultured/cultivated ecosystem properties **change with time** (e.g., avifauna of a 50-year-old residential association differs from a 2-year-old one), suggesting "age of association" as an additional (7th-level) classification criterion — a concept applicable to successional dynamics in constructed or restored aquatic/wetland habitats.
- The classification system is proposed as a tool for **land-use planners, wildlife managers, and biologists** to predict biotic potential of cultivated fields and adjacent natural vegetation, including implications for wetland/waterfowl habitat value near agricultural vs. urban vs. wilderness lands.
- No fish species, fish families, water chemistry, or aquatic community data are provided; aquatic content is limited to the wetland/submergent-crop classification categories and the single water-milfoil association example.
