# Colorado Squawfish and Humpback Chub Population and Habitat Monitoring

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered_species #freshwater_ecology

## Executive Summary

This job progress report documents the second year (October 1, 1978 to September 30, 1979) of the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]]'s endangered fish monitoring program targeting [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Field crews sampled five trend zones across the [[Colorado River]], [[Gunnison River]], [[White River]], and [[Yampa River]] using seines, dip nets, and electrofishing from July through August 1978. A total of 18,042 fishes were processed from 269 collections at 57 sites. Native fishes showed better representation than in 1977, and 18 endangered fishes were captured, including both target species. The two endangered species appear to be sympatric in portions of the [[Colorado River]], particularly in the [[Black Rocks]] area of [[Ruby Canyon]].

## Background & Description

The monitoring program was established to track reproductive success and year class abundance of two federally endangered cyprinids in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Five trend zones were selected based on: (1) recent occurrence (within 10 years) of target species, (2) accessibility for field crews, and (3) representativeness of broader river conditions. Trend zones included:

- **[[Yampa River]]**: 16 km between Maybell and Sunbeam, [[Moffat County]]
- **[[White River]]**: 16 km between Rio Blanco Lake and County Road 65 bridge, [[Rio Blanco County]]
- **[[Colorado River]]** (Palisade-Clifton): ~12 km in [[Mesa County]]
- **[[Gunnison River]]**: ~19 km between Whitewater and Redlands Dam, [[Mesa County]]
- **[[Colorado River]]** (Loma to state line): ~31 km including [[Horsethief Canyon]], [[Ruby Canyon]], and [[Black Rocks]] area

Special sites were also sampled on the Yampa River including [[Juniper Hot Springs]], [[Lily Park]], and [[Cross Mountain Canyon]].

Sampling employed multiple gear types: fine mesh seines (<0.6mm), coarse mesh seines (>0.6mm), dip nets, and boat electrofishing. Habitats were categorized as backwater, pool, shoreline, chute channel, riffle, or run. Water temperature during sampling ranged from 16-32°C across sites. All specimens were measured (total length in mm), assigned to length groups (≤20, 21-50, 51-150, 151-300, >301 mm TL), and most were released after processing. Larvae and early juveniles were preserved in 5% formalin with erythorbic acid for laboratory identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1978 sampling occurred during a year of higher, colder water compared to 1977, which influenced species composition and spawning timing. Native fishes maintained greatest dominance in the [[White River]] trend zone, where no non-native species had successfully spawned by the July 22 sampling date. The higher velocity runoff in 1978 likely forced many intermediate-size fishes downstream, and considerably fewer fish were collected by seine despite substantial effort.

Native suckers ([[Catostomus latipinnis]], *C. discobolus*, *C. commersoni*) comprised a greater proportion of all size categories in 1978, with increased numbers in the ≤20 mm category attributed to delayed spawning from colder temperatures. In the [[Yampa River]], flannelmouth suckers, bluehead suckers, and white suckers showed considerably higher percentages in the recently-spawned category, while fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*) percentages were greatly reduced.

Several introduced species have become established in the Yampa River: common carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), white sucker (*Catostomus commersoni*), fathead minnow, redside shiner (*Richardsonius balteatus*), and sandshiner (*Notropis stramineus*). Hybridization between white sucker and native suckers is common in the upper Yampa River above Maybell. Two additional specimens of a suspected hybrid between redside shiner and speckled dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*) were captured in 1978, including a 130 mm specimen—the largest collected to date.

On the [[Gunnison River]] and [[Colorado River]] trend zones, native fish species were more common in 1978 collections compared to 1977, especially in the ≤20 mm category, again due to higher, cooler water and earlier sampling. However, flannelmouth suckers, though common as adults, were poorly represented in smaller size categories. Red shiners (*Notropis lutrensis*) continued to dominate the intermediate size category in the Colorado River, while roundtail chubs (*Gila robusta*) dominated this size group on the Gunnison.

## Key Findings

**Endangered Species Captures**: A total of 18 endangered fishes were collected during 1978:
- **Colorado Squawfish**: 4 from lower [[Colorado River]] trend zone, 1 from [[Gunnison River]] below Redlands Dam, 6 from upper canyon areas of [[Yampa River]] (2 from west base of [[Cross Mountain Canyon]], 4 from east end of [[Juniper Springs Canyon]])
- **Humpback Chub**: 7 from lower [[Colorado River]] trend zone

**Size and Age Structure**: Colorado squawfish ranged 500-820 mm TL. The largest specimen (820 mm, estimated 12 pounds) from [[Juniper Canyon]] represented one of the largest documented captures from the Yampa in years. Age estimates from scales ranged 8-12 years. Humpback chubs showed size variation (131, 258, 300, 330, 331, 335 mm) potentially representing four year classes. The smallest specimen (131 mm) was likely two years old, spawned in 1976.

**Habitat Associations**: All endangered fish captures were associated with low velocity water near sandy shorelines, sandbars, or eddies near boulders. Water depth varied 0.5-3 m over sand and silt substrates. Most endangered fishes had access to deep water habitat associated with nearby canyon areas.

**Sympatric Populations**: The capture of several Colorado squawfish in the [[Black Rocks]] area indicates co-existence of two endangered fish populations. This is the only site within the study area where both Colorado squawfish and humpback chubs were collected. One male squawfish (560 mm) was running ripe and emitted milt, and a 540 mm specimen appeared to have a distended abdomen, possibly a spent female, suggesting spawning activity in the area.

**Geographic Distribution**: One humpback chub was captured approximately 4 km above the [[Black Rocks]] area, indicating the population is spread throughout [[Ruby Canyon]] rather than concentrated only at Black Rocks.

**Notable Species Records**: Two brassy minnows (*Hybognathus hankinsoni*) were captured at station A' on the lower Colorado trend zone near Loma—the first documented report of this species in the basin since 1975. Two smallmouth bass (240 mm and >300 mm) were captured below Redlands Dam on the Gunnison River, believed to be the first recaptures since 1,800 fingerlings were stocked near Delta in 1973. Plains killifish (*Fundulus kansae*) was collected at station D on the Yampa River, confirming its presence documented in 1977 collections.

**Cross Mountain Canyon Survey**: Special sampling on October 11-12, 1978 explored [[Cross Mountain Canyon]]. Although no humpback chubs were captured, habitat within the canyon appeared favorable. The upper half was boulder-strewn with many pools and eddies, water depths 1-2 m. A large half-acre pool approximately 1.8 km within the canyon yielded 3 roundtail chubs and 1 channel catfish in 10 minutes fishing effort. Electrofishing at the lower end yielded two Colorado squawfish (720 and 550 mm) captured just outside the canyon mouth within 10 m of shore over sand and boulder substrate.

**Juniper Canyon**: Four Colorado squawfish (500, 510, 610, 820 mm) were electrofished in the upper end of [[Juniper Springs Canyon]] on October 12. All were captured in a constricted area in the middle of the pooled upper canyon where water velocity appeared to quicken. Water depth varied 0.5-1.5 m over sand and boulder substrate.

**Total Fish Processed**: 18,042 fishes from 269 collections at 57 sites. Species composition varied by trend zone, with native fishes better represented across all waters than during 1977.
