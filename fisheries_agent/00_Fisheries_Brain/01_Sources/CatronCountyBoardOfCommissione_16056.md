# Catron County v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Legal Complaint

#ichthyology #endangered-species #cyprinidae #administrative-law #critical-habitat

## Executive Summary

This document is a legal complaint filed by the Board of County Commissioners of [[Catron County]], New Mexico, against the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and its officials in U.S. District Court (Case No. CIV 93-730-HB). The complaint challenges FWS actions regarding two threatened fish species—the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)—native to streams in New Mexico and Arizona. Catron County alleges that FWS violated the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA), [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA), and [[Administrative Procedure Act]] (APA) by: (1) failing to provide actual notice to the county during listing and critical habitat designation processes; (2) failing to designate critical habitat within statutory timeframes; (3) adopting recovery plans before designating critical habitat; and (4) failing to prepare required environmental impact documentation. The county seeks declaratory judgment, injunctive relief, and writs of mandamus to compel FWS compliance with federal law.

## Background & Description

The [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) are small cyprinid minnows native to various streams and rivers in [[New Mexico]] and [[Arizona]]. Both species were listed as threatened by FWS in 1986 pursuant to the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Their historic habitat and present distribution include the [[Gila River]] and its tributaries, portions of which flow through [[Catron County]], New Mexico.

When FWS proposed listing these species in 1985 (50 FR 25380 for loach minnow; 50 FR 25390 for spikedace, published June 18, 1985), the agency concurrently proposed designation of critical habitat that included several stream reaches within Catron County. The final listing regulations were adopted in 1986 (51 FR 23769 for spikedace, July 1, 1986; 51 FR 39468 for loach minnow, October 28, 1986), but critical habitat designation was postponed until June 18, 1987, pursuant to 16 U.S.C. Section 1533(b)(6)(A). As of the complaint filing date (1993), critical habitat remained "under administrative review" and had not been formally designated.

FWS completed technical/agency review drafts of recovery plans in July 1988 and advertised revised drafts in the Federal Register in October 1990. Final Recovery Plans for both species were issued September 30, 1991. The complaint alleges these plans were adopted prematurely, before critical habitat designation, and without required NEPA documentation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Gila River]] basin represents critical aquatic habitat for multiple endemic fish species in the southwestern United States. The complaint references that both recovery plans advocate "natural hydrographs" including flooding to protect these species, though environmental effects of such flooding are not addressed in the plans.

The recovery plans state that land and water rights will be acquired to protect existing populations and that restrictions may be imposed on private property rights and natural resource use in areas of spikedace and loach minnow habitat. Such habitat areas include lands within [[Catron County]].

The complaint notes concern about "single-species management" repercussions on the overall physical and human environment. Catron County hired a biologist to review threatened and endangered species issues in relation to the county's physical and human environment.

Historic and current threats to these species are implied to include grazing, forestry practices, mining, and agriculture, though the complaint requests detailed documentation of studies showing detrimental effects from these activities.

## Key Findings

**Legal and Procedural Violations Alleged:**

1. **Notice Violations**: FWS failed to provide actual notice to Catron County of proposed listing regulations and critical habitat designations as required by 16 U.S.C. Section 1533(b)(5)(A)(ii) and 50 C.F.R. Section 424.16(c)(1). No summary of proposed regulations appeared in newspapers of general circulation within Catron County (the Catron County Firestarter or Catron County Courier).

2. **Critical Habitat Designation Failure**: FWS failed to designate critical habitat within the statutory two-year maximum period following the June 18, 1985 proposed regulation, in violation of 16 U.S.C. Section 1533(b)(6)(C). As of 1993, designation remained "under administrative review" more than seven years after the initial proposal.

3. **Premature Recovery Plans**: FWS adopted recovery plans on September 30, 1991, before designating critical habitat, contrary to ESA requirements that recovery plans include "site-specific management actions" (16 U.S.C. Section 1533(f)(1)(B)(i)). The complaint argues recovery plans cannot be workable without first identifying critical habitat.

4. **NEPA Violations**: FWS failed to prepare environmental impact statements or environmental assessments for either the proposed critical habitat designation or the recovery plans, despite these constituting "major Federal actions significantly affecting the quality of the human environment" (42 U.S.C. Section 4332(2)(C)). FWS relied on a 1983 notice (48 FR 49244) asserting ESA Section 4 actions are "exempt from NEPA review 'as a matter of law.'"

5. **APA Violations**: Administrative records lack required findings, conclusions, and reasons for decisions to postpone critical habitat designation, propose critical habitat without NEPA compliance, and adopt recovery plans without critical habitat designation (5 U.S.C. Section 557(c)).

**Relief Requested:**

- Declaratory judgment that FWS violated ESA, NEPA, and APA
- Declaratory judgment voiding the species listing, proposed critical habitat designation, and recovery plans
- Permanent injunctions preventing implementation of listing, critical habitat designation, and recovery plans
- Writs of mandamus compelling FWS to provide actual notice, withdraw and restart listing/critical habitat processes with proper notice, withdraw recovery plans, and comply with NEPA and APA requirements
- Attorney fees pursuant to 16 U.S.C. Section 1540(g)(4)

**Discovery Requests:**

The complaint includes extensive interrogatories and document production requests seeking:
- Names and roles of FWS personnel responsible for listing, recovery plans, and critical habitat designation
- All reasons why critical habitat has not been designated
- All economic analyses prepared for any phase of the process
- Documentation of actual notice provided to Catron County
- All studies showing detrimental effects from grazing, forestry, mining, and agriculture
- Population count data for both species
- Records of cooperation with state and local governments
- Status of work by contractor Karl Souder and Associates
- Specific draft documents and internal memoranda (21 specific items listed, dated 1986-1992)

**County Interests and Standing:**

Catron County asserts standing based on: (1) direct adverse effects on citizens, environmental quality, economic stability, and tax base; (2) ownership or property interests in areas of proposed critical habitat; (3) statutory duty to "provide for the safety, preserve the health, promote the prosperity" of inhabitants (Section 4-37-1, NMSA 1978); (4) dependence on federal land revenues through Payments in Lieu of Taxes and other programs; and (5) concern for ecosystem management beyond single-species approaches.

The county sent a letter in March 1990 requesting notification prior to any FWS actions affecting the county. A February 27, 1992 Notice of Intent to Sue was issued by the Coalition of Arizona/New Mexico Counties for Stable Economic Growth, of which Catron County is a member. An additional Notice of Intent to Sue was issued March 16, 1992.
