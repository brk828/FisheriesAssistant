# San Pedro Habitat Management Plan: Draft Actions and Objectives (1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This is an April 13, 1990 cover letter from the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Safford District to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University). It summarizes the April 5, 1990 meeting of the Biological Resources Technical Committee in Tucson and encloses draft planned actions and management objectives (as of 4/5/90) for the San Pedro Habitat Management Plan (HMP). The HMP covers the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]] and must be completed by September 1990. Reviewer comments were due May 1, 1990, with a preliminary draft targeted for June 15, 1990. The plan includes evaluating the reintroduction of several native fishes and restoring riparian and wetland habitat.

## Background & Description
- Letter signed by Erick G. Campbell, San Pedro Project Manager; contact also Mark Fredlake.
- The overall objective is to conserve, protect and enhance the riparian ecosystem and its aquatic and terrestrial wildlife values, as mandated by the Arizona-Idaho Conservation Act of 1988 and consistent with the San Pedro Riparian Management Plan.
- Actions are to be implemented cooperatively by BLM, AGFD, USFWS and other parties.
- Committee suggestions: evaluate additional native species for reintroduction beyond threatened and endangered ones.
  - Fish: [[Gila Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Sonoran Sucker]].
  - Mammals: river otter, jaguarundi, beaver.
- The committee also suggested adding an objective on managing exotic species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Habitats named: mainstem [[San Pedro River]], [[Saint David Cienega]], [[Young Block Gravel Pit Ponds]], detention ponds (Contention, Carr Canyon), [[Walnut Gulch]], [[Escapule Wash]], and riparian woodlands (cottonwood/willow, mesquite, mesquite/sacaton).
- Disturbances addressed: livestock grazing (15-year moratorium within the NCA; 6,500 acres presently under lease), off-road vehicle use, gravel pits (Sierra Ready Mix, Young Block, Walnut Gulch) and gully erosion at Palominas and the Boquillas land grant.
- Restoration tools: fencing, erosion-control structures (gabions, drop structures), native tree planting, experimental re-seeding of 160 acres of abandoned farm fields, prescribed fire, and wildlife water developments (wells at Boquillas Ranch, Fairbank, Summers Corral, Palominas; troughs with escape ramps and storage of at least 2,000 gallons).
- Non-native threat: [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) in the cienega.

## Key Findings
**Aquatic and fish-related planned actions**
- Inventory aquatic habitats in the NCA to judge the suitability of the Saint David Cienega for [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]], and of the mainstem San Pedro for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]].
- Evaluate reintroducing Gila topminnow and desert pupfish to the Saint David Cienega and Young Block Ponds; if feasible, renovate the cienega to remove mosquitofish and restock (consistent with the 1984 Gila and Yaqui topminnow recovery plan).
- Evaluate reintroducing loach minnow and spikedace to the mainstem (consistent with the 1988 recovery plans).
- Evaluate reintroducing [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (called Colorado River squawfish in the document) and [[Razorback Sucker]] to Young Block Ponds and the mainstem (consistent with the 1988 squawfish recovery plan).
- Run public education meetings (Saint David, Sierra Vista) to build support and reduce illegal stocking of non-native fish.
- Survey streamside and aquatic habitats for two state-listed species, the leopard frog and the [[Mexican Garter Snake]].
- Inventory aquatic and terrestrial invertebrates to establish baseline data on the forage base and find rare species.
- Monitor aquatic and terrestrial habitat trends using the San Pedro Riparian Monitoring Plan, then formulate mitigating measures from the evaluations.

**Other planned studies and reintroductions**
- Intensive studies of gray hawk, green kingfisher, yellow-billed cuckoo and white-tailed deer, plus a visitor-use/wildlife interaction study.
- Surveys for bats (Sanborn's long-nosed bat) and rare plants (Huachuca water umbel).
- Evaluations of reintroducing northern aplomado falcon, Gould's turkey, masked bobwhite and ocelot, following BLM manual 6820.

**Management objectives (draft)**
- Increase habitat diversity for threatened and endangered native fish along the mainstem, especially deep pools and riffles with boulder, cobble and gravel substrates.
- Improve cienega wetland and existing pond habitat for native fish and favor their reintroduction.
- Increase nesting gray hawk territories from fifteen to twenty.
- Increase cottonwood/willow gallery forest acreage, canopy cover, and floodplain mesquite and sacaton habitats.
- Improve conditions for seven riparian obligate birds, including yellow-billed cuckoo, Bell's vireo and Lucy's warbler.

**Schedule**
- Comments due May 1, 1990; preliminary draft June 15, 1990; final HMP due September 1990.
