# Trammel Netting of Colorado Squawfish and Bonytails at Palm Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
A two-day trammel netting survey conducted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] at **Palm Lake** on the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] near Wickenburg, Arizona, on November 19–20, 1992, captured 12 [[Colorado Squawfish]], 13 [[Bonytail]], and approximately 125 [[Yellow Bullhead]]. All squawfish and bullheads were released on-site. The 13 bonytails, none of which bore prior PIT tags, were newly tagged and transported live to the [[Horsethief Refugia Ponds]] near Grand Junction, Colorado, with zero recorded mortality during transfer. This effort represents a targeted conservation collection supporting captive refugia stocking of an endangered native Colorado River Basin fish.

## Background & Description
This letter, authored by Frank K. Pfeifer (Project Leader, [[Colorado River Fishery Project]], U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Grand Junction, CO), documents a joint field collection effort with Chuck McAda and Mike Baker, coordinated with Dr. Wendel L. Minckley and staff at Arizona State University. The purpose was to survey and collect two federally endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes — the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, now commonly known as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]) and the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) — from an isolated lentic refuge population at Palm Lake, using trammel nets.

**Gear & Effort:**
- **Night 1 (Nov 19):** 4 trammel nets, total length 750 ft, fished ~5 hours.
- **Night 2 (Nov 20):** 3 trammel nets, total length 450 ft, fished ~3 hours.

**Catch Summary:**
| Species | Count |
|---|---|
| Colorado squawfish | 12 |
| Bonytail | 13 |
| Yellow bullhead | ~125 |

All squawfish and bullheads were released back into Palm Lake following processing. None of the endangered fish captured had pre-existing PIT tags, indicating this was likely a previously unsampled or naturally recruiting population within the preserve.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Palm Lake, situated within the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]], functions as an isolated refugium habitat supporting remnant populations of Colorado River Basin native fishes outside their historic mainstem range — a common strategy for endangered cyprinid conservation given extensive main-river habitat degradation. The co-occurrence of [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail]] with an abundant non-native/naturalized [[Yellow Bullhead]] population illustrates the mixed-species composition typical of such refuge ponds.

**Transport Protocol for Bonytail:**
- Water temperature: 6°C
- Salt (NaCl) concentration: 0.5% by weight
- Furacin (antimicrobial): 25 ppm
- Outcome: No mortality during transport to [[Horsethief Refugia Ponds]]

This standardized hauling protocol (cool water, salinity buffering, antimicrobial treatment) reflects best practices for minimizing stress- and injury-related mortality during live transfer of sensitive endangered cyprinids.

## Key Findings
- **12 Colorado squawfish** and **13 bonytail** were collected via trammel netting at Palm Lake over two nights (total netting effort: 1,200 ft of net across two sessions).
- **13 bonytail** were PIT-tagged and translocated to the [[Horsethief Refugia Ponds]] for conservation broodstock/refugia purposes; **zero mortality** occurred in transit.
- **None of the captured squawfish or bonytail bore pre-existing PIT tags**, suggesting an unmarked, possibly self-sustaining or previously unsampled population.
- Approximately **125 yellow bullhead** were also captured and released, indicating this species is abundant in the lake relative to the endangered target species.
