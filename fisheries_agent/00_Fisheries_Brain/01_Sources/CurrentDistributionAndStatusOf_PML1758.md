# Current Distribution and Status of the Little Colorado Spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*) in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document comprises a 1984–1985 status package for the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), a cyprinid endemic to the [[Little Colorado River]] drainage of eastern Arizona. It includes a cover letter to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (dated February 14, 1985), an internal Arizona Game and Fish Department memo (February 12, 1985), and the full report *Current Distribution and Status of Lepidomeda vittata in Arizona* by C. O. Minckley (Northern Arizona University, December 1984). The 1983 field survey surveyed 54 localities, found the species at 11 (nine previously reported, two new), and recommended Federal listing as Threatened with Critical Habitat designation. Principal threats are dams and diversions, groundwater pumping, exotic fishes and drought.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** Described by [[Edward Drinker Cope]] in 1874; a cyprinid of the tribe Plagopterini (Miller and Hubbs 1960). It occurs only in Arizona.
- **Historical range:** Originally known from the Little Colorado River and its northflowing tributaries (Silver, Show Low, Chevelon, East Clear and Nutrioso creeks). Four collection records existed before 1939.
- **Population history:** A 1960 survey found the species apparently exterminated across its known range; a single specimen was taken from Clear Creek in 1960 and a large population was found in East Clear Creek in 1961. Re-invasion occurred 1963–1966, followed by renewed decline and classification as endangered in 1973. A new population was discovered in lower Chevelon Creek in 1977.
- **Morphology and coloration:** Silvery sides with near-vertical dark lines, olivaceous-to-lead-grey back, white venter, and a conspicuous cream spot at the dorsal fin origin when viewed from above.
- **Reproduction:** Initial spawning surge in June with sporadic spawning in summer and autumn; females may spawn more than once a year. Fecundity estimated at 610–5,600 eggs (Minckley and Carufel 1967). Smallest ripe male recorded was 50 mm standard length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preferences:** Clear, flowing pools of medium depth over fine gravel; avoids the deepest, heavily shaded pools and shallow open areas. Large numbers occur in midwater of open pools with undercut banks or boulder cover.
- **Associated fishes:** In East Clear Creek, [[Roundtail Chub]], speckled dace, flannelmouth sucker, bluehead sucker, rainbow trout and brown trout. In lower Chevelon Creek, carp, fathead minnow, golden shiner, bluegill, green sunfish, flannelmouth sucker, Rio Grande killifish and channel catfish.
- **Survey methods:** Seines of 1.8 × 1.8 × 3.2 m or 0.9 × 0.9 × 3.2 m mesh; ten seine hauls per site; densities expressed as fish/m²; fish anesthetized with MS-222 for length measurements. Population status was rated poor (<10 fish), fair (10–20) or good (>20 with several year classes or evidence of reproduction).
- **Key localities (1983):** Little Colorado River near St. Johns and Springerville; Nutrioso Creek (Hunter Ranch and below [[Nelson Reservoir]]); East Clear Creek (two sites); [[Leonard Canyon]] (Dines Tank); lower Chevelon Creek (the Steps and Hugo Meadow Wildlife Area).
- **Threats:** Dams and diversions (Chevelon Canyon Lake, Bear Canyon Lake, Blue Ridge Reservoir, Woodruff Reservoir, Lyman Lake), groundwater pumping, irrigation diversion, logging-related sedimentation, exotic fishes, and drought. Parasitism by *Ichthyophthirius* sp. was observed in East Clear Creek in July 1983.

## Key Findings

**Distribution and status (1983 survey)**
- 54 localities surveyed; *L. vittata* persisted at 11 localities, 9 previously reported and 2 new (Nutrioso Creek, two sites).
- Status ratings:
  - **Good:** Little Colorado River at Springerville and St. Johns; Leonard Canyon (Dines Tank); lower Chevelon Creek; Nutrioso Creek below Nelson Reservoir.
  - **Fair:** East Clear Creek at Jones Crossing; Nutrioso Creek at Hunter Ranch.
  - **Poor:** East Clear Creek at the junction of Forest Service Roads 95 and 96 (one specimen in July, six in September).
- Reproduction was documented at most sites but not at Springerville (Little Colorado River), 8.0 km south of St. Johns, or in Nutrioso Creek.
- Lower Chevelon Creek at Hugo Meadow held "several thousand" young-of-the-year fish in one seine haul.

**Substrate and habitat**
- Substrate ranged from mud to gravel, sand, bedrock or combinations; the species was taken more often in pools than other habitats, in slow to moderate current.

**Conservation recommendations (Minckley 1984)**
1. List *L. vittata* as Federally Threatened and declare Critical Habitat for Chevelon, East Clear, Nutrioso and Leonard Canyon creeks and the Little Colorado River near St. Johns and at Springerville.
2. Establish annual monitoring of each population surveyed in 1983 (density, reproduction, population structure, associated species, status shifts).
3. Resurvey the Little Colorado Basin every five or ten years using the 1983 methodology.

**Agency position**
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department (Director Bud Bristow, letter to Regional Director Michael Spear) supported the threatened listing and the Critical Habitat designations; the internal memo by Jim Brooks concurred, citing a much-reduced range.
