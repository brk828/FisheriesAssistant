# Zuni Bluehead Sucker: Proposed Endangered Listing and Critical Habitat Designation (Federal Register, January 25, 2013) with Public Comment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This note covers two linked proposed rules published by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] on January 25, 2013 (Federal Register Vol. 78, No. 17): a proposal to list the [[Zuni Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus yarrowi*) as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and a proposal to designate critical habitat for it in New Mexico and Arizona. The Service finds the species threatened by Factors A (habitat destruction and modification), C (disease and predation), D (inadequate regulatory mechanisms), and E (other natural or manmade factors, including habitat fragmentation). The proposed critical habitat totals approximately 472 km (293 mi) across three units. The package also includes a formal public comment letter from [[Paul C. Marsh]], PhD, of Marsh & Associates, LLC, and Arizona State University, who judged both documents well written, comprehensive, and scientifically sound, while offering numerous corrections and suggestions.

## Background & Description

**Species description.** The Zuni bluehead sucker has a fusiform, slender body and a subterminal mouth. Most individuals do not exceed 20.3 cm (8 in) in total length, though the species has been known to exceed 25 cm (9 in). It has a bluish head, silvery-tan to dark green back, and yellowish to silvery-white sides. Breeding males develop intense black dorsal coloration, a bright red horizontal bar, and white abdomen, with tubercles on the rear fins and caudal peduncle.

**Taxonomy.** The first specimen was collected from the Zuni River near Zuni Pueblo in 1873. Morphological and genetic evidence support recognizing the Zuni bluehead sucker as distinct from both the [[Rio Grande Sucker]] and the [[Little Colorado River Bluehead Sucker]] (*C. discobolus*). The Service concludes it is a valid subspecies. Genetic analyses indicate that Kinlichee Creek fish share markers with New Mexico Zuni bluehead suckers, though these results were unpublished at the time.

**Status history.** The species was a Category 2 candidate in 1985, was dropped from candidacy in 1996, and was re-added to the candidate list in 2001. It holds a listing priority number of 3, reflecting imminent and high-magnitude threats. A 2004 petition contained no new information, and the species had already been found warranted for proposed listing.

**Note on this document.** The 2013 rule is a Federal Register proposal rather than primary research. The comment letter is a peer-review style critique. The content below reflects the proposals, cited data, and the reviewer's comments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and physical features.** The species occupies small desert streams, including isolated headwater springs and mainstem reaches, with clean, hard substrate, flowing water, and abundant riparian vegetation. Reported parameters include:
- Water velocities of 0 to 0.35 m/s, depths of 0.2 to 2.0 m, and water temperatures of 9.9 to 25.2 °C during surveys (year-round loggers recorded -3.2 °C to 24.7 °C).
- Spawning from early April to early June at 6 to 15 °C, peaking near 10 °C, over gravel beds.
- Fine sediment is the primary water-quality threat, since it smothers eggs, reduces algal food, and clogs substrate.

**Diet.** Adults and juveniles feed primarily on periphytic algae scraped from cobble, boulder, and bedrock surfaces. They also consume aquatic invertebrates, including caddisflies, mayflies, midges, and terrestrial insects. Larvae under 25 mm feed on diatoms, zooplankton, and dipteran larvae. The reviewer notes that diet preferences have not been established, because only gut contents were measured without quantifying food availability.

**Distribution.** The species historically occurred in the Little Colorado and Zuni River drainages. Its range has been reduced by over 90% in the past 90 years. It now persists in:
- Three semi-isolated populations over about 4.8 km (3 mi) in the upper [[Rio Nutria]] drainage, New Mexico (Agua Remora, Tampico Spring, Tampico Draw).
- Low-density populations in Kinlichee Creek, Black Soil Wash, Scattered Willow Wash, and related Arizona streams on the [[Navajo Nation]].
- Canyon de Chelly streams, including Wheatfields, Whiskey, Tsaile, Sonsela, and Crystal creeks, which are presence or absence records only.

The Zuni River, Rio Pescado, and Cebolla Creek are unoccupied historical range.

**Threats (Factor A).** The main threats are:
- **Water withdrawal** from wells, irrigation, and the proposed Tampico Springs Ranch subdivision (490 lots).
- **Sedimentation** from logging, livestock grazing, and road construction.
- **Impoundments and beaver dams**, which convert stream habitat into ponds.
- **Wildfire and ash flows**, given that wildfire frequency since the mid-1980s is nearly four times the 1970 to 1986 average.
- **Long-term drought** linked to climate change, with severe to extreme drought reported in occupied areas.

**Predation and competition (Factor C).** Nonnative green sunfish, fathead minnow, northern crayfish, and other predatory fishes threaten the species. Green sunfish dominance at Agua Remora is linked to limited recruitment. Black grub has been documented in the range but its population effects remain unclear.

**Regulatory context (Factor D).** The species is listed as endangered in New Mexico (Group 2), which prohibits direct take but provides no habitat protection. It is a candidate in Arizona, with no habitat protection. Both states classify it as a Species of Greatest Conservation Need with no regulatory force. The Forest Service lists it as sensitive, and the Zuni Pueblo prohibits fishing but not habitat loss. The Service concludes existing mechanisms are inadequate.

**Climate change.** Downscaled projections for the Southwest predict warmer, drier conditions, reduced snowpack, and increased drought severity. The Service concludes that climate change is currently a threat and will likely remain one.

## Key Findings

**Proposed listing (Rule 1):**
- The Zuni bluehead sucker is proposed as **endangered** under the Act. The Service found threatened status inappropriate because the range has contracted about 90% and threats are ongoing, widespread, and expected to continue.
- Threat Factors A, C, D, and E apply. Factor B (overutilization) is not a threat, since the species is not a game fish and collection is regulated.
- Public comments were requested, and at least three independent peer reviewers were to be sought.

**Proposed critical habitat (Rule 2):**
- Total proposed length: about **472 km (293 mi)** across three units:
  - **Unit 1, Zuni River Unit:** Subunit 1a (Headwaters), 74.2 km (46.1 mi), occupied; Subunit 1b (Mainstem), 107.8 km (67.0 mi), unoccupied but essential for connectivity.
  - **Unit 2, Kinlichee Creek Unit:** Subunit 2a, 86.9 km (54.0 mi), occupied; Subunit 2b (Red Clay Wash), 9.6 km (6.0 mi), historically occupied.
  - **Unit 3, San Juan River Unit:** Subunit 3a (Canyon de Chelly), 187.9 km (116.7 mi), occupied; Subunit 3b (Little Whiskey Creek), 8.9 km (5.5 mi), unoccupied.
- Primary constituent elements include dynamic flows, perennial or connective flow, suitable microhabitat, depths under 2 m, velocities under 35 cm/s, clear cool water (9.0 to 28.0 °C), no pollutants, riparian shade, an abundant invertebrate food base, and nonnative-free areas.
- Critical habitat is found prudent and determinable. The Service excluded Palisades Creek because it is a small, severely degraded tributary.
- Tribal lands within the proposal (primarily [[Navajo Nation]] and [[Zuni Pueblo]]) are being considered for exclusion under section 4(b)(2), with government-to-government consultation underway.
- Economic and other exclusions remain under evaluation. The rule is found not significant under Executive Order 12866.

**Key comments from [[Paul C. Marsh]]:**
- Overall praise: the documents are well written and scientifically sound, drawing on current credible sources.
- The "Diet preferences" statement is premature without quantitative food availability data.
- Several unsupported or unclear claims require citations or clarification, including the predator-related cover statement, the definitions of "recent" and "current" (which span 13 to 30 years), and the phrase "extirpated from these sites."
- Literature cited issues: several references are missing from the list (for example, Propst et al. 1986 and NMDGF 2007), several are uncited, and some dates, periods, or page citations are missing or incorrect.
- Taxonomy clarification: investigators correctly identified bluehead sucker, and later systematic or genetic work reassigned some specimens to the Zuni subspecies. They were not misidentified.
- The Kinlichee and Canyon de Chelly connectivity argument needs stronger genetic support before the Zuni subspecies assignment is extended to Tsaile, Sonsela, Crystal, and Whiskey creeks.
- The reviewer disagrees with exclusions based on other relevant impacts.
- Suggested improvements include a stream-length column in Table 1, numbered document pages, and language on how habitat fragmentation prevents genetic exchange.
