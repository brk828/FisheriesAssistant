# Cichlidae of the Genus Tilapia in Israel

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cichlidae

## Executive Summary
This talk-based review (L. Fishelson, Tel-Aviv University) describes the biology, breeding behaviour and early development of the four main *Tilapia* species of Israel. It contrasts bottom-spawners (*T. zillii*) with mouth-breeders (*T. aurea*, *T. nilotica*) and the intermediate *T. galilaea*. It also discusses egg predation as a possible reason for the low abundance of *T. galilaea*, and argues from embryology that mouth-breeding evolved from bottom-spawning ancestors.

## Background & Description
- Cichlids differ morphologically and physiologically from other Israeli freshwater fish families. They are closely related to marine Perciformes, especially the [[Pomacentridae]], with which they share mating behaviour, territoriality, brood care and embryological development, suggesting a common origin.
- Cichlids are tropical and subtropical in the Old and New World. Their tropical origin shows in their thermal preference for reproduction.
- Israeli tilapias probably spread from their tropical centre during the Upper Miocene and/or Upper Pliocene (Steinitz 1954). Israel lies near the northern edge of their range, likely reached via the [[Jordan Valley]] and the coastal plain. Low temperature probably limits further northward spread.
- Two reproductive modes exist: **mouth-breeders** (typified by [[Tilapia Nilotica]]) and **bottom-spawners** (in Israel, [[Tilapia Zillii]]).

**Species descriptions**
- **[[Tilapia Zillii]]**: the most common cichlid in Israel (Jordan System and coastal rivers). Mature fish have a red lower head and abdomen, blackish-green flanks, white-spotted dorsal and caudal fins, black pelvic fins, a bluish upper lip and white lower lip, and usually a persistent black "Tilapia mark" on the dorsal fin. Males have prolonged dorsal and anal fins. The author notes the possibility of a mixed population of two forms.
- **[[Tilapia Aurea]]**: the most common mouth-breeder, long confused with *T. nilotica*. It is bluish with a light silvery abdomen, reddish margins on the unpaired fins, and a red eye crossed by a dark line. It lacks regular white tail stripes. It is widespread in the Jordan System and coastal rivers.
- **[[Tilapia Nilotica]]**: reddish, especially when breeding, with regular vertical white stripes on the red caudal fin, a dark dorsal fin with black margin, and a red eye without a cross-line. It occurs in small numbers in the coastal rivers, mainly the [[Yarkon River]].
- **[[Tilapia Galilaea]]**: yellowish-silvery with an orange iris crossed by a black line and a black opercular spot (visible from 3 cm). Nuptial colour turns silvery with vertical dark stripes and a dark caudal fin. It is found in all water bodies of Israel.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Temperature**: minimal temperature for normal growth and development is above 16°C, and normal reproduction occurs above 22°C. Below 16°C tilapias become inactive, and breeding behaviour and colour change begin at about 20°C. A 2°C change within the 20°C range alters behaviour fundamentally.
- *T. zillii* prefers shallow, warm water rich in *[[Phragmites]]*, with pebble and sand bottoms. Males start reproductive behaviour above 20°C. At warm springs ([[Beth Shean]], [[Lake Kinneret]]) breeding begins about one month earlier.
- *T. aurea* breeding starts at 20–22°C.
- Species distribution: Jordan System and all Mediterranean coastal plain rivers.
- Study background: Liebman 1933, Trewavas 1942, Steinitz 1951, 1959, Ben-Tuvia 1959, Fishelson and Heinrich 1963.

## Key Findings
**[[Territoriality]] and pairing**
- *T. zillii*: males defend about 5–6 m² in open places and 2–2.5 m² in plant-covered areas. In the laboratory pair formation takes several days, and pair bonds are strong (changing a partner can take days to a week). Pairs dig a pit together, and the female spawns 600–1500 olive-green eggs in rows, attached by sticky threads. Both parents fan the eggs.
- *T. aurea*: in dense populations the minimum territory is about 0.7–1.0 m². Males without territory cannot breed and lose their biological potential. The male digs a pit before meeting a female. The female lays batches of 20–40 eggs, the male fertilises them, and she picks them up into her mouth immediately. Pair bonds are loose, so a male can mate with several females, even on the same day. Clutches may reach 2000 eggs.
- *T. nilotica* reproductive behaviour is quite similar to *T. aurea*.

**Intermediate behaviour of *T. galilaea***
- Pairs stay together for several days to two weeks. Females lay batches of 20–40 eggs but keep laying until the whole clutch is in the pit. Sticky threads glue the eggs into one mass. The eggs are left untouched for 10–15 minutes before being taken into the mouth by the male, the female or both.
- In Africa only the female takes the eggs, whereas in Israel the male tends to take them first. In *T. macrocephala* only males incubate.
- **Egg predation**: in captivity small *Haplochromis flavii-josephi* and *T. zillii* entered the territory undisturbed and ate eggs; only large tilapias were chased out. This may explain the lowest numbers and biomass of *T. galilaea* where tilapias coexist. It may also explain the gradual decrease in *T. galilaea* yield in [[Lake Kinneret]] as stocking with *T. aurea* fingerlings intensified. *T. aurea* is considered the more advanced species and may increase at the expense of *T. galilaea*.

**Development of young**
- *T. zillii*: embryos hatch after about 2 days. Three pairs of adhesive glands on the head attach them to the pit for 3–5 days, and they live on yolk. Swimming begins 5–6 days after hatching. Parents move the young by mouth. At 6–8 days the fry school 10–15 cm from the parents, begin feeding, and family bonds loosen.
- Mouth-breeders (*T. nilotica*, *T. aurea*): the female rolls the eggs in her mouth, which is necessary because stationary eggs have lipids that sink and destroy egg structure. Artificial incubation without movement failed, while bottom-spawner eggs developed normally. Hatching starts about 3 days after oviposition. At 25–27°C fry are first expelled after about 14 days. They retreat into the mother's mouth, or any dark space, when threatened (an innate hiding reaction). Fry pass from an endotrophic stage to an exotrophic feeding stage, then avoid larger fish including the mother.
- Removing clutches allowed one female to produce **11 clutches per year** in the Tel-Aviv University laboratory, with total egg number 10 times the usual. This resembles Red Sea pomacentrids.

**Evolution of mouth-breeding**
- Bottom-spawner embryos have adhesive glands that disappear on day 6–8. Two-day-old mouth-breeder embryos show non-active rudiments lost by day 4. The author concludes the ancestor was a bottom-spawner, and mouth-breeders are more advanced. "In mouth-taking" in dense populations may have gradually become "in mouth-keeping" through selective pressure.
