# Central Arizona Project FY98 Budget Meeting Memorandum - Fish Protection Funding Dispute

#ichthyology #endangered-species-act #gila-river #administrative-correspondence

## Executive Summary

This April 1997 memorandum from Bureau of Reclamation Area Manager Dennis E. Schroeder documents a contentious budget meeting regarding the Central Arizona Project (CAP) Fiscal Year 1998 appropriations. The Central Arizona Water Conservation District (CAWCD) recommended $6 million in cuts, including elimination of funding for the [[Gila River]] Fish Opinion implementation and Endangered Species Act compliance. The document reveals that CAWCD's FY97 budget recommendations had already resulted in Congress zeroing funds for Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs), potentially contributing to litigation by the Southwest Center for Biological Diversity. The memorandum exposes fundamental conflicts between water district political strategy and federal obligations for fish protection and endangered species compliance.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] is a major water conveyance system managed by the Bureau of Reclamation's Phoenix Area Office (PXAO). By 1997, the project had transitioned from construction to operations, with PXAO staff reduced from 700 employees (1991) to approximately 180 expected by FY98. CAP-specific staffing dropped from 404 employees (1991) to approximately 75 (1997), representing an 80% reduction over six years.

The CAWCD Board submitted a "Statement to the House Committee on Appropriations" (March 28, 1997) recommending cuts targeting "Other Project Costs-Fish Protection (Gila River Fish Opinion)" and noncontract line items. The meeting occurred following Senator Kyl's December 1996 admonition that FY97 budget disputes had been "an embarrassment" requiring resolution.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document references multiple endangered species and environmental compliance requirements:

- **[[Gila River]] Fish Opinion**: A biological opinion requiring implementation of Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs) for fish protection
- **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]**: Listed species requiring ongoing funding at [[Roosevelt Lake]]
- **[[Santa Cruz River]] Biological Opinion**: Separate ESA compliance effort in development
- **Environmental Enhancement and Mitigation Requirements**: Ongoing obligations at multiple CAP facilities

The memorandum notes that Roosevelt Lake activities included "continuing funding of Forest Service activities and the southwestern willow flycatcher, reasonable and prudent alternatives to implement" with 22% noncontract costs budgeted. CAWCD recommended these costs be "eliminated."

## Key Findings

**Budget Cuts and Fish Protection Funding:**
- CAWCD recommended $6 million total cuts to FY98 CAP budget
- At least $2.7 million targeted from Phoenix Area Office noncontract costs (50% of $5.4 million total)
- Remaining cuts targeted "Other Project Costs-Fish Protection (Gila River Fish Opinion)"
- Congress had already eliminated Gila River Fish Opinion RPA funding in FY97 at CAWCD's recommendation

**Endangered Species Act Compliance Risks:**
- Reclamation warned CAWCD was "miscalculating the risk of ignoring the Gila River Fish Opinion and the Environmental Species Act requirements"
- Lawsuit by Southwest Center for Biological Diversity "may have been avoided had the funding for the RPA's been approved in FY97"
- CAWCD strategy: "anticipates that a judge will deal with the Gila River Fish Opinion; therefore, nothing should be done (i.e., no implementation) in the interim"
- Congress zeroed FY97 funds for fish protection, described as "Risky, but it apparently is working"

**Political Strategy Over Scientific Compliance:**
Mr. Gammage's candid responses revealed CAWCD's approach:
- "Cannot separate the litigation from the budget issues" because "It gets attention!! It irritates the heck out of Reclamation and the Department!!"
- "Even if there is total agreement in the field that PXAO's budget is reasonable, the CAWCD will still take issue with it before Congress because they want the forum"
- "CAWCD Board is aware that the staff recommendations are not necessarily factual and do not have Reclamation input in many instances, and the CAWCD Board supports that approach"
- "CAWCD recognizes that third parties are being injured by its budget recommendations, but the CAWCD believes they have to be consistent in going after the noncontract costs"

**Impacts on Fish Protection Work:**
- FY97 budget delays prevented implementation of fish protection measures
- Work delays at Lake Pleasant and elsewhere on the project
- CAP Repository delayed 1 year, adding $300,000-$400,000 in costs (nonreimbursable feature)
- No new contracts issued at Lake Pleasant pending Congressional approval
- CAWCD supported "limited funds" for Santa Cruz Biological Opinion in FY98, characterized as "strictly a politically correct gesture for appearance sake"

**Inconsistent Treatment of Environmental Costs:**
- New Waddell: 40% noncontract costs deemed "appear excessive" despite construction, contract administration, design, and surveying activities
- Roosevelt Lake: 22% noncontract costs for Forest Service activities and flycatcher RPAs should be "eliminated"
- Indian Distribution Division: 7% noncontract costs "warrant scrutiny"
- Final CAWCD recommendation: cut all noncontract costs by 50% with "no rhyme or reason" or foundation

**Administrative Conclusion:**
Schroeder concluded: "I personally find it irresponsible of the CAWCD to casually recommend budget cuts that take away legitimate jobs and necessary CAP work to promote its narrow political agenda."
