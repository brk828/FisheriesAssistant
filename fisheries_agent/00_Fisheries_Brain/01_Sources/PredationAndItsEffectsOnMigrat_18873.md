# Predation and Its Effects on Migration and Speciation in African Fishes: A Comment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1964 scientific exchange in the journal *Proceedings of the Zoological Society of London*. It comprises a critique by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] of a paper by [[P. B. N. Jackson]] on the impact of predation by [[Tiger Fish]] (*Hydrocynus vittatus*), with further comments by [[P. H. Greenwood]], a reply by Jackson, a footnote and a 1964 postscript by Fryer. The debate centres on [[Worthington Hypothesis]], which holds that the large predators [[Nile Perch]] (*Lates*) and *Hydrocynus* have retarded speciation in African lakes. Fryer argues that these predators act like other predators, that predation facilitates speciation, and that predators cannot explain the upstream spawning migrations of African fishes. Jackson maintains that predation by *Hydrocynus* is a key controller of fish behaviour, including cover-seeking and migration. Greenwood agrees with Fryer's rejection of the predator-induced migration theory but argues that physico-chemical egg conditions and nursery cover are important.

## Background & Description

The paper is a formal comment with several contributors, followed by replies and a postscript. Its main subjects are:

- **Predators under review:** [[Nile Perch]] (*Lates* spp.) and [[Tiger Fish]] (*Hydrocynus vittatus*, synonym *H. lineatus*), a swift, open-water predator, with a usual maximum length of 18–20 cm in the cited work. The document notes *Lates* is "fully" a fast predator only in some species, and that *Luciolates* is also present in Lake Tanganyika.
- **Study organisms:** Mainly cichlids (including [[Tilapia]] and endemic [[Haplochromis]]), non-cichlids (clupeids, Bagrus, Clarias, mormyrids), and [[African Lungfish]] (*Protopterus aethiopicus*).
- **Literature:** The document cites about 60 references, including works by Greenwood, Corbet, Iles, Fryer, Jackson, Poll and Coulter. A full list is omitted here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Tanganyika:** The lake holds three species of *Lates* and two of *Hydrocynus* alongside abundant endemic speciation. Fryer argues that *Lates* species differ in habit: *L. microlepis* and *L. mariae* are partly pelagic, while *L. angustifrons* is littoral and benthic. Coulter (1962) reports that clupeid feeding is seasonal. Poll (1953) reports *H. lineatus* as "assez commun", whereas Jackson calls it relatively rare.
- **Lakes Albert and Rudolf:** Both have an essentially Nilotic fauna with few endemics of full specific rank. Fryer attributes this to recent connection with the Nile after desiccation, rather than to predation. Greenwood notes that Lake Edward had a typically Nilotic non-cichlid fauna throughout the Pleistocene.
- **Lake Victoria and Lake Nyasa:** These lakes have large species flocks of small fishes and numerous predators. Greenwood (1959a, 1959b) and Fryer (1959a, 1960) argue that predation reduces interspecific competition. Jackson argues that Nyasan predators are milder, with few reaching large sizes.
- **Cover and nurseries:** Jackson links the use of cover by small fishes (below about 20 cm) with predation risk from *Hydrocynus*. Coulter (1962) found Lake Tanganyika fish within *Ceratophyllum* beds between 6 and about 24 cm. Fryer (1961b) found predation on 8 cm *Tilapia variabilis* slight within cover.
- **Lake Victoria anadromes:** From Whitehead's (1959) data for 15 species with a good prima facie case for anadromy, Greenwood reports eight with a modal adult size below 20 cm, one with a mode below 20 cm but commonly larger individuals, and six with a mode well above 20 cm.
- **Egg conditions:** Greenwood argues that water flow keeps egg membranes clean and that bacteria and fungi are harmful in still water. Shaw & Aronson (1954) showed high mortality of non-rotated cichlid eggs.

## Key Findings

1. **Predation facilitates speciation (Fryer):** Fryer argues there is little factual evidence that *Lates* and *Hydrocynus* restrict speciation. He reports that small fishes make up most of the diet of predators in every lake studied, and that the effects of these predators match those of other predators. He argues predation helps isolate populations by confining small species to inshore waters.
2. **Lake-specific histories (Fryer, Greenwood):** Fryer argues that the faunas of [[Lake Albert]] and [[Lake Rudolf]] reflect short lacustrine history and recent colonization from the Nile. Greenwood adds that each lake must be considered in its own historical context, including topography, drainage and volcanism.
3. **Rejection of predator-induced migration:** Fryer argues that the upstream spawning migration cannot have been started by *Lates* and *Hydrocynus*, because the habit is worldwide (Indian cyprinids, European salmonids) and is an ancestral riverine habit. He proposes that upstream migration ensures dispersal over the whole colonizable river course. Greenwood accepts this refutation of the predator-induced theory.
4. **Greenwood's alternative mechanisms:** Greenwood argues that flowing water keeps eggs clean and oxygenated, that mouth-brooding and nursery selection solve egg problems in cichlids, and that cover is essential for post-larvae. He concludes it is unlikely that predators alone provided the initial stimulus for migration.
5. **Jackson's behavioural hypothesis:** Jackson argues that predation by *Hydrocynus* controls the behaviour of small fishes (cover use, shoaling, nursery use) and that the 20 cm size limit correlates with cover use. He argues that Nyasan predators are "milder", that endemic non-migrants lost migration behaviour, and that this facilitates speciation.
6. **Disputed anadromy data:** Jackson states that many anadromous species exceed 20 cm. Greenwood, using Whitehead's data, counts eight of fifteen with a modal adult size below 20 cm, which contradicts Jackson's claim.
7. **Predation and prey stability (Slobodkin; Fryer postscript):** Slobodkin's experiments with [[Hydra]] showed that predation pressure allowed two competing species to coexist, whereas one eliminated the other without predation. Fryer's 1964 postscript cites Mayr (1963) on predation impeding colonization of new niches, and notes that predation is not the only biotic regulator of population density.
8. **Unresolved points:** The participants agree that more field data are needed on cover use, anadromy and predator diet. They disagree on whether *Hydrocynus* is a key controller of behaviour and speciation, and on the relative mildness of Lake Nyasa predators.
