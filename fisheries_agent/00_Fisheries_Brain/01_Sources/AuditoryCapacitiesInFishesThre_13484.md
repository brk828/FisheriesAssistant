# Auditory Thresholds in the Blue-Striped Grunt and Behaviour of the African Cichlid *Pelmatochromis guentheri*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pomadasyidae #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This source combines two papers. The first, by [[William N. Tavolga]] and [[Jerome Wodinsky]], measures auditory thresholds in the [[Blue-Striped Grunt]] (*[[Haemulon Sciurus]]*) using [[Avoidance Conditioning]]. It tests 421 threshold determinations on 23 subjects and examines intra- and inter-individual variability. The authors conclude that thresholds fall with repeated testing experience and that individual differences are large, particularly below 300 c.p.s. The second paper, by [[Arthur A. Myrberg Jr.]], gives a descriptive account of the behaviour of the West African cichlid [[Pelmatochromis Guentheri]], based on 15 juvenile fish kept in aquaria. The excerpt contains only the introduction and methods; the behavioural results are not included.

## Background & Description

### Auditory Thresholds (Tavolga & Wodinsky)

Most earlier fish hearing studies tested only one or a few frequencies, or sought the highest detectable frequency. Complete [[Audiogram]]s were attempted for very few species, such as the [[Brown Bullhead]] and the [[Bull Shark]]. Earlier studies by the same authors covered nine marine teleosts, but each curve rested on two or three animals. The present study aimed to use more subjects and tests so that the threshold statistics would be more reliable.

The authors describe threshold variability on three levels: the spread of responses within one test series, intra-individual variation between test days, and inter-individual variation. They chose *H. sciurus* because it showed the greatest variability and the clearest separation of two sets of low-frequency thresholds.

Subjects were immature fish of 7 to 9 cm standard length, collected near the laboratory dock at Bimini and held in 2-gallon aquaria with running sea water. Of 40 animals, several died and some failed to learn the escape task. Eighteen were trained and tested fully, and five more were tested at only a few frequencies.

### Myrberg's Cichlid Study

*P. guentheri* is a West African cichlid that aquarists rarely keep. It differs from its congener *P. kribensis* in its behaviour and is distinguished within the genus by a papillose pad along the dorsal branchial arches. Its native habitat is coastal rivers and lagoons. Large numbers have been recorded in highly saline lagoons 100 to 200 m behind the coastal dunes at Cape Palmas, Liberia. The first importation to Germany, around 1913, came from the mouth of the Niger River. Earlier accounts describe mouth brooding, with the male taking the eggs into his mouth and not feeding until the young are released. The sources disagree on which parent provides this care.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Test conditions (grunt):** Trials used a two-compartment tank divided by a shallow barrier. Fish crossed the barrier to escape shock. A 5-gallon tank with plexiglass inserts and rubberized-hair damping gave a near-uniform sound field. Reverberation was negligible across 50 to 1,000 c.p.s. Ambient noise was 10 to 20 dB below the thresholds measured, so it was judged to have no effect. Sound levels were accurate to about ±1 dB at the fish's position.

**Husbandry (cichlid):** Fish were kept at 25 to 27 °C, with one tank occasionally dropping to 23 °C. Water had a hardness of 3 to 6 DH, a pH of 6 to 7, and salt content of 500 to 700 (units illegible in the scan). Illumination was fluorescent, and strobe-light photography supplemented direct observation.

**Growth (cichlid):** In the first year, adults reached an average total length of 115 mm in small to moderate aquaria. In a 6,000-litre tank, males approached 160 mm and females 145 mm.

## Key Findings

### Tavolga & Wodinsky: Auditory Capacity of *Haemulon sciurus*

- **Pooled thresholds:** Across 421 determinations on 23 subjects, thresholds from 50 to 200 c.p.s. averaged about 20 dB below one microbar. Above 300 c.p.s. the curve rose to about 39 dB above one microbar at 1,000 c.p.s.
- **Frequency pattern:** Thresholds at 50, 100, 150 and 200 c.p.s. did not differ significantly. The curve rose curvilinearly from 300 to 1,000 c.p.s.
- **Measurement accuracy:** Combined equipment and positioning error was about ±2.5 dB.
- **Experience effect:** Repeated testing lowered thresholds. In 83 of 126 repeat cases the threshold fell, with a mean change of −2.25 dB (t = 2.9, P < 0.005). Between the second and third tests the mean drop was −2.42 dB (38 of 55 cases). Between first and second tests on successive days it was −3.79 dB (t = 3.7, P < 0.0005). When the repeat test came after two or more days of testing other frequencies, no significant drop occurred.
- **Retention:** Once reached, the lowest threshold at one frequency was kept for many days. It did not transfer to other frequencies, which the authors cite as weak evidence of frequency discrimination.
- **Individual differences:** Inter-individual differences were significant (F = 2.8, P < 0.005). Standard deviations per frequency ranged from about 3.4 to 8.4 dB, and some animals differed by more than 10 dB. The differences were largest below 300 c.p.s. The authors could not link them to morphology or behaviour. Initial position in the tank may contribute.
- **Step size:** Two-dB and 5-dB staircase steps gave virtually identical mean thresholds, although the 5-dB steps produced larger variance.
- **Response behaviour:** Avoidance latency rose as threshold was approached (1.54 s versus 2.36 s, t = 5.21, P < 0.0005). Intertrial crossings fell by an average of 1.18 per trial (P < 0.02). The authors argue that this contradicts [[Signal Detection Theory]] and may reflect attention shifting to the signal or a stress response.
- **Hypotheses:** The authors weigh two explanations for the earlier apparent threshold shift. One is a switch from the [[Inner Ear]] and [[Swim Bladder]] system to the [[Lateral Line System]]. The other is simple learning from testing experience. The data favour the second, but the authors do not discard the first, citing a morphological duality in fish hearing.

### Myrberg: Behaviour of *Pelmatochromis guentheri*

- **Sample:** The study used 15 juvenile fish from a rarely imported species. Observations covered the first, second and third generations.
- **Diagnostic trait:** The genus is held together mainly by a well-developed papillose pad on the dorsal branchial arches, which is only slightly developed in *Serranochromis*.
- **Reproduction in literature:** Earlier reports describe mouth brooding, and disagree on whether the male or both parents brood the young.
- **Scope:** The excerpt ends before the behavioural results are presented, so the paper's findings cannot be assessed from this text.
