# Intergovernmental Agreement Between Arizona State University and Arizona Game and Fish Commission

#ichthyology #marine-biology #wildlife-management #education #internship

## Executive Summary

This intergovernmental agreement, drafted in February 1994 and effective through April 30, 1995, establishes an internship program between [[Arizona State University]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Commission]]. The agreement facilitates placement of university students in field positions with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to provide practical wildlife research, wildlife management, and administrative experience. Students receive compensation at minimum wage plus reimbursement for living expenses and travel costs. The program aims to enrich curriculum and provide future Department employees with field experience prior to completing academic training.

## Background & Description

The agreement operates under the legal authority of the Arizona Board of Regents to enter contracts and establish curricula on behalf of [[Arizona State University]], and the Commission's contracting power pursuant to A.R.S. § 17-231.B.7. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] maintains research and wildlife management activities at various sites throughout Arizona and operates administrative support activities at central offices. The internship program addresses the Department's desire for future employees to gain field experience during their academic training while providing university students with on-the-job experience in wildlife-related fields.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The internship positions encompass wildlife research, wildlife management, and related administrative programs conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] at various field sites throughout Arizona. Students must be enrolled in curricula leading to Bachelor's or Master's degrees and pursuing majors in [[Wildlife Biology]] or related administrative areas. The program provides exposure to Department methods and objectives across multiple branches of wildlife management operations.

## Key Findings

**Department Obligations:**
- Accept students for training and provide variety of facilities and benefits
- Provide job descriptions for all available positions
- Offer interpretation and training in methods and objectives of each branch
- Provide on-the-job experience in wildlife research, wildlife management, or related administrative programs
- Compensate interns at minimum hourly rate
- Reimburse students for living expenses and transport/travel costs between assignments via per diem payments
- Select appointees without discrimination based on race, ethnic background, creed, national origin, sex, or age

**University Responsibilities:**
- Refer all qualified candidates to Department without discrimination, including honorably discharged veterans
- Provide advisor and internship supervisor to evaluate written reports
- Recommend grades and credits for students
- Advise accepted students of volunteer status under Department direction and supervision

**Student Eligibility Requirements:**
- Enrollment in Bachelor's or Master's degree program on substantially full-time basis (usually 12 semester hours)
- Major field of study related to [[Wildlife Biology]] or related administrative area
- Recommendation by appropriate university staff
- Legal age requirement

**Agreement Conditions:**
- Either party may terminate with 30 days written notice
- Agreement must be filed with Arizona Secretary of State before becoming effective
- Parties agree to arbitration for monetary disputes of $15,000 or less after exhausting administrative reviews
- Compliance with Executive Order 75-5 (nondiscrimination order)
- Subject to cancellation by Governor pursuant to A.R.S. § 38-511
- All records subject to inspection and audit by State for five years after contract completion per A.R.S. §§ 35-214, 35-215, and 41-1279.04
