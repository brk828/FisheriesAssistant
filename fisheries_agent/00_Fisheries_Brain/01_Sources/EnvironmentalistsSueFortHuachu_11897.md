## Executive Summary

This document is a news article rather than a primary scientific source, reporting on a 1990s lawsuit filed by the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] and 13 allied environmental organizations against [[Fort Huachuca]], a U.S. Army installation in Arizona. The suit alleges violations of the federal [[Endangered Species Act]] due to habitat destruction affecting five federally listed fish species and one bird species, the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], all associated with the [[San Pedro River]] riparian corridor. While the article contains no direct morphological or ecological data collected by the authors, it identifies the fish taxa of conservation concern and describes the anthropogenic (groundwater pumping, surface water diversion, urban growth) pressures threatening their habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat**: The [[San Pedro River]] and its associated riparian zone, described as "the only relatively intact ecosystem in the Southwest," located near [[Fort Huachuca]] and the [[Sierra Vista, Arizona|Sierra Vista]] region.
- **Hydrological threats**:
  - Groundwater pumping by the military reservation and the city of Sierra Vista is intercepting water that would otherwise sustain surface base flows in the San Pedro River.
  - Surface water diversion in the Garden Canyon area on the post further reduces flow contributions to the river.
  - The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (per supervisor Sam Spiller) projected that current water-use rates would result in complete de-watering of the San Pedro River within approximately 20 years.
- **Conservation designation**: The affected area includes the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]], underscoring its recognized ecological significance for endangered aquatic and riparian species.
- **Co-occurring endangered taxon**: The [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], a riparian-obligate bird, shares the same threatened habitat corridor, indicating that habitat degradation impacts both aquatic and terrestrial/riparian species assemblages simultaneously.
