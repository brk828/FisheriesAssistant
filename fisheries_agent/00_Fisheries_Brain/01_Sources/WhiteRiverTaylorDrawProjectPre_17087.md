# White River Taylor Draw Project pre- and postimpoundment fish community investigations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document is the **Literature Cited** section of a 1986 final report prepared by Patrick J. Martinez (Colorado Division of Wildlife) for the Water Users Association No. 1 and the Colorado River Water Conservation District. The report addresses fish community impacts associated with the **Taylor Draw Project**, a hydroelectric/reservoir impoundment on the **White River** near Rangely, Colorado. Rather than presenting raw data itself, the extracted text is a bibliography compiling foundational research on the ichthyofauna of the [[White River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Green River]], and broader [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], with a strong emphasis on endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] biology, distribution, and the ecological consequences of stream regulation and impoundment (i.e., the Taylor Draw Reservoir/hydroelectric station).

## Background & Description

No direct morphological measurements, meristic data, or anatomical descriptions are present in this excerpt (it is a reference list). However, the cited literature indicates that morphological and life-history studies were conducted on the following taxa relevant to this system:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) — Larval identification and life-history morphology addressed in Haynes et al. (1984), Snyder (1981 — larval Cypriniform key), and Seethaler (1978).
- **[[Bluehead Sucker]]** (*Catostomus discobolus*, family [[Catostomidae]]) — Maturity and fecundity characterized by McAda and Wydoski (1983).
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** (*Catostomus latipinnis*, family [[Catostomidae]]) — Growth and reproductive morphology described by McAda and Wydoski (1985).
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Population and habitat monitoring referenced via Prewitt et al. (1978) and Wick et al. (1981, 1985), implying morphological/taxonomic identification work in monitoring protocols.
- **Channel Catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus*, family Ictaluridae) — Early life-history data from Lemons (1955) "Channel cat study."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The cited works collectively define the ecological context of the [[White River]] fish community and the impact of impoundment/regulation:

- **Riverine Regulation Effects**: Holden (1979), Simons (1979), and Ward & Stanford (1979) provide foundational ecological theory on how stream regulation (damming, flow alteration, thermal modification) affects channel morphology, zoobenthos, and riverine fish assemblages — directly relevant to predicting **Taylor Draw Reservoir** impacts.
- **Reservoir Colonization Dynamics**: Mahon and Ferguson (1981) document species composition, growth, and condition of fishes invading a newly formed reservoir — a key predictive analog for post-impoundment community shifts at Taylor Draw.
- **Habitat Suitability Modeling**: McConnell et al. (1984) supply Habitat Suitability Index (HSI) models for coolwater/coldwater reservoir planning, likely used to model the post-dam [[Taylor Draw Reservoir]] environment.
- **Endangered Species Distribution & Migration**: Multiple sources (Tyus 1984; Tyus & McAda 1984; Tyus et al. 1982; Wick et al. 1981, 1983, 1985; Archer & Tyus 1984; Radant et al. 1983) establish the spawning migration, homing behavior, and habitat preferences of the [[Colorado Squawfish]] across the **Green**, **White**, and **Yampa Rivers** — critical baseline data for assessing dam-related habitat fragmentation.
- **Baseline Faunal Surveys**: Carlson et al. (1979) and Lanigan & Berry (1981) document fish and macroinvertebrate distribution in the White and Yampa Rivers, forming the pre-impoundment baseline community structure.
- **Site-Specific Aquatic Assessment**: Behnke (1981) and the Ecosystem Research Institute (1983) conducted aquatic biology assessments directly at/near the Taylor Draw Reservoir site and broader White River, addressing fish distribution, habitat, and food preferences.
- **Water Quality/Flow Impacts**: Clyde (1980) modeled downstream sediment and water flow impacts of the proposed dam; May (1970) examined biota and chemistry of Piceance Creek, a regional tributary system.
- **Regulatory/Engineering Context**: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (1982a, 1982b) EIS documents and Western Engineers (1985) FERC license application frame the legal/engineering basis for the Taylor Draw hydroelectric project.

## Key Findings

- The report situates the **Taylor Draw Project** within a well-documented regional research program (the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Colorado River Fishery Project**) focused on endangered Colorado River basin fishes, particularly the [[Colorado Squawfish]].
- A substantial body of prior work (1970s–1980s) established pre-impoundment baseline data for fish distribution, spawning behavior, migration, and larval identification in the White, Yampa, and Green Rivers — enabling comparative pre/post-impoundment analysis.
- Reservoir ecology literature (Mahon & Ferguson 1981; McConnell et al. 1984) suggests the report likely predicts fish community reorganization (species composition, growth, condition changes) following creation of the **Taylor Draw Reservoir**.
- Regulatory-effects literature (Holden 1979; Ward & Stanford 1979; Simons 1979) indicates the report's analytical framework draws on general stream regulation ecology to anticipate downstream thermal, hydrological, and morphological changes from the dam.
- Endangered species concerns are central: repeated citation of Colorado squawfish spawning, migration, and habitat monitoring studies (Archer & Tyus 1984; Tyus & McAda 1984; Wick et al. 1981–1985) underscores that a primary motivation for the pre/post-impoundment study was assessing risk to this federally listed species.
- Sucker species life-history data (McAda & Wydoski 1983, 1985) on the [[Bluehead Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] indicate these catostomids were also tracked as indicator species for basin-wide fish community health.
