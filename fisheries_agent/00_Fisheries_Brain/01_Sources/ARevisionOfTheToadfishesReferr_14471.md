# Revision of the Toadfishes Referred to Porichthys and Related Genera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #batrachoididae

## Executive Summary

This 1939 taxonomic revision by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Leonard P. Schultz]], published in the *Proceedings of the United States National Museum*, reassesses the toadfishes long assigned to the American genus [[Porichthys]]. Based on museum specimens from the U.S. National Museum, the University of Michigan and Stanford University, the authors erect one new genus, [[Aphos]], recognize [[Nautopaedium]] as a separate Atlantic genus, and describe two new species, [[Porichthys Myriaster]] and [[Porichthys Analis]]. The work rests on comparisons of photophore arrangement, fin-ray counts, dentition and body coloration, with anal-ray counts emphasized as the most useful character for separating species.

## Background & Description

Toadfishes of the family [[Batrachoididae]] in this group share several diagnostic traits: two dorsal spines and one strong opercular spine, all solid and without connected poison glands; a small subopercle without spine; some caninelike teeth; no scales; and four lateral lines.

Most species in the group bear rows of many [[Photophores]] that follow the multiple lateral lines. The authors found that photophore presence does not consistently track lateral-line number, so they judged it insufficient to support the family Porichthyidae proposed by Ribeiro (1915).

Taxonomic changes made in the revision:

- **[[Aphos]] (new genus):** Type species *Aphos porosus* (originally *Batrachus porosus* Valenciennes). It is the only member of the group lacking photophores and occurs in Peru and Chile. The authors treat *Porichthys afuerae* as a synonym. Its photophore absence contradicts an earlier report, which the authors attribute to confusion between pores and photophores.
- **[[Porichthys]]:** Restricted to the Pacific coast, with 5 or 6 species, from southern Alaska to Colombia.
- **[[Nautopaedium]]:** Retained for *Nautopaedium porosissimum*, the single widely distributed Atlantic species. Its generic separation rests mainly on dentition: palatine teeth point forward in adults, and the adult male's posterior premaxillary teeth also point forward.

Species are distinguished by fin-ray counts, branchiostegal photophore pattern, pleural photophore extent, presence of dorsal saddles or blotches, and anal-fin margination. Table 1 (anal-ray counts) was the authors' main diagnostic tool.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The genus is centered on the [[Gulf of California]] and [[Cape San Lucas]] region, where *P. notatus*, *P. margaritatus* and two or three additional forms co-occur.

- **Habitat partitioning:** *P. myriaster* is the characteristic toadfish of muddy and sandy bays in [[Southern California]] and the outer coast of Lower California. Along open shores it lives in shallower water than *P. notatus*. The only *P. myriaster* specimen known from deeper than 25 fathoms was one adult taken at 69 fathoms.
- **Spawning behavior:** *P. notatus* migrates into the intertidal zone of rocky reefs to spawn, from about Point Conception northward. *P. myriaster* apparently does not, and is described as more a bay than a reef inhabitant.
- **Bathymetric range of *P. notatus*:** From the intertidal zone to as deep as 145 fathoms. It is common in bays from central California northward and largely replaced in southern bays by *P. myriaster*.
- **Geographic range of *P. notatus*:** From Sitka, Alaska, to the Gulf of California, occurring almost as far south as Cape San Lucas.
- **Aphos habitat:** Specimens were collected from Peru and Chile, including Lobos Islands, Independencia Bay, Callao and Valparaíso. The authors note that the material was largely very small individuals.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic outcomes**

- *Aphos porosus* is the sole species of the new genus *Aphos*, which lacks photophores. The authors consider *P. afuerae* a synonym and find no head-proportion basis for separating it from *P. porosus*.
- *Porichthys myriaster* (new species) is distinguished by a U-shaped forward-directed branchiostegal photophore commissure, larger needlelike palatine teeth, conspicuous juvenile saddles, and a dark-margined anal fin. The holotype (U.S.N.M. no. 8488) is 306 mm standard length and 347 mm total length, collected by Cassidy at San Diego.
- *Porichthys analis* (new species) is known from two specimens dredged in the Gulf of California (holotype 95.5 mm standard length, U.S.N.M. no. 46645; one paratype, U.S.N.M. no. 106503). It has a V-shaped branchiostegal photophore row, a pleural photophore row extending to the caudal fin, and 36 anal rays.
- *Porichthys notatus* shows geographic variation. Anal-ray counts are highest in California and decline toward both the north and south. Lower California and Gulf populations have slightly fewer anal rays and retain saddles longer.
- *Porichthys margaritatus* is linked to the Gulf of Fonseca and Panama region. The authors suggest that the name may be misapplied to the species called *nautopaedium*. Their analysis also flags a need for specimens from the Cape San Lucas region.
- *Porichthys greenei* is a small species from the Panama coast, distinguished by conjoined vertical fins, simple lateral-line pores, and rounded pectoral fins.
- *Nautopaedium porosissimum* is distinguished mainly by palatine tooth direction and the adult male's enlarged teeth. Its published descriptions vary because of age variation and sexual dimorphism.

**Other conclusions**

- The genus *Porichthys* (with its photophores) is not sufficiently distinct to justify the family Porichthyidae.
- Several earlier records of *P. notatus*, *P. porosissimus* and *P. margaritatus* from the Gulf of California and Central America are regarded as misidentifications, and some need re-examination.
- An undescribed *Porichthys* species, represented by three young specimens (U.S.N.M. no. 3004) from Cape San Lucas with 27–28 anal rays, was left unnamed pending new material.
