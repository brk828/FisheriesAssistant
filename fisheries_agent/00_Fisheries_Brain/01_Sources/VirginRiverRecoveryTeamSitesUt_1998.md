# Virgin River Recovery Team Sites - Utah (1998)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae #virgin-river-chub #woundfin

## Executive Summary
This document consists of field survey records and data figures compiled by the [[Virgin River Recovery Team]] for monitoring stations along the Virgin River in Utah during 1998. The source material is a heavily degraded OCR scan of handwritten field notes (Spring 1998) and typed site-occupancy tables (Fall 1998), accompanied by two length-frequency histograms for the [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Woundfin]] populations. Due to the poor quality of the original scan, much of the narrative field-note content is illegible; however, the quantitative survey structure and population length-distribution data remain partially interpretable. The document reflects ongoing endangered species monitoring efforts tied to the [[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Program]].

## Background & Description
Specific morphometric descriptions of individual specimens are not legible in the source text. However, the length-frequency histograms provide population-level size structure data:

- **[[Virgin River Chub]]**: Total body length measurements recorded in 5mm size bins, ranging from approximately 56mm to 196mm+ total body length, with the sampled population showing multiple size classes present (suggesting multiple age cohorts detected in Fall 1998 sampling).
- **[[Woundfin]]**: Total body length measurements recorded in 5mm size bins, ranging from approximately 46mm to 70mm+ total body length, indicating a narrower observed size range in this sampling event, consistent with a smaller-bodied cyprinid species.

Both species belong to the family [[Cyprinidae]], native minnows adapted to the harsh, turbid, and flow-variable conditions of the Virgin River system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The Virgin River is a warm-water, turbid, flashy tributary system within the [[Colorado River Basin]], characterized by highly variable discharge, fine sediment substrates, and fragmented flow regimes due to water diversion structures.
- **Site Distribution (Fall 1998)**: Survey data reference multiple numbered stations (12, 13, 14, 15) with recorded site codes, some indicating presence ("w/ fish") and others absence ("w/o fish") of target species at a given site — a pattern typical of patchy distribution surveys used to assess population fragmentation and habitat occupancy along the river continuum.
- **Co-occurring Species**: The [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Woundfin]] are both federally listed endangered fishes endemic to this system, and their co-occurrence at monitoring stations is a key indicator of habitat health and recovery program success.
- **Threats Context**: Both species are historically imperiled by habitat alteration, non-native species introduction, and water diversion — consistent with the rationale for organized recovery team monitoring reflected in this document.

## Key Findings
- The [[Virgin River Recovery Team]] conducted at least two monitoring events in 1998 (Spring and Fall) across multiple designated stations in Utah.
- Fall 1998 site data indicate variable presence/absence of fish across sampled stations (e.g., stations 12–15), suggesting non-uniform distribution of target species within the surveyed reach.
- Length-frequency analysis of [[Virgin River Chub]] in Fall 1998 shows a broader size range (~56–196+ mm), implying detection of multiple age/size classes and potentially successful recruitment.
- Length-frequency analysis of [[Woundfin]] in Fall 1998 shows a narrower, smaller size range (~46–70+ mm), which may indicate either a recent recruitment pulse of juveniles/young-of-year or limited capture of larger adults during this sampling period.
- The original source material's poor OCR quality significantly limits extraction of detailed quantitative counts, exact site coordinates, and narrative observations; only structural and partial numerical data could be confidently reconstructed.
- This document serves as a historical snapshot within the longer-term dataset used by the [[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Program]] to track endangered cyprinid population trends in Utah segments of the Virgin River.
