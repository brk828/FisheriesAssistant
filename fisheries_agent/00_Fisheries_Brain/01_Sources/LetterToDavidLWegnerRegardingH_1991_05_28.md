## Executive Summary
This document is a logistical correspondence from Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) to David L. Wegner of the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies program, dated May 1991. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the letter outlines field research support needs for an ongoing [[Humpback Chub]] population study in the [[Little Colorado River]] gorge within the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem. Key logistical elements include computer data access, emergency radio communications, secure equipment storage, PIT-tagging supplies, and the establishment of two remote field base camps (Fowal Canyon and Salt Trail Canyon). The letter provides indirect but valuable insight into contemporaneous (1991) field methodologies used to study this endangered cyprinid in a remote, physically challenging riverine environment.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) are provided in this letter, as it is administrative/logistical in nature rather than a species description or survey report. However, the described research methodology (use of **PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags** and **scanners**) implies that individual-level tracking of body size, growth, and movement was a central objective of the associated [[Humpback Chub]] research program. The intent to tag "all native fishes" suggests a broader multi-species morphometric and demographic dataset was being compiled concurrently, likely including other endemic Colorado River basin cyprinids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary Study Habitat:** [[Little Colorado River]] gorge, a tributary canyon system of the [[Colorado River]] within the [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona — a critical spawning and rearing habitat for the [[Humpback Chub]].
- **Field Camps:** Two base camps were being installed to support fieldwork across distinct habitat zones:
  - **Fowal Canyon** base camp (installed 09–13 June 1991), coincident with installation of an **experimental weir** — indicating active monitoring of fish movement/passage within a constrained channel section.
  - **Salt Trail Canyon** base camp (planned for installation the week of 01 July 1991), marking initiation of a second phase of field data collection.
- **Environmental Constraints:** The remote, canyon-bound terrain necessitated helicopter-based logistical support (USBR helicopters) and hand-held radio systems for both emergency and routine inter-camp communication, underscoring the physically demanding and hazardous nature of accessing [[Humpback Chub]] habitat in this system.
- **Multi-species Consideration:** The mention of PIT-tagging protocols being extended to "all native fishes" reflects an ecosystem-level approach to native fish conservation monitoring in the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River, not solely a single-species focus.

## Key Findings
- **Research Infrastructure (1991):** ASU's humpback chub research program required access to USGS mainframe computing resources for data management, indicating a quantitative, data-intensive field monitoring effort.
- **Tagging Program:** Three [[PIT Tag]] scanners were in hand, with a full complement of 2,000 PIT tags expected imminently — supporting large-scale individual fish identification and recapture studies.
- **Tagging Policy Urgency:** The unresolved question of whether to tag non-target native fish species was flagged as time-sensitive; the research team proposed to proceed with tagging **all native fishes** starting with experimental collections during 09–13 June 1991, absent objection — reflecting proactive, precautionary data collection to avoid loss of baseline information.
- **Experimental Weir:** Installation of an experimental weir at the Fowal Canyon camp (June 1991) suggests use of physical fish-passage or capture structures to monitor movement, likely related to [[Humpback Chub]] spawning migration studies in the [[Little Colorado River]].
- **Storage & Equipment Needs:** A dedicated, secure ~200 sq ft storage facility was requested for sensitive electronic equipment (data loggers, generators) and general field gear (nets), reflecting the technical sophistication of the field program.
- **Communication Logistics:** Reliable radio communication (for emergencies and coordination with USBR helicopters) was identified as a critical but lagging logistical component, with an inter-camp radio system not expected to be operational until later in the 1991 field season — highlighting recurring challenges in remote riverine fieldwork logistics.
- **Broader Context:** This letter is emblematic of the collaborative framework between academic researchers (ASU) and federal resource agencies (Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies) established to support recovery efforts for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] under environmental compliance associated with [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations.
