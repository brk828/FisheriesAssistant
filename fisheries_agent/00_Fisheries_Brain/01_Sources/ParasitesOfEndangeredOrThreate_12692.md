## Executive Summary

This progress report documents a deworming (anthelmintic) intervention and parasitological investigation of tapeworms infecting the endangered [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) held at the U.S. Willow Beach Fish Hatchery, Arizona. Eight adult squawfish were treated with Di-N-Butyl Tin Oxide (DNDTO) over three escalating doses (Oct 4–6, 1979), resulting in the expulsion of numerous cestode strobilae and fragments. Based on scolex morphology and proglottid anatomy, the recovered tapeworms were tentatively identified as **[[Ophiotaenia fragilis]]** (Proteocephalidea: Proteocephalidae), a species previously reported from squawfish in Lake Powell (Buckner 1972). Notably, tapeworm-like segments were also observed in the raceway of hatchery-reared squawfish that had never consumed wild prey, raising important questions about the parasite's life cycle and whether it can be completed via a single intermediate host (likely a copepod) present in the hatchery's unfiltered river water supply.

## Background & Description

**Host (*Ptychocheilus lucius*):**
- Adult specimens maintained in hatchery raceways; exact size/age not specified for treated fish, though hatchery-reared comparison fish were six years old.
- Fed juvenile (~4 inch) rainbow trout as part of medicated feeding regimen.

**Parasite (cestode, tentatively *Ophiotaenia fragilis*):**
- All recovered specimens were **immature**; only **four scoleces** were recovered in total.
- Diagnostic features observed in near-mature proglottids: testes appear to be **segregating into two large lateral fields** — a character used to distinguish *Ophiotaenia* from *[[Proteocephalus]]* (e.g., *P. ambloplites*).
- Recovered worms ranged from small fragments/pieces to long intact strobilae.
- Specimens were rinsed in frog Ringer's solution, compressed between glass plates, and fixed in Alcohol-Formol-Acetic (AFA) fixative.
- Staining trials: Harris' Hematoxylin proved superior to Gatenby's Alcoholic Borax-Carmine and Semichon's Acetic-Carmine for revealing internal morphology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host habitat:** *P. lucius* is native to the Colorado River basin; hatchery specimens held in raceways supplied with **unfiltered river water**, which retains natural copepod populations (e.g., *Cyclops* spp.) — a likely route for parasite transmission.
- **Parasite transmission pathway (proposed):** Modeled on the life cycle of the congener *[[Ophiotaenia filaroides]]* (Mead & Olsen 1971), which infects *Ambystoma tigrinum* (tiger salamander) as adult worms, using *Cyclops vernalis* as an intermediate host:
  - Metacestodes develop in copepods within 8–9 days (25–26°C), already infective for the final host.
  - Fully developed plerocercoids develop within 10–11 days, also infective.
  - Metacestodes are histotrophic in the final host; plerocercoid histotrophy is variable.
- **Implication for squawfish:** If *O. fragilis* shares this life cycle pattern, infection of hatchery-reared squawfish fed only synthetic food suggests the parasite may require **only a single intermediate host** (a copepod) rather than a complex multi-host cycle, since these fish had no access to wild prey items other than the copepod-containing hatchery water.
- **Comparative parasitology:** Contrasts with *Proteocephalus ambloplites*, reported by Vanicek & Kramer (1969) from wild squawfish in the Green River, Utah — suggesting possible geographic or host-specific variation in proteocephalid cestode fauna among *P. lucius* populations.

## Key Findings

1. **Successful chemotherapeutic deworming protocol**: DNDTO administered via medicated trout capsules (escalating doses: 480 mg → 960 mg → 1120 mg over 3 days) effectively induced expulsion of cestodes from eight adult *P. lucius* within hours of each feeding.
2. **Tentative species identification**: Recovered tapeworms are provisionally identified as *[[Ophiotaenia fragilis]]* based on scolex morphology and bilateral testes segregation pattern, distinguishing them from *Proteocephalus ambloplites* reported in other squawfish populations.
3. **Immaturity of specimens limits definitive taxonomy**: All recovered worms were immature with only 4 scoleces total recovered, preventing conclusive species confirmation.
4. **Consistency with prior record**: Findings align with Buckner (1972), who recovered 39 tapeworms (only 1 mature) from a wild *P. lucius* hindgut at Lake Powell, AZ (1967), also identified as *O. fragilis*.
5. **Novel life-cycle hypothesis**: Observation of tapeworm segments in raceways housing hatchery-origin (never wild-fed) squawfish suggests the *O. fragilis* life cycle may be completable with a single intermediate host (copepod), analogous to *[[Ophiotaenia filaroides]]* in tiger salamanders (Mead & Olsen 1971).
6. **Taxonomic history note**: The species was originally described as *Crepidobothrium fragile* (Essex, 1929) from channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) in Illinois, later reassigned to genus *Ophiotaenia*.
7. **Conservation relevance**: This work directly informs parasite management for a federally endangered/threatened cyprinid fish species undergoing captive propagation efforts in Arizona.
