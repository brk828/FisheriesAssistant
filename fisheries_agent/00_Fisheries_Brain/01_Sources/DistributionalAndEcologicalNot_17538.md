# Distributional and Ecological Notes on the Halfbeaks of the Eastern Gulf of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hemiramphidae

## Executive Summary

This study revises the Mexican distribution of halfbeaks in the genus [[Hyporhamphus]] along the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean coasts of Mexico. It addresses long-standing misidentification in the literature, largely caused by confusion over [[Hyporhamphus Unifasciatus]] (including its synonym *H. roberti*). The authors examined museum specimens from UANL, UNAM, IPN and ECO-CH, applied the diagnostic characters of [[Banford and Collette 1993]], and produced a distribution map and a provisional identification key for the three eastern Mexican species. The paper reports new Mexican records of [[Hyporhamphus Meeki]], clarifies the range of *H. unifasciatus*, and documents the first marine record of the freshwater species [[Hyporhamphus Mexicanus]].

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context**
- [[Hyporhamphus Meeki]] was described by [[Banford and Collette 1993]] from the northern Gulf of Mexico and western North Atlantic. Those authors examined 60 specimens in seven lots from four localities in Mexican waters.
- *H. unifasciatus* was restricted by Banford and Collette to the northern form, ranging from Florida south through the southern Gulf of Mexico, the West Indies and Uruguay. Its synonym *H. roberti* is treated the same way.
- *H. unifasciatus* records from the eastern Pacific were treated as an undescribed species. Re-identification of those records was deferred to a future paper.
- *H. mexicanus* was previously known only from freshwater, from the [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]] and [[Rio Usumacinta]].

**Materials and methods**
- Specimens came from the UANL (Facultad de Ciencias Biologicas, Monterrey), UNAM-P (Instituto de Biologia, Mexico City), IPN/ENCB-P (Escuela Nacional de Ciencias Biologicas, Mexico City) and ECO-CH (Colegio de la Frontera Sur, Unidad Chetumal) collections.
- Diagnostic characters followed the standard methods of Hubbs and Lagler (1947), as applied by Banford and Collette (1993): total gill rakers on the first and second arches, pectoral rays, the ratio of preorbital length to orbit diameter, and scales in the lateral series and anal rays.
- Meristic ranges are given as totals, with usual counts in parentheses.
- A field character is the width of the longitudinal streak relative to orbit diameter.

**Species descriptions (as reported)**

- **Lateral streak:** In *H. unifasciatus* it widens gradually toward the tail, from pupil to eye diameter. In *H. meeki* it is as wide as the pupil and widens abruptly to twice its width just before and below the dorsal fin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and salinity**
- *H. unifasciatus* is euryhaline. Its Tamaulipas, Veracruz and Yucatan localities are coastal lagoons or estuaries with relatively turbid water, except Mocambo Beach. The Campeche locality (Muelle Los Piratas, Ciudad Campeche) is open and sandy, influenced by submerged phreatic freshwater springs, and is therefore considered brackish.
- *H. meeki* is also euryhaline. The coralline [[Isla De Lobos]] is its only Mexican record from strictly marine, reef waters. The Yucatan Peninsula records are from areas with freshwater influence. All other collections are from coastal lagoons or estuaries, usually turbid.
- *H. mexicanus* was taken in a beachside pool just below the high tide mark, about 1 km east of the mouth of the [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]]. Salinity was not measured, but the water tasted very salty, and no freshwater source was nearby. The catch also included nine other euryhaline species and one barracuda.

**Co-occurrence (sympatry)**
- *H. meeki* and *H. unifasciatus* occur together in several lagoons: [[Laguna Madre]] (Tamaulipas), [[Laguna De Tamiahua]] (Veracruz), and Laguna Sontecomapan (Veracruz).
- They have also been collected together at Indian River Lagoon, Florida, and at Ensenada Cucharas and Ensenada Tigre (Laguna de Tamiahua), and at Playa Margaritas, Celestun (Yucatan).

**Geographic distribution**
- *H. unifasciatus* ranges from [[Laguna Madre]] (Tamaulipas) around the Gulf of Mexico to the eastern and southwestern Florida Peninsula, through the Caribbean Sea, and south to the Rio de la Plata, Uruguay.
- *H. meeki* is mostly northern. It occurs through the Gulf of Mexico (most Mexican states, though not yet recorded from Tabasco), around Florida, and north to Cape Cod-area localities, Passamaquoddy Bay, New Brunswick and the Carolinas. The [[Laguna De Bacalar]] record (Quintana Roo) is the first from the Caribbean region.
- *H. mexicanus* is known from the [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]] and [[Rio Usumacinta]] drainages and the neighboring Gulf of Mexico. Its Chiapas and Tabasco records are from freshwater.

**Literature and identification problems**
- Records from Tamaulipas (Gomez-Soto and Contreras-Balderas 1988) are a composite of *H. unifasciatus* and *H. meeki*.
- Veracruz records under *H. unifasciatus* and *H. roberti* often included *H. meeki*. Most unassigned literature records probably contain a mixture of species and cannot be evaluated without matching museum catalogue entries.

## Key Findings

- **Three species confirmed in eastern Mexico:** The *H. unifasciatus* complex comprises *H. unifasciatus*, the recently described [[Hyporhamphus Meeki]] (western Atlantic), and an undescribed eastern Pacific species. *H. mexicanus* is a third eastern Mexican species.
- **New *H. meeki* records:** The study adds Mexican records of *H. meeki* from Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Yucatan and Quintana Roo. Earlier records were known only from United States waters and the Yucatan coast.
- **First Caribbean record of *H. meeki*:** Specimen ECO-CH 3086 (1 specimen, 145 mm SL) was collected at Playa El Arenal, Laguna de Bacalar, Quintana Roo.
- **Gill raker maximum:** The gill raker count of *H. meeki* rose to a maximum of 43 (UANL 7724, Laguna Madre at Boca de Catan), the highest recorded for the species in this study.
- **Largest *H. meeki* specimen:** The maximum size is 232 mm SL (IPN P-2478, Barra de Sontecomapan, north of the lagoon).
- **Marine record for *H. mexicanus*:** The species, previously known only from freshwater, was recorded for the first time from a marine tide pool on the Gulf coast 1 km east of the mouth of the [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]]. The authors also report a few new inland localities.
- **Discrepancy in *H. mexicanus* size data:** The beach specimen is listed as 188 mm, yet the stated maximum SL for the species is 118 mm. The source does not resolve this, so the maximum should be treated with caution.
- **Gill raker range for *H. meeki* differs between sections:** The recognition section gives 31-43 (32-39) for the first arch, while the key gives 31-40, usually 32-37. Both are reported here as published.
- **Mexican distribution map and key:** The authors provide a distribution map (Figure 1, based on Mexican collections only) and a provisional key to the eastern Mexican *Hyporhamphus* species, separating them by fin scalation, gill raker count, pectoral ray count and lateral band width.
- **Key separation:** The key first separates species by scales on the dorsal and anal fin membranes (present in *H. unifasciatus* and *H. meeki*, absent in *H. mexicanus*), then uses gill raker counts, pectoral rays and streak width to separate *H. unifasciatus* from *H. meeki*.
