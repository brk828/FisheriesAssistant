## Executive Summary
This document outlines a multi-part conservation and research strategy by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation to protect and study the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) population in the [[Grand Canyon]] and [[Little Colorado River]]. The plan addresses population status, dam-operation impacts, taxonomic ambiguity within the genus *Gila*, and coordination with other endangered fish recovery efforts (e.g., [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]]). Central themes include the effects of Glen Canyon Dam tailwater temperatures on native fish recovery, competition with nonnative ("exotic") fishes, and the need for genetic/taxonomic clarification of *Gila* forms (humpback chub, bonytail, roundtail chub).

## Background & Description
- Direct morphological description of *Gila cypha* is not detailed in this text; the source instead emphasizes that **morphological characteristics alone are insufficient** to resolve taxonomic boundaries between related *Gila* forms.
- Three forms within the genus *Gila* are referenced as taxonomically problematic:
  - **Humpback chub** (*Gila cypha*) — federally endangered
  - **Bonytail** (*Gila elegans*) — federally endangered
  - **Roundtail chub** (*Gila robusta*) — not federally listed
- Proposed resolution methods include:
  - Principal components analysis (morphometric)
  - Electrophoresis (protein/allozyme analysis)
  - Mitochondrial DNA analysis
- These studies aim to clarify whether these forms warrant separate listing, consolidation, or delisting under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat**: Colorado River mainstem within the Grand Canyon and the [[Little Colorado River]], the latter serving as a critical spawning and recruitment area.
- **Thermal regime**: Cold tailwater releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] are identified as a limiting factor — current temperatures are considered unsuitable for reintroduction of other rare native fishes (squawfish/[[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) in the Grand Canyon reach.
- **Life history factors under study**:
  - Growth, reproduction, movement, and general life history of humpback chub
  - Seasonal importance of the Colorado mainstem vs. Little Colorado River to spawning, recruitment, and movement
  - Larval drift dynamics from the Little Colorado River into the colder Colorado River mainstem, with associated **thermal shock risk**
- **Biotic interactions**: Both direct and indirect competition between humpback chub and nonnative ("tailwater exotic") fish species is a key concern, to be evaluated via literature review and field studies, and cross-referenced with Upper Basin exotic fish studies.
- **Population structure**: Currently a single known self-sustaining population dependent on the Little Colorado River; management plans propose establishing a second, independent population within the Grand Canyon via stocking.

## Key Findings
1. **Four-year status study** planned by Reclamation to assess distribution, abundance, and life history of humpback chub in the Grand Canyon, correlating dam operations (fluctuating, high, and low flows) with habitat requirements.
2. **Impact analysis** of operational dam releases on chub and habitat is required; if impacts are found, mitigation recommendations and benefit quantification will follow.
3. **Temperature studies** (literature + field data) will evaluate the prudence of modifying [[Lake Powell]] release temperatures, including assessing thermal shock risk to larval chubs drifting from the Little Colorado River into the mainstem.
4. **Coordination** of Grand Canyon findings with ongoing [[Yampa River]]/[[Green River]] endangered fish studies.
5. **Long-term monitoring program** to be established alongside research to track annual changes in chub abundance and reproductive success, designed for potential independent continuation.
6. **Second population establishment**: Reclamation will attempt to stock a second, self-sustaining humpback chub population in the Grand Canyon (independent of the Little Colorado River), contingent on habitat suitability findings; stocking would proceed annually for up to five years, discontinued if unsuccessful.
7. **No recovery action planned** for other rare native fishes (squawfish, bonytail, razorback sucker) in the Grand Canyon absent modification of Glen Canyon Dam thermal releases.
8. **Taxonomic clarification initiative**: A dedicated study under the Upper Basin Recovery Implementation Plan will use PCA, electrophoresis, and mitochondrial DNA analysis to resolve species/subspecies boundaries within *Gila*, incorporating Little Colorado River/Grand Canyon humpback chub data.
9. **Captive propagation**: Reclamation will ensure *Gila cypha* from the Little Colorado River/Grand Canyon are included in basinwide assessments of holding facility and artificial propagation needs; hatchery fish would support both reintroduction and research.
10. **Interagency habitat protection**: Reclamation will collaborate with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[National Park Service]], [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[Navajo Nation]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to develop management strategies protecting humpback chub habitat in the Little Colorado River from catastrophic events or adverse habitat modification.
