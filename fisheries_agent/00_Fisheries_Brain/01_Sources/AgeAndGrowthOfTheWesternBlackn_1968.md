# Age and Growth of the Western Blacknose Dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*) in Doe Run, Meade County, Kentucky

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by Donald C. Tarter (1968) investigates the age and growth dynamics of the [[Western Blacknose Dace]] (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*), a short-lived cyprinid, in [[Doe Run]], a spring-fed tributary of the Ohio River in Meade County, Kentucky. Based on 3,358 specimens collected across three stations, the study combined the [[Scale Aging Method]] and [[Length-Frequency Analysis]] to establish discrete age classes (Age Group 0 through III). Key findings include a length-weight relationship demonstrating positive allometric growth (n = 3.20), seasonal and sexual variation in the [[Coefficient of Condition]] (K), and a detailed mapping of scale ontogeny relative to fish size. Growth in length was most rapid in the first two years of life, while growth in weight showed a delayed but accelerating pattern, peaking in the second and third years.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** *Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris* Agassiz, a subspecies of the [[Blacknose Dace]] within the family [[Cyprinidae]].
- **Size Range:** Total length observed ranged from 8 mm (smallest fry) to 124 mm (largest adult specimens).
- **Length Conversion:** Standard length (SL) to Total length (TL) conversion factor established from 274 specimens:
  $$ SL = \frac{TL}{1.23} $$
- **Scale Ontogeny (Squamation Pattern):**
  - No scales present on dace < 19 mm TL.
  - At **19 mm TL:** First cycloid scales appear along the lateral line on the caudal peduncle.
  - Scale development proceeds **anteriorly** from the caudal peduncle, with more rows developing *below* the lateral line than above.
  - At **21 mm TL:** Scale pattern extends beyond the origin of the dorsal fin.
  - At **23 mm TL:** Scale pattern reaches the pectoral fin area.
  - At **26–27 mm TL:** Fish is completely scaled.
  - Annuli identified via **crowding of circuli** and **"cutting over" of circuli** near the scale's lateral margin (per Lagler, 1956).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Doe Run]] is described as a large torrent spring rising near Ekron, Meade County, KY, flowing ~3.5 miles into Doe Valley Lake (a 500+ acre impoundment) before continuing to the [[Ohio River]].
- **Population Structure:** The study area supported three complete age classes (0, I, II) with a minor presence of Age Group III individuals, indicating this is characteristic of a short-lived species suited to spring-fed stream environments.
- **Sampling Constraints:** High seasonal discharge (winter) and secretive spawning behavior (spring) led to inadequate sampling during certain months, causing erratic growth data points for Age Groups I and II during those periods.
- **Diet/Condition Link:** Seasonal decline in male condition during winter correlated with a documented high percentage of empty stomachs (cross-referenced to Tarter, in press), suggesting winter feeding stress.
- **Reproductive Ecology:** Coefficient of condition peaked in both sexes just prior to spawning (spring), followed by a decline post-spawning, indicative of resource allocation to gonadal development.

## Key Findings

### Length-Weight Relationship
- Formula: $$ W = 3.42 \times 10^{-6} L^{3.20} $$
  - W = weight (g), L = total length (mm)
- High correlation coefficient: **r = 0.90**
- Exponent (n = 3.20) > 3.0 indicates dace become **relatively heavier-bodied** as they grow longer (positive allometry).

### Coefficient of Condition (K)
- Formula: $$ K = \frac{W \times 10^5}{L^3} $$
- **Sexes Combined (n=3,358):** Mean K = 0.82 (Range: 0.46–1.06)
- **Sexed Specimens (n=743):**
  - Females Mean K = **1.22** (higher condition)
  - Males Mean K = **1.04**
  - Combined Mean = 1.13 (Range: 0.94–1.57)
- K value increases with age/length, confirming fish gain weight proportionately faster than length as they mature.
- **Seasonal Sexual Dimorphism:** Males showed sharp condition decline in early winter; Females showed sudden condition rise in early fall and maintained better condition through winter/early spring.

### Age and Growth Determination
- **Method:** Length-frequency histograms (2-mm groups) verified against scale annuli counts.
- **Age Groups:** Designated by Roman numerals (0, I, II, III) based on annuli count. Overlap between older age classes (I/II) required scale verification to supplement length-frequency data.

### Growth in Length (Table 2 Summary)
| Year of Life | Total Length (mm) | Increment (mm) | % Increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 47 | 47 | — |
| II | 88 | 41 | 87% |
| III | 104 | 16 | 18% |

- 45% of total length (relative to Year III) achieved by end of Year I; 85% achieved by end of Year II.
- Young-of-year (Age 0) averaged 10 mm in June, growing to 32–39 mm by Sept–Nov, with growth stalling over winter (~38 mm by early spring).

### Growth in Weight
- Weight growth curve is antithetical to length: very slow in Year I, then rises sharply in Years II and III.
- At end of Year III (10.6 g = 100%):
  - Only **9%** of total weight gain achieved by end of Year I.
  - An additional **50%** achieved by end of Year II.
  - An additional **41%** achieved during Year III.
- Percentage weight increase during Years II and III was **6x and 4x greater**, respectively, than the percentage increase in length during the same periods, highlighting the decoupling of linear growth from mass accumulation in later life stages.
