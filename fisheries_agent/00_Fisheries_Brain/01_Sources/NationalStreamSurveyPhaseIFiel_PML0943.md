# National Stream Survey, Phase I Field Operations Report (1986)

#limnology #freshwater-ecology #stream-chemistry

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Project Summary, published September 1988 as EPA/600/S4-88/023, reports field operations of the [[National Stream Survey]] (NSS). The NSS was a synoptic chemical survey of streams in the [[Mid-Atlantic Region]] and southeastern United States that were thought to be potentially susceptible to [[Acid Deposition]]. It was conducted during spring 1986 and had three components: the [[Phase I Survey]], the [[Screening Survey]], and the [[Episodes Pilot Survey]].

The report covers survey planning, protocol development, personnel requirements, field operations, and logistics. Of 479 streams visited, 447 were sampled. Samples were shipped at 4 °C and received within 24 hours by a central processing laboratory in Las Vegas, Nevada. The document is a field-operations and methods report rather than a study of aquatic organisms, so no fish or other biota are described.

## Background & Description

The NSS was part of the EPA's National Surface Water Survey. Its purpose was to characterize stream chemistry in regions expected to have low [[Acid Neutralizing Capacity]] (ANC) and relatively high acidic deposition. Results were intended to determine the percentage, extent, and location of streams that were already acidic and those susceptible to acidification (low-ANC streams).

The three components were:

- **Phase I Survey (mid-Atlantic):** The area was bounded roughly by the Catskill and Pocono Mountains to the north, the northern margin of North Carolina to the south, the western boundaries of Pennsylvania and West Virginia to the west, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. A total of 276 stream reaches were scheduled, each to be sampled twice during spring baseflow to quantify temporal variance within the season.
- **Screening Survey:** This covered areas thought to receive lower acidic deposition, including the southern Appalachians, the Piedmont, the Ozark and Ouachita Mountains, and parts of the Florida Panhandle and Peninsula. Screening streams were sampled only once. Protocols matched Phase I except for hydrologic measurements and sampling frequency.
- **Episodes Pilot Survey:** This ran on a subset of Phase I streams and replaced routine sampling during rain events. Its goal was a preliminary assessment of the frequency, duration, and characteristics of storm episodes in the mid-Atlantic states, along with evaluation of sampling designs and logistics for future episode studies.

For logistics, the mid-Atlantic Phase I and Episodes region was split into an upper and lower mid-Atlantic subregion, and the Screening area into southeastern and Arkansas-Florida subregions. Field work used four subregions, each containing roughly the same number of streams.

Techniques for the NSS, except hydrologic measurement protocols, were developed in the earlier [[National Stream Survey Phase I Pilot]], conducted in 1985 on a subset of Phase I streams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling timing:** A scheduling priority based on spring leafout (phenology) was applied across subregions. This aimed to keep sampling consistent among subregions and to reduce the influence of external factors on stream chemistry during the season of maximum plant growth. Florida was an exception because leafout was nearly complete before the survey began.
- **Site selection:** Subregion boundaries were drawn around areas that historical water-quality data suggested were predominantly low in ANC. In the mid-Atlantic, streams in the lowest ANC strata were selected with higher probability. Phase I and Screening streams were chosen without regard to accessibility. The Episodes Pilot used a model-based design targeting streams with low or moderate ANC and small to moderate watersheds.
- **Base sites:** Each subregion had a base site, chosen for proximity to study streams, access to express courier and commercial air freight, accommodations, and banking.
- **Stream exclusions:** Of 479 streams visited, 32 could not be sampled. These were dry, tidal, inaccessible, or had conductivity above 500 µS/cm.
- **Holding times:** Earlier NSSPS holding-time experiments indicated that samples from diverse surface waters could be held for several days under controlled conditions without major chemical change. Mobile field laboratories were judged infeasible given the large geographic area.

## Key Findings

**Survey outcomes**
- The survey was completed on schedule with an excellent safety record.
- 479 streams were visited and 447 were sampled.
- Only nine episode streams were sampled, during six rain events, because of an unusually dry spring and severe time constraints.
- Most samples reached the central processing laboratory within 24 hours of collection.

**Field protocols**
- Each sampling team had two people and visited an upstream and a downstream site at each stream. Teams sampled one or two streams per day, depending on distance from the base site and access.
- Each subregion had a base coordinator, who ran field operations and scheduling, and a logistics coordinator, who handled permissions, supplies, and cooperator contacts.
- One team member checked and recalibrated instruments and measured pH, conductivity, and dissolved oxygen. The second collected water samples, blanks, and duplicates and took hydrologic measurements.
- Routine samples comprised a 21-L bulk sample plus syringe subsamples for pH, dissolved inorganic carbon, and inorganic monomeric aluminum. Water was pumped through tubing with a battery-powered peristaltic pump into four 60-mL polycarbonate syringes fitted with gastight fittings to avoid atmospheric exposure.
- Samples were kept cool with refrigerant packs during transport. Blanks and duplicates followed the quality assurance plan.

**Episode sampling**
- Forecasts of approaching storm fronts triggered a switch to episode sampling.
- Stage, cumulative rainfall, pH, temperature, conductivity, and dissolved oxygen were measured on arrival and every 30 minutes during the rising stage.
- Water samples were collected at four points: base stage (before rain), rising stage (when pH fell at least 0.3 units below base pH), peak stage (when the staff gauge stopped rising between two successive readings), and falling stage (when the stream dropped to one-third of its total peak rise).
- Flow was measured four times during each event.
- Phase I sampling resumed 12 hours after events of 8 hours or less, or 24 hours after longer events, and only after streams were checked for high turbidity, high flow, or other signs of storm impact.

**Recommendations**
- The report includes a detailed evaluation of episode sampling with recommendations for future investigators, and records observations of NSS field operations.
