# Follow-up Work Assignments from Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal administrative memo from W.L. Minckley of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), dated December 12, 1989, distributing follow-up tasks after a recovery team meeting. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the text outlines conservation management actions concerning three imperiled desert fish taxa: the [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Desert Pupfish]]. Key themes include recommendations against downlisting the Gila topminnow under the Endangered Species Act, the need for updated genetic studies to inform delisting/downlisting criteria, removal of potentially hybridized pupfish populations, coordination with Mexican biologists on transboundary populations, and archiving of species-specific literature. The memo assigns specific tasks to team members (Stefferud, Marsh/Sada, Hendrickson, Dahlem, Divine) reflecting a multi-institutional recovery planning effort.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan) are presented in this source. The document is purely administrative/managerial in nature, focused on conservation planning logistics rather than descriptive ichthyology. Genetic characterization is referenced only in the context of future work needed to support taxonomic and conservation decisions (see [[Key Findings]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All three focal taxa—[[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Desert Pupfish]]—inhabit fragmented, isolated desert aquatic habitats (springs, streams, and artificial refugia) characteristic of the arid American Southwest and northern Mexico (Sonoran Desert drainages).
- The [[Desert Pupfish]] population at **Tohono Chul** (Arizona) is specifically flagged as a site of concern due to suspected **hybridization**, threatening genetic integrity of the population and necessitating removal and restocking with fish of known, pure genetic lineage.
- Cross-border ecological continuity is implied by the plan to coordinate with **Mexican biologists** regarding pupfish populations in Mexico, suggesting shared drainage systems or subspecies ranges spanning the U.S.–Mexico border.
- The [[Yaqui Topminnow]] is ecologically and taxonomically distinct enough from the [[Gila Topminnow]] to warrant separate recovery plan treatment, being folded into a broader "Yaqui fishes recovery plan" rather than the Gila topminnow's dedicated plan.

## Key Findings
- **Downlisting Recommendation Rejected**: The recovery team recommended that the [[Gila Topminnow]] **not** be downlisted at the time, pending development of new, more rigorous downlisting/delisting criteria for the revised recovery plan.
- **Taxonomic/Planning Split**: The [[Yaqui Topminnow]] is to be moved out of the current Gila topminnow-focused recovery plan and incorporated into a separate, in-preparation **Yaqui fishes recovery plan**; the [[Gila Topminnow]] will receive its own standalone recovery plan.
- **Genetic Research Priority**: Urgent (ASAP) genetic studies were called for on the [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] to establish scientifically defensible downlisting and delisting criteria for future recovery plans.
- **Hybridization Management**: A management action was recommended to remove a potentially hybridized [[Desert Pupfish]] population at **Tohono Chul** and replace it with individuals of verified, non-hybridized genetic stock — an early example of applied conservation genetics in desert fish management.
- **Literature Archiving Effort**: The team planned to consolidate and archive all desert pupfish literature gathered during the recovery planning process at an appropriate scientific institution, with duplicate copies retained for U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) files.
- **Binational Collaboration**: Explicit plans were made to engage Mexican biologists to incorporate data on Mexican pupfish populations into the recovery plan, reflecting the transboundary distribution of the species.
- **Institutional Coordination**: Task assignments were distributed among team members (Stefferud, Marsh/Sada, Hendrickson, Dahlem, Divine) reflecting a structured, multi-agency approach typical of Endangered Species Act recovery planning under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
