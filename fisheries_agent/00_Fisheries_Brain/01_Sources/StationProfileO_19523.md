## Executive Summary

This document is a **station profile** for the **Private John Allen National Fish Hatchery** (originally the Tupelo National Fish Hatchery), a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service warmwater production facility located in Tupelo, Mississippi. Established under Federal authority in 1901 and operational since 1904, the hatchery has historically supplied warmwater sportfish for stocking programs across the southeastern United States. It was renamed in 1982 to honor Congressman [[John Allen]], who secured original appropriations for the facility. The hatchery's historic manager's residence was added to the National Register of Historic Homes in 1990.

Rather than a single-species biological study, this profile documents the hatchery's mission, infrastructure, funding, and multi-species production/distribution data across fiscal years 1990–1992, supporting mitigation, restoration, and recreational fisheries programs in Mississippi, Tennessee, South Carolina, and Louisiana.

## Background & Description

No morphological or anatomical data on the fish species themselves are provided in this source. The document is administrative/operational in nature, focusing on hatchery infrastructure rather than species biology. Species produced include:

- **[[Paddlefish]]** (*Polyodon spathula*) — a primitive chondrostean species
- **[[Striped Bass]]** (*Morone saxatilis*) — anadromous/riverine sport and forage species
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*) — primary warmwater sportfish
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** and **[[Blue Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus* spp.)
- **[[Bluegill]]** and **[[Redear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis* spp.) — panfish for farm pond stocking

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** The hatchery utilizes 15 earthen ponds supplied by a natural spring, simulating warmwater pond and reservoir conditions suitable for centrarchid and ictalurid propagation.
- **Production purpose by species:**
  - **Paddlefish, largemouth bass, channel catfish, blue catfish** — reared for mitigation stocking of **[[Tennessee Valley Authority]]** impounded waters.
  - **Striped bass** — reared for restoration of river systems draining into the **[[Gulf of Mexico]]**, supporting anadromous population recovery.
  - **Largemouth bass, bluegill, redear sunfish** — supplied to **Farm Pond Programs** in Mississippi and Tennessee, supporting private landowner pond fisheries.
- **Disaster recovery role:** Largemouth bass were distributed to South Carolina following [[Hurricane Hugo]] (1989) and to Louisiana following [[Hurricane Andrew]] (1992), illustrating the hatchery's role in post-disturbance ecosystem/fishery restoration.
- **Distribution range:** Federal waters, state waters, and private waters across Mississippi, Tennessee, and South Carolina.

## Key Findings

- **Historical continuity:** Operating continuously since 1904, making it one of the longer-standing National Fish Hatcheries in the southeastern U.S., originally reliant on rail transport for interstate fish distribution.
- **Infrastructure growth:** Expansion occurred in the mid-1950s with additional ponds and a fish holding house (20 holding tanks).
- **Multi-species production model:** The hatchery simultaneously supports mitigation (TVA), restoration (Gulf of Mexico river systems, hurricane-impacted states), and recreational/private stocking (Farm Pond Programs) — a diversified conservation and management portfolio uncommon among single-mission hatcheries.

- **Funding trend (in thousands of dollars):** Operations funding increased steadily from $173.7K (1991 actual) to an estimated $204.6K (1994), while maintenance funding fluctuated ($86.0K → $46.0K → $127.0K), alongside a modest increase in FTE staffing (3.8 → 4.8 → 4.8).
- **Institutional/political context:** The hatchery's continued funding reflects support from the Mississippi congressional delegation, including Senators [[Thad Cochran]] and [[Trent Lott]], and Representative [[Jamie L. Whitten]].
- **Related programs:** Data connect to broader USFWS mitigation and restoration frameworks, including [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] reservoir mitigation and Gulf of Mexico anadromous fish restoration efforts.
