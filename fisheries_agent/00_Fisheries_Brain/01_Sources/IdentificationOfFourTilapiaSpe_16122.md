# Identification of Four Tilapia Species from Lake Kinneret by Scale Form

#ichthyology #marine-biology #tilapia

## Executive Summary

This technical note by [[Chervinski]] (Fish and Aquaculture Research Station, Dor, Israel) presents a dichotomous key for identifying four tilapiine forms from [[Lake Kinneret]]: [[Sarotherodon Galilaeus]], [[Oreochromis Niloticus]], [[Oreochromis Aureus]], and their hybrid. Identification rests on the shape of body scales. The work addresses a practical problem: the native [[Oreochromis Aureus]] population is small, and stocked [[Oreochromis Aureus]] and hybrids make field separation difficult. The author advises using several scales per identification.

## Background & Description

- **Native tilapiine fauna of Lake Kinneret** (after [[Ben-Tuvia]], 1981): six taxa are listed: [[Tilapia Zillii]], [[Sarotherodon Galilaeus]], [[Oreochromis Aureus]], [[Tristramella Simonis]], [[Tristramella Sacra]], and [[Astatotilapia Flaviijosephi]].
- **Native status of O. aureus:** the species is native but persists only as a rather small population without stocking.
- **Stocking history:** [[Oreochromis Aureus]] and its hybrids with [[Oreochromis Niloticus]] have been introduced, which created the need to distinguish native from introduced fish.
- **Scale morphology in adult tilapia:** adult *Sarotherodon* and *Oreochromis* have [[Cycloid Scales]]. Each scale has a focus, and grooves (radii) run from or near the focus toward the margin.
- **Scales as identification tools:** scales have been used as an auxiliary characteristic in fish identification and classification. They also persist in archaeological and paleontological deposits, fish stomachs, bird nests and sediments, where they may be the only intact fish remains.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Positional variation:** scales differ by body region. Scales from the head, the lateral line, areas adjacent to the fins, and regenerated scales show shape modifications.
- **Standard sampling position:** the key is built on typical body scales taken from between the lateral line and the dorsal fin, which keeps regional variation from confounding identification.
- **Stocking pressure:** the presence of introduced hybrids in the lake is the ecological driver for this work. It complicates conservation assessment of the native *O. aureus* population.

## Key Findings

- **Central result:** the four forms (*S. galilaeus*, *O. niloticus*, *O. aureus*, and the *O. niloticus* × *O. aureus* hybrid) differ in scale shape, which allows identification by a dichotomous key.
- **Diagnostic characters in the key:**
  - Whether the fringes of the embedded portion of the scale are rounded.
  - Whether the scale is compressed dorso-ventrally.
  - Whether the fringes resemble those of *O. niloticus*.
- **Key outcomes (OCR partly garbled):**
  - Rounded fringes with dorso-ventral compression: *S. galilaeus* (Fig. 1a).
  - Scale not compressed dorso-ventrally: *O. niloticus* (Fig. 1b).
  - Fringes not rounded as in *O. niloticus*: hybrid (Fig. 1c).
  - *O. aureus* is shown in Fig. 1d, and the exact branch leading to it is not fully legible in the scan.
- **Hybrid parentage:** the hybrid is designated *O. niloticus* × *O. aureus*. The sex symbols in the source are garbled, so the cross direction cannot be confirmed from this text.
- **Caveats:**
  - Use the key with caution.
  - Examine several scales for any single identification.
  - Use body scales from the standard position only.
- **Sample size:** the note reports no sample sizes, counts or statistics. It is a method note supported by illustrated scale morphology (Fig. 1).
