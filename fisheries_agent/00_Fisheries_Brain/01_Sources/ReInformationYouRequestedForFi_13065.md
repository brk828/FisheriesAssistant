# Colorado River Fish Otolith Ageing Correspondence and Data

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of correspondence and tabulated data from an [[Otolith Ageing]] project on large-bodied fishes of the Colorado River basin. It contains three letters and a series of age-reading tables. The work was carried out under contract between [[Arizona State University]] (Dr. [[W. L. Minckley]]) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Colorado River Fish Project]], with otolith readings also made by [[David L. Schultz]] (University of Michigan) and [[Hawkins]] (Colorado State University).

The species covered are [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[White Sucker]], and several putative sucker hybrids. The authors describe the ages as tentative, because validation was limited and key biological information was unavailable. The management stakes were significant: the results were expected to inform a [[Biological Opinion]] for [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and water acquisition for the [[Yampa River]].

## Background & Description

**Correspondence (December 1987 to August 1988):**

- **Minckley to Tyus, 29 December 1987:** A transmittal of tentative ages for otoliths of Colorado Squawfish, Razorback Sucker, Flannelmouth Sucker, Bluehead Sucker, and putative sucker hybrids. It was sent as a first-phase draft report under Purchase Order 20181-87-00216 (dated 24 December 1986, amended 3 December 1987). Data consist of two independent readings per otolith, with a third reading to be completed by the end of January.
- **Schultz, 26 March 1987:** A cover letter from the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] enclosing otoliths, photographs, negatives, data, and summary analysis. It describes the sectioning method, which is published in press in the *North American Journal of Fisheries Management*.
- **Tyus (USFWS), 26 August 1988:** Requests finalization of the ageing contract for Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker. The letter notes that fish without capture dates were presumed to be summer collections, gathered by Karl Seethaler and others at Utah State. TL values and vertebrae ages were available for all fish from this location. The letter also encloses a preliminary draft manuscript on a flow study and asks for literature on drift of these species.

**Methods and sample handling:**

- **Structures:** Sagittal otoliths (labelled "lapillus" in the tables) and, for some fish, vertebrae.
- **Sectioning:** Because large sagittae are strongly convex on the outer surface and concave on the inner surface, planar sections removed many edge annuli before central annuli appeared. Schultz therefore sectioned in the [[Sagittal Plane Sectioning]]. The convex surface often showed the clearest annuli.
- **Viewing:** About 60x magnification under a compound microscope, with diaphragm adjusted for each otolith.
- **Annuli scoring:** Clear distinct lines were scored, along with apparent discontinuities in the bone (the only way to see outer-margin annuli). Daily growth increments were checked at about 150x for the first 2 to 4 annuli. True annuli show very fine, nearly disappearing increments. False annuli show widely spaced increments.
- **Age convention:** Age equals the number of annuli. A fish with 4 annuli is in its fifth year. Where areas differed, the count on the convex surface was generally used.
- **Table convention:** Ages are listed by reader (I, II, III), with "+" indicating a possible additional year. "X or Y" indicates uncertainty.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Known-age validation:** Only [[Razorback Sucker]] from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (derived from lower [[Colorado River Basin]] stock) were known-age fish. Validation was therefore limited, and a known-age [[Colorado River Squawfish]] set was proposed for future examination.
- **Missing biological data:** Fish sizes (standard length or TL) were withheld by prior agreement, and times of death were unknown. This prevented the use of edge annuli to refine age estimates and made it impossible to compare structures among individuals.
- **Growth patterns:** Colorado Squawfish appeared to grow slowly for the first 1 to 2 years, then accelerate for about 3 to 4 years, then slow again. This was noted as an assumption that otolith growth is proportional to body growth.
- **Regional comparison:** Otolith growth in upper basin Razorback Suckers closely resembled that of Razorback Suckers from [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Sample bias:** Flannelmouth and Bluehead suckers were selected for large size or apparent age, which may bias age estimates upward.
- **Management context:** The ageing results were to inform the [[Biological Opinion]] for Flaming Gorge Dam and water resources and acquisition decisions on the Yampa River.

## Key Findings

1. **Tentative status of ages:** Minckley (1987) asked that all ages be treated as tentative until a third reading was completed, the University of Michigan estimates were compared, and sizes and death times were resolved.
2. **Reader agreement issues:** Some readers gave substantially different counts for the same otolith. One University of Michigan low estimate for a Razorback Sucker was flagged as possibly aberrant.
3. **Preparation quality:** Results were affected by variable drying times and technician handling. Future protocols were proposed: soaking each otolith, then drying for a set time or under controlled conditions.
4. **Sectioning method:** Sagittal sectioning was used for large otoliths, especially Razorback Sucker, because planar sections removed too many edge annuli.
5. **Age ranges recorded (as read in the tables, subject to OCR uncertainty):**
   - Colorado Squawfish: roughly 2 to 13 annuli across the series, with some readings marked as uncertain.
   - Razorback Sucker: often in the 20s to 40s, with some very old-looking fish and some incomplete counts marked "+".
   - Flannelmouth Sucker: 13 to 18 or more annuli, with one putative hybrid reading of 21+ or 22/21/19.
   - Bluehead Sucker: about 8 to 13 annuli.
   - White Sucker: 8 and 11+ annuli on two readings.
   - Putative hybrids: Flannelmouth x Razorback reads of 21+ and 13, and a Flannelmouth x Bluehead read of 7 to 23.
6. **Specimen loss:** One Razorback Sucker otolith (00047) was lost or not present in shipments.
7. **Planned follow-up:** Additional small Colorado Squawfish (under 200 mm) were to be sectioned, and the University of Michigan researcher was to provide independent ages, sizes, and times of death.
8. **Publication timing:** Tyus (1988) wanted to publish quickly because of the management urgency. Minckley (1987) asked for delay until validation and reconciliation were complete.
