## Executive Summary

This study, conducted between 1952 and 1956 on [[Wheeler Reservoir]] (a Tennessee River mainstream impoundment in northern Alabama), quantified sport-fishing exploitation of [[Largemouth Bass]] via a tag-and-recapture program. A single sport fisherman tagged 595 largemouth bass using rod-and-reel capture, with tag returns incentivized by a local sportsmen's association paying one dollar per returned tag. Overall, 74 tags (12.4%) were recovered, with nearly all recoveries occurring within the same year of tagging or the following year. The study confirms that predator species such as largemouth bass and [[Spotted Bass]] experience substantially higher angling exploitation than pan species like [[White Crappie]] and [[Black Crappie]], and that harvest rates vary considerably year-to-year, consistent with patterns observed on other TVA reservoirs.

## Background & Description

- **Tagging method:** No. 3 Monel strap tag applied over the left maxillary and premaxillary bones.
- **Size range of tagged fish:** 5 to 18 inches total length.
- **Length distribution (n=595):**
  - 5–10 inches: ~20% of tagged fish
  - 11–14 inches: ~67% of tagged fish (modal size class)
  - 15–18 inches: ~16% of tagged fish
- **Untagged captures:** An additional 86 largemouth bass were caught but not tagged, distributed as: <10 in.—8, 10–13 in.—35, 14–15 in.—8, 16–17 in.—5, 18–19 in.—11, ≥20 in.—19.
- Data recorded per fish included date, capture location, species, and total length (inches).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[Wheeler Reservoir]] is characterized as a mainstream reservoir, distinct from TVA storage reservoirs (e.g., [[Norris Reservoir]], [[Cherokee Reservoir]], [[Watauga Reservoir]], [[South Holston Reservoir]]) in that water levels fluctuate much less.
- **Movement patterns:**
  - Average movement from tagging to recapture: 2.7 miles (max 22 miles).
  - Average time between tagging and recapture: 68.2 days (range: 23–717.9 days).
  - No significant difference between upstream (avg. 4.1 mi, n=27) and downstream (avg. 4.2 mi, n=17) movement.
  - Fish moving across current averaged 1.1 miles (n=10); those moving ≤0.25 mile averaged 0.2 mile (n=20).
  - Comparatively, largemouth bass in [[Norris Reservoir]] (a storage reservoir) moved further on average (4 miles) than in Wheeler, supporting the hypothesis that reduced water-level fluctuation in mainstream reservoirs limits fish movement relative to storage reservoirs.
  - [[Smallmouth Bass]] in Norris Reservoir moved less (1.2 miles average) than largemouth bass.
- **Comparative species movement (historical data through 1950):** Crappie averaged 3.2 miles in Wheeler and 2.0 miles in [[Guntersville Reservoir]]; [[White Bass]] averaged 5.1 miles in Wheeler.
- **Predator vs. pan-fish exploitation:** Largemouth bass and spotted bass (predator species) consistently show higher angler exploitation rates than crappies and other pan fish, aligning with findings from Manges (1950b) and Chance (1955).

## Key Findings

- **Overall tag return rate:** 74 of 595 tagged largemouth bass (12.4%) were recovered between 1952–1956.
- **Recovery timing:** 65 of 74 returns (88%) occurred in the same calendar year as tagging; the remaining 9 (12%) were recovered in the first calendar year after tagging. No fish were recovered beyond the first year post-tagging.
- **Annual variability in harvest:** Yearly recovery percentages ranged from 5.7% (1956 cohort) to 15.2% (1952 cohort), demonstrating significant year-to-year variability similar to that seen in other TVA reservoir studies.
- **Comparison with [[Spotted Bass]] on [[Guntersville Reservoir]]:** Of 1,592 spotted bass tagged (1952–1956), 248 tags (15.6%) were returned, with annual variation from 7.3% to 26.5% — a similar exploitation pattern to largemouth bass on Wheeler.
- **Comparison with storage reservoirs:** Largemouth bass harvest on [[Norris Reservoir]] (1947–1953) varied from 11.1% to 33.3%; on [[Cherokee Reservoir]], from 7.1% to 35.7% — generally higher than Wheeler's mainstream reservoir rates, suggesting storage reservoirs tend to produce higher harvest percentages.
- **Historical baseline (pre-1952 data):** Of 6,028 fish tagged on Wheeler through August 1950, only 1.5% of tags were returned; of 5,506 fish tagged on Guntersville, tag returns were also low. Notably, white crappie returns were only 0.8% and black crappie 1.0%, while spotted bass returns were markedly higher at 16.1%, reinforcing the predator/pan-fish exploitation disparity.
- **TVA-wide tag return context:** Other TVA storage reservoirs showed tag returns ranging from 17.4% ([[Norris Reservoir]]) and 26.3% ([[Hiwassee Reservoir]]) up to 40.8% ([[Watauga Reservoir]]) and 41.1% ([[South Holston Reservoir]]), indicating substantial variability in exploitation rates across reservoir types and management contexts.
- **Sustainability implication:** No evidence was found that year-round fishing adversely affected the annual largemouth bass population/crop in either TVA mainstream or storage reservoirs.
- **Methodological note:** This study represents a unique citizen-science collaboration, with a single angler (Hulse) performing all tagging via rod-and-reel/artificial lures (since largemouth bass are rarely captured in commercial hoop nets), and a local sportsmen's club ([[Flint Branch of the Morgan County Sportsmen and Conservation Association]]) financially incentivizing tag returns.
