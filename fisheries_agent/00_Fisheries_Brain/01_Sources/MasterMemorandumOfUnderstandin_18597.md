## Executive Summary

This document is a 1992 **Master Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** (FS No. 92-SMU-015) establishing a cooperative framework among the **USDA Forest Service (FS)**, **USDI Bureau of Land Management (BLM)**, and the **[[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC)**. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological fish data, the text is a policy/administrative instrument aimed at promoting conservation, public education, and stewardship of **native desert fish ecosystems** on federal lands. It quantifies the scale of federal aquatic habitat under FS and BLM jurisdiction and highlights the disproportionate importance of these lands for federally listed and candidate fish species under the **Endangered Species Act (ESA)**. The agreement formalizes collaboration on public outreach, training, restoration projects (e.g., "Bring Back The Natives"), and information exchange, without committing funds or creating binding programmatic obligations.

## Background & Description

No morphological, taxonomic, or anatomical data on specific fish species are provided in this source. The document does not describe body plans, meristics, coloration, or diagnostic features of any [[Native Desert Fish]] taxa. It refers generically to "native desert fishes" and "aquatic invertebrates" as a collective conservation target rather than detailing species-specific characteristics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not a biological survey, the MOU provides substantial **habitat and landscape-scale ecological context** relevant to desert fish conservation:

- **Forest Service (FS) lands**: 191 million acres across 43 states, encompassing:
  - ~128,000 miles of fishable streams and rivers
  - 2.2 million acres of ponds, lakes, and reservoirs
  - 16,500 miles of coastal and shoreline habitat

- **Bureau of Land Management (BLM) lands**: Over 270 million acres across 12 western states, encompassing:
  - ~155,000 miles of fishable streams and rivers
  - 4.2 million acres of ponds, lakes, and reservoirs

- These lands support a mix of ecological functions including **commercial fish production**, **sport fishing**, **non-consumptive uses** (fish viewing, interpretation), and **conservation of aquatic biological diversity**.

- Federal lands are explicitly identified as critical habitat reservoirs for imperiled aquatic species, situating [[Desert Fish Conservation]] within a broader framework of **riparian-wetland ecosystem management** (referencing the BLM's "Riparian-Wetland Initiative for the 1990's") and species-focused programs such as the FS's "Rise to the Future" fisheries program and "Every Species Counts" initiative.

- The DFC's role is framed as a **scientific-educational liaison**, connecting government agencies, academic institutions, and international stakeholders (U.S., Mexico, Canada) around desert fish ecosystem stewardship — implicitly acknowledging that desert fish habitats and species ranges cross national and jurisdictional boundaries.

## Key Findings

- **~46%** of fishes and aquatic invertebrates listed as **threatened or endangered** under the ESA occur on **National Forest System (NFS)** lands managed by the FS.
- **~36%** of ESA **candidate fish species** occur on NFS lands.
- **~46%** of ESA-listed fishes and **~30%** of ESA candidate fishes occur on **BLM-managed lands**.
- These statistics underscore that federal multiple-use lands (FS and BLM) collectively provide habitat for a disproportionately large share of the nation's imperiled fish fauna, particularly relevant to endemic and range-restricted [[Desert Fish]] species found in isolated springs, streams, and relict aquatic habitats of the arid West.
- The agreement establishes non-binding cooperative mechanisms (public information dissemination, training material development, annual DFC meetings, joint restoration projects) but explicitly **does not obligate funding** or create enforceable programmatic commitments — any funds/property transfers require separate, independently authorized agreements.
- The MOU is designed to remain in effect **indefinitely**, subject to amendment or 60-day termination notice by any party, reflecting a long-term institutional commitment to [[Aquatic Biodiversity Conservation]] on public lands rather than a time-limited research or management action.
- Signed by representatives of the DFC ([[Edwin P. Pister]]), BLM ([[Cy Jamison]]), and FS ([[George M. Leonard]] / [[F. Dale Robertson]]) between March and April 1992.
