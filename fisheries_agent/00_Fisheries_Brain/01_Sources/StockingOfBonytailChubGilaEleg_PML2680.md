# Bonytail Chub Stocking into Davis Cove, Lake Mohave (October 1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-elegans

## Executive Summary

This document is a 6 October 1992 memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Parker Fish and Wildlife Office]] (Parker FAO, Arizona). It reports the stocking of hatchery-reared [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) into [[Davis Cove]] on [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona, on 5 October 1992. About 6,617 fish were transferred from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and released within two hours of receipt, with mortality estimated below 0.5%. Two further pages are largely illegible handwritten tallies and a partly completed stocking record form.

## Background & Description

The memorandum is addressed to Jim Hansen, Project Leader at the Pinetop FAO, and George Divine of the Albuquerque endangered species files, with copies to interested persons. It was prepared by the Fishery Biologist at the Parker FAO and concerns the stocking of bonytail chub, a native Colorado River fish that appears in the document as a species of conservation concern under the endangered species program.

The fish originated at Dexter National Fish Hatchery. Personnel named as Sosa and Lay met the transport at [[Katherines Landing]], Lake Mohave, at 1300 hours.

The scanned record also includes two pages of handwritten or numeric entries that are not legible enough to extract reliable values. Page 2 is a partly filled stocking record form with headings for date, total miles, total cost, species, number of applications, farm ponds stocked, and acreage stocked. Most entries are missing or unreadable, so the form contributes no usable data beyond its structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Davis Cove is an embayment of Lake Mohave, a reservoir on the lower Colorado River. The memorandum describes the cove as the release site, where fish were held briefly in a live car to allow mortality culling and measurement before release.

Handling was designed to minimise stress. Fish moved from the hatchery live box into a live box on a [[Bureau of Reclamation]] boat and were taken straight to the cove. The memorandum records that less than two hours elapsed between receipt and release.

Sampling of mortalities supports genetic monitoring. All dead fish were frozen for [[Mtdna Analysis]] (mitochondrial DNA) and [[Allozyme Analysis]], which are methods for assessing the genetic origin and diversity of the stocked population. The memorandum does not state the outcome of these analyses.

The Lake Mead National Recreation Area was represented at the release by Ross Haley, which indicates that the reservoir is managed jointly by several agencies. The [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]] is part of the broader management context for Lake Mohave.

## Key Findings

- **Stocking event:** On 5 October 1992, approximately 6,617 bonytail chub were transferred from Dexter National Fish Hatchery and released into Davis Cove, Lake Mohave, Arizona.
- **Transport and release timing:** The interval between receipt of the fish and their release was less than 2 hours.
- **Mortality:** The memorandum estimates mortality at less than 0.5% (the scanned text reads "<0.54"). Dead fish were frozen for genetic analysis.
- **Size range:** Fish ranged from 38 to 314 mm total length, with a mean total length of 71.69 (units not stated in the scan, presumably mm).
- **Sampling:** The total length of 200 individuals was measured before release, with mortality culled first in a live car.
- **Genetic sampling:** Mortalities were preserved for [[Mtdna Analysis]] and [[Allozyme Analysis]].
- **Personnel:** Participants included Tom Burke, Joe Kahl, Gene Herzog and an assistant (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation); Ross Haley (Lake Mead National Recreation Area); and C.O. Minckley and Dixie Potter (Parker FAO).
- **Unreadable pages:** Pages 1 and 2 contain largely illegible handwritten or numeric tallies and an incomplete stocking record form. No reliable values could be extracted from them.
