# Removal of Radiophosphorus Contaminant from Water in an Aquarium Community

#ichthyology #marine-biology #radioecology

## Executive Summary

This 1952 Hanford report by R. H. Whittaker (document HW-28636, declassified) uses three experimental aquaria containing a small biological community to trace how [[Radiophosphorus]] (P-32) moves through water. Living components, including [[Plankton]], [[Side-Wall Algae]], snails and small fish, rapidly absorbed and concentrated the tracer, while [[Sediment Adsorption]] and surface binding removed the remainder. Within the 18-day-plus experiment, most of the tracer left the water, with roughly 53% of the initial amount assumed to be bound to aquarium surfaces. The study was framed as a model for understanding radioisotope contamination hazards in natural waters near the [[Hanford Site]] on the [[Columbia River]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To study the transfer and concentration of P-32 in an aquatic community and to inform problems of radioisotope contamination in aquatic environments.
- **Isotope context:** Radiophosphorus is a radioactive isotope of phosphorus released in effluent from Hanford reactor operations. Earlier Hanford documents cited in the bibliography examined radioactive plankton and invertebrates, and the accumulation of radioactivity in Columbia River fish near the works.
- **Experimental system:** Three 60-gallon aquaria were lined with aluminum trays (most holding a shallow mud layer), with two glass side walls that could be removed for sampling side-wall organisms.
- **Setup timeline:**
  - October 9, 1952: aquaria filled with uncontaminated [[Columbia River]] water and seeded with pond plankton organisms.
  - Six weeks of development without interference.
  - November 20, 1952: twelve snails and twelve small fish added to each aquarium, followed by the P-32 tracer.
- **Sampling:** Water, plankton, algae, mud, sediment, fish and snails were sampled and their activity compared with the water. The tracer amount is garbled in the scan and cannot be reliably reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Biological concentration:** Living components concentrated P-32 far above water levels. Activity density ratios (activity per unit mass relative to water) were:

| Community fraction | Activity density ratio (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Plankton | ~360,000 (abstract reads ~300,000) |
| Side-wall algae | 300,000 |
| Organic sediment | 200,000 |
| Fish | 120,000 |
| Snail body | 9,000 |
| Algae (dry weight) | 5,400 |
| Bottom mud | low (OCR garbled) |
| Snail shell | low (OCR garbled) |

- **Comparison with other systems:** Whittaker compared these values with a lake studied by [[Coffin et al.]] (about 40,000) and river phytoplankton on the Columbia River at Hanford studied by [[Cooper]] (about 2,700). Cooper's value represented only a few hours of exposure and included other radioisotopes, some scarcely absorbed by organisms.
- **Phosphorus cycling:** The work follows [[Phosphorus Cycling]] research in lakes by [[Hutchinson]] and [[Hayes et al.]], which showed that phosphorus exchange between water and biota is an important removal pathway.
- **Hazard implication:** Biological processes can strongly amplify radioisotope concentrations, so contamination hazards can exceed those predicted from water activity alone.

## Key Findings

1. **Rapid initial uptake by plankton:** Plankton absorbed P-32 quickly. Maximum plankton activity was reached about 15 hours after tracer introduction, and plankton activity then declined.
2. **Intermediate phase on surfaces:** Side-wall algae and bottom algae and mud became the main reservoirs, stabilizing at about 18 days, while plankton activity dropped.
3. **Slow final phase:** Sedimentation from plankton and binding to surfaces dominated the late stage, gradually accumulating the remaining P-32 in sediments and on surfaces while other fractions declined.
4. **Mass balance at experiment end:** A deficit of 72% of the introduced P-32 was recorded. Accounting for this:
   - Up to 8% in fish and snails.
   - About 5% in dense algae growing on the putty sealing the aquarium walls.
   - About 6% on aquarium side walls above the water line.
   - The remaining 53% was assumed to have become closely associated with aquarium surfaces, mainly through adsorption and biological uptake by bacteria and other organisms on surfaces.
5. **Surface removal evidence:** Acid removal from surfaces gave about 3.7 × 10^? activity per glass plate and about 4.7 × 10^? for the bottom tray. The exponents are garbled in the scan. Whittaker noted that these values could not be directly extrapolated to the enamel walls and bottoms.
6. **Comparison with lake data (Hayes et al.):** Of the P-32 introduced to a lake, about 50% remained in water and plankton after 2 days, 10% after 21 days, and 4% after 60 days. The aquarium pattern matched this fast-then-slow removal.
7. **Three-phase removal model:**
   - Initial phase: rapid absorption by plankton.
   - Intermediate phase: the largest share moves into side-wall and bottom algae and mud while plankton activity declines.
   - Final phase: slow sedimentation and surface binding dominate.

The document is a partly garbled OCR scan. Several numbers (the tracer quantity, some exponents, the bottom-mud and snail-shell ratios, and some timing values) are illegible, so they are reported only where the text is reasonably clear. Declassification markings appear on each page and were ignored.
