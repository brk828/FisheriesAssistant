# Fishes and Amphibians of Lake Kinneret

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This chapter, from the [[Lake Kinneret]] monograph (Volume 32 of *Monographiae Biologicae*, edited by J. Illies and C. Serruya, 1978), surveys the fish fauna of the lake, its zoogeographical origins, the taxonomy of its species, and the development of its commercial fishery. The fauna comprises 24 permanent species in eight families, five of which were introduced by humans. Endemism is high, and the pelagic [[Mirogrex Terraesanctae]] (lavnun) dominates both biomass and catch. The chapter closes with a brief note on three amphibian species of the Jordan Valley. The author states that knowledge of the fish is "far from being satisfactory," particularly concerning life histories and population dynamics.

## Background & Description

The lake's fishes have been documented since antiquity. [[Josephus Flavius]] referred to *Clarias lazera* (under the Roman name *Coracinus*), and Hasselquist (1757) provided material from which Artedi described *Sarotherodon galilaeus*. The systematic treatment follows Goren (1974), with nomenclatural changes noted:

- *Sarotherodon* is used for *S. galilaeus* and *S. aureus*, formerly *Tilapia* (after Trewavas, 1973).
- *Tor canis* replaces *Barbus canis* (after Karaman, 1971).
- *Tylognathus steinitziorum* is treated as a junior synonym of *Hemigrammocapoeta nanus*.

A dichotomous key to fresh-water fish families is provided, followed by species accounts. Each account gives biology, size, distribution and fishing data. Representative species include:

- **[[Mirogrex Terraesanctae]]**: a schooling planktivore that forms large schools near the surface, feeds mainly on Cladocera, and is endemic to the lake.
- **[[Sarotherodon Galilaeus]]**: a mouthbrooding cichlid that feeds mostly on phytoplankton, especially *Peridinium cinctum* fa. *westii*.
- **[[Sarotherodon Aureus]]**: a mouthbrooding cichlid, cultivated in ponds since 1956.
- **[[Barbus Longiceps]]** and **[[Tor Canis]]**: important commercial bottom and pelagic barbels.
- **[[Clarias Lazera]]**: a demersal catfish able to breathe air and move short distances on land.
- **[[Gambusia Affinis]]**: introduced in the 1920s for mosquito control, bearing 2 to 100 young per brood.
- **[[Hypophthalmichthys Molitrix]]** ([[Silver Carp]]): introduced in 1969 and a pelagic plankton feeder.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Zoogeography.** Nineteen native species or subspecies represent 66% of the 29 species known from former Palestine and the Golan Heights. The lake lies in the [[Levant]], a transitional zone between Palaearctic and Palaeotropic faunas that has served as a faunal bridge between Eurasia and Africa since the Miocene. Exchange likely occurred through the [[Jordan River System]] and Rift Valley rivers. Of the species listed in the zoogeographical table, nine are Ethiopian, six Palaearctic, two Oriental and two Tethys relicts (*Aphanius mento* and *Salaria fluviatilis*). Almost half of all species are of Ethiopian origin, including all the cichlids.

**Endemism.** Endemic forms make up 25% of the lake's species. These include *Mirogrex terraesanctae terraesanctae*, *Tristramella simonis simonis* and *Tristramella sacra*, all found only in the lake, and *Haplochromis flaviijosephi*, endemic to Israel. The author notes that this points to considerable speciation within the Jordan Valley system.

**Habitat use.** Most species are benthic and inhabit shallow inshore waters. Pelagic species are fewer but dominate fish biomass. *Aphanius mento* and *Gambusia affinis* live among submerged vegetation. Spawning habits vary: several Cyprinidae spawn in winter in shallow waters and tributary streams, while *Tor canis* spawns in summer.

**Reproductive timing.** *Mirogrex terraesanctae* begins spawning in late November, when water temperature falls below 20 °C, and continues until mid-March. Eggs are demersal, mainly along the eastern shores.

**Amphibians.** Three amphibians occur in the Jordan Valley: *Bufo viridis* (green toad), *Hyla arborea* (lemon yellow tree frog) and *Rana ridibunda* (edible frog). *Rana ridibunda* lays eggs on lake beaches only in very quiet water, because it is disturbed by turbulence, and also uses irrigation canals. *Bufo viridis* spawns in vegetation-free pools, whereas *Hyla arborea* uses vegetation in shallow pools.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 24 permanent species belong to eight families and 20 genera. Cyprinids (10 species) and cichlids (7) are the most diverse. Mugilids have two species, and Cobitidae, Clariidae, Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae and Blenniidae have one each.
- **Introductions:** Five species were introduced in the last 50 years: *Gambusia affinis*, *Cyprinus carpio*, *Mugil cephalus*, *Liza ramada* and *Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*. Incidental arrivals include a juvenile *Dicentrarchus labrax* and an adult salmonid, probably *Salmo gairdneri*, from Kibbutz Dan.
- **Origin categories:** Primary freshwater fishes (Cyprinidae, Cobitidae, Clariidae); secondary freshwater fishes (Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae, Cichlidae, Blenniidae); euryhaline marine species introduced by humans (Mugilidae).
- **Fishery history:** Catches were 200 to 500 t during the British Mandate. Catches reached 1,000 t in 1957 and a peak above 2,000 t in 1971. The 1971–1975 average was 1,682 t.
- **Catch composition (1971–1975 average):** lavnun 61.3%, cichlids 16%, grey mullets 12%, all others 11%.
- **Lavnun dependence:** Total yield depends largely on lavnun. Annual catch is 700 to 1,100 t, about half the lake's total, taken mostly by purse-seine with light attraction.
- **Management:** Legal mesh sizes were set in the 1930s–40s (lavnun 14 mm today; large cyprinids 37 mm today; cichlid trammel-nets 45 mm). Minimum landing sizes are 30 cm for commercial cyprinids, 18 cm for cichlids and 12 cm for lavnun.
- **Stocking effects:** *Sarotherodon aureus* stocking (from 350,000 fingerlings in 1958 to almost 2,500,000 in 1972) was estimated at about 60 t per million fingerlings, with 25–30% reaching commercial size. Stocking had no detectable effect on *Sarotherodon galilaeus* landings.
- **Grey mullet stocking:** 1 to 3 million fingerlings were stocked annually from 1962, since the mullets do not reproduce in the lake. Recent Mediterranean fry shortages have limited stocking.
- **Silver carp:** Introduced with 40,000 fingerlings in 1969, 25,000 in 1970 and over one million in 1974. It does not reproduce in the lake, reaches about 18 kg in five years, and yielded 23 t in 1975.
- **Knowledge gaps:** Life-history data are missing for most species, and population dynamics relative to the fishery are poorly understood. The author calls for further studies to separate environmental effects from fishing pressure.
