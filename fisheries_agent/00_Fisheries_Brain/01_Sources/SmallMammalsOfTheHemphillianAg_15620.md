# Small Mammals of the Hemphillian White Cone Local Fauna, Northeastern Arizona

#paleontology #mammalia #rodentia #cricetidae #heteromyidae

## Executive Summary

This paleontological paper by [[Jon A. Baskin]] describes the small-mammal assemblage of the [[White Cone Local Fauna]], collected from the upper member of the [[Bidahochi Formation]] in northeastern Arizona. The fauna comprises two insectivore taxa, one rabbit, and eleven rodent taxa. Two new rodent species are named: [[Perognathoides Bidahochiensis]] and [[Paronychomys Alticuspis]]. The author concludes the fauna is middle to late [[Hemphillian Land Mammal Age]] (late Miocene), not the latest Hemphillian as previously assumed, and that it is among the earliest known late Hemphillian faunas. The fauna also provides evidence for a Hemphillian radiation of cricetine rodents in southwestern North America, including [[Calomys]], the earliest known Neotropical cricetine.

The document as supplied also contains a bibliography page, a short journal announcement, and a fragmentary note on fossil sponges. These are summarized at the end.

## Background & Description

The White Cone fauna was well known in mammalian paleontology because it appears in the Wood Committee correlation report (Wood et al., 1941) and in the [[Potassium-Argon Dating]] chronology of North American Cenozoic rocks (Evernden et al., 1964). Its small mammals had been largely ignored since [[Dipoides Williamsi]] was described by Stirton (1936). In 1973 the University of Arizona Laboratory of Paleontology (UALP) began screen-washing sediments from White Cone Peak. Nearly all material was collected for this study and is on loan to UALP, owned by the Navajo and Hopi Indian Tribes. Specimens carry the prefix WC.

The author reports measurements in millimeters. Abbreviations include N (number), O.R. (observed range), and X (mean).

**Systematic content (summary):**

- **Insectivora:** ?Domninoides sp. (Talpidae), a right M₁ (WC 21; length 2.7, width 1.8 mm). *Notiosorex* sp. (Soricidae) is based on five specimens. It is the first pre-Blancan record of the genus.
- **Lagomorpha:** [[Hypolagus Vetus]] (Leporidae), represented by many isolated teeth. Known also from Thousand Creek (Nevada), Rome (Oregon), and Juniper Creek (Oregon).
- **Rodentia:**
  - [[Mylagaulus]] cf. *monodon* (Mylagaulidae): two premolars (WC 28 length 12.8, width 6.4 mm; WC 30 length 12.4, width 5.3 mm).
  - [[Spermophilus]] sp. (Sciuridae): a very small ground squirrel similar in size to *S. gidleyi*, but lacking a mesostylid.
  - Sciurinae gen. et sp. indet.: two teeth too incomplete for generic assignment.
  - [[Dipoides Williamsi]] (Castoridae): the most abundant beaver. Its referred material is large, and the author provides an emended diagnosis and comparisons with *D. stirtoni*, *D. smithi*, *D. wilsoni*, and *D. vallicula*.
  - [[Perognathus]] sp. (Heteromyidae): a very small species, known from isolated teeth.
  - [[Perognathoides Bidahochiensis]] (Heteromyidae), new species. The holotype is WC 66, a right P₄. Its diagnosis includes mesolophodont cheek teeth, an accessory cuspule on P⁴, and a hypostylid on P₄.
  - [[Copemys]] sp. (Cricetidae): transitional between advanced *Copemys* and primitive *Peromyscus*.
  - [[Paronychomys Alticuspis]] (Cricetidae), new species. The holotype is WC 249, a left M₁. Its name refers to steep-walled cusps. It is slightly smaller than *P. lemredfieldi*.
  - Cricetinae gen. et sp. indet. (Cricetidae): two molars, with comparisons to *Gnomomys saltus* and Blancan material.
- **Calomys:** Two subgenus *Bensonomys* species, *C. yazhi* and *C. gidleyi*, were described elsewhere (Baskin, 1978). Their primitive features suggest that Neotropical cricetines evolved in North America and dispersed to South America in the late Pliocene to early Pleistocene.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stratigraphy and dating.** The fauna lies about 20 m above the volcanic (limburgite tuff) middle member of the Bidahochi Formation. Evernden et al. (1964) dated the middle member at 4.1 Ma, which had made White Cone the youngest radiometrically dated Hemphillian fauna. That date is considered a minimum, because of the fine grain size of the plagioclase. A basalt traced into the middle member at Roberts Mesa, about 15 km northwest, was dated at 6.69 ± 0.16 Ma (Scarborough et al., 1974). This places White Cone early in the known late Hemphillian sequence. The author notes that the dated basalt at White Cone itself comes from a diatreme unconformable on the upper member.

**Paleomagnetism.** The Bidahochi Formation is predominantly normal polarity with two short reversals. The basalt dated at 6.69 Ma lies in a lower reversed event. The middle normal zone containing the White Cone fauna probably corresponds to the approximately 6.3 Ma or 6.6 Ma event in the paleomagnetic time scale.

**Correlation.** The Coffee Ranch local fauna, which lies in the stratotype of the Hemphillian, is dated at 6.6 ± 0.8 Ma by zircon fission tracks, and may share this normal zone. Younger glass-shard dates for Coffee Ranch (4.7 and 5.3 Ma) are considered too low, possibly because of track annealing.

**Paleoclimate.** The author cites increasing aridity beginning in the Hemphillian as a likely driver of cricetine diversification. Hypsodonty and a shift toward grazing diets, marked by simplified dental patterns and cusp alignment for oblique shear, are interpreted as adaptations to semi-arid environments. These adaptations may have allowed Calomys to follow a savanna corridor into South America after formation of the Panamanian land bridge.

**Mollusks.** Taylor (1966) identified nine mollusk species from the fauna, two of which are extinct. He assigned an early to middle Hemphillian age, and the fauna shows little resemblance to early Blancan faunas of Arizona and Kansas.

## Key Findings

1. **Age revised.** The White Cone fauna is Hemphillian (latest Miocene) in age, but not latest Hemphillian. It is one of the earliest known late Hemphillian faunas.
2. **Index taxa.** The co-occurrence of [[Mylagaulus]] and [[Dipoides Williamsi]] is characteristic of Hemphillian rodent faunas. The derived status of *D. williamsi* relative to *D. stirtoni* points to a middle to late Hemphillian age. The species also occurs in the late Hemphillian Chamita Formation of New Mexico.
3. **Immigrant taxon.** [[Plesiogulo]] is a Eurasian immigrant whose first appearance marks the late Hemphillian. Its presence supports the older date for Coffee Ranch and the lower boundary of the late Hemphillian.
4. **Relict taxa.** Survival of ?*Domninoides*, *Perognathoides*, and *Copemys*, which are typical of older deposits, supports a pre-latest Hemphillian age.
5. **New species.** Two new species are named: *Perognathoides bidahochiensis* and *Paronychomys alticuspis*.
6. **First record.** *Notiosorex* is recorded for the first time from pre-Blancan deposits.
7. **Cricetine radiation.** At least five autochthonous cricetine genera are known from the late Hemphillian of Arizona. The author argues this regional diversity marks the start of a radiation that later expanded explosively in South America.
8. **Calomys origin.** The early North American record of *Calomys* supports a North American origin for Neotropical cricetines.
