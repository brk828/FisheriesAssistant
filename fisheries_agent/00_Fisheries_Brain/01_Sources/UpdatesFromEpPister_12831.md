# Desert Fishes Council Administrative Correspondence and Book Announcement

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #conservation #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

Two administrative memoranda from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] dated May 31, 1990, announcing organizational changes and soliciting financial support for a major conservation biology publication. The correspondence documents the retirement of Executive Secretary E.P. Pister from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] to focus on desert fish conservation, and announces progress on the book "Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West." The documents also reference upcoming symposium activities in [[Ensenada]] featuring the [[San Pedro Martir Trout]] ([[Oncorhynchus mykiss nelsoni]]).

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Fishes Council]] is an organization dedicated to the preservation of America's desert fishes. As of February 7, 1990, the Executive Secretary transitioned from state employment to focus exclusively on conservation biology of desert fishes, Council administration, and teaching environmental philosophy and ethics. New headquarters established in [[Bishop, California]] (P.O. Box 337, telephone 619-872-8751).

The [[San Pedro Martir Trout]] ([[Oncorhynchus mykiss nelsoni]]) is identified as a "remarkable fish" inhabiting the [[Rio San Rafael]] in the [[San Pedro Martir]] region. This subspecies is the subject of upcoming Ph.D. research by Gorgonio Ruiz under Salvador Contreras at la Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo León.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The correspondence reflects the broader context of western North American desert fish conservation in 1990. The [[Desert Fishes Council]] serves as a coordinating body for conservation efforts across the American West, with international collaboration extending to [[Mexico]]. The organization maintains regular symposia and publishes proceedings volumes documenting conservation biology research.

The [[Rio San Rafael]] habitat represents critical habitat for the endemic [[San Pedro Martir Trout]], warranting field trip attention during the November 1990 symposium. The broader conservation effort encompasses "virtually all phases of the conservation biology of western fishes" as indicated by the scope of the forthcoming book.
